> [!abstract] Severe IBD Flare
> Life-threatening acute exacerbation of **Ulcerative Colitis (Acute Severe Ulcerative Colitis [ASUC])** or **Crohn's Disease** marked by systemic toxicity, high-output bloody diarrhea, or transmural complications (abscess, obstruction, toxic megacolon, perforation), carrying a **20–30% risk of emergent colectomy** during index admission.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Mucosal / Transmural Cytokine Storm ($\text{TNF-}\alpha, \text{IL-1}, \text{IL-6}$):** Deep ulceration strips the colonic epithelium $\rightarrow$ massive intraluminal loss of blood, water, potassium, and albumin (**protein-losing enteropathy**, which accelerates renal/fecal clearance of Infliximab!).
> - **Toxic Megacolon:** Inflammation extends into the muscularis propria and myenteric plexus $\rightarrow$ neutrophils and macrophages generate massive **Inducible Nitric Oxide Synthase (iNOS / NO)** $\rightarrow$ smooth muscle paralysis, acute non-obstructive colonic dilation (**>6 cm**), bacterial translocation, and free perforation.
> - **Prothrombotic State:** Systemic inflammation upregulates tissue factor/platelets and depletes antithrombin $\rightarrow$ **3–4× increased risk of fatal DVT/PE and mesenteric vein thrombosis**.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Give DVT Prophylaxis (LMWH) EVEN with bloody stools:** Active severe IBD is a massive hypercoagulable state; **pharmacologic VTE prophylaxis (subcutaneous Enoxaparin) is MANDATORY on Day 1** in all hospitalized IBD flares and does _not_ worsen mucosal rectal bleeding (withhold only if life-threatening hemodynamic hemorrhage).
> - **Day 3 is the critical decision point in ASUC (Oxford / Travis Criteria):** Assess response after **72 hours of IV corticosteroids**: if the patient still has **>8 stools/day**, OR **3–8 stools/day with CRP >45 mg/L**, there is an **85% probability of needing colectomy**—start **Rescue Therapy (Infliximab or Cyclosporine)** or proceed to surgery immediately!
> - **Always rule out _C. difficile_ AND CMV on admission:** Up to 10–15% of severe flares are driven by **_C. difficile_** (stool toxin/PCR) or **Cytomegalovirus (CMV) reactivation** (diagnosed via **unprepped flexible sigmoidoscopy with mucosal biopsy**—serum CMV PCR alone is insufficient).
> - **In Crohn's flares, CT BEFORE Steroids if fever/mass:** Never start high-dose IV steroids or Infliximab in a febrile Crohn's patient with focal abdominal pain until **CT Abdomen/Pelvis** rules out an **intra-abdominal or perirectal abscess** (drain the abscess first!).

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Acute Severe Ulcerative Colitis (ASUC):** **≥6 bloody bowel movements per day** (often 10–20/day), nocturnal bloody diarrhea, severe tenesmus, crampy abdominal pain, anorexia, fever, malaise, and orthostatic dizziness.
> - **Severe Crohn's Flare:** Severe RLQ or diffuse abdominal pain, high fever/rigors (suggests abscess/perforation), bilious vomiting + obstipation (suggests acute-on-chronic stricture/obstruction), or severe perianal pain/purulent drainage.
> - **Mandatory Trigger History:** **Recent NSAID use** (major flare precipitant), **recent antibiotics** (_C. difficile_), **non-adherence or abrupt cessation of biologics/steroids**, recent **smoking cessation** (in UC) or **active smoking** (in Crohn's), or recent use of **opioids, loperamide, or anticholinergics** (precipitates toxic megacolon).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Systemic Toxicity:** **Fever (>37.8°C)**, **tachycardia (>90 bpm)**, orthostatic or supine hypotension, pallor, cachexia, peripheral edema (hypoalbuminemia).
> - **Abdominal Exam:**
>   - _Uncomplicated Severe Colitis:_ Diffuse or LLQ tenderness without rigidity.
>   - _Toxic Megacolon / Perforation:_ Progressive **tympanitic abdominal distention**, **hypoactive or absent bowel sounds**, and **rebound tenderness / involuntary guarding** (_Warning:_ **High-dose corticosteroids mask fever, leukocytosis, and peritoneal signs**—a patient on steroids can have a **free colonic perforation with a deceptively soft abdomen**!).
> - **Perianal Inspection:** Check for fluctuant **perianal/ischiorectal abscesses** or draining fistulas in Crohn's disease.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Acute Severe Ulcerative Colitis (ASUC) — Truelove and Witts Criteria (Requires BOTH):**
>   1. **Bloody Stool Frequency ≥6 per day**, **PLUS**
>   2. **≥1 Marker of Systemic Toxicity:**
>      - **Temperature >37.8°C**,
>      - **Heart Rate >90 bpm**,
>      - **Hemoglobin <10.5 g/dL**, OR
>      - **ESR >30 mm/hr (or CRP >30 mg/L)**.
> - **Toxic Megacolon — Jalan's Criteria (Requires Radiographic Dilation + ≥3 Systemic Criteria + ≥1 Toxicity Criterion):**
>   1. **Radiographic Colonic Dilation:** **Transverse colon (or right colon) diameter >6.0 cm** (or cecum >9 cm) with loss of haustration on abdominal X-ray or CT, **PLUS**
>   2. **≥3 of 4 Systemic Signs:** **Fever >38.0°C**, **HR >120 bpm**, **Neutrophilic Leukocytosis >10,500/µL**, or **Anemia**, **PLUS**
>   3. **≥1 of 4 Toxicity Features:** **Dehydration**, **Altered Mental Status**, **Electrolyte Derangements**, or **Hypotension**.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs (Admission "Day 1" Bundle)**
>
> - **Inflammatory & Resuscitation Labs (Check Daily):** CBC, CMP (**Potassium and Magnesium** depletion directly worsen colonic dilation; **Serum Albumin <2.5–3.0 g/dL** predicts steroid failure and rapid fecal clearance of Infliximab), **CRP** (daily to track steroid response), PT/INR, Type and Screen, Lactate.
> - **Mandatory Infectious Exclusion:**
>   1. **Stool _Clostridioides difficile_ GDH + Toxin EIA / PCR** (mandatory in 100% of hospitalized IBD flares!).
>   2. **Stool Multiplex Enteric PCR / Culture** (_Campylobacter, Salmonella, Shigella, EHEC_) + **Ova & Parasites (_Entamoeba histolytica_)** if travel/endemic exposure.
>   3. **Blood Cultures (×2 sets)** if febrile (>38.0°C) or toxic.
> - **Pre-Rescue Biologic / Cyclosporine Labs (Order on Admission Day 1 — Do NOT Wait Until Day 3!):**
>   - **QuantiFERON-TB Gold (IGRA) + Chest X-Ray** (for Infliximab/Upadacitinib).
>   - **Hepatitis B Triple Panel (HBsAg, Anti-HBs, Total Anti-HBc)** + HIV.
>   - **Fasting Lipid Panel (Total Cholesterol) + Serum Magnesium + Creatinine:** Mandatory before **IV Cyclosporine** (severe **hypocholesterolemia [<100–120 mg/dL] and hypomagnesemia** markedly increase the risk of **cyclosporine-induced grand mal seizures**!).
>
> **Imaging & Endoscopy**
>
> - **Supine & Upright Abdominal X-Ray (KUB) + Upright Chest X-Ray:** Order on admission and **repeat daily** if abdominal distention, pain, or toxicity is present to measure **transverse colon diameter (>6 cm = Toxic Megacolon)** and detect subdiaphragmatic **free air (perforation)** or mucosal "thumbprinting" (severe wall edema).
> - **CT Abdomen and Pelvis with IV Contrast:**
>   - **Mandatory first-line test in Severe Crohn's Disease Flares** to rule out **intra-abdominal/pelvic abscesses**, **fistulas**, **mechanical small bowel obstruction**, or **microperforation** _before_ escalating immunosuppression.
>   - Order in ASUC if perforation, toxic megacolon, or ischemic/infectious mimic is suspected.
> - **Unprepped Flexible Sigmoidoscopy with Mucosal Biopsy (Within 24–48 Hours of Admission in ASUC):**
>   - **Mandatory** to assess endoscopic severity (deep punched-out ulcers predict steroid failure) AND to obtain **mucosal biopsies from ulcer bases for H&E and Immunohistochemistry (IHC) to rule out Cytomegalovirus (CMV) Colitis**!
>   - _Safety Rule:_ **Perform WITHOUT bowel preparation and with minimal $\text{CO}_2$ insufflation**; **NEVER perform a full colonoscopy** in ASUC due to extreme perforation risk!

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Transverse colon diameter >6 cm** on KUB/CT + fever/tachycardia ($\rightarrow$ **Toxic Megacolon**).
> - **Pneumoperitoneum** (free air) or generalized peritonitis ($\rightarrow$ **Colonic Perforation**—mortality approaches 30–50%; requires **immediate subtotal colectomy**).
> - **Failure to respond by Day 3 of IV Corticosteroids (Travis/Oxford Criteria):** **>8 stools/day** OR **3–8 stools/day + CRP >45 mg/L** on Day 3 ($\rightarrow$ 85% steroid failure rate; must initiate rescue therapy or colectomy on Day 3!).
> - **Massive lower GI hemorrhage** causing hemodynamic instability or requiring ongoing pRBC transfusions.
> - **Intra-abdominal or perirectal abscess on CT** in Crohn's disease (absolute contraindication to starting Anti-TNF until drained).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss (Superinfections & Mimics of Severe Flare)**
>
> - **Superimposed _Clostridioides difficile_ Colitis:** Up to 10× more common in IBD patients; often lacks classic pseudomembranes on endoscopy in IBD; dramatically increases colectomy and mortality risk.
> - **Cytomegalovirus (CMV) Colitis Reactivation:** Occurs in **30–35% of steroid-refractory ASUC patients**; diagnosed by **"owl's eye" intranuclear viral inclusions and positive CMV Immunohistochemistry (IHC)** on sigmoidoscopy biopsy.
> - **Fulminant _Entamoeba histolytica_ Colitis (Ameboma / Toxic Megacolon):** Travel/immigrant history; giving high-dose corticosteroids to undiagnosed amebic colitis causes **fatal colonic perforation (>50% mortality)**.
> - **Ischemic Colitis or Mesenteric Venous Thrombosis:** IBD patients are hypercoagulable and at high risk for **portal/mesenteric vein thrombosis** presenting with acute severe abdominal pain.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Hemodynamic stabilization, rapid control of mucosal inflammation (**<3 non-bloody stools/day and CRP drop ≥50% by Day 3–5**), and prevention of toxic megacolon or perforation.
> - **Secondary Target:** Achieve timely medical rescue by **Day 3–5** in steroid-non-responders OR perform **early Semi-Elective Subtotal Colectomy** before septic decompensation/perforation occurs.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (Day 1 Admission Protocol for ASUC)**
>
> - **Admit to a monitored inpatient unit, obtain Early Colorectal Surgery Consultation on Day 1** (joint medical-surgical co-management from Day 1 is mandatory for all ASUC patients!), and track strict daily stool counts (frequency, blood), vitals, abdominal exams, CBC, electrolytes, albumin, and CRP.
> - **Infuse isotonic IV crystalloids**, transfuse pRBCs if **Hb <7–8 g/dL**, and **aggressively replete Potassium ($\text{K}^+ >4.0\text{ mEq/L}$) and Magnesium ($\text{Mg}^{2+} >2.0\text{ mg/dL}$)** to prevent colonic atony and toxic megacolon.
> - **Start Pharmacologic VTE Prophylaxis immediately with Subcutaneous Low-Molecular-Weight Heparin (Enoxaparin 40 mg SC daily) or Unfractionated Heparin** in **ALL** hospitalized IBD patients (including those with non-life-threatening hematochezia).
> - **Administer First-Line IV Corticosteroids immediately:** **Methylprednisolone 60 mg IV daily (as 20 mg IV q8h or 30 mg IV q12h)** OR **Hydrocortisone 100 mg IV q6–8h** (_Note: Doses higher than Methylprednisolone 60 mg/day provide ZERO additional benefit; keep duration **strictly ≤3–5 days** before deciding on rescue/surgery, as extending IV steroids beyond 7–10 days is futile and doubles postoperative septic complications!_).
> - **Continue Enteral (Oral) Nutrition** in all patients who do not have toxic megacolon, perforation, or mechanical bowel obstruction (_Bowel rest / NPO does NOT improve colitis remission and worsens hypoalbuminemia and bacterial translocation!_).
> - **Treat Concurrent Infections Only When Indicated:**
>   - **Do NOT give routine empiric antibiotics** for uncomplicated ASUC without sepsis or toxic megacolon.
>   - **If _C. difficile_ is Positive:** Start **Oral Vancomycin (125 mg PO QID for 10–14 days)** or **Fidaxomicin (200 mg PO BID)** (use Vancomycin 500 mg PO/NG QID + IV Metronidazole 500 mg q8h if fulminant/ileus).
>   - **If CMV Colitis is Confirmed on Biopsy (High Viral Load / Deep Ulcers):** Start **IV Ganciclovir (5 mg/kg IV q12h)** (transitioning to oral Valganciclovir for 14–21 days) while tapering steroids.
>
> **Day 3–5 Medical Rescue Therapy (For Steroid-Refractory ASUC — ~30–40% of Patients)**
>
> - **Initiate Medical Rescue Therapy on Day 3 to 5** if the patient fails to respond to IV corticosteroids (provided there is NO toxic megacolon, free perforation, or surgical emergency):
>   - **Option 1 — Infliximab (First-Line in Biologic-Naive Patients):** Administer **Infliximab 5 mg/kg IV** (or **accelerated high-dose regimen: 10 mg/kg IV** at Day 0, with early repeat dose at Day 3–7 if **serum albumin is <2.5–3.0 g/dL** or CRP remains high, to overcome fecal protein loss of the antibody!).
>   - **Option 2 — IV Cyclosporine (Calcineurin Inhibitor; Equal Efficacy to Infliximab per CYSIF & CONSTRUCT Trials):** Administer **Continuous IV Cyclosporine infusion at 2 mg/kg/day** (targeting trough levels of 150–250 ng/mL; preferred if patient previously failed Anti-TNF and is bridging to **Vedolizumab or Ustekinumab/Risankizumab** for long-term maintenance; **add TMP-SMX for PJP prophylaxis**).
>   - **Option 3 — Upadacitinib (45 mg PO daily or 30 mg BID) or Tofacitinib (10 mg PO TID for 9 doses, then BID) (JAK Inhibitors):** Emerging rapid-acting oral rescue option for multi-biologic/Anti-TNF-refractory ASUC at specialized centers.
>
> **Management of Toxic Megacolon & Emergent Surgical Indications**
>
> - **Toxic Megacolon Protocol (First 24–48 Hours):** **Keep strictly NPO**, insert a **Nasogastric (NG) Tube** on suction, **STOP all opioids, anticholinergics, and loperamide immediately**, **replete $\text{K}^+$ and $\text{Mg}^{2+}$**, instruct the patient to **roll into the Prone or Knee-Chest Position for 10–15 minutes every 2–3 hours** (redistributes colonic gas to the rectum to aid decompression!), and start **Broad-Spectrum IV Antibiotics (Ceftriaxone + Metronidazole OR Piperacillin-Tazobactam) + IV Methylprednisolone (60 mg/day)**.
> - **Proceed to Emergent Surgery — Laparoscopic or Open Subtotal (Total Abdominal) Colectomy with End Ileostomy and Hartmann's Rectal Stump** (preserving the rectum for future Ileal Pouch-Anal Anastomosis [IPAA] reconstruction once off steroids and nourished) for ANY of the following **Absolute Surgical Indications**:
>   1. **Free Colonic Perforation** or generalized peritonitis (Immediate OR!),
>   2. **Toxic Megacolon that fails to improve within 24–48 hours** of medical decompression (or shows clinical deterioration at any point),
>   3. **Massive Refractory Colonic Hemorrhage** causing hemodynamic instability, OR
>   4. **Failure to respond within 4–7 days of Medical Rescue Therapy** (Infliximab or Cyclosporine).
>
> **Management of Severe Crohn's Disease Flares (By Phenotype on CT)**
>
> - **Severe Inflammatory Luminal Flare (Abscess & Obstruction Excluded on CT):** Treat with **IV Methylprednisolone (40–60 mg/day)** + **VTE prophylaxis** and bridge rapidly to **Infliximab (or Risankizumab/Upadacitinib)**.
> - **Penetrating Flare with Intra-Abdominal / Pelvic Abscess:** **Withhold steroids and biologics initially!** Administer **Broad-Spectrum IV Antibiotics (Piperacillin-Tazobactam or Ceftriaxone + Metronidazole) PLUS CT-Guided Percutaneous Catheter Drainage** (if abscess **≥3 cm**); once sepsis resolves (3–5 days), initiate nutritional optimization and Anti-TNF or delayed elective surgical resection.
> - **Acute Inflammatory vs. Fibrotic Small Bowel Obstruction:** Decompress with **NG tube + IV fluids** and give a trial of **IV Methylprednisolone** (which resolves acute inflammatory mucosal edema); if obstruction is due to a fixed **fibrotic stricture** failing medical therapy, perform **surgical strictureplasty or limited ileocecal resection**.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Opioids, Loperamide/Diphenoxylate, Anticholinergics (Dicyclomine, Hyoscyamine), Antidepressants (TCAs), and NSAIDs** in any patient with a severe IBD flare, as antimotility/opioid/anticholinergic drugs paralyze colonic smooth muscle and **directly trigger Toxic Megacolon**!
> - **Avoid Performing a Full Colonoscopy, Bowel Preparation (PEG), or Barium Enema in Acute Severe Ulcerative Colitis or Toxic Megacolon**—colonic walls are paper-thin and friable, and air/barium insufflation causes **fatal iatrogenic perforation** (use only an **unprepped, gentle flexible sigmoidoscopy**).
> - **Avoid Prolonging IV Corticosteroids beyond 5–7 Days without Rescue Therapy or Colectomy**, and **Avoid Sequential Third-Line Medical Rescue** (e.g., stacking Cyclosporine immediately after Infliximab failure without surgery in unstable patients) due to prohibitive rates of **fatal opportunistic sepsis (_Pneumocystis jirovecii_, _Aspergillus_, bacterial peritonitis)** and surgical mortality.
> - **Avoid Performing a Primary Ileal Pouch-Anal Anastomosis (IPAA) during an Emergent Colectomy for ASUC or Toxic Megacolon**, as patients are malnourished, hypoalbuminemic, and on high-dose steroids/biologics, leading to catastrophic **pelvic anastomotic leak and sepsis** (always perform a **staged Subtotal Colectomy with End Ileostomy** first!).
> - **Avoid Administering IV Cyclosporine in patients with Hypocholesterolemia (Total Cholesterol <100–120 mg/dL), Hypomagnesemia, Uncontrolled Hypertension, or Renal Insufficiency** to prevent **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES), grand mal seizures, and nephrotoxicity**.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Colonic Perforation (With or Without Preceding Megacolon):** Mortality jumps from **<1–2% (with timely elective/semi-elective colectomy)** to **30–50% once free perforation occurs** $\rightarrow$ maintain a low threshold for upright CXR/CT if tachycardia, leukocytosis, or lactic acidosis worsens (even if abdominal pain seems mild due to steroid masking).
> - **Venous Thromboembolism (DVT, Pulmonary Embolism, Portal/Mesenteric Vein Thrombosis, Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis):** Leading preventable cause of in-hospital death in young IBD patients $\rightarrow$ prevented by mandatory **LMWH prophylaxis**.
> - **Opportunistic Infections on Dual/Triple Immunosuppression (Steroids + Anti-TNF/Cyclosporine + Thiopurine):** **Pneumocystis jirovecii Pneumonia (PJP)** (_mandatory **TMP-SMX prophylaxis** if on triple immunosuppression or Cyclosporine/high-dose steroids!_), invasive candidiasis/aspergillosis, listeriosis, and disseminated HSV/VZV.
> - **Rectal Stump Blowout:** Dehiscence of the closed Hartmann's rectal stump in the pelvis following emergent subtotal colectomy $\rightarrow$ prevent by placing a transanal rectal decompression catheter or creating a mucous fistula at the time of colectomy.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 29-year-old man with a 3-year history of left-sided ulcerative colitis presents with 12 bloody bowel movements per day for 4 days, severe tenesmus, fever (38.5°C), HR 112 bpm, and BP 104/64 mmHg (**Truelove and Witts ASUC**). Labs show Hb 9.4 g/dL, WBC 16,200/µL, platelets 490,000/µL, potassium 3.1 mEq/L, albumin 2.4 g/dL, and **CRP 78 mg/L**; stool _C. difficile_ is negative and a supine KUB shows diffuse colonic thumbprinting with a transverse colon diameter of 4.8 cm (no toxic megacolon). On **Day 1**, **Colorectal Surgery is consulted**, opioids/NSAIDs are strictly avoided, potassium is repleted, **subcutaneous Enoxaparin** is started for DVT prophylaxis (despite bloody stools), **IV Methylprednisolone (20 mg IV q8h)** is initiated, and an **unprepped flexible sigmoidoscopy** shows deep coalescing ulcers with biopsies negative for CMV. On **Day 3**, he still has 9 bloody stools/day and his CRP is 62 mg/L (**meeting Travis/Oxford criteria for steroid failure**); because his admission QuantiFERON-TB and Hepatitis B panels are negative and his albumin is low (2.4 g/dL), he is given **accelerated high-dose Rescue Infliximab (10 mg/kg IV)**, achieving clinical remission and avoiding colectomy.
