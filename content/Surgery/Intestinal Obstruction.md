> [!abstract] Intestinal Obstruction
> Impairment of normal aboral transit of intestinal contents due to either a **physical luminal/extrinsic blockage (Mechanical Obstruction: Small Bowel [80%] vs. Large Bowel [20%])** OR **neuromuscular failure of peristalsis without a structural transition point (Functional Obstruction: Paralytic Ileus vs. Ogilvie Syndrome)**.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Third-Spacing & Electrolyte Depletion:** Proximal to the obstruction, swallowed air and intestinal secretions accumulate $\rightarrow$ bowel wall distention impairs absorption and stimulates mucosal $\text{H}_2\text{O}/\text{Cl}^-$ secretion $\rightarrow$ massive **intraluminal/bowel-wall fluid sequestration ("third-spacing")** + vomiting $\rightarrow$ profound **hypovolemic shock**, prerenal AKI, and **hypochloremic, hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis** (in proximal SBO).
> - **Closed-Loop Obstruction & Strangulation:** When a bowel segment is occluded at **two points** (e.g., **volvulus**, incarcerated hernia, tight adhesive band, or **LBO with a competent ileocecal valve**), intraluminal pressure rises exponentially $\rightarrow$ **venous drainage collapses first** (engorgement/edema) $\rightarrow$ **arterial inflow occludes** $\rightarrow$ **transmural gangrene, bacterial translocation, and perforation** (most commonly at the **cecum**, which has the largest radius and highest wall tension per **Laplace's Law [$T = P \times r$]**!).

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Top 3 causes of SBO vs. LBO:**
>   - **Small Bowel Obstruction (SBO):** (1) **Postoperative Adhesions (60–75%)**, (2) **Incarcerated Hernias (10–15% — #1 cause in patients WITHOUT prior abdominal surgery!)**, (3) **Malignancy / Crohn's stricture (10%)**.
>   - **Large Bowel Obstruction (LBO):** (1) **Colorectal Adenocarcinoma (60%)**, (2) **Volvulus (Sigmoid > Cecal, 15–20%)**, (3) **Diverticular Stricture (10%)**.
> - **3 – 6 – 9 Rule of Maximal Normal Bowel Diameter:** **Small Bowel ≤3 cm**, **Colon (transverse) ≤6 cm**, **Cecum ≤9 cm** (if **cecum is ≥12 cm**, imminent perforation is likely!).
> - **Gastrografin (Water-Soluble Contrast) Challenge in Adhesive SBO:** In uncomplicated adhesive SBO, administering 100 mL of hyperosmolar **Gastrografin** via the NG tube draws interstitial edema out of the bowel wall (therapeutic!) and predicts surgery: **if contrast reaches the colon on abdominal X-ray within 24 hours, >95% resolve WITHOUT surgery**; if it fails to reach the colon by 24h, operate!
> - **Sigmoid Volvulus vs. Cecal Volvulus management is OPPOSITE:** Uncomplicated **Sigmoid Volvulus** is treated FIRST with **endoscopic detorsion (flexible sigmoidoscopy + rectal tube)** followed by same-admission sigmoid colectomy; **Cecal Volvulus** CANNOT be safely detorsed endoscopically and requires **immediate surgical Right Hemicolectomy**!

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Classic Cardinal Tetrad:**
>   1. **Colicky Abdominal Pain:** Paroxysmal, crampy visceral pain occurring in waves (every 4–5 min in proximal SBO; every 15–20 min in distal SBO/LBO). _Warning:_ **Transition from crampy colicky pain to constant, severe, unremitting pain indicates Strangulation / Bowel Ischemia!**
>   2. **Nausea & Vomiting:** Early, profuse bilious vomiting in **proximal SBO**; late, **feculent (foul-smelling, brown-orange) vomiting** in **distal SBO or LBO** (due to bacterial overgrowth).
>   3. **Abdominal Distention:** Minimal in very proximal jejunal obstruction; **massive distention** in distal SBO, LBO, or **Volvulus**.
>   4. **Obstipation:** Complete inability to pass **flatus or stool** (hallmark of **complete** obstruction; patients with **partial** SBO or early LBO may still pass flatus or liquid diarrhea distal to the blockage!).
> - **Targeted Risk History:** **Prior abdominal/pelvic surgeries** (adhesions), known groin/umbilical/incisional bulges (hernias), recent **weight loss/hematochezia/caliber change** (colon cancer), **Crohn's disease**, **gallstones** (gallstone ileus), or **chronic constipation/neuropsychiatric institutionalization** (sigmoid volvulus).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Vital Signs:** Tachycardia and orthostatic hypotension (dehydration); **Fever, persistent tachycardia despite fluid resuscitation, and tachypnea** are red flags for **strangulation/sepsis**.
> - **Inspection:** **Visible surgical scars** (confirming adhesive risk), asymmetric tympanitic distention (volvulus), visible peristaltic waves in thin patients.
> - **Auscultation & Percussion:**
>   - _Early Mechanical Obstruction:_ **Hyperactive, high-pitched "tinkling" metallic bowel sounds** and peristaltic rushes.
>   - _Late Obstruction / Paralytic Ileus / Ogilvie:_ **Hypoactive or completely absent ("silent") bowel sounds**.
>   - _Percussion:_ Diffuse **tympany**; percussion tenderness indicates peritonitis.
> - **Palpation & Mandatory Orifice Exam:**
>   - **Involuntary guarding, rigidity, or rebound tenderness** = **Strangulation or Perforation** (requires immediate laparotomy).
>   - **Examine ALL Hernia Orifices (Inguinal, Femoral, Umbilical, Incisional):** A tense, tender, irreducible, erythematous groin or umbilical mass confirms an **incarcerated/strangulated hernia**.
>   - **Digital Rectal Exam (DRE):** **Empty collapsed rectal vault** in complete mechanical obstruction; hard **fecal impaction** (mimic); palpable **rectal mass** or gross blood (malignancy); **"currant-jelly" stool** (intussusception).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Mechanical Bowel Obstruction (CT Abdomen/Pelvis with IV Contrast — Gold Standard):**
>   - **Proximal bowel dilation** (**Small bowel >3 cm**; **Colon >6 cm**; **Cecum >9 cm**) **PLUS**
>   - **Distal collapsed/decompressed bowel** separated by a discrete **"Transition Point"** (abrupt change in bowel caliber at the site of obstruction).
> - **Functional Obstruction (Paralytic Ileus / Ogilvie Syndrome):**
>   - Diffuse dilation of the small and large bowel (**Paralytic Ileus**) or isolated massive dilation of the cecum and right/transverse colon **>10–12 cm** (**Acute Colonic Pseudo-Obstruction [Ogilvie Syndrome]**) **WITH ABSENCE of a mechanical transition point** on CT or water-soluble contrast enema.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **Mandatory Initial Panel:**
>   1. **CBC with Differential:** Hemoconcentration (elevated Hct) from dehydration; **WBC >15,000–20,000/µL with left shift** strongly suggests **strangulation/ischemia**.
>   2. **BMP / Electrolytes:** **Hypokalemia, hypochloremia, metabolic alkalosis** (from vomiting/NG losses), and elevated **BUN/Creatinine** (prerenal AKI); **Hypomagnesemia and Hypocalcemia** (must check in Paralytic Ileus / Ogilvie syndrome).
>   3. **Serum Lactate & Arterial/Venous Blood Gas:** **Metabolic acidosis (low $\text{HCO}_3^-$) and elevated Lactate (>2.0–4.0 mmol/L)** indicate **bowel ischemia/strangulation** (_Caution: Normal lactate does NOT exclude early strangulation!_).
>   4. **Type and Screen + Coags** (preoperative).
>
> **Imaging**
>
> - **CT Abdomen and Pelvis with IV Contrast (NO Routine Oral Contrast):** **First-line gold-standard diagnostic test (>95% sensitivity/specificity)** for both SBO and LBO.
>   - _Why IV contrast?_ Essential to assess **bowel wall perfusion/enhancement** and detect **strangulation**!
>   - _Why NO oral contrast initially?_ The fluid-distended bowel already acts as natural neutral contrast; oral contrast causes vomiting/aspiration and delays CT.
>   - _Key CT Signs:_ **"Small Bowel Feces Sign"** (particulate feculent matter mixed with gas bubbles just proximal to the transition point), **"Whirl Sign"** (twisting of mesenteric vessels in **volvulus or internal hernia**), **"Target / Bull's-Eye Sign"** (**Intussusception**).
> - **Abdominal X-Ray Series (Supine & Upright KUB + Upright Chest X-Ray):** Useful as a rapid bedside screen or to track progression, though sensitivity is only ~60–75%:
>   - **SBO:** Central dilated bowel loops (>3 cm) with **plicate circulares (valvulae conniventes)** extending **completely across the lumen**, **multiple air-fluid levels in a "stepladder" configuration**, and **paucity of colonic/rectal gas**.
>   - **LBO:** Peripheral dilated colon (>6 cm) with **haustral markings** that **do NOT cross the entire lumen**.
>   - **Sigmoid Volvulus:** Giant ahaustral loop arising from the pelvis toward the **Right Upper Quadrant** forming the classic **"Coffee-Bean Sign" (or "Bent Inner-Tube Sign")** (\*On water-soluble contrast enema: **"Bird's Beak Sign"\***).
>   - **Cecal Volvulus:** Dilated comma/kidney-bean-shaped cecum displaced toward the **Left Upper Quadrant** with associated SBO.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> **Signs of Strangulation, Closed-Loop Obstruction, or Impending Perforation (Mandate EMERGENT Surgery):**
>
> - **Clinical "4 T's" of Strangulation:** **T**emperature (Fever), **T**achycardia (persistent after fluid bolus), **T**enderness (Focal/diffuse **peritonitis, guarding, or constant severe pain**), and **T**oxic Leukocytosis (**WBC >15,000/µL, Metabolic Acidosis, or Elevated Lactate**).
> - **CT Signs of Bowel Ischemia / Strangulation:**
>   1. **Decreased or Absent Bowel Wall Enhancement** on IV contrast CT,
>   2. **Pneumatosis Intestinalis** (gas within the bowel wall) or **Portal Venous Gas**,
>   3. **Mesenteric Edema, Engorgement, or Free Intraperitoneal Fluid**,
>   4. **Closed-Loop Obstruction** (C- or U-shaped distended loop with two adjacent transition points and **"Whirl Sign"**), OR
>   5. **Pneumoperitoneum** (free air = perforation).
> - **Cecal Diameter ≥12 cm:** High risk of imminent **cecal ischemia and perforation** in both mechanical LBO and Ogilvie syndrome!

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss (Specific Mechanical Etiologies)**
>
> - **Strangulated External or Internal Hernia:** Inguinal, femoral (high strangulation rate in older women!), umbilical, incisional, **Obturator hernia** (frail elderly women presenting with SBO + medial thigh pain = **Howship-Romberg sign**), or **Internal hernia after Roux-en-Y Gastric Bypass (Petersen's defect)** (_Crucial Pearl:_ **In a bariatric Roux-en-Y patient with acute abdominal pain, normal labs and even a normal CT do NOT rule out an internal hernia—have a very low threshold for diagnostic laparoscopy!**).
> - **Colonic Volvulus (Sigmoid [70%] vs. Cecal [25%]):** Axial rotation of a redundant colonic segment around its narrow mesenteric pedicle.
> - **Obstructing Colorectal Adenocarcinoma:** #1 cause of LBO in adults; gradual onset of constipation, narrowing stools, weight loss, and anemia culminating in acute obstipation (if the **ileocecal valve is competent [~75%]**, it creates a **closed-loop obstruction** between the tumor and the valve, causing **cecal perforation**!).
> - **Adult Intussusception:** Telescoping of a proximal bowel segment (**intussusceptum**) into a distal segment (**intussuscipiens**); _Crucial distinction:_ Unlike in children (where 90% are idiopathic at the ileocecal junction and reduced with an **air/pneumatic enema**), **90% of Adult Intussusceptions have a pathological "Lead Point" (50% Malignant: adenocarcinoma, lymphoma, metastasis; 50% Benign: lipoma, Peutz-Jeghers polyp, Meckel diverticulum)** $\rightarrow$ **Adult intussusception requires SURGICAL RESECTION (do NOT reduce via enema)!**
> - **Gallstone Ileus:** Elderly woman with SBO + **Pneumobilia** + ectopic calcified stone at the ileocecal valve (**Rigler's Triad**).
>
> **Most Likely (Functional / Non-Mechanical Mimics — Never Resect These!)**
>
> - **Postoperative / Paralytic (Adynamic) Ileus:** Temporary inhibition of GI motility affecting both small bowel and colon after **abdominal surgery** (normal physiologic duration: Small bowel recovers in 0–24h; Stomach in 24–48h; **Colon takes 48–72 hours**), **opioids**, **hypokalemia/hypomagnesemia**, **retroperitoneal hematoma/pancreatitis**, or **sepsis/pneumonia**; presents with painless distention, nausea, and absent bowel sounds without a transition point.
> - **Acute Colonic Pseudo-Obstruction (Ogilvie Syndrome):** Acute, massive isolated dilation of the **cecum and right/transverse colon (>10–12 cm)** WITHOUT a mechanical obstruction in hospitalized/ICU patients (post-orthopedic/hip/spine surgery, cardiac surgery, severe trauma, sepsis, Parkinson's, opioids, anticholinergics, electrolyte derangements) due to **autonomic imbalance (excess sympathetic tone / decreased sacral parasympathetic [S2–S4] output)**.
> - **Chronic Intestinal Pseudo-Obstruction (CIPO):** Rare chronic neuropathic or myopathic motility disorder (scleroderma, amyloidosis, mitochondrial cytopathy, paraneoplastic **anti-Hu** antibodies from small-cell lung cancer).

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Restore intravascular volume and electrolyte balance, decompress the distended GI tract, and **immediately identify and operate on strangulated or closed-loop obstructions** to save ischemic bowel.
> - **Secondary Target:** Resolve ~70–80% of uncomplicated adhesive SBOs non-operatively, safely bridge or resect malignant LBOs, and prevent cecal perforation in Ogilvie syndrome.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (Resuscitation & "Drip and Suck")**
>
> - **Keep strictly NPO, place two large-bore IVs, infuse aggressive isotonic IV crystalloids (Lactated Ringer's or Normal Saline)**, place a **Foley catheter** to monitor hourly urine output (target ≥0.5 mL/kg/hr), and **aggressively replete Potassium ($\text{K}^+ >4.0\text{ mEq/L}$) and Magnesium ($\text{Mg}^{2+} >2.0\text{ mg/dL}$)**.
> - **Insert a large-bore Nasogastric (NG) Tube (e.g., 14–18 Fr Salem Sump) on continuous low wall suction** in all patients with SBO, LBO with vomiting, or severe ileus to evacuate gastric/proximal intestinal fluid, relieve distention, and prevent fatal **feculent aspiration pneumonia**.
> - **Proceed IMMEDIATELY to Emergent Exploratory Laparotomy (or Diagnostic Laparoscopy)** (plus broad-spectrum IV antibiotics: **Cefoxitin**, **Ceftriaxone + Metronidazole**, or **Piperacillin-Tazobactam**) if the patient has **ANY sign of Strangulation, Peritonitis, Closed-Loop Obstruction, Pneumatosis Intestinalis, Free Air, Cecal Volvulus, Adult Intussusception, or an Incarcerated/Strangulated Hernia**.
>   - _Intraoperative Bowel Viability Check:_ After releasing the band/hernia/volvulus, wrap dusky bowel in warm saline-soaked sponges for 10–15 minutes and assess **color (pink), peristalsis, mesenteric arterial pulsations (Doppler), and Indocyanine Green (ICG) fluorescence** $\rightarrow$ **resect any frankly gangrenous/non-viable bowel**!
>
> **Stepwise Management of Uncomplicated Adhesive Small Bowel Obstruction (SBO) (Bologna Guidelines)**
>
> - **Initiate Non-Operative Management ("Drip and Suck": NPO + NG decompression + IV fluids)** for **up to 48–72 hours** in hemodynamically stable patients with a history of prior abdominal surgery and **NO signs of strangulation or peritonitis** (~70–85% resolve without surgery).
> - **Administer a Water-Soluble Contrast (Gastrografin) Challenge:** After 2–4 hours of complete gastric decompression via the NG tube, instill **50–100 mL of Gastrografin** through the NG tube, clamp the tube for 1–2 hours, and obtain an **Abdominal X-Ray at 8–24 hours**:
>   - **Contrast reaches the colon within 24 hours:** Confirms partial SBO resolving $\rightarrow$ remove NG tube, **start oral liquid diet**, and prepare for discharge.
>   - **Contrast FAILS to reach the colon at 24 hours (OR patient fails to resolve after 72 hours of conservative care, OR develops new fever/leukocytosis/worsening pain):** **Proceed to Surgical Exploration and Adhesiolysis!**
> - _Special SBO Populations (Lower Threshold for Surgery):_
>   - **"Virgin Abdomen" (SBO with ZERO prior abdominal surgery or external hernia):** While mild adhesive bands can occur, high rates of occult malignancy, internal hernia, or Meckel's warrant careful scrutiny and often early diagnostic laparoscopy.
>   - **Crohn's Disease SBO:** If due to an **acute inflammatory flare** $\rightarrow$ treat with **IV corticosteroids** + NG decompression; if due to a **chronic fibrotic stricture** $\rightarrow$ requires **endoscopic balloon dilation, surgical strictureplasty, or segmental resection**.
>   - **Peritoneal Carcinomatosis (Malignant SBO):** Avoid high-morbidity laparotomy if diffuse multifocal carcinomatosis; manage palliatively with **Octreotide** (reduces GI secretions), **Dexamethasone** (reduces peritumoral edema), **Haloperidol/Olanzapine**, and a **venting PEG tube**.
>
> **Management of Large Bowel Obstruction (LBO) & Volvulus**
>
> - **Sigmoid Volvulus:**
>   - **Uncomplicated (No peritonitis, no gangrene, no perforation):** **Perform Emergent Flexible Sigmoidoscopic Detorsion** and leave a **soft rectal decompression tube** in place for 48–72 hours (successful in >80–90%), **FOLLOWED by Elective Sigmoid Colectomy during the SAME index hospitalization** (recurrence rate is 50–70% if discharged without resection!).
>   - **Complicated (Peritonitis, mucosal gangrene seen on scope, or failed detorsion):** **Perform Emergent Laparotomy and Sigmoid Colectomy (Hartmann's procedure)** (_Do NOT untwist gangrenous bowel intraoperatively before clamping the vessels, to prevent systemic release of potassium, lactate, and endotoxin ["reperfusion shock"]!_).
> - **Cecal Volvulus:** **Perform Immediate Surgical Right Hemicolectomy** (with primary ileocolic anastomosis if stable, or ileostomy if unstable; **never attempt endoscopic detorsion or simple cecopexy** due to high perforation and recurrence rates).
> - **Malignant Large Bowel Obstruction (Colorectal Cancer):**
>   - **Right-Sided Obstructing Colon Cancer:** **Perform Urgent Right Hemicolectomy** with primary ileocolic anastomosis.
>   - **Left-Sided / Rectosigmoid Obstructing Colon Cancer:** Perform either **Surgical Resection** (Hartmann's procedure or on-table lavage with primary anastomosis) **OR** place a **Self-Expanding Metal Stent (SEMS)** via colonoscopy as a **"bridge to elective surgery"** (decompresses the colon so the patient can be optimized and undergo a single-stage laparoscopic resection without a colostomy) or for **definitive palliation** in stage IV metastatic disease.
>
> **Management of Paralytic Ileus & Acute Colonic Pseudo-Obstruction (Ogilvie Syndrome)**
>
> - **Step 1 (Conservative Care for 24–48 Hours — Resolves ~85%):** **Keep NPO**, place NG tube (if vomiting) and a gentle **rectal tube**, **ambulate / reposition the patient frequently** (knee-chest or prone/lateral positions help expel gas), **replete $\text{K}^+ >4.0$ and $\text{Mg}^{2+} >2.0$**, treat underlying sepsis, and **STOP all Opioids, Anticholinergics, and Calcium Channel Blockers** (_Consider **Alvimopan** [peripherally acting $\mu$-opioid antagonist] prophylactically before elective bowel resection to prevent postoperative ileus_).
> - **Step 2 (Pharmacologic Decompression for Ogilvie Syndrome):** **Administer IV Neostigmine (2 mg slow IV push over 3–5 minutes)** if **cecal diameter is ≥10–12 cm** or fails 24–48 hours of conservative care (**acetylcholinesterase inhibitor** that restores colonic parasympathetic acetylcholine tone; induces rapid flatus/defecation within 10–30 minutes in >85% of patients!).
>   - _Mandatory Neostigmine Safety Protocol:_ Patient **must be on continuous telemetry monitoring** with **IV Atropine (0.5–1 mg) at the bedside** to immediately reverse severe **cholinergic bradycardia/heart block**, and **glycopyrrolate** for sialorrhea/bronchospasm.
> - **Step 3 (Endoscopic / Surgical Salvage for Ogilvie):** **Perform Colonoscopic Decompression** (with placement of a decompression tube) if Neostigmine fails or is contraindicated; reserve **Surgical Cecostomy or Right Hemicolectomy** strictly for **cecal ischemia or perforation**.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Administering IV Neostigmine until Mechanical Large Bowel Obstruction has been 100% Excluded** via CT with IV/rectal contrast or water-soluble contrast enema (giving neostigmine against a mechanical colon cancer or volvulus causes **catastrophic colonic perforation**!).
> - **Avoid Neostigmine in patients with Baseline Bradycardia (<60 bpm), Second/Third-Degree Heart Block, Active Bronchospasm (Severe Asthma), Recent MI, or Renal Insufficiency (Cr >3.0 mg/dL)** (neostigmine is renally cleared).
> - **Avoid Oral Barium Contrast in Suspected High-Grade Bowel Obstruction (especially LBO)**, as barium inspissates into a rock-hard barium impaction above a colonic stricture and causes **lethal barium peritonitis** if perforation occurs (always use **water-soluble contrast [Gastrografin]**).
> - **Avoid Prolonging Non-Operative "Drip and Suck" Management beyond 72 hours (3–5 days max)** in adhesive SBO that is not resolving, as mortality and bowel resection rates rise sharply after 72 hours.
> - **Avoid Prokinetics (Metoclopramide, Erythromycin) or Stimulant Laxatives in Complete Mechanical Bowel Obstruction**, as stimulating forceful contractions against a closed obstruction increases pain, vomiting, and perforation risk.
> - **Avoid Completing Endoscopic Detorsion of Sigmoid Volvulus if Dark/Black Gangrenous Mucosa or Ulceration is Visualized** on flexible sigmoidoscopy—abort the scope immediately and go **straight to the Operating Room for emergency Hartmann's resection**!

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Transmural Bowel Infarction, Perforation, & Feculent Peritonitis:** Mortality increases from **<3% in simple non-strangulated SBO** to **20–35% once strangulation and perforation occur** $\rightarrow$ requires emergent bowel resection and peritoneal washout.
> - **Aspiration Pneumonia / Mendelson Syndrome:** Vomiting of feculent gastric contents prior to NG tube placement or during anesthesia induction $\rightarrow$ **always decompress the stomach with an NG tube BEFORE performing Rapid Sequence Intubation (RSI)** in a patient with bowel obstruction!
> - **Short Bowel Syndrome:** Resulting from massive small bowel resection (<100–200 cm remaining small bowel) after delayed diagnosis of strangulated volvulus, internal hernia, or mesenteric ischemia.
> - **Intra-Abdominal Adhesions (Recurrent SBO):** Every laparotomy creates new peritoneal adhesions (~10–15% lifetime risk of recurrent SBO; **laparoscopic surgery significantly reduces adhesion formation** compared to open laparotomy).

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> **Scenario 1 (Adhesive SBO):** A 54-year-old woman with a history of an open hysterectomy 8 years ago presents with 18 hours of crampy periumbilical pain coming in waves every 5 minutes, bilious vomiting, abdominal distention, and inability to pass gas. Vitals are stable (afebrile, HR 88 bpm), her abdomen is distended and tympanitic with high-pitched tinkling bowel sounds but **no rebound or guarding**, WBC is 10,200/µL, and lactate is 1.2 mmol/L. **CT abdomen/pelvis with IV contrast** shows dilated proximal jejunal/ileal loops (4 cm) with normal wall enhancement, a discrete **transition point** in the pelvis from adhesions, and collapsed distal ileum/colon (no closed loop or ischemia). She is managed with **NPO, IV Lactated Ringer's, Potassium repletion, and NG tube decompression**, followed by a **Gastrografin challenge**; contrast is seen in the ascending colon on an 8-hour X-ray, her flatus returns, and she is discharged without surgery.
> **Scenario 2 (Ogilvie Syndrome):** A 78-year-old man on high-dose oxycodone 4 days after a total hip arthroplasty develops massive, painless abdominal distention and absent bowel sounds. CT with IV contrast shows a **12.5-cm dilated cecum** and right colon with **no mechanical transition point**, no free air, and normal wall enhancement. Opioids are stopped, $\text{K}^+$ and $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ are repleted, and because the cecum is >12 cm, he is placed on continuous cardiac telemetry (with atropine at the bedside) and given **Neostigmine 2 mg slow IV**, resulting in immediate passage of large-volume flatus and stool with rapid cecal decompression.
