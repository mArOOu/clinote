> [!abstract] Perforated Viscus - Peritonitis
> **Perforated Hollow Viscus (Secondary Peritonitis):** Full-thickness disruption of the gastrointestinal tract wall spilling luminal acid, bile, enzymes, or polymicrobial fecal flora into the sterile peritoneal cavity (or mediastinum/retroperitoneum), representing a surgical emergency with **30–50% mortality if source control is delayed**.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Chemical $\rightarrow$ Bacterial Peritonitis Biphasic Progression (Classic in Perforated Peptic Ulcer):**
>   - _Phase 1 (0–6 hours — Chemical Peritonitis):_ Spillage of sterile gastric HCl, bile, and pancreatic enzymes causes an intense **chemical burn** of the peritoneum (instantaneous agonizing pain).
>   - _Phase 2 (6–12 hours — "Delusional / Latent Phase"):_ Massive peritoneal fluid exudation **dilutes the acid**, sometimes causing a **deceptive, temporary improvement in pain** while massive **third-spacing** (up to 6–10 L into the peritoneum/bowel wall) drives hypovolemic shock.
>   - _Phase 3 (>12–24 hours — Bacterial/Suppurative Peritonitis):_ Superinfection by enteric flora (**E. coli, Klebsiella, Streptococcus, Enterococcus**, and anaerobes like **_Bacteroides fragilis_**; plus **_Candida_** in upper GI perforations) $\rightarrow$ paralytic ileus, abdominal compartment syndrome, and **septic shock**.
> - **Colonic Perforation (Diverticulitis / Cancer / Volvulus):** Spills a massive inoculum ($10^{11}\text{–}10^{12}\text{ CFU/g}$) of **polymicrobial Gram-negative rods and anaerobes** immediately at T=0 (**feculent peritonitis**).

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Primary (SBP) vs. Secondary Peritonitis (Perforation) in Cirrhosis:** **Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP)** is **monomicrobial** and treated **strictly with antibiotics (surgery kills the patient!)**; **Secondary Peritonitis** from a perforated viscus is **polymicrobial**, meets **≥2 of 3 Runyon's Criteria** on ascitic tap (**Total Protein >1 g/dL, Glucose <50 mg/dL, LDH > serum ULN**), and has **~100% mortality WITHOUT emergent surgery**!
> - **Anterior vs. Posterior Duodenal Ulcers:** **Anterior** duodenal ulcers perforate freely into the peritoneal cavity (**pneumoperitoneum**); **Posterior** duodenal ulcers erode into the **gastroduodenal artery** (massive UGIB) or penetrate into the **pancreas** (elevated lipase, back pain, no free air on X-ray).
> - **Upright CXR misses 20–30% of perforations:** Absence of subdiaphragmatic free air on an upright Chest X-Ray **does NOT rule out a perforated viscus** (especially retroperitoneal duodenal/rectal or walled-off appendiceal/diverticular perforations)—**CT Abdomen/Pelvis with IV contrast** is >98% sensitive!
> - **Boerhaave Syndrome (Esophageal Perforation):** Forceful vomiting followed by **Mackler's Triad** (**Vomiting, Severe Lower Thoracic/Epigastric Pain, and Subcutaneous Neck Emphysema/Crepitus**) + **left pleural effusion** + **Hamman's crunch** (pneumomediastinum).

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Instantaneous, "thunderclap" severe abdominal pain:** Patient can often state the exact minute the perforation occurred; pain begins regionally (epigastric in peptic ulcer; LLQ in diverticulitis) and rapidly generalizes across the entire abdomen.
> - **Patient lies completely motionless** (frequently with knees drawn up): Even slight movement, deep breathing, coughing, or hitting a bump in the ambulance causes excruciating somatic pain.
> - **Referred Shoulder-Tip Pain (Kehr's Sign):** Subdiaphragmatic free air, acid, or blood irritates the **diaphragm (C3–C5 phrenic nerve)**, referring sharp pain to the **top of the shoulder** (supraclavicular nerve).
> - **Etiologic Clues:**
>   - _Upper GI (Stomach/Duodenum):_ Heavy **NSAID/aspirin/steroid** use, _H. pylori_, crack cocaine/methamphetamine (vasospastic perforation), smoking.
>   - _Esophagus:_ Forceful retching/vomiting after alcohol binge (**Boerhaave**), **recent EGD/dilation/TEE** (#1 cause of esophageal perforation overall = **iatrogenic**!).
>   - _Lower GI (Ileum/Colon):_ Diverticulosis, obstructing colon cancer, **recent colonoscopy/polypectomy**, IBD (toxic megacolon), typhoid fever (_Salmonella_ Typhi perforates **Peyer's patches** in the terminal ileum in week 3), ingested **toothpick/fishbone/chicken bone** or **button battery**.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Systemic Shock Signs:** Fever (or hypothermia <36°C in late sepsis), marked tachycardia, tachypnea (shallow breathing due to diaphragmatic splinting), hypotension, oliguria, mottled extremities.
> - **"Board-Like" Abdominal Rigidity (Hallmark):** Involuntary, unyielding reflex tonic spasm of the rectus abdominis muscles upon light palpation, accompanied by **diffuse percussion/cough tenderness** and **rebound tenderness**.
> - **Percussion & Auscultation:** **Loss of normal hepatic dullness** (tympanitic resonance over the right costal margin due to free air layering over the liver) and a **completely silent abdomen** (paralytic ileus).
> - **Populations with Masked / Blunted Peritoneal Signs (High Missed-Diagnosis Mortality!):** **Elderly/frail patients** (lax abdominal wall musculature), **patients on high-dose Corticosteroids or Immunosuppressants**, **obese patients**, **neutropenic patients**, and **patients with spinal cord injury or heavy ICU sedation** may have a **soft abdomen** despite frank feculent peritonitis!

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Radiologic Confirmation of Hollow Viscus Perforation:**
>   - **Pneumoperitoneum:** Extraluminal **free intraperitoneal air** (crescentic lucency beneath the hemidiaphragm on upright CXR, over the liver on left lateral decubitus X-ray, or tiny gas bubbles/free fluid on **CT Abdomen/Pelvis**), **Pneumoretroperitoneum** (air outlining the right kidney/psoas muscle in posterior duodenal/colon perforation), OR **Pneumomediastinum + Contrast Extravasation** (esophageal perforation).
> - **Secondary Bacterial Peritonitis in Ascitic Fluid (Runyon's Criteria — Differentiates Perforation from SBP):**
>   - **Polymicrobial Gram stain/culture** (multiple bacteria + fungi) AND **≥2 of the following 3 ascitic fluid findings**:
>     1. **Total Protein >1.0 g/dL**,
>     2. **Glucose <50 mg/dL**, AND
>     3. **LDH > Upper Limit of Normal for Serum**.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **STAT Preoperative & Sepsis Bundle:**
>   1. **CBC with Differential:** Marked leukocytosis (>15,000–25,000/µL) with bandemia, OR **leukopenia (<4,000/µL)** in overwhelming Gram-negative endotoxemia; **hemoconcentration (elevated Hct)** from massive peritoneal third-spacing.
>   2. **Serum Lactate & Arterial Blood Gas:** **High anion-gap lactic acidosis** (tissue hypoperfusion/sepsis) or **hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis** (if preceded by gastric vomiting).
>   3. **CMP & Serum Lipase:** Prerenal AKI (elevated BUN/Cr); **Serum Lipase is frequently mildly elevated (1–3× ULN)** in perforated peptic ulcers due to peritoneal absorption of spilled luminal pancreatic enzymes!
>   4. **Blood Cultures (×2 sets), Type and Crossmatch, PT/INR, Fibrinogen.**
>   5. _If Pleural Effusion in Suspected Boerhaave Syndrome:_ Thoracentesis shows **pleural fluid Amylase (salivary isoform) >250 U/L**, **pH <6.0**, and food particles/squamous cells.
>
> **Imaging (Stratified by Stability & Location)**
>
> - **Portable Upright Chest X-Ray (or Left Lateral Decubitus Abdominal X-Ray if Unable to Sit Upright):**
>   - **Fastest initial bedside test** (detects as little as **1–2 mL of free air** under the right/left hemidiaphragm if the patient sits upright for 5–10 minutes prior to the film).
>   - _Classic Supine X-Ray Signs of Pneumoperitoneum (Seen in ICU patients who cannot sit upright):_
>     - **Rigler's Sign ("Double-Wall Sign"):** Air visible on **BOTH** the inside (lumen) and outside (serosa) of the bowel wall.
>     - **Falciform Ligament Sign ("Silver Saddle Sign"):** Free air outlining the falciform ligament in the RUQ.
>     - **Football Sign:** Massive pneumoperitoneum outlining the entire oval peritoneal cavity in infants/children.
> - **CT Abdomen and Pelvis with IV Contrast:** **Gold standard (>98% sensitivity)** in hemodynamically stable patients; detects microscopic extraluminal gas bubbles, **pinpoints the exact anatomic site of perforation** (focal bowel wall discontinuity, peri-visceral fat stranding, extraluminal fluid/fecalith), and stages **Hinchey Classification** in perforated diverticulitis.
>   - _Crucial Rule:_ If an **unexplained upright CXR already shows massive free air in a patient with a rigid surgical abdomen and shock**, **DO NOT delay for CT—go straight to the Operating Room!**
> - **CT Esophagram / Fluoroscopic Esophagram with Water-Soluble Contrast (Gastrografin):** **Gold standard for Esophageal Perforation (Boerhaave or Iatrogenic)** (_If initial Gastrografin swallow is negative but suspicion remains high, repeat immediately with thin Barium, which detects small mucosal tears; **NEVER use Barium first**, as barium in the mediastinum causes lethal granulomatous mediastinitis!_).

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Septic Shock (MAP <65 mmHg despite fluids, Lactate >4 mmol/L), Acute Kidney Injury, or Coagulopathy.**
> - **Delay from perforation onset to surgery >24 hours** (each hour of surgical delay increases mortality by ~2–5%; mortality jumps **4–8× if surgery is delayed >24 hours**!).
> - **Hinchey Stage III (Generalized Purulent Peritonitis) or Stage IV (Generalized Feculent Peritonitis)** in perforated diverticulitis.
> - **Boerhaave Syndrome (Transmural distal left posterolateral esophageal rupture):** Mortality is **<10–15% if repaired within <24 hours**, rises to **>50% after 24 hours**, and approaches **~90–100% if left untreated**.
> - **Boey Score ≥2 in Perforated Peptic Ulcer:** (1 point each for: **Preoperative Shock [SBP <90]**, **Severe Medical Comorbidity [ASA III–V]**, and **Prolonged Perforation >24 hours**; Score 0 = <1% mortality; Score 3 = **>40–60% mortality**).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss (Non-Perforated Surgical Emergencies)**
>
> - **Acute Severe Necrotizing Pancreatitis:** Mimics perforated peptic ulcer with sudden epigastric pain, guarding, shock, and third-spacing; distinguished by **Lipase >3× ULN**, pancreatic inflammation on CT, and **absence of pneumoperitoneum** (**strictly non-surgical in the acute phase!**).
> - **Ruptured Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (AAA):** Sudden abdominal/back pain, hypotension, pulsatile mass; retroperitoneal hematoma on CT/FAST without free air.
> - **Acute Mesenteric Ischemia (Early Pre-Perforation):** Pain out of proportion to exam; arterial occlusion on CTA before transmural perforation develops.
>
> **Most Surprising ("Benign / Non-Surgical Pneumoperitoneum" — Free Air WITHOUT a Perforated Viscus!)**
>
> - **Normal Postoperative Pneumoperitoneum:** Free intraperitoneal air is **completely normal for up to 7–10 days (occasionally up to 2 weeks) after Open Laparotomy** and **3–5 days after Laparoscopy** (should progressively _decrease_ on serial films; increasing free air + peritonitis = **anastomotic leak**!).
> - **Chilaiditi Sign / Syndrome:** Benign interposition of the **hepatic flexure of the colon** between the liver and the right hemidiaphragm, **mimicking subdiaphragmatic free air on CXR** (distinguished by visible **haustral markings** inside the gas pocket and absence of peritonitis!).
> - **Thoracic / Ventilator Barotrauma:** PEEP/mechanical ventilation, pneumothorax, or vigorous CPR tracking air down through diaphragmatic hiatuses into the peritoneum.
> - **Peritoneal Dialysis Catheter, Recent PEG Tube Placement, or Gynecologic Insufflation** (post-pelvic exam, douching, or vigorous orogenital insufflation via patent fallopian tubes).

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Immediate hemodynamic resuscitation (**MAP ≥65 mmHg, Urine Output ≥0.5 mL/kg/hr**), administration of **broad-spectrum IV antimicrobials within <1 hour**, and **emergent surgical source control (closure, diversion, or resection + peritoneal washout) as rapidly as possible**.
> - **Secondary Target:** Eradicate _H. pylori_ (in perforated PUD), exclude underlying malignancy (in perforated gastric ulcer or colon perforation), and prevent abdominal compartment syndrome and tertiary peritonitis.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (First 0–60 Minutes — Resuscitation & Sepsis Bundle)**
>
> - **Keep strictly NPO, place two large-bore IVs (14–16G), infuse aggressive balanced IV crystalloids (Lactated Ringer's 30 mL/kg bolus)** to combat massive peritoneal third-spacing, start **Norepinephrine** if MAP remains <65 mmHg, and place a **Foley catheter** to track hourly urine output.
> - **Insert a Nasogastric (NG) Tube on continuous low wall suction** to decompress the stomach and prevent ongoing spillage of gastric/intestinal contents through the perforation (_Exception: In **Esophageal Perforation [Boerhaave]**, pass an NG tube **only under direct endoscopic or fluoroscopic guidance** so you do not push the tube out through the esophageal tear into the mediastinum!_).
> - **Administer Broad-Spectrum IV Antibiotics WITHIN 1 HOUR of presentation** (plus an **IV PPI [Pantoprazole 80 mg IV bolus, then 40 mg IV BID]** for upper GI / esophageal perforations):
>   - **Community-Acquired Perforation (Mild-to-Moderate):** **Piperacillin-Tazobactam (3.375–4.5 g IV q6h)** OR **Ceftriaxone (2 g IV daily) + Metronidazole (500 mg IV q8h)**.
>   - **Septic Shock, Feculent Peritonitis, Healthcare-Associated, or Immunocompromised:** **Meropenem (1 g IV q8h)** OR **Cefepime (2 g IV q8h) + Metronidazole (500 mg IV q8h) + Vancomycin/Ampicillin** (for _Enterococcus_).
>   - **Add Empiric Antifungal Therapy (Fluconazole 400–800 mg IV or an Echinocandin [Micafungin 100 mg IV daily])** for **Upper GI Perforations (Esophagus, Stomach, Duodenum)**, recurrent/postoperative **Anastomotic Leaks**, or septic shock, as _Candida_ frequently colonizes the upper GI tract and worsens mortality!
>
> **Definitive Surgical Source Control by Anatomic Site (WSES Guidelines)**
>
> - **Perforated Peptic Ulcer (Duodenal vs. Gastric):**
>   - **Perforated Duodenal Ulcer (<2 cm) or Small Benign Gastric Ulcer:** **Perform Emergent Laparoscopic (or Open) Graham Omental Patch Repair** (plugging the perforation with a vascularized tongue of greater omentum secured with interrupted sutures) + thorough peritoneal irrigation.
>   - **Large (>2 cm) or Non-Flickable / Suspected Malignant Gastric Ulcer:** **Perform Biopsy of the ulcer margins** (mandatory in ALL gastric ulcer perforations to rule out **gastric adenocarcinoma**!) or **Distal Gastrectomy (Billroth I/II or Roux-en-Y)** if the tissue edges are too friable/necrotic to hold a patch.
> - **Perforated Sigmoid Diverticulitis (Stratified by Modified Hinchey Classification):**
>   - **Hinchey Ia (Pericolic phlegmon/microperforation) or Ib / II (Contained pericolic or pelvic abscess):** **Non-Operative Management!** Administer **IV Antibiotics alone** (if abscess **<3–4 cm**) OR **IV Antibiotics + CT-Guided Percutaneous Catheter Drainage** (if abscess **≥3–4 cm**).
>   - **Hinchey III (Generalized Purulent Peritonitis) or Hinchey IV (Generalized Feculent Peritonitis):** **Perform Emergent Laparotomy & Sigmoid Resection**—either **Hartmann's Procedure** (sigmoid resection, oversewing the rectal stump, and creating an **end descending colostomy**; gold standard in **unstable/septic patients or Hinchey IV**) OR **Sigmoid Resection with Primary Anastomosis + Diverting Loop Ileostomy** (in hemodynamically stable, immunocompetent Hinchey III patients).
> - **Esophageal Perforation (Boerhaave Syndrome / Iatrogenic):**
>   - **Contained Cervical or Small Iatrogenic Thoracic Perforation (Minimal contamination, no sepsis):** Non-operative management with **NPO, TPN/jejunostomy feeding, IV PPI, IV antibiotics + fluconazole**, and **Endoscopic Fully Covered Self-Expanding Metal Stent (SEMS) or Endoluminal Vacuum (EndoVAC) Therapy**.
>   - **Uncontained Thoracic Perforation (Boerhaave Syndrome) with Mediastinitis / Pleural Empyema:** **Perform Emergent Left Thoracotomy (or VATS/Laparoscopy) within <24 hours for Primary Two-Layer Esophageal Repair Reinforced with a Vascularized Flap (Intercostal muscle, Pleura, or Thal/Dor Fundoplication)** + mediastinal debridement and chest tube drainage.
> - **Damage Control Surgery (For Profound Septic Shock, Acidosis [pH <7.2], Hypothermia [<35°C], & Coagulopathy):**
>   - **Perform Abbreviated Laparotomy:** Staple off/resect the perforated segment **without creating an anastomosis or stoma**, wash out the abdomen, apply a **Temporary Abdominal Closure (Negative-Pressure Wound Therapy / Bogota Bag)** to prevent Abdominal Compartment Syndrome, resuscitate in the ICU for 24–48 hours, and return to the OR for definitive reconstruction.
>
> **Postoperative Duration & Follow-up**
>
> - **Stop Postoperative Antibiotics after 4 to 5 days** once adequate surgical source control is achieved and clinical signs of sepsis resolve (**STOP-IT trial**).
> - **Test for and Eradicate _H. pylori_**, **permanently discontinue NSAIDs**, and maintain **daily PPI therapy** in all patients after a perforated peptic ulcer; perform **follow-up EGD in 6–8 weeks** for all perforated gastric ulcers and **follow-up Colonoscopy in 6–8 weeks** after any conservatively managed perforated diverticulitis to rule out occult malignancy.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Operating on Primary Bacterial Peritonitis (Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis [SBP] in Cirrhosis or CAPD-Associated Peritonitis)**, as exploratory laparotomy in decompensated cirrhosis carries **>80% mortality** from liver failure and hemorrhage—always check **Runyon's criteria** and abdominal CT first if ascites is present!
> - **Avoid Oral or Rectal Barium Contrast whenever GI Perforation is Suspected**, as extravasated barium sulfate cannot be absorbed by the peritoneum or mediastinum and causes **fatal fibrogranulomatous peritonitis/mediastinitis** (use **water-soluble Gastrografin** ONLY).
> - **Avoid Performing Laparoscopic Peritoneal Lavage Alone (without resecting the perforated sigmoid colon) in Hinchey III/IV Perforated Diverticulitis**, as randomized trials (**LOLA / SCANDIV trials**) demonstrated significantly higher rates of persistent intra-abdominal sepsis and emergency reoperation compared to sigmoid resection.
> - **Avoid Performing a Primary Bowel Anastomosis (without diversion) in a patient with Feculent Peritonitis (Hinchey IV), Septic Shock requiring high-dose Vasopressors, Severe Malnutrition (Albumin <2.5 g/dL), or Bowel Edema**, as the anastomosis will break down (**anastomotic leak**)—perform a **Hartmann's procedure (end colostomy) or damage control surgery** instead!
> - **Avoid Forceful Blind Nasogastric Tube Insertion in Suspected Boerhaave Syndrome**, as the tube can perforate through the friable esophageal tear directly into the pleural cavity or aorta.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Tertiary (Persistent) Peritonitis:** Recurrent or persistent intra-abdominal infection persisting >48 hours after adequate surgical source control of secondary peritonitis in immunocompromised/ICU patients $\rightarrow$ driven by nosocomial low-virulence/multidrug-resistant organisms (**Coagulase-negative _Staphylococci_, _Enterococcus faecium_ [VRE], _Pseudomonas aeruginosa_, and _Candida_ spp.**) $\rightarrow$ high mortality (>50%).
> - **Postoperative Intra-Abdominal Abscess (Subphrenic, Subhepatic, Interloop, or Pelvic):** Presents 5–10 days post-op with recurrent "picket-fence" spiking fevers, leukocytosis, ileus, or **intractable hiccups / ipsilateral pleural effusion** (subphrenic abscess irritating the diaphragm) $\rightarrow$ diagnose with **CT Abdomen/Pelvis** and treat with **CT-guided percutaneous catheter drainage + antibiotics**.
> - **Anastomotic Leak or Enterocutaneous Fistula (ECF):** Breakdown of a surgical repair or anastomosis $\rightarrow$ presents on **Post-Op Day 5–7** with unexplained tachycardia (often the earliest sign!), fever, oliguria, delirium, and enteric/bilious/feculent drainage from the surgical wound or drain:
>   - _Uncontained Leak with Generalized Peritonitis:_ **Emergent re-laparotomy**, washout, and proximal ostomy diversion.
>   - _Contained Low-Output Enterocutaneous Fistula (<200–500 mL/day):_ Manage conservatively using **SNAP protocol** (**S**epsis control/drainage, **N**utrition [TPN or enteral], **A**natomy definition via fistulogram, **P**lanning delayed surgery at >3–6 months if it fails to close).
>   - **Factors Preventing Spontaneous Fistula Closure (Mnemonic: FRIENDS):** **F**oreign body (mesh/suture), **R**adiation enteritis, **I**nflammation/Infection (**IBD/Crohn's**), **E**pithelialization of a short tract (<2 cm), **N**eoplasm, **D**istal bowel obstruction, **S**teroids/High output (>500 mL/day).
> - **Abdominal Compartment Syndrome (ACS):** Massive visceral edema and third-spacing after closing a tight abdomen under tension $\rightarrow$ bladder pressure **>20 mmHg** + oliguria/high peak inspiratory pressures $\rightarrow$ requires **emergent decompressive laparotomy (open abdomen)**.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 58-year-old man with knee osteoarthritis taking high-dose naproxen daily presents with 4 hours of sudden-onset, agonizing generalized abdominal pain that began abruptly in his epigastrium and radiates to his right shoulder. Vitals show T 38.2°C, HR 122 bpm, and BP 92/58 mmHg; he lies completely still on the stretcher with a **"board-like" rigid abdomen**, diffuse rebound tenderness, and absent bowel sounds. Labs show WBC 19,500/µL and lactate 3.4 mmol/L; a portable **upright Chest X-Ray** reveals **crescentic free intraperitoneal air beneath both hemidiaphragms (pneumoperitoneum)**. He is immediately resuscitated with **IV Lactated Ringer's (30 mL/kg)**, an **NG tube** is placed, **IV Piperacillin-Tazobactam, Fluconazole, and IV Pantoprazole** are administered within 30 minutes, and he is taken **directly to the Operating Room (bypassing CT)** for an **emergent laparoscopic Graham omental patch repair** of a 1-cm anterior duodenal bulb perforation.
