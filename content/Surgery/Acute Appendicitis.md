> [!abstract] Acute Appendicitis
> Acute inflammation of the vermiform appendix triggered by luminal obstruction, representing the most common acute surgical emergency of the abdomen worldwide (peak incidence ages 10–30 years).

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Luminal obstruction:** Caused by **lymphoid hyperplasia** (#1 in children/young adults, often post-viral) or a **fecalith / appendicolith** (#1 in adults; also foreign bodies, parasites [*Enterobius*], or **cecal adenocarcinoma/carcinoid** in older adults).
> - **Closed-loop progression (24–48 hours):** Continued mucosal mucus secretion increases intraluminal pressure (**>85 mmHg**) $\rightarrow$ **venous/lymphatic congestion** (visceral periumbilical pain via **T8–T10** afferents) $\rightarrow$ bacterial overgrowth (_E. coli, Bacteroides fragilis_) + **arterial ischemia/infarction** $\rightarrow$ transmural inflammation irritates parietal peritoneum (**somatic RLQ pain at McBurney's point**) $\rightarrow$ **gangrene and perforation** (typically after 24–36 hours).

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Pain ALWAYS precedes vomiting in Appendicitis:** Classic chronological sequence (**Murphy's sequence**) is **Periumbilical Pain $\rightarrow$ Anorexia/Nausea/Vomiting $\rightarrow$ RLQ Pain Localization $\rightarrow$ Low-grade Fever**; if vomiting starts _before_ abdominal pain, suspect acute gastroenteritis instead.
> - **Appendicolith on CT = Surgery (Skip antibiotics-first):** While uncomplicated appendicitis _can_ be treated with antibiotics alone (CODA trial), the presence of an **appendicolith** on CT doubles the failure/perforation rate (~40% fail) and is a **strong indication for upfront Laparoscopic Appendectomy**.
> - **Complicated mass/abscess presenting late (>5 days): Do NOT operate immediately!** Operating acutely on a walled-off periappendiceal phlegmon or abscess risks damaging the inflamed cecum and requiring an ileocecectomy; treat with **IV antibiotics + CT-guided percutaneous drainage (if ≥3 cm)**, then perform an **interval colonoscopy (if age ≥40)** to rule out cecal malignancy!
> - **Pregnancy shifts the appendix UP:** In the 2nd and 3rd trimesters, the gravid uterus displaces the appendix superiorly into the **Right Upper Quadrant (RUQ)**, mimicking acute cholecystitis.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Classic Visceral-to-Somatic Pain Migration (50–60% of cases):** Dull, colicky **periumbilical or epigastric pain** that migrates over **4–12 hours** to become sharp, constant **Right Lower Quadrant (RLQ) pain** worsened by coughing, walking, or hitting speed bumps on the drive to the hospital.
> - **Anorexia ("negative hamburger sign"):** Almost universal (~80–90%), accompanied by nausea and 1–2 episodes of vomiting (occurring _after_ pain onset) and low-grade fever (~38.0°C).
> - **Atypical Anatomical Presentations (Depend on Appendiceal Tip Position):**
>   - **Retrocecal Appendix (Most common anatomical position, ~65%):** Shielded from anterior peritoneum $\rightarrow$ **blunted/absent RLQ tenderness**, presents with **right flank/back pain** and a positive **Psoas sign** (often misdiagnosed as pyelonephritis or renal colic due to **sterile pyuria/microscopic hematuria** from ureteral irritation!).
>   - **Pelvic Appendix (~30%):** Suprapubic pain, **tenesmus/diarrhea** (rectal irritation), **urinary frequency/dysuria** (bladder irritation), and positive **Obturator sign**.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **McBurney's Point Tenderness:** Maximal tenderness located **one-third of the distance from the Anterior Superior Iliac Spine (ASIS) to the umbilicus** along the spinoumbilical line, with localized guarding and percussion/rebound tenderness.
> - **Classic Provocative Signs:**
>   - **Rovsing's Sign:** Deep palpation of the **Left Lower Quadrant (LLQ)** reproduces pain in the **RLQ** (retrograde displacement of colonic gas/peritoneal stretch).
>   - **Psoas Sign (Retrocecal appendix):** RLQ pain upon **passive extension of the right hip** (with patient lying on left side) or active right hip flexion against resistance (inflamed appendix overlies the iliopsoas muscle).
>   - **Obturator Sign (Pelvic appendix):** RLQ/hypogastric pain upon **passive internal rotation of the flexed right hip and knee** (inflamed appendix abuts the internal obturator muscle).
>   - **Dunphy's Sign:** Increased sharp RLQ pain with coughing.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Cross-Sectional Imaging Confirmation (CT or Ultrasound):**
>   - **Dilated, fluid-filled, blind-ending tubular structure >6 mm in outer-to-outer diameter** arising from the cecum,
>   - **Wall thickening (>2 mm) and targetoid hyperenhancement**, AND
>   - **Periappendiceal fat stranding** (± calcified **appendicolith** or **non-compressibility** on ultrasound).
> - **Histopathologic Confirmation (Gold Standard Post-Appendectomy):** **Transmural neutrophilic infiltration** extending into the **muscularis propria** of the appendix.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **Mandatory Initial Workup:**
>   1. **Urine/Serum $\beta$-hCG:** **Mandatory in ALL females of reproductive age** to exclude ectopic pregnancy.
>   2. **CBC with Differential:** Mild-to-moderate **leukocytosis (WBC 10,000–18,000/µL)** with a **left shift (>75% neutrophils)** (_Note: WBC >20,000/µL strongly suggests **perforation/abscess**; a normal WBC does NOT rule out appendicitis_).
>   3. **CRP:** Elevated CRP + leukocytosis has >95% sensitivity for acute appendicitis.
>   4. **Urinalysis:** Excludes UTI/nephrolithiasis (_Caution: Up to 30% of appendicitis patients have mild **sterile pyuria (<20 WBC/HPF) or microscopic hematuria** when the inflamed appendix lies against the right ureter or bladder!_).
> - **Clinical Risk Scores (Used to stratify need for imaging, NOT to diagnose alone):**
>   - **Alvarado Score (MANTRELS — 10 points total):** **M**igration of pain (1), **A**norexia (1), **N**ausea/vomiting (1), **T**enderness in RLQ (2), **R**ebound pain (1), **E**levated temp ≥37.3°C (1), **L**eukocytosis >10,000 (2), **S**hift to left >75% neutrophils (1). (_Score **0–3**: Low risk, discharge/observe; **4–6**: Intermediate risk, **order imaging**; **≥7**: High risk, order CT or surgical consult_).
>   - **Adult Appendicitis Score (AAS) or Pediatric Appendicitis Score (PAS):** More accurate modern alternatives.
>
> **Imaging (Stratified by Patient Population — WSES / ACR Guidelines)**
>
> - **Non-Pregnant Adults:** **CT Abdomen and Pelvis with IV Contrast** is the **first-line gold standard** (Sensitivity >98%, Specificity >98%; _oral contrast is NOT required_).
> - **Children & Adolescents:** **Graded-Compression RUQ/RLQ Ultrasound** is **mandatory first-line** (visualizes a **non-compressible, blind-ending aperistaltic tube >6 mm** with target sign; avoids ionizing radiation). If ultrasound is **equivocal/non-visualized**, proceed to **Low-Dose CT with IV contrast OR Non-Contrast MRI**.
> - **Pregnant Patients:** **Graded-Compression Ultrasound** is **first-line**. If ultrasound is inconclusive (common in 2nd/3rd trimester), **Non-Contrast MRI Abdomen/Pelvis** is the **mandatory second-line test** (Sensitivity >96%, zero fetal radiation).

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Sudden, temporary "relief" of severe RLQ pain followed by diffuse worsening abdominal pain, high fever (>38.5–39°C), tachycardia, and WBC >18,000–20,000/µL:** Classic presentation of **acute appendiceal perforation** (rupture transiently relieves intraluminal tension before spilling pus across the peritoneum).
> - **Palpable RLQ mass** or symptom duration **>5 days** ($\rightarrow$ **walled-off periappendiceal phlegmon or abscess**; changes management from immediate surgery to percutaneous drainage + antibiotics).
> - **Age ≥40–50 years presenting with acute appendicitis:** Up to **1–3% of uncomplicated and 10–15% of complicated/abscessed appendicitis** in patients >40 years is caused by an underlying obstructing **cecal adenocarcinoma or appendiceal mucinous neoplasm**!
> - **Diffuse peritonitis or septic shock** ($\rightarrow$ free perforation requiring emergent surgical exploration).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Ruptured Ectopic Pregnancy & Ovarian Torsion (Reproductive-Age Females):** Always check $\beta$-hCG and perform pelvic/transvaginal Doppler ultrasound if adnexal pathology is suspected.
> - **Cecal Adenocarcinoma or Appendiceal Carcinoid / Mucinous Neoplasm:** Obstructs the appendiceal orifice in older adults.
> - **Testicular Torsion (Boys / Young Men):** Can present with isolated RLQ/suprapubic pain and vomiting; always examine the scrotum!
> - **Perforated Cecal Diverticulitis or Perforated Duodenal Ulcer** (_Valentino's Syndrome:_ gastroduodenal fluid tracks down the right paracolic gutter into the RLQ, mimicking acute appendicitis).
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Mesenteric Adenitis:** #1 mimic in **children/adolescents** following a recent viral URI; ultrasound/CT shows clustered **enlarged mesenteric lymph nodes (>8–10 mm)** with a completely normal appendix; self-limiting (**caused by _Yersinia enterocolitica/pseudotuberculosis_ or enteric viruses**).
> - **Gynecologic Mimics:** **Mittelschmerz** (mid-cycle ovulatory pain, self-limiting), **Ruptured Ovarian Follicular/Corpus Luteum Cyst**, **Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID) / Tubo-Ovarian Abscess** (bilateral lower quadrant pain, cervical motion tenderness, purulent cervical discharge).
> - **Ileocecal Crohn's Disease:** Subacute/chronic RLQ pain, diarrhea, weight loss, oral ulcers, terminal ileal wall thickening on CT.
> - **Meckel Diverticulitis:** Inflammation of an ileal remnant of the vitelline duct (2 feet from ileocecal valve); clinically indistinguishable from appendicitis prior to CT/surgery.
> - **Right Ureterolithiasis or Acute Pyelonephritis:** Flank-to-groin pain, costovertebral angle tenderness, gross hematuria or bacteriuria.
> - **Epiploic Appendagitis:** Torsion/ischemic infarction of a colonic subserosal fat appendage; presents with sudden localized RLQ/LLQ pain in a well-appearing patient WITHOUT fever, vomiting, or leukocytosis; CT shows a pathognomonic **2–3 cm oval fat-density lesion with a hyperattenuating ring ("ring sign")** $\rightarrow$ **strictly non-surgical** (treat with oral NSAIDs).

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Definitive source control (appendectomy or bacterial eradication), pain relief, and prevention of appendiceal perforation/sepsis.
> - **Secondary Target:** Minimize surgical site infections, avoid unnecessary bowel resection (ileocecectomy) in walled-off abscesses, and rule out occult cecal malignancy in patients ≥40 years.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Keep NPO, infuse isotonic IV crystalloids (Lactated Ringer's), and administer IV Opioid/Multimodal Analgesia + Antiemetics** immediately upon presentation.
> - **Administer Broad-Spectrum IV Antibiotics covering enteric Gram-negatives and Anaerobes within 1 hour of diagnosis** in **ALL** patients (whether undergoing surgery or non-operative management): **Cefoxitin (2 g IV)**, **Ceftriaxone (2 g IV) + Metronidazole (500 mg IV)**, OR **Piperacillin-Tazobactam (3.375–4.5 g IV)**.
>
> **Uncomplicated Acute Appendicitis (No Perforation, No Abscess, No Mass)**
>
> - **Perform Laparoscopic Appendectomy within 24 hours of admission** as the **first-line gold-standard definitive treatment** (superior to open appendectomy: less pain, lower wound infection rate, faster recovery; **mandatory in pregnant patients, immunocompromised hosts, and whenever an appendicolith is present**).
>   - _Postoperative Antibiotics:_ **Discontinue antibiotics immediately after surgery** (within 24 hours; single preoperative dose is sufficient for uncomplicated non-perforated appendicitis!).
> - **Non-Operative Antibiotic-First Alternative (Shared Decision-Making — CODA Trial):** May offer **10 days of antibiotics** (1–3 days IV ertapenem/ceftriaxone+metronidazole followed by oral ciprofloxacin/cefuroxime + metronidazole) ONLY in **carefully selected, non-pregnant adults with uncomplicated appendicitis AND NO appendicolith on CT** who strongly wish to avoid surgery (_Counsel patient that ~30% require appendectomy within 1 year and ~50% within 5 years due to recurrence_).
>
> **Complicated Acute Appendicitis (Free Perforation vs. Walled-Off Abscess/Phlegmon)**
>
> - **Free Perforation with Generalized Peritonitis or Septic Shock:** **Perform Emergent Laparoscopic (or Open) Appendectomy with Peritoneal Lavage** immediately, and continue **postoperative IV/oral broad-spectrum antibiotics for 4 days** after adequate source control (**STOP-IT trial**).
> - **Contained / Walled-Off Periappendiceal Phlegmon or Abscess (Typically Presenting Late >5 Days):**
>   - **Do NOT perform immediate surgery** (high risk of iatrogenic enterotomy, fistula, and forced right hemicolectomy).
>   - **Treat with IV Broad-Spectrum Antibiotics alone** (if phlegmon or abscess **<3 cm**) OR **IV Antibiotics + CT/Ultrasound-Guided Percutaneous Catheter Drainage** (if abscess **≥3–4 cm**), continuing antibiotics for 4–7 days after drainage.
>
> **Follow-up, Interval Management, & Incidental Findings**
>
> - **Perform a Mandatory Screening Colonoscopy in 6–8 weeks** in **ALL patients aged ≥40 years** who were managed non-operatively (either for complicated periappendiceal abscess/phlegmon OR antibiotic-first uncomplicated appendicitis) to rule out an underlying obstructing **cecal adenocarcinoma or appendiceal neoplasm** (~10–15% incidence in >40 y with complicated appendicitis!).
> - **Interval Appendectomy (at 6–8 weeks after abscess resolution):** **NOT routinely required** in young asymptomatic patients (<40 y) after successful percutaneous drainage, unless they have **recurrent symptoms** or a **persistent appendicolith** on imaging (though many surgeons offer elective interval laparoscopy in patients ≥40 y alongside colonoscopy).
> - **Management of Appendiceal Neuroendocrine Tumor (Carcinoid) Found Incidentally on Pathology:**
>   - **Tumor <1 cm:** **Simple appendectomy alone is 100% curative** (no further workup or resection needed).
>   - **Tumor >2 cm** (or **1–2 cm with high-risk features**: mesoappendiceal invasion >3 mm, base/margin involvement, lymphovascular invasion, or high grade): **Perform an Oncologic Right Hemicolectomy** with regional lymphadenectomy.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Non-Operative (Antibiotic-Only) Management in Pregnant Patients, Immunocompromised Patients, or Patients with an Appendicolith on CT**, as treatment failure and perforation rates are unacceptably high (maternal perforation carries a **20–35% fetal mortality rate** vs. <1.5% with prompt laparoscopic appendectomy!).
> - **Avoid Immediate Surgical Exploration of a Walled-Off Periappendiceal Phlegmon or Abscess** in a hemodynamically stable patient without generalized peritonitis, as friable inflamed tissues prevent safe closure of the appendiceal stump and convert a simple appendicitis into a high-morbidity **ileocecectomy or right hemicolectomy**.
> - **Avoid Continuing Postoperative Antibiotics >24 hours after Appendectomy for Uncomplicated (Non-Perforated) Appendicitis** to prevent _C. difficile_ colitis and antimicrobial resistance.
> - **Avoid Removing a Normal-Appearing Appendix during Laparoscopy IF Crohn's Disease Directly Involves the Base of the Appendix / Cecum**, as dividing an inflamed Crohn's cecal base leads to a high rate of **enterocutaneous fistula and stump blowout** (if the cecal base is healthy and only the terminal ileum shows Crohn's, appendectomy is safe to prevent future diagnostic confusion).

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Surgical Site Infection (Wound Infection) & Postoperative Intra-Abdominal Abscess:** Most common complications following appendectomy (especially after perforated appendicitis) $\rightarrow$ presents on **Post-Op Day 5–10** with spiking fevers, leukocytosis, ileus, or diarrhea/tenesmus (pelvic abscess) $\rightarrow$ diagnose with **CT Abdomen/Pelvis** and treat deep abscesses with **CT-guided percutaneous drainage + antibiotics**.
> - **Stump Appendicitis:** Recurrent appendicitis occurring months to years after an incomplete appendectomy that left behind a **long appendiceal stump (>0.5 cm)** $\rightarrow$ requires completion stump resection.
> - **Pylephlebitis (Suppurative Thrombophlebitis of the Portal Vein):** Rare, life-threatening complication of untreated perforated appendicitis or diverticulitis $\rightarrow$ ascending septic thrombosis of the **superior mesenteric and portal veins** (_Bacteroides fragilis, E. coli_) $\rightarrow$ presents with high spiking fevers ("picket-fence"), rigors, RUQ pain, jaundice, and multiple **pyogenic liver abscesses** $\rightarrow$ diagnose via **CT with IV contrast / Doppler US** and treat with **prolonged IV broad-spectrum antibiotics (4–6 weeks) + anticoagulation**.
> - **Pseudomyxoma Peritonei:** Rupture of an **Appendiceal Mucinous Neoplasm (LAMN/mucocele)** seeding mucin-producing cells across the peritoneum ("jelly belly") $\rightarrow$ never grasp or rupture a mucocele during laparoscopy!

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 21-year-old woman presents with 12 hours of abdominal pain that began as a dull ache around her umbilicus and migrated 4 hours ago to her right lower quadrant, followed by anorexia, nausea, and one episode of vomiting. Vitals show T 38.1°C, HR 94 bpm, and BP 118/72 mmHg; exam reveals focal tenderness and guarding at **McBurney's point** and a positive **Rovsing's sign**. Urine $\beta$-hCG is negative, WBC is 14,800/µL (82% neutrophils), and **CT abdomen/pelvis with IV contrast** shows a 10-mm dilated, wall-thickened appendix with periappendiceal fat stranding and a calcified **appendicolith** without abscess. She receives IV **ceftriaxone + metronidazole**, IV fentanyl, and undergoes an uncomplicated **laparoscopic appendectomy** within 12 hours (chosen over antibiotics-alone due to the appendicolith), with antibiotics stopped immediately postoperatively.
