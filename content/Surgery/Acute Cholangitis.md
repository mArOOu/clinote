> [!abstract] Acute Cholangitis
> Life-threatening bacterial infection of an obstructed biliary system (most commonly the common bile duct), driven by the dual requirement of **biliary stasis/obstruction** AND **bacterial colonization** (predominantly ascending from the duodenum).

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Biliary Obstruction:** Caused by **Choledocholithiasis** (#1, 50–70%), **Malignant Biliary Stricture** (10–30%: pancreatic head adenocarcinoma, cholangiocarcinoma, ampullary cancer—typically triggered _after_ prior ERCP/stent manipulation), **Benign Strictures** (Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis [PSC], post-cholecystectomy injury, chronic pancreatitis), or **Occluded Biliary Stent**.
> - **Cholangiovenous & Cholangiolymphatic Reflux:** Normal intrabiliary pressure is **7–14 $\text{cmH}_2\text{O}$**. When obstruction raises biliary pressure above the hepatic secretory pressure (**>20–25 $\text{cmH}_2\text{O}$**), hepatic tight junctions rupture, forcing bacteria (**E. coli** #1 [25–50%], _Klebsiella_ [15–20%], _Enterococcus_ [10–20%], _Enterobacter_, and _Bacteroides/Clostridium_ [in prior sphincterotomy/biliary-enteric anastomosis]) and endotoxin directly into the **hepatic sinusoids and systemic bloodstream**, triggering fulminant Gram-negative bacteremia and septic shock.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Antibiotics alone DO NOT cure Acute Cholangitis:** Because intrabiliary pressure **>25 $\text{cmH}_2\text{O}$** blocks hepatic excretion of antibiotics into bile, systemic antibiotics only control bacteremia—**mechanical biliary decompression via ERCP is mandatory** for survival!
> - **Charcot Triad is specific (~90%), NOT sensitive (~50–70%):** Up to 30–50% of patients (especially the elderly) lack the full triad on presentation; never rule out cholangitis just because fever or clinical jaundice is absent—rely on **Tokyo Guidelines (TG18)**!
> - **Early transaminase spike mimics Acute Hepatitis:** In acute CBD stone impaction, **AST and ALT often surge transiently to >500–1,000 U/L** in the first 24–48 hours before rapidly falling as **Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP), GGT, and Conjugated Bilirubin** climb.
> - **Keep ERCP simple in Septic Shock:** In critically ill **Grade III (Reynolds Pentad)** cholangitis, placing a rapid **biliary stent or nasobiliary drain** via ERCP is safer and faster than attempting prolonged sphincterotomy and complex stone extraction, which can be completed electively once sepsis resolves.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Charcot Triad (Present in ~50–70%):**
>   1. **Fever** (present in >90%, often with rigors/shaking chills),
>   2. **Right Upper Quadrant (RUQ) or Epigastric Pain** (~70%), AND
>   3. **Jaundice** (~60–70%, accompanied by dark tea-colored urine, pale acholic stools, and pruritus).
> - **Reynolds Pentad (Present in ~5–15% — Defines Grade III / Suppurative Cholangitis with ~50% mortality if untreated):**
>   - **Charcot Triad PLUS** (4) **Hypotension / Septic Shock** and (5) **Altered Mental Status / Lethargy**.
> - **Atypical / Elderly Presentation:** Elderly or immunocompromised patients may present solely with **unexplained delirium, hypotension, or a fall** without localizing RUQ pain!
> - **Key History Clues:** Prior gallstones/biliary colic, prior **ERCP or indwelling biliary stent** (stent occlusion is a classic cause), history of **Ulcerative Colitis / PSC**, or East/Southeast Asian origin (**Recurrent Pyogenic Cholangitis / Oriental Cholangiohepatitis** caused by _Clonorchis sinensis_ or _Ascaris lumbricoides_).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **High fever (>38.5–39.5°C), tachycardia, hypotension.**
> - **Scleral icterus and cutaneous jaundice.**
> - **Moderate RUQ tenderness to palpation**, hepatomegaly (_Note:_ **Peritoneal signs / rigid guarding and a positive Murphy's sign are typically ABSENT** unless there is concurrent acute cholecystitis or perforation, because the extrahepatic bile duct is deep and retroperitoneal!).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> **Tokyo Guidelines (TG18) — Definite Diagnosis** requires **1 item from A + 1 item from B + 1 item from C**:
>
> - **A. Systemic Inflammation:**
>   1. **Fever (>38.0°C)** and/or shaking chills, OR
>   2. Laboratory evidence of inflammatory response (**WBC <4,000 or >10,000/µL**, OR **CRP ≥1 mg/dL**).
> - **B. Cholestasis:**
>   1. **Jaundice (Total Bilirubin ≥2.0 mg/dL)**, OR
>   2. **Abnormal Liver Chemistries** (**ALP, GGT, AST, and/or ALT >1.5× Upper Limit of Normal [ULN]**).
> - **C. Biliary Imaging Findings:**
>   1. **Biliary dilatation** (**Common Bile Duct >6 mm** on ultrasound/CT/MRCP; or >8–10 mm if post-cholecystectomy) and/or intrahepatic biliary ductal dilatation, OR
>   2. **Imaging evidence of the underlying etiology** (e.g., **CBD stone [choledocholithiasis]**, biliary stricture, tumor, or occluded stent).

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **Mandatory STAT Workup:**
>   1. **CBC with Differential:** Leukocytosis with left shift (or leukopenia in severe sepsis) + thrombocytopenia (in septic DIC or cirrhosis).
>   2. **Hepatic Panel (CMP):** Elevated **Conjugated (Direct) Bilirubin**, **Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP)**, and **GGT** (cholestatic pattern), often with an early acute spike in **AST and ALT**.
>   3. **Blood Cultures (×2 sets):** **Mandatory before starting antibiotics** (positive in 30–70% of cases; guides step-down therapy).
>   4. **Serum Lipase:** Mandatory to identify concurrent **Acute Gallstone Pancreatitis** (seen in ~10–20% when a stone is impacted at the ampulla of Vater).
>   5. **Sepsis / Organ Failure Panel (Tokyo Severity Grading):** **Serum Lactate**, **Creatinine / BUN**, **PT/INR** (**prolonged PT/INR** in cholangitis results from BOTH **sepsis-induced DIC** and **Vitamin K malabsorption** due to lack of intraluminal bile salts!), **Arterial Blood Gas ($\text{PaO}_2/\text{FiO}_2$)**.
>
> **Imaging (Do NOT Delay Resuscitation or Urgent ERCP for Advanced Imaging)**
>
> - **Right Upper Quadrant Ultrasound (RUQ US):** **First-line initial bedside imaging test** (fast, portable; highly specific for **intrahepatic and extrahepatic bile duct dilatation [CBD >6 mm]** and gallbladder stones, though sensitivity for visualizing the actual distal CBD stone is only ~30–50% due to overlying duodenal gas!).
>   - _Crucial Rule:_ **A normal RUQ Ultrasound does NOT rule out early acute cholangitis** (in acute abrupt stone impaction, the bile ducts may take 24–48 hours to dilate!).
> - **CT Abdomen/Pelvis with IV Contrast:** Preferred initial or adjunct test in **hemodynamically unstable/septic patients** or when the diagnosis is uncertain; rules out other intra-abdominal catastrophes, detects distal radiopaque stones, identifies **pancreaticobiliary masses**, and detects complications (**pyogenic hepatic abscesses, portal vein thrombosis**).
> - **MRCP (Magnetic Resonance Cholangiopancreatography) or Endoscopic Ultrasound (EUS):** **95% sensitivity for CBD stones/strictures**; reserve strictly for cases where the diagnosis remains uncertain after US/CT and the patient is stable (if cholangitis is clinically definite, **skip MRCP and go straight to therapeutic ERCP**!).

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> **Tokyo Guidelines (TG18) Severity Grading (Dictates Timing of Biliary Drainage):**
>
> - **Grade III (Severe) Acute Cholangitis — Requires EMERGENT Biliary Drainage (<12–24 hours) + ICU Admission:** Presence of **≥1 New-Onset Organ Dysfunction**:
>   1. **Cardiovascular:** **Hypotension** requiring dopamine ≥5 mcg/kg/min or **any dose of Norepinephrine**.
>   2. **Neurological:** **Disturbance of consciousness / Encephalopathy** (**Reynolds Pentad**).
>   3. **Respiratory:** $\text{PaO}_2/\text{FiO}_2 \text{ ratio } <300$.
>   4. **Renal:** Oliguria or **Serum Creatinine >2.0 mg/dL**.
>   5. **Hepatic:** **PT-INR >1.5**.
>   6. **Hematological:** **Platelet count <100,000/µL**.
> - **Grade II (Moderate) Acute Cholangitis — Requires EARLY Biliary Drainage (<24 hours):** No organ failure, but **≥2 of the following 5 high-risk features**:
>   1. **WBC >12,000/µL or <4,000/µL**, (2) **High Fever ≥39.0°C**, (3) **Age ≥75 years**, (4) **Hyperbilirubinemia (Total Bilirubin ≥5.0 mg/dL)**, or (5) **Hypoalbuminemia (<0.7× lower limit of normal)**.
> - **Grade I (Mild) Acute Cholangitis:** Does not meet Grade II or III criteria (perform ERCP within **24–48 hours**).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Pyogenic Liver Abscess:** Fever, chills, RUQ pain, tender hepatomegaly, elevated ALP, and a hypodense rim-enhancing hepatic lesion on CT/US (can be either a _mimic_ OR a direct _complication_ of untreated ascending cholangitis!).
> - **Acute Gallstone Pancreatitis:** Epigastric pain radiating to back + **Lipase ≥3× ULN**; frequently coexists with acute cholangitis when a stone impacts at the ampulla of Vater.
> - **Pylephlebitis (Septic Portal Vein Thrombosis):** Spiking fevers + RUQ pain + jaundice secondary to appendicitis or diverticulitis; diagnosed by Doppler US / CT with IV contrast.
> - **Right Lower Lobe Pneumonia / Empyema or Perforated Duodenal Ulcer.**
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Acute Calculous Cholecystitis:** RUQ pain >6h + fever + **positive Murphy's sign** + gallbladder wall thickening/pericholecystic fluid on US, but typically **normal Bilirubin (<2 mg/dL) and normal CBD caliber (≤6 mm)**.
> - **Uncomplicated Choledocholithiasis:** RUQ pain + obstructive jaundice (elevated conjugated bilirubin, ALP, GGT) + dilated CBD on US, **WITHOUT fever, leukocytosis, or sepsis**.
> - **Mirizzi Syndrome:** Impacted cystic duct stone compressing the common hepatic duct; can become superinfected and progress to true acute cholangitis.
> - **Acute Viral or Alcoholic Hepatitis:** Markedly elevated AST/ALT or AST:ALT ≥2:1 without biliary dilatation on imaging.
>
> **Less Common**
>
> - **HIV / AIDS Cholangiopathy:** CD4 count **<100 cells/mm³**; biliary strictures (papillary stenosis + sclerosing cholangitis) caused by **_Cryptosporidium parvum_** (#1), **CMV**, or _Microsporidia_; presents with RUQ pain and markedly elevated **ALP** (bilirubin is often normal/mildly elevated); treated with **ERCP sphincterotomy + Antiretroviral Therapy (ART)**.
> - **IgG4-Related Sclerosing Cholangitis:** Painless obstructive jaundice + elevated serum **IgG4** + autoimmune pancreatitis ("sausage-shaped" pancreas); responds dramatically to **corticosteroids** (typically does not present with bacterial fever/sepsis unless instrumented).

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Immediate hemodynamic stabilization (**MAP ≥65 mmHg**), bloodstream sterilization, and **urgent mechanical biliary decompression (within 12–24 hours for severe/moderate disease; within 24–48 hours for mild disease)**.
> - **Secondary Target:** Definitive clearance of common bile duct stones and **same-admission laparoscopic cholecystectomy** (if gallstone etiology) to prevent fatal recurrence.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (First 0–3 Hours — Sepsis Bundle)**
>
> - **Keep NPO, infuse balanced IV crystalloids (Lactated Ringer's 30 mL/kg if hypotensive or lactate ≥4 mmol/L), and start Norepinephrine** (first-line vasopressor) if MAP remains <65 mmHg.
> - **Draw 2 sets of Blood Cultures and administer Broad-Spectrum IV Antibiotics WITHIN 1 HOUR** (in septic shock/Grade III) or **within 3 hours** (Grade I/II) covering Gram-negative Enterobacterales, _Enterococcus_, and Anaerobes:
>   - **Grade I/II (Community-Acquired):** **Piperacillin-Tazobactam (3.375–4.5 g IV q6h)** OR **Ceftriaxone (2 g IV daily) + Metronidazole (500 mg IV q8h)** (_Note: Add **Metronidazole** to cephalosporins if the patient has had a prior biliary-enteric anastomosis, sphincterotomy, or biliary stent_).
>   - **Grade III (Severe / Septic Shock), Healthcare-Associated, or Indwelling Biliary Stent:** **Piperacillin-Tazobactam (4.5 g IV q6h)**, **Cefepime (2 g IV q8h) + Metronidazole** (+ **Vancomycin or Ampicillin** to cover _Enterococcus spp._), OR **Meropenem / Imipenem (1 g IV q8h)** (if risk of ESBL organisms).
> - **Correct Coagulopathy (Administer IV Vitamin K 10 mg, or 4-factor PCC / FFP if urgent ERCP sphincterotomy is required)** and **replete electrolytes ($\text{K}^+, \text{Mg}^{2+}$)**.
>
> **Definitive Biliary Decompression (Stepwise Modality Selection — TG18 / ASGE Guidelines)**
>
> - **First-Line Gold Standard: Perform Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) with Biliary Drainage:**
>   - _Timing:_ **Urgent (<12–24 hours)** for **Grade III (Severe)** and **Grade II (Moderate)** cholangitis; **within 24–48 hours** for **Grade I (Mild)** cholangitis (overall in-hospital mortality is significantly lower when ERCP is performed **<24–48 hours** from admission!).
>   - _Technique:_ Perform **Biliary Stent placement** (plastic or covered metal stent) or **Endoscopic Sphincterotomy with Balloon/Basket Stone Extraction** (_Crucial Pearl:_ In **unstable Grade III patients or severe uncorrected coagulopathy**, perform **rapid Biliary Stent placement WITHOUT sphincterotomy** to immediately decompress pus and avoid sphincterotomy bleeding/prolonged anesthesia; return for stone extraction later once sepsis resolves!).
>   - _Prophylaxis during ERCP:_ Administer **Rectal Indomethacin (100 mg suppository)** during ERCP to prevent **Post-ERCP Pancreatitis (PEP)**.
> - **Second-Line Salvage (If ERCP Fails or Anatomy is Altered [e.g., Roux-en-Y Gastric Bypass / Whipple]):**
>   - **Perform EUS-Guided Biliary Drainage (EUS-BD)** (at expert centers) OR **Percutaneous Transhepatic Cholangiography / Biliary Drainage (PTBD)** via Interventional Radiology to decompress the intrahepatic/extrahepatic biliary tree externally.
> - **Third-Line Last Resort:** **Open or Laparoscopic Surgical Common Bile Duct Exploration with T-Tube Drainage** (rarely performed today due to high mortality [20–40%] in acutely septic patients; used only if both ERCP and PTBD fail/are unavailable).
>
> **Post-Drainage Antibiotic Duration & Definitive Prevention**
>
> - **Continue IV/PO Antibiotics for 4 to 7 days AFTER successful biliary drainage** (per Tokyo Guidelines; extend to **2 weeks** if **Gram-positive bacteremia [*Enterococcus* or *Streptococcus*]** is present due to infective endocarditis risk, or **4–6 weeks** if complicated by a **pyogenic hepatic abscess**).
> - **Perform Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy during the SAME hospital admission** (once sepsis resolves) in all surgically fit patients whose cholangitis was caused by **gallstones (choledocholithiasis)** to prevent a >25–40% risk of recurrent cholangitis or gallstone pancreatitis.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid relying on Antibiotics Alone ("medical management") without Biliary Decompression**, as purulent bile under pressure cannot be sterilized by systemic antibiotics and rapidly progresses to **Reynolds pentad, refractory septic shock, and death**.
> - **Avoid performing Diagnostic ERCP** in patients with low-to-intermediate probability of CBD stones _without_ cholangitis (always use **MRCP or EUS** for pure diagnosis; reserve **ERCP strictly for therapeutic intervention** due to its 5–10% risk of **Post-ERCP Pancreatitis**, bleeding, and retroduodenal perforation).
> - **Avoid performing Endoscopic Sphincterotomy during ERCP in a patient with Severe Uncorrected Coagulopathy (INR >1.5–2.0, Platelets <50,000/µL, or active P2Y12/DOAC therapy)**—instead, place a **transpapillary plastic biliary stent WITHOUT cutting the sphincter** to safely decompress the bile duct with zero bleeding risk!
> - **Avoid high-pressure Contrast Injection into the Biliary Tree during ERCP** in a patient with acute suppurative cholangitis; **aspirate pus/bile FIRST** before injecting minimal contrast to avoid driving bacteria into the hepatic veins (**cholangiovenous reflux**) and precipitating immediate on-table septic shock!

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Pyogenic Hepatic Abscesses:** Direct ascending extension of untreated biliary infection into the liver parenchyma $\rightarrow$ multiple coalescing microabscesses or large cavitary abscesses $\rightarrow$ persistent spiking fevers despite ERCP $\rightarrow$ treat with **4–6 weeks of IV antibiotics + percutaneous aspiration/catheter drainage (if >3–5 cm)**.
> - **Post-ERCP Pancreatitis (PEP):** Most common serious complication of ERCP (~5–10%); presents with severe epigastric pain and lipase ≥3× ULN within 2–24 hours post-procedure $\rightarrow$ prevented by **rectal indomethacin (100 mg)**, guidewire-assisted cannulation, and prophylactic pancreatic duct stenting in high-risk cases.
> - **Post-Sphincterotomy Bleeding or Retroperitoneal Duodenal Perforation:**
>   - _Bleeding:_ Treat with endoscopic epinephrine injection, thermal coagulation, hemoclips, or fully covered metal stent tamponade.
>   - _Perforation:_ Presents with severe post-ERCP abdominal/back pain, subcutaneous emphysema, and **retroperitoneal air around the right kidney** on CT.
> - **Secondary Biliary Cirrhosis & Cholangiocarcinoma:** Long-term sequelae of chronic recurrent cholangitis (e.g., **Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis [PSC]** or **Recurrent Pyogenic Cholangitis** from _Clonorchis sinensis_ / hepatolithiasis).

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 76-year-old man with a history of cholelithiasis is brought to the ED with 18 hours of high fever, shaking chills, RUQ pain, and new-onset confusion. Vitals show T 39.3°C, HR 118 bpm, and BP 84/50 mmHg; exam reveals scleral icterus, lethargy, and moderate RUQ tenderness (**Reynolds Pentad = Tokyo Grade III Severe Acute Cholangitis**). Labs show WBC 21,400/µL, platelets 88,000/µL, total bilirubin 6.8 mg/dL, alkaline phosphatase 460 U/L, AST 310 U/L, ALT 340 U/L, INR 1.7, and lactate 4.6 mmol/L; bedside **RUQ Ultrasound** demonstrates a dilated **11-mm Common Bile Duct** and gallstones. Blood cultures are drawn, resuscitation with **Lactated Ringer's, Norepinephrine, IV Vitamin K, and IV Piperacillin-Tazobactam** is initiated within 30 minutes, and he undergoes **emergent ERCP (<12 hours)** where purulent bile is aspirated and a **plastic biliary stent** is placed (deferring sphincterotomy due to coagulopathy/instability), resulting in rapid hemodynamic recovery followed by definitive stone extraction and same-admission laparoscopic cholecystectomy.
