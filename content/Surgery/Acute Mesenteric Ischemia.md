> [!abstract] Acute Mesenteric Ischemia
> Sudden interruption or critical reduction of blood flow to the small intestine (and right colon)—almost exclusively within the **Superior Mesenteric Artery (SMA) or Vein (SMV)** distribution—leading to cellular hypoxia, mucosal sloughing, **transmural bowel necrosis within 6–12 hours**, and **50–80% mortality** if revascularization is delayed.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Why the Superior Mesenteric Artery (SMA)?** The SMA branches off the abdominal aorta at an **acute oblique angle (~45°)** and has a large caliber, making it a direct funnel for cardiogenic emboli; it supplies the entire **midgut (distal duodenum, jejunum, ileum, cecum, ascending colon, and proximal two-thirds of the transverse colon)**.
> - **Inside-Out Ischemic Progression:**
>   - **0–6 hours (Reversible Mucosal Phase):** The metabolically active **villus tips of the mucosa** undergo ischemia first $\rightarrow$ Intense visceral spasm (**"pain out of proportion to exam"** + forceful **"gut emptying"** vomiting/diarrhea), while the outer **serosa remains pink and uninflamed** (hence **ZERO peritoneal signs on exam**!).
>   - **>6–12 hours (Irreversible Transmural Infarction):** Necrosis extends through the muscularis propria to the **serosa** $\rightarrow$ bacterial translocation, **pneumatosis intestinalis**, **lactic acidosis**, paralytic ileus, **peritonitis**, perforation, and fatal septic shock.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Classic Triad = Sudden Severe Pain Out of Proportion to Exam + Gut Emptying (Vomiting/Diarrhea) + Embolic/Vascular Source (Atrial Fibrillation):** Any patient with atrial fibrillation or recent MI who develops acute severe abdominal pain with a **soft, benign-feeling abdomen** has **Acute Mesenteric Ischemia** until proven otherwise!
> - **A NORMAL Serum Lactate DOES NOT rule out Acute Mesenteric Ischemia:** Lactate is cleared efficiently by the liver via the portal vein during early mucosal ischemia; **elevated lactate (>2–4 mmol/L) is a LATE sign indicating already established transmural bowel gangrene**!
> - **Order a Biphasic CT Angiography (CTA)—NOT a standard Contrast CT:** Standard portal-venous-phase abdominal CT misses up to 30% of early SMA occlusions; you must order a **CTA Abdomen/Pelvis (arterial + venous phases, NO oral contrast)**, and **never withhold IV contrast for elevated Creatinine/AKI**!
> - **Master the 4 Etiologic Subtypes ("50 – 25 – 20 – 5 Rule"):**
>   1. **SMA Arterial Embolism (50%):** Cardiogenic (**Atrial Fibrillation**, LV mural thrombus post-MI, endocarditis, cardioversion); lodges **3–10 cm distal to the SMA origin** (just distal to the **middle colic artery**), **sparing the proximal jejunum and transverse colon**.
>   2. **SMA Arterial Thrombosis (25%):** Acute plaque rupture at the **ostium (origin) of the SMA** in a patient with diffuse atherosclerosis and prior **Chronic Mesenteric Ischemia ("intestinal angina" / food fear + weight loss)**; causes catastrophic necrosis of the **entire midgut from ligament of Treitz to mid-transverse colon**.
>   3. **Non-Occlusive Mesenteric Ischemia [NOMI] (20%):** Intense splanchnic arterial vasoconstriction WITHOUT a clot in **critically ill ICU patients** in **cardiogenic/septic shock on high-dose alpha-adrenergic vasopressors (norepinephrine, phenylephrine, vasopressin)**, post-cardiac/CPB surgery, hemodialysis, or **cocaine/digoxin/ergot** toxicity.
>   4. **Mesenteric Venous Thrombosis [MVT] (5–10%):** Thrombosis of the **Superior Mesenteric Vein** in patients with **hypercoagulable states** (Factor V Leiden, JAK2/myeloproliferative neoplasms, paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria, OCPs, malignancy), **cirrhosis/portal hypertension**, or local inflammation (**pancreatitis, IBD, diverticulitis**); presents subacutely over days to 2 weeks and has the **best prognosis (~80–90% survival)** with anticoagulation alone!

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Sudden-onset, excruciating, constant periumbilical pain** (instantaneous in SMA embolism; progressive over hours in thrombosis/NOMI; subacute over 2–14 days in MVT).
> - **Urgent "Gut Emptying" at Onset:** Reflex hyperperistalsis in response to acute ischemia causes forceful vomiting and **urgent defecation**.
> - **Hematochezia / "Currant-Jelly" Stool (Late Sign):** Occurs hours later as necrotic mucosa sloughs into the lumen.
> - **Prodromal History of "Intestinal Angina" (Present in 50–70% of SMA Arterial Thrombosis):** Dull, cramping postprandial epigastric pain occurring **15–60 minutes after every meal**, leading to **sitophobia ("food fear")** and **profound unintended weight loss**.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Early Phase (First 0–6 Hours — The Diagnostic Window to Save the Bowel):**
>   - **"Pain Out of Proportion to Physical Examination" (Hallmark):** Patient is writhing in agony and diaphoretic, yet abdominal palpation reveals a **soft, non-distended abdomen with minimal tenderness and NO guarding or rebound**.
>   - **Cardiovascular:** Irregularly irregular tachycardia (**Atrial Fibrillation**), carotid/femoral/abdominal bruits, or signs of heart failure/shock.
> - **Late Phase (>6–12 Hours — Transmural Gangrene):**
>   - **Generalized Peritonitis:** **Rigid "board-like" abdomen**, rebound tenderness, tympanitic distention, **absent ("silent") bowel sounds**, feculent emesis, and refractory septic shock.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> **Biphasic CT Angiography (CTA) of the Abdomen and Pelvis (Gold Standard — Sensitivity 94%, Specificity 95%):**
>
> - **Vascular Criterion:** Intraluminal **filling defect (embolus or thrombus)** within the **Superior Mesenteric Artery** (or SMV in venous thrombosis) OR diffuse Pruning/beading spasm of SMA branches in **NOMI**, **PLUS**
> - **Intestinal Ischemia Signs:** Decreased or absent bowel wall contrast enhancement, bowel wall thickening ("target sign" in MVT/reperfusion) or paper-thinning (in arterial infarction), mesenteric fat stranding, or late **pneumatosis intestinalis / portal venous gas**.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs (Order STAT — Remember: No Lab Can Rule Out Early AMI!)**
>
> - **CBC with Differential:** Marked **Leukocytosis (WBC >15,000–25,000/µL)** with a severe left shift/bandemia (often one of the earliest lab abnormalities!) and hemoconcentration.
> - **Serum Lactate & Arterial Blood Gas:** **High anion-gap metabolic acidosis (pH <7.30, low $\text{HCO}_3^-$) and elevated L-Lactate (>2.0–4.0 mmol/L)** (_Crucial Pitfall: **Normal in up to 50% of early reversible AMI**—if you wait for the lactate to rise before ordering a CTA, the bowel is already dead!_).
> - **D-Dimer:** **>95% sensitivity (High Negative Predictive Value)**—a completely normal D-dimer makes acute thromboembolic mesenteric ischemia very unlikely, though specificity is poor.
> - **CMP, Lipase,LDH, CK, Phosphate, Potassium:** **Hyperkalemia and hyperphosphatemia** develop late from massive smooth muscle cell lysis (necrotic bowel); mild amylase/lipase elevation (<2–3× ULN) is common.
> - **ECG, Troponin, Type & Crossmatch (4 units pRBCs), PT/INR, aPTT.**
>
> **Imaging**
>
> - **Biphasic CT Angiography (CTA) Abdomen/Pelvis (Non-contrast + Arterial Phase + Portal Venous Phase, NO Oral Contrast):**
>   - **Mandatory immediate first-line imaging test** whenever AMI is suspected (**do NOT delay for renal function/creatinine checks, and NEVER give oral contrast**, which obscures intraluminal IV contrast enhancement and delays the scan!).
> - **Catheter-Based Selective Mesenteric Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA):** Performed in a hybrid OR / IR suite for simultaneous diagnosis and **endovascular revascularization** (or intra-arterial vasodilator infusion in **NOMI**).
> - **Plain Abdominal X-Ray (KUB):** **Insensitive and non-specific (normal in 25% of early AMI)**; used only to quickly exclude free air (Late X-ray signs: adynamic ileus, "thumbprinting" from submucosal edema/hemorrhage, **pneumatosis intestinalis**, and **portal venous gas**).

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> **Indicators of Established Transmural Bowel Infarction (Mandate Immediate Laparotomy):**
>
> - **Peritoneal Signs on Exam:** Involuntary guarding, abdominal rigidity, or rebound tenderness.
> - **Hemodynamic Instability / Septic Shock** or worsening **Lactic Acidosis / Hyperkalemia**.
> - **Ominous CT Findings:**
>   1. **Pneumatosis Intestinalis** (intramural gas dissecting along the necrotic bowel wall),
>   2. **Hepatic Portal Venous Gas** (gas tracking into the peripheral <2 cm of the liver capsule; differentiates from benign _pneumobilia_, which stays central $>2\text{ cm}$ from the capsule!),
>   3. **Absent Bowel Wall Enhancement** ("paper-thin" unenhancing bowel wall), OR
>   4. **Pneumoperitoneum** (free intraperitoneal air = perforation).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Acute Mesenteric Ischemia vs. Ischemic Colitis (Crucial High-Yield Distinction!):**
>   | Feature | **Acute Mesenteric Ischemia (AMI)** | **Ischemic Colitis (Most Common GI Ischemia)** |
>   | :--- | :--- | :--- |
>   | **Vessel & Territory** | **Superior Mesenteric Artery (SMA)** $\rightarrow$ **Small Bowel & Right Colon** | Non-occlusive microvascular hypoperfusion of **Inferior Mesenteric Artery (IMA) "Watershed" zones** (**Splenic Flexure [Griffith's point]** & **Rectosigmoid [Sudeck's point]**; rectum is spared!) |
>   | **Presentation** | **Sudden agonizing periumbilical pain** out of proportion to exam; hematochezia is _late_ | **Mild-to-moderate cramping LLQ pain** followed within 24 hours by **frank bloody diarrhea / hematochezia** |
>   | **Management & Mortality** | **Emergent Anticoagulation + Surgical/Endovascular Revascularization ± Resection** (50–80% mortality) | **85% resolve with Supportive Care alone** (NPO, IV fluids, antibiotics); surgery only if transmural gangrene/peritonitis (<10–15% mortality) |
> - **Ruptured Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (AAA), Perforated Peptic Ulcer, Severe Acute Pancreatitis, & Strangulated Closed-Loop SBO / Volvulus.**
>
> **Most Likely (Chronic Variant)**
>
> - **Chronic Mesenteric Ischemia ("Intestinal Angina"):** Severe atherosclerotic stenosis of **≥2 of the 3 major mesenteric arteries (Celiac Axis, SMA, IMA)** in older female smokers; presents with postprandial pain 30 min after eating, **sitophobia (food fear)**, and weight loss; diagnosed by **CTA or Duplex Ultrasound** and treated electively with **Endovascular Angioplasty and Stenting** (first-line, lower perioperative morbidity) or **Open Surgical Mesenteric Bypass** (more durable long-term patency).
> - **Median Arcuate Ligament Syndrome (MALS / Celiac Axis Compression Syndrome):** Extrinsic compression of the **Celiac Artery** and celiac ganglion by the fibrous median arcuate ligament of the diaphragm during expiration in young thin women; causes postprandial epigastric pain and an **expiratory epigastric bruit**.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target ("Time is Bowel"):** Immediate systemic anticoagulation, hemodynamic resuscitation, **urgent restoration of SMA arterial blood flow (<6 hours)**, and **surgical resection of all non-viable gangrenous bowel**.
> - **Secondary Target:** Preserve maximal small bowel length (prevent **Short Bowel Syndrome**), prevent reperfusion abdominal compartment syndrome, and initiate lifelong secondary prevention (anticoagulation for A-fib/MVT).
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (First 0–30 Minutes — Apply to ALL 4 Subtypes)**
>
> - **Keep strictly NPO, place a Nasogastric (NG) Tube for decompression, and infuse aggressive isotonic IV crystalloids (Lactated Ringer's)** (massive third-spacing into the ischemic bowel wall requires 5–10 L of resuscitation; avoid alpha-adrenergic vasoconstrictors whenever possible, using **Dobutamine, Milrinone, or low-dose Norepinephrine** only after volume repletion to preserve splanchnic blood flow).
> - **Start Systemic Anticoagulation IMMEDIATELY with an IV Unfractionated Heparin Infusion (Bolus 80 units/kg IV, then 18 units/kg/hr)** upon diagnostic confirmation (or high clinical suspicion while awaiting OR, provided there is no active exsanguinating hemorrhage) to halt clot propagation into the mesenteric arcades.
> - **Administer Broad-Spectrum IV Antibiotics covering Gram-negatives and Anaerobes within 1 hour** (**Piperacillin-Tazobactam 4.5 g IV q6h**, OR **Ceftriaxone 2 g IV + Metronidazole 500 mg IV**) because mucosal ischemia rapidly triggers transmural bacterial translocation and portal bacteremia.
> - **Consult Vascular Surgery, General Surgery, and Interventional Radiology STAT** as a multidisciplinary "Mesenteric Ischemia Team."
>
> **Definitive Management by Subtype & Bowel Viability (WSES / ESVS Guidelines)**
>
> - **1. Acute Arterial Occlusion (SMA Embolism or Thrombosis) WITH Peritonitis or CT Signs of Infarction (Pneumatosis / Portal Venous Gas / Free Air):**
>   - **Proceed IMMEDIATELY to the Operating Room (Ideally a Hybrid OR) for Emergent Exploratory Laparotomy** (do NOT delay in an outpatient IR suite if the patient has peritonitis!):
>     1. **Revascularize FIRST (Before Resecting Borderline Bowel)** unless a segment is already frankly perforated/spilling stool: Perform **Open Surgical SMA Fogarty Balloon Embolectomy** (for **SMA Embolism**) OR **Retrograde Open Mesenteric Stenting (ROMS) / Aorto-Mesenteric Bypass** (for **SMA Ostial Thrombosis**).
>     2. **Assess Bowel Viability (15–20 Minutes After Reperfusion):** Inspect for return of pink color, visible peristalsis, palpable/Doppler mesenteric arcade pulses, or **Indocyanine Green (ICG) fluorescence angiography**; **resect all frankly black/gangrenous bowel**.
>     3. **Implement Damage Control Surgery with a Mandatory Planned "Second-Look" Laparotomy at 24–48 Hours:** **Do NOT perform a primary bowel anastomosis or stoma at the index operation!** Leave the stapled bowel ends in discontinuity, apply a **Temporary Abdominal Closure (Abthera / Negative-Pressure Wound Therapy)**, resuscitate in the ICU for 24–48 hours, and **return to the OR at 24–48 hours** to reassess borderline bowel segments (saves vital centimeters of small bowel!) and complete the anastomosis.
> - **2. Early Acute Arterial Occlusion (SMA Embolism or Thrombosis) WITHOUT Peritonitis or CT Infarction Signs:**
>   - **Perform Emergent Endovascular Revascularization** (**Mechanical Aspiration Thrombectomy / Embolectomy** ± local catheter-directed thrombolysis for **Embolism**; **Percutaneous Transluminal Angioplasty and Stenting** for **Ostial Thrombosis**), followed by **close ICU serial abdominal exams/lactate monitoring** (with immediate diagnostic laparoscopy/laparotomy if any clinical deterioration or rising lactate occurs!).
> - **3. Non-Occlusive Mesenteric Ischemia (NOMI — 20%):**
>   - **Treat Primarily Medically / Endovascularly (Avoid Surgery UNLESS Peritonitis/Gangrene Develops!):**
>     1. **Correct the Underlying Hemodynamic Trigger:** Aggressively resuscitate intravascular volume, optimize cardiac output/arrhythmias, and **wean or stop offending splanchnic vasoconstrictors (phenylephrine, vasopressin, high-dose norepinephrine, digoxin)**.
>     2. **Perform Selective Mesenteric Angiography with Continuous Intra-Arterial Infusion of a Vasodilator—specifically Papaverine (30–60 mg/hr), Prostaglandin $\text{E}_1$ (Alprostadil), or Nitroglycerin**—directly into the SMA to break the splanchnic arterial spasm.
>     3. **Perform Exploratory Laparotomy** ONLY if peritoneal signs or bowel necrosis develop.
> - **4. Mesenteric Venous Thrombosis (MVT — 5–10%):**
>   - **Manage Non-Operatively with Immediate Therapeutic Anticoagulation (IV Unfractionated Heparin Infusion $\rightarrow$ transition to a DOAC or Warfarin for ≥6 months, or lifelong if permanent thrombophilia/JAK2)** + NPO, NG decompression, IV fluids, and antibiotics (_>85–90% resolve completely without surgery; perform laparotomy or endovascular transjugular thrombectomy ONLY if peritonitis/transmural infarction develops_).
>
> **Postoperative & Long-Term Secondary Prevention**
>
> - **Prescribe Lifelong Oral Anticoagulation (DOAC or Warfarin)** for all patients with **SMA Arterial Embolism** (atrial fibrillation/cardioembolic source) or **unprovoked Mesenteric Venous Thrombosis**.
> - **Prescribe Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (Aspirin + Clopidogrel for 1–6 months, then lifelong Aspirin) + High-Intensity Statin** for patients who undergo stenting or bypass for **Atherosclerotic SMA Thrombosis**.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Waiting for Peritoneal Signs or an Elevated Serum Lactate before Ordering a CTA**—peritonitis and hyperlactatemia indicate that reversible mucosal ischemia has already progressed to **irreversible transmural bowel death**!
> - **Avoid Ordering a Standard Abdominal CT (Portal Venous Phase Only) or Giving Oral Contrast** when AMI is on the differential, as oral contrast delays imaging by hours and obscures intraluminal mesenteric contrast and bowel wall enhancement (always order a **Biphasic CTA without oral contrast**).
> - **Avoid Purely Endovascular Therapy Alone (Without Surgical Exploration) in any Patient who Already has Peritonitis, Pneumatosis Intestinalis, or Portal Venous Gas**, as restoring blood flow to dead gangrenous bowel without resecting it causes fatal **ischemia-reperfusion shock and perforation**.
> - **Avoid Resecting All Dusky/Borderline Bowel at the First Operation BEFORE Revascularizing the SMA**, as re-establishing SMA flow frequently rescues large segments of borderline midgut and prevents crippling **Short Bowel Syndrome** (always revascularize first and use a **24–48 hour Second-Look Laparotomy**!).
> - **Avoid Creating a Primary Bowel Anastomosis during the Initial Emergency Laparotomy for AMI**, as ongoing splanchnic hypoperfusion, vasopressors, and reperfusion edema cause an **anastomotic leak rate >30–50%** (leave bowel in discontinuity until the Second-Look operation).

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Ischemia-Reperfusion Injury & Systemic Shock:** Restoring arterial oxygen to ischemic gut generates massive **reactive oxygen species (xanthine oxidase)**, releasing **potassium (fatal hyperkalemic arrest), hydrogen ions/lactate (severe metabolic acidosis), cytokines, and bacterial endotoxin** into the portal and systemic circulation $\rightarrow$ causes acute **myocardial depression, ARDS, multiorgan failure**, and **Abdominal Compartment Syndrome** (reason for leaving a temporary open abdomen after revascularization!).
> - **Short Bowel Syndrome (Intestinal Failure):** Occurs when extensive small bowel resection leaves **<100 cm of small bowel (without a colon)** or **<60 cm of small bowel (with an intact colon in continuity)** $\rightarrow$ causes severe malabsorptive diarrhea, dehydration, electrolyte wasting, and lifelong dependence on **Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN)** (or **Teduglutide** [GLP-2 analogue] / intestinal transplantation).
> - **Ischemic Bowel Stricture:** Patients who survive a partial-thickness (mucosal/submucosal) ischemic insult without resection may develop fibrotic **small bowel or colonic strictures** 2–8 weeks later, presenting as subacute mechanical bowel obstruction.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 74-year-old man with paroxysmal atrial fibrillation (non-adherent to rivaroxaban) and heart failure presents with 3 hours of sudden, agonizing periumbilical pain, forceful vomiting, and one urgent loose stool. Vitals show HR 128 bpm (irregularly irregular) and BP 118/74 mmHg; he is writhing in bed in severe distress, yet abdominal palpation reveals a **soft, non-distended abdomen with mild diffuse tenderness and ZERO guarding or rebound ("pain out of proportion to exam")**. Labs show WBC 18,400/µL and a **normal serum lactate of 1.5 mmol/L**. Recognizing classic early **Acute Mesenteric Ischemia**, the clinician does not wait for lactate elevation, starts an **IV unfractionated heparin infusion**, administers **IV Piperacillin-Tazobactam** and IV crystalloids, and orders a STAT **Biphasic CT Angiography (CTA)** without oral contrast, which reveals an **embolus occluding the Superior Mesenteric Artery** 5 cm distal to its origin (just past the middle colic artery) with preserved bowel wall enhancement. He is taken immediately to a **hybrid OR for emergent SMA embolectomy and diagnostic laparotomy** with a planned **24-hour second-look laparotomy**, saving his entire small bowel.
