> [!abstract] Gallstone Disease
> Formation of crystalline concretions (**Cholelithiasis**) within the gallbladder or biliary tract due to bile supersaturation, hypomotility, and crystal nucleation—ranging from **Asymptomatic Cholelithiasis (80%)** and **Biliary Colic** (transient cystic duct obstruction) to complicated **Choledocholithiasis** (common bile duct stones), **Acute Cholecystitis**, **Acute Cholangitis**, and **Gallstone Pancreatitis**.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **3 Major Gallstone Types:**
>   1. **Cholesterol Stones (80–85% — Radiolucent):** Hepatic hypersecretion of cholesterol OR bile salt depletion (**terminal ileal Crohn's/resection**) overwhelms micellar solubilization by bile salts and phosphatidylcholine (lecithin) + gallbladder stasis (**pregnancy, TPN, octreotide, rapid weight loss**) + mucin gel nucleation.
>   2. **Black Pigment Stones (10–15% — Radiopaque [Calcium Bilirubinate]):** Sterile gallbladder precipitation of unconjugated bilirubin + calcium in **chronic intravascular/extravascular hemolysis** (sickle cell, hereditary spherocytosis, thalassemia, prosthetic heart valves) or cirrhosis.
>   3. **Brown Pigment Stones (5% — Radiolucent/Radiopaque, form INSIDE Bile Ducts):** Bacterial or parasitic (**Clonorchis sinensis, Ascaris lumbricoides**) $\beta$-glucuronidase hydrolyzes conjugated bilirubin back into insoluble unconjugated bilirubin in infected, static bile ducts.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **The 4-Disease Spectrum of Gallstones (Master the Discriminators):**
>   - **Biliary Colic:** Transient cystic duct blockage $\rightarrow$ RUQ pain **<4–6 hours**, **NO fever**, **Normal WBC**, **Normal LFTs/Bilirubin**.
>   - **Acute Cholecystitis:** Persistent cystic duct blockage $\rightarrow$ RUQ pain **>6 hours**, **Fever**, **High WBC**, **Positive Murphy's sign**, **Normal Bilirubin/CBD**.
>   - **Choledocholithiasis:** Common bile duct (CBD) blockage $\rightarrow$ RUQ pain **>6 hours**, **NO fever**, **Normal WBC**, **Elevated Conjugated Bilirubin/ALP/ALT** + **Dilated CBD (>6 mm)**.
>   - **Acute Cholangitis:** Infected CBD blockage $\rightarrow$ **Charcot Triad (Fever + Jaundice + RUQ Pain)**, **High WBC**, **Elevated Bilirubin/ALP**, **Dilated CBD** ($\rightarrow$ requires urgent ERCP).
> - **"Biliary Colic" is a misnomer:** Unlike true intestinal or renal colic (which comes in 5-minute cramping waves), biliary colic is a **constant, steady crescendo-plateau ache** lasting **1 to 5 hours** that subsides once the gallbladder relaxes and the stone falls back into the lumen.
> - **Never operate on Asymptomatic Gallstones (Except in 5 High-Risk Scenarios):** >80% of incidental gallstones never cause symptoms; operate prophylactically **ONLY** for: (1) **Porcelain gallbladder**, (2) **Stone >3 cm**, (3) **Gallbladder polyp ≥10 mm** (or any size polyp with **PSC** or concurrent gallstones), (4) **Native American ancestry / Anomalous pancreaticobiliary ductal union**, or (5) **Sickle cell disease** undergoing concurrent abdominal surgery.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Asymptomatic Cholelithiasis (~80% of carriers):** Discovered incidentally on abdominal ultrasound or CT; <2% per year develop symptoms.
> - **Biliary Colic (Symptomatic Uncomplicated Cholelithiasis):**
>   - **Episodic, severe, steady RUQ or epigastric pain** triggered **30–90 minutes after a fatty or large meal** (or occurring **at night** when supine, as stones roll into the gallbladder neck/infundibulum).
>   - **Duration:** Rises to a steady plateau over 30–60 minutes, lasts **1 to 5 hours**, and resolves completely (**if pain persists >6 hours continuously, suspect progression to Acute Cholecystitis, Choledocholithiasis, or Pancreatitis!**).
>   - **Radiation:** Radiates to the **interscapular back, right inferior scapula (Boas sign), or right shoulder**, accompanied by nausea, diaphoresis, and vomiting; patient is **completely pain-free between attacks**.
> - **Choledocholithiasis (Common Bile Duct Stone):**
>   - Prolonged RUQ/epigastric pain accompanied by **obstructive jaundice** (yellow skin/sclerae), **dark tea/cola-colored urine** (conjugated bilirubinuria), **pale acholic (clay-colored) stools**, and generalized pruritus, **WITHOUT fever or chills**.
> - **Risk Factors ("The 5 F's" & Medications):** **F**emale, **F**at (obesity), **F**ertile (pregnancy/estrogen/OCPs: estrogen increases cholesterol secretion; progesterone slows gallbladder emptying), **F**orty, **F**air/Native American/Hispanic; **Rapid weight loss** (bariatric surgery, GLP-1 receptor agonists [semaglutide/tirzepatide], very-low-calorie diets), **Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN)**, **Octreotide**, **Fibrates** (inhibit cholesterol $7\alpha$-hydroxylase), **Ceftriaxone** (precipitates as calcium-ceftriaxone pseudolithiasis/sludge).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Biliary Colic:** Mild visceral RUQ/epigastric tenderness during the acute attack (or **completely normal exam** once the episode resolves); **afebrile**, **NO peritoneal signs**, and **negative Murphy's sign**.
> - **Choledocholithiasis:** **Scleral icterus** (detectable when total bilirubin >2.5–3.0 mg/dL), jaundice, and mild RUQ tenderness **without fever or guarding** (_Compare with **Courvoisier's Law**: a **painless, palpable distended gallbladder + jaundice** indicates **malignant biliary obstruction** [pancreatic head cancer or cholangiocarcinoma], because chronic gallstone disease causes a fibrotic, scarred gallbladder that cannot distend!_).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Cholelithiasis (RUQ Ultrasound — Gold Standard):** Mobile, echogenic intraluminal foci within the gallbladder that **cast a posterior acoustic shadow** and shift with changes in patient position (_Biliary sludge_ is echogenic layering material _without_ acoustic shadowing).
> - **Uncomplicated Biliary Colic:** Characteristic postprandial RUQ/epigastric pain lasting <6 hours + ultrasound-confirmed gallstones/sludge + **normal WBC, normal liver chemistries, and absence of sonographic cholecystitis findings**.
> - **Definite Choledocholithiasis:** Direct visualization of a stone within the Common Bile Duct (CBD) on **RUQ Ultrasound, MRCP, Endoscopic Ultrasound (EUS), or ERCP/Intraoperative Cholangiogram (IOC)**.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs (Order in All Symptomatic Patients to Exclude Complications)**
>
> - **CBC with Differential:** **Completely normal** in biliary colic and uncomplicated choledocholithiasis (if WBC >10,000/µL, suspect **Acute Cholecystitis** or **Acute Cholangitis**).
> - **Hepatic Panel (AST, ALT, Alkaline Phosphatase, GGT, Total & Direct Bilirubin):**
>   - _Biliary Colic:_ **Completely normal.**
>   - _Choledocholithiasis:_ **Cholestatic pattern** with elevated **Direct (Conjugated) Bilirubin** and **Alkaline Phosphatase / GGT** (_Pearl: In the first 24–48 hours of acute CBD stone impaction, **AST and ALT often spike acutely to 300–1,000 U/L** before rapidly declining as ALP and bilirubin rise!_).
> - **Serum Lipase:** Mandatory to rule out **Acute Gallstone Pancreatitis** (**Lipase ≥3× ULN** + ALT >150 U/L).
>
> **Imaging & ASGE Risk-Stratified Choledocholithiasis Pathway**
>
> - **Right Upper Quadrant Ultrasound (RUQ US):** **Mandatory first-line test (>95% sensitivity/specificity for gallbladder stones)**.
>   - _Measure the Common Bile Duct (CBD) diameter:_ Normal is **≤6 mm** (plus 1 mm per decade of life over age 60, or up to **8–10 mm after prior cholecystectomy**).
>   - _Pitfall:_ While RUQ US is >95% sensitive for _gallbladder_ stones, its sensitivity for directly visualizing a _distal CBD stone_ is only **~30–50%** because duodenal gas obscures the retroduodenal CBD!
> - **Stepwise Evaluation for Suspected Choledocholithiasis (2019 ASGE Guidelines):**
>   - **High Probability (>50% Risk) — Requires ANY 1 of the following 3 Very Strong Predictors:**
>     1. **CBD stone directly seen on Ultrasound/CT**,
>     2. **Clinical Ascending Cholangitis** (Charcot triad), OR
>     3. **Total Bilirubin >4.0 mg/dL PLUS a Dilated CBD (>6 mm)** on ultrasound.
>     - $\rightarrow$ **Action:** **Skip MRCP/EUS and proceed DIRECTLY to Preoperative Therapeutic ERCP**, followed by same-admission Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy!
>   - **Intermediate Probability (10–50% Risk) — Presence of ANY Moderate Predictor (Without High-Risk Criteria):**
>     - **Abnormal liver biochemical test** (elevated AST, ALT, ALP, or **Total Bilirubin 1.8–4.0 mg/dL**), **Dilated CBD (>6 mm) alone**, **Age >55 years**, or **Clinical Gallstone Pancreatitis**.
>     - $\rightarrow$ **Action:** **Do NOT go straight to ERCP!** Order **MRCP (Magnetic Resonance Cholangiopancreatography) or Endoscopic Ultrasound (EUS)** first to confirm a CBD stone (if positive $\rightarrow$ ERCP; if negative $\rightarrow$ proceed to Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy), **OR** proceed directly to **Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy with Intraoperative Cholangiography (IOC)**.
>   - **Low Probability (<10% Risk) — Normal LFTs/Bilirubin AND Normal CBD Caliber (≤6 mm) on Ultrasound:**
>     - $\rightarrow$ **Action:** **Proceed directly to Elective Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy** (no MRCP, EUS, or ERCP needed).

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Pain persisting >6 hours continuously** (signals progression from reversible biliary colic to **Acute Cholecystitis, Choledocholithiasis, or Pancreatitis**).
> - **Fever / Chills + Jaundice + RUQ Pain (Charcot Triad) or Hypotension / Confusion (Reynolds Pentad):** Indicates **Acute Ascending Cholangitis** $\rightarrow$ medical/endoscopic emergency requiring **IV antibiotics + Urgent ERCP (<24h)**.
> - **Jaundice, dark urine, acholic stools, or Total Bilirubin >1.8–4.0 mg/dL:** Indicates **Choledocholithiasis** or malignant biliary obstruction.
> - **Epigastric pain radiating to the back + Lipase ≥3× ULN:** Indicates **Acute Gallstone Pancreatitis**.
> - **Painless jaundice + palpable distended gallbladder (Courvoisier's sign) + weight loss:** Indicates **Pancreatic Head Adenocarcinoma or Distal Cholangiocarcinoma** (order **multiphasic pancreatic-protocol CT**).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Acute Calculous Cholecystitis, Acute Cholangitis, & Gallstone Pancreatitis** (The major complicated gallstone emergencies).
> - **Malignant Biliary Obstruction (Pancreatic Head Adenocarcinoma, Cholangiocarcinoma, Ampullary Carcinoma, Gallbladder Adenocarcinoma):** Painless progressive jaundice, weight loss, pruritus, Courvoisier's sign, CA 19-9 elevation.
> - **Acute Coronary Syndrome (Inferior MI):** Epigastric/RUQ discomfort with diaphoresis and nausea; rule out with STAT ECG and troponin.
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Peptic Ulcer Disease / Dyspepsia / GERD:** Burning epigastric pain relieved by food (DU) or worsened immediately by eating (GU), acid regurgitation; normal RUQ ultrasound.
> - **Functional Gallbladder Disorder (Biliary Dyskinesia):** Classic postprandial biliary colic pain lasting ≥30 minutes with a **completely normal RUQ ultrasound (no stones or sludge)** and normal LFTs/EGD; diagnosed via **CCK-stimulated HIDA scan showing a reduced Gallbladder Ejection Fraction (GBEF <35–40%)** and cured in >80% by **Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy**.
> - **Sphincter of Oddi Dysfunction (SOD):** Recurrent biliary pain **after prior cholecystectomy** with elevated transaminases AND a dilated bile duct (**Type I SOD** responds to ERCP sphincterotomy; **Type III SOD** [pain alone with normal labs and normal duct caliber] **does NOT benefit from ERCP** per the **EPISOD trial** and should be managed medically!).
>
> **Most Surprising**
>
> - **Gilbert Syndrome:** Benign congenital UDP-glucuronosyltransferase (**UGT1A1**) deficiency causing mild **unconjugated (indirect) hyperbilirubinemia (<3–4 mg/dL)** triggered by fasting, stress, or illness, with completely normal AST, ALT, and ALP (do not confuse with obstructive choledocholithiasis, which causes **conjugated [direct] hyperbilirubinemia**!).
> - **Ceftriaxone-Induced Biliary Pseudolithiasis** or **Pregnancy-Induced Biliary Sludge**: Reversible upon drug discontinuation or postpartum delivery.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Rapid acute pain relief during biliary colic episodes, complete clearance of any common bile duct stones, and **definitive prevention of recurrent attacks via Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy**.
> - **Secondary Target:** Prevent life-threatening gallstone complications (acute cholecystitis, ascending cholangitis, gallstone pancreatitis, and gallbladder carcinoma).
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (Acute Biliary Colic Attack in the ED)**
>
> - **Administer First-Line Parenteral NSAIDs—specifically Ketorolac (15–30 mg IV) or Diclofenac (75 mg IM)**—upon presentation with acute biliary colic (provided renal function is normal and there is no active GI bleed), as **NSAIDs are superior to opioids** because they not only relieve pain but also **inhibit gallbladder prostaglandin synthesis, reduce intraluminal pressure, and reduce progression to acute cholecystitis by >50%!**
> - **Add Titrated IV Opioids (Morphine, Hydromorphone, or Fentanyl) + IV Antiemetic (Ondansetron 4 mg IV)** if NSAIDs are contraindicated or provide incomplete pain relief (_Note: The historical belief that Morphine should be avoided over Meperidine due to Sphincter of Oddi spasm is a clinical myth—Morphine/Hydromorphone/Fentanyl are completely safe and preferred over Meperidine!_).
>
> **Definitive Management by Clinical Syndrome**
>
> - **Asymptomatic Cholelithiasis:**
>   - **Reassure and Observe Expectantly (No Surgery and No Medical Dissolution Therapy)** in >95% of patients, as the annual risk of developing symptoms is <2% and surgical risks outweigh benefits.
>   - **Perform Prophylactic Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy ONLY** if the patient has one of the **5 High-Risk Gallbladder Cancer / Complication Indications**: (1) **Porcelain Gallbladder** (calcified wall), (2) **Giant Gallstone >3 cm**, (3) **Gallbladder Polyp ≥10 mm** (or **any size polyp** in a patient with **Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis [PSC]**, age >50, Indian ethnicity, or concurrent gallstones), (4) **Native American ancestry / Anomalous pancreaticobiliary ductal union**, or (5) **Sickle Cell Disease / Chronic Hemolysis** undergoing another abdominal procedure.
> - **Symptomatic Uncomplicated Cholelithiasis (Biliary Colic):**
>   - **Advise a Low-Fat Diet** to prevent postprandial CCK-mediated gallbladder contractions while awaiting surgery.
>   - **Refer for Elective Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy** as the **definitive first-line cure** (prevents the 30–50% annual recurrence rate of biliary colic and eliminates the 1–3% annual risk of severe complications like pancreatitis or cholangitis).
>   - **Prescribe Oral Ursodeoxycholic Acid (Ursodiol 8–10 mg/kg/day PO divided BID–TID for 6–24 months)** (a hydrophilic bile acid that reduces hepatic cholesterol secretion and dissolves small cholesterol stones) **ONLY in two specific situations**:
>     1. **Poor surgical candidates** (or patients who refuse surgery) who have **small (<5–10 mm), non-calcified (radiolucent on CT/X-ray) cholesterol stones** and a **patent cystic duct** (_Note: 50% of stones recur within 5 years of stopping Ursodiol!_).
>     2. **Prophylaxis during Rapid Weight Loss (600 mg/day PO for 6 months after Bariatric Surgery)** to prevent _de novo_ cholesterol gallstone formation!
> - **Uncomplicated Choledocholithiasis (Common Bile Duct Stone Without Cholangitis):**
>   - **Clear the Common Bile Duct AND Remove the Gallbladder during the SAME Hospitalization** using either a **Two-Stage** or **Single-Stage** approach:
>     - **Two-Stage Approach (Most Common):** **Perform Preoperative ERCP with Endoscopic Sphincterotomy and Balloon/Basket Stone Extraction** (administering **100 mg Rectal Indomethacin** during ERCP to prevent Post-ERCP Pancreatitis!), **followed by Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy during the SAME index admission** (_In frail elderly patients with prohibitive surgical risk, ERCP sphincterotomy alone may serve as definitive therapy_).
>     - **Single-Stage Surgical Approach:** **Perform Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy with Intraoperative Cholangiography (IOC) and Laparoscopic Common Bile Duct Exploration (LCBDE)** (transcystic or choledochotomy stone extraction in a single procedure).

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Performing Diagnostic ERCP in patients with Low or Intermediate Probability of Choledocholithiasis**, as ERCP carries a **5–10% risk of Post-ERCP Pancreatitis (PEP)**, post-sphincterotomy hemorrhage, and retroduodenal perforation—always confirm intermediate-risk stones non-invasively with **MRCP or EUS** first!
> - **Avoid Discharging a Patient after ERCP Stone Extraction for Choledocholithiasis or Mild Gallstone Pancreatitis WITHOUT performing a Same-Admission Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy** (in surgically fit patients), as leaving the stone-filled gallbladder in place carries a **25–45% risk of recurrent biliary emergencies within 6 weeks**.
> - **Avoid Prescribing Ursodeoxycholic Acid (Ursodiol) for Radiopaque (Calcified / Pigment) Stones, Large Stones (>1–1.5 cm), or Acute Cholecystitis (Obstructed Cystic Duct)**, as Ursodiol cannot dissolve calcium/pigment stones and cannot enter the gallbladder if the cystic duct is obstructed.
> - **Avoid Extracorporeal Shock Wave Lithotripsy (ESWL)** for gallstones (unlike kidney stones, gallbladder ESWL is obsolete due to high stone recurrence and risk of biliary pancreatitis from stone fragments passing down the cystic duct).

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Progression to Acute Calculous Cholecystitis (10–20%), Choledocholithiasis (10–15%), Acute Cholangitis, or Acute Gallstone Pancreatitis** (gallstones migrating through the ampulla of Vater are the #1 cause of acute pancreatitis worldwide).
> - **Mirizzi Syndrome:** Large stone impacted in the cystic duct or Hartmann's pouch externally compressing the **common hepatic duct** $\rightarrow$ obstructive jaundice + recurrent cholangitis; high risk of iatrogenic CBD transection during laparoscopic cholecystectomy (often requires subtotal fenestrating cholecystectomy!).
> - **Gallstone Ileus & Bouveret Syndrome:** Chronic cholecystoduodenal fistula allowing a stone >2.5 cm to obstruct the **ileocecal valve** (**Gallstone Ileus**; **Rigler's Triad**: pneumobilia + SBO + ectopic calcified stone) or the **pylorus/duodenal bulb** (**Bouveret Syndrome**).
> - **Gallbladder Adenocarcinoma:** Rare, highly lethal malignancy (5-year survival <15% if invasive); **>85% are associated with chronic cholelithiasis** and chronic inflammation (**porcelain gallbladder, stones >3 cm, _Salmonella_ Typhi chronic gallbladder carriage, or Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis**).

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> **Scenario 1 (Uncomplicated Biliary Colic):** A 38-year-old woman presents to clinic after two episodes of severe, steady RUQ pain radiating to her back that started 1 hour after eating pizza, lasted 3 hours, and resolved completely by morning. She is afebrile with a benign abdominal exam; CBC, AST, ALT, alkaline phosphatase, total bilirubin (0.6 mg/dL), and lipase are all normal. **RUQ Ultrasound** reveals multiple mobile, shadowing gallstones in a thin-walled gallbladder with no pericholecystic fluid and a normal **3-mm Common Bile Duct** (**Low Probability for Choledocholithiasis**). She is prescribed PRN oral diclofenac/ketorolac, advised on a low-fat diet, and referred for **elective outpatient Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy** (without needing MRCP or ERCP).
> **Scenario 2 (Intermediate-Risk Choledocholithiasis):** A 52-year-old woman presents with 8 hours of steady RUQ pain, dark tea-colored urine, and scleral icterus, without fever or chills (T 36.9°C, WBC 7,800/µL). Labs show **Total Bilirubin 2.9 mg/dL (Direct 2.3 mg/dL)**, ALP 310 U/L, ALT 220 U/L, and normal lipase; RUQ Ultrasound shows cholelithiasis and a mildly **dilated 7-mm CBD** without a directly visualized CBD stone (**ASGE Intermediate Probability**). Rather than proceeding directly to ERCP, an **MRCP** is ordered first, which confirms a 6-mm distal CBD stone; she then undergoes **preoperative ERCP with sphincterotomy and stone extraction** (plus rectal indomethacin) followed by **same-admission Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy**.
