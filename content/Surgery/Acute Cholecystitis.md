> [!abstract] Acute Cholecystitis
> Acute transmural inflammation of the gallbladder wall triggered in **90–95% of cases by persistent impaction of a gallstone in the cystic duct or Hartmann's pouch (Calculous Cholecystitis)**, or in **5–10% by gallbladder stasis and ischemia in critically ill ICU patients without gallstones (Acalculous Cholecystitis)**.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Cystic duct obstruction + mucosal irritation:** Impacted stone prevents bile outflow $\rightarrow$ concentrated bile salts and mucosal **phospholipase A** convert lecithin into toxic **lysolecithin** + prostaglandin ($\text{PGE}_2, \text{PGI}_2$) release $\rightarrow$ mucosal glycoprotein secretion, intraluminal distention, and **venous/lymphatic congestion**.
> - **Secondary bacterial infection (occurs in 50–80%, NOT the primary event):** Enteric organisms (**E. coli** #1, _Klebsiella, Enterococcus, Bacteroides, Clostridium_) proliferate in static bile $\rightarrow$ **arterial ischemia of the gallbladder fundus** (perfused by the end-artery **cystic artery**) $\rightarrow$ gangrene and perforation.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Biliary Colic vs. Acute Cholecystitis (6-Hour Rule):** Biliary colic is a transient cystic duct obstruction causing waxing/waning RUQ pain that **resolves within <4–6 hours** and has **no fever or leukocytosis**; **Acute Cholecystitis** causes **unrelenting pain >6 hours**, **fever**, **leukocytosis**, and a **positive Murphy's sign**.
> - **Uncomplicated Acute Cholecystitis = NORMAL Bilirubin & ALP:** Because the obstruction is strictly in the **cystic duct** (leaving the common bile duct patent), total bilirubin and alkaline phosphatase are typically **normal**; if **Bilirubin is >2–4 mg/dL**, suspect **concurrent Choledocholithiasis, Acute Cholangitis, or Mirizzi Syndrome**!
> - **Operate EARLY (Within 24–72 Hours on Index Admission):** Early **Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy** during the initial hospitalization reduces complications, length of stay, and recurrent biliary emergencies compared to delayed interval surgery at 6 weeks.
> - **Unexplained Fever + Leukocytosis in an ICU Patient = Acalculous Cholecystitis:** Always order a bedside RUQ Ultrasound in critically ill patients (burns, trauma, TPN, prolonged fasting, sepsis) with unexplained sepsis/jaundice; treat with **Percutaneous Cholecystostomy Tube drainage**.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Persistent, severe RUQ or epigastric pain lasting >6 hours**, often precipitated by a large or fatty meal (cholecystokinin [CCK] triggers gallbladder contraction against an impacted stone).
> - **Classic pain radiation:** Radiates around the right costal margin to the **inferior angle of the right scapula or right shoulder tip** (**Boas Sign**: hyperesthesia inferior to the right scapula via shared **T7–T9 / phrenic nerve** fibers).
> - **Systemic symptoms:** Fever, chills, anorexia, nausea, and bilious vomiting.
> - **Gallstone Risk Factors ("The 5 F's" & Beyond):**
>   - _Cholesterol stones (80–85%, radiolucent):_ **F**emale, **F**at (obesity OR **rapid weight loss** after bariatric surgery/GLP-1 agonists), **F**ertile (pregnancy/multiparity, estrogen/OCPs), **F**orty (increasing age), **F**air/Native American/Hispanic; **Fibrates**, **Octreotide**, **Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN)**, and **Terminal Ileal Crohn's/Resection** (bile acid malabsorption supersaturates cholesterol).
>   - _Black pigment stones (radiopaque, calcium bilirubinate):_ **Chronic Hemolysis** (sickle cell, hereditary spherocytosis, G6PD, prosthetic valves) or cirrhosis.
>   - _Brown pigment stones:_ Biliary tract infection/parasites (_Clonorchis sinensis, Ascaris_) hydrolyzing conjugated bilirubin.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Fever, tachycardia**, and patient lying still due to localized parietal peritonitis.
> - **Murphy's Sign (97% sensitivity when performed via Ultrasound probe):** **Abrupt inspiratory arrest** caused by sharp pain when the examiner palpates deeply in the RUQ (subcostal midclavicular line) as the patient takes a deep breath.
> - **Palpable, tender globular RUQ mass** (distended gallbladder wrapped in omentum; palpable in ~30%).
> - _Crucial Discriminator:_ **Absence of jaundice** in uncomplicated acute cholecystitis (unlike Courvoisier's sign—a _painless_ palpable gallbladder + jaundice, which indicates malignant biliary obstruction).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> **Tokyo Guidelines (TG18) — Definite Diagnosis** requires **1 item from A + 1 item from B + 1 item from C**:
>
> - **A. Local Signs of Inflammation:** (1) **Murphy's sign**, OR (2) RUQ mass, pain, or tenderness.
> - **B. Systemic Signs of Inflammation:** (1) **Fever (>38.0°C)**, (2) **Elevated CRP (>3 mg/dL)**, OR (3) **Elevated WBC count (>10,000/µL)**.
> - **C. Characteristic Imaging Findings (Ultrasound, HIDA, or CT):**
>   - **RUQ Ultrasound (First-Line):** Gallstones/sludge (specifically an **impacted stone in the gallbladder neck/cystic duct** that does not move with positioning) **PLUS**: (1) **Gallbladder wall thickening >3–4 mm**, (2) **Pericholecystic fluid**, AND/OR (3) **Positive Sonographic Murphy's Sign**.
>   - **HIDA Scan (Cholescintigraphy — Gold Standard if US is Equivocal):** **Non-visualization of the gallbladder at 1–4 hours** (or 30 min after morphine administration) despite normal hepatic uptake and excretion of $^{99\text{m}}\text{Tc-HIDA}$ into the common bile duct and duodenum (confirms **cystic duct obstruction** with >97% accuracy).

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **Mandatory Initial Panel:**
>   1. **CBC with Differential** (Leukocytosis 12,000–15,000/µL with left shift; WBC >20,000/µL suggests gangrenous/perforated cholecystitis or cholangitis).
>   2. **Comprehensive Metabolic Panel (AST, ALT, Alkaline Phosphatase, GGT, Total & Direct Bilirubin):** Mandatory to risk-stratify for **concurrent Choledocholithiasis (CBD stone)**!
>   3. **Serum Lipase:** Mandatory to rule out concurrent **Gallstone Pancreatitis**.
>   4. **Blood Cultures (×2 sets):** Obtain prior to antibiotics in moderate/severe cholecystitis, fever >38.5°C, or elderly/immunocompromised patients.
>   5. **Urine $\beta$-hCG** (if reproductive-age female) and **ECG/Troponin** (if epigastric pain).
>
> **ASGE Risk Stratification for Concurrent Choledocholithiasis (Determines Need for Pre-Op ERCP vs. MRCP):**
>
> - **High Probability (>50% risk) $\rightarrow$ Proceed DIRECTLY to Preoperative ERCP (before Cholecystectomy):** Any **1** of:
>   1. **CBD stone directly visualized** on ultrasound/cross-sectional imaging,
>   2. **Clinical Ascending Cholangitis** (Charcot triad), OR
>   3. **Total Bilirubin >4 mg/dL PLUS a dilated Common Bile Duct (>6 mm**; or >8–10 mm if prior cholecystectomy).
> - **Intermediate Probability (10–50% risk) $\rightarrow$ Order MRCP or Endoscopic Ultrasound (EUS) FIRST (or Intraoperative Cholangiogram [IOC]):**
>   - Total Bilirubin **1.8–4.0 mg/dL**, OR dilated CBD (>6 mm) alone, OR abnormal AST/ALT/ALP, OR clinical gallstone pancreatitis, OR Age >55.
> - **Low Probability (<10% risk) $\rightarrow$ Proceed DIRECTLY to Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy (No MRCP or ERCP needed):**
>   - Normal LFTs/bilirubin AND normal CBD caliber (≤6 mm) on ultrasound.
>
> **Imaging**
>
> - **Right Upper Quadrant Ultrasound (RUQ US):** **Mandatory first-line imaging test** in all patients (Sensitivity ~85%, Specificity ~95% for stones; acoustic shadowing).
> - **HIDA Scan:** Order **ONLY** when clinical suspicion for acute cholecystitis remains high despite an **equivocal or negative RUQ Ultrasound** (_Note: HIDA has high false-positives in critically ill fasting/TPN patients or severe liver disease with bilirubin >5 mg/dL_).
> - **CT Abdomen/Pelvis with IV Contrast:** Inferior to US for detecting cholesterol gallstones (75% of gallstones are **isodense to bile and invisible on CT!**), but **superior for detecting complications**: **Emphysematous Cholecystitis** (gas in gallbladder wall), **Gangrenous Perforation / Pericholecystic Abscess**, or **Gallstone Ileus**.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Tokyo Guidelines Grade III (Severe) Acute Cholecystitis:** Acute cholecystitis accompanied by **any organ dysfunction** (Hypotension requiring vasopressors, Altered mental status, $\text{PaO}_2/\text{FiO}_2 <300$, Creatinine $>2.0\text{ mg/dL}$, INR $>1.5$, or Platelets $<100,000/\mu\text{L}$).
> - **Charcot Triad (Fever + Jaundice + RUQ Pain) or Reynolds Pentad (+ Hypotension + Confusion):** Indicates **Acute Ascending Cholangitis** $\rightarrow$ requires emergent biliary decompression via **ERCP within 24 hours**.
> - **Crepitus in the RUQ or Gas in the Gallbladder Wall on Imaging in an Elderly Diabetic Male:** **Emphysematous Cholecystitis** (caused by gas-forming _Clostridium perfringens, E. coli, Klebsiella_) $\rightarrow$ high mortality (~15–20%) and gangrene risk; requires **emergent cholecystectomy**.
> - **Sudden transient decrease in RUQ pain followed by generalized peritonitis or septic shock:** **Gallbladder Perforation** (most commonly at the **fundus**, which has the most distal blood supply from the cystic artery).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Acute Ascending Cholangitis:** Bacterial infection of an obstructed **common bile duct** (choledocholithiasis, benign stricture, or pancreaticobiliary tumor); high mortality without urgent ERCP.
> - **Acute Gallstone Pancreatitis:** Epigastric pain radiating to the back + **Lipase ≥3× ULN** + ALT >150 U/L.
> - **Perforated Peptic Ulcer:** Sudden severe epigastric/RUQ pain, rigid abdomen, subdiaphragmatic free air on upright CXR.
> - **Inferior Myocardial Infarction or Right Lower Lobe Pneumonia / Pulmonary Embolism:** Always exclude cardiopulmonary causes of acute RUQ/epigastric pain.
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Uncomplicated Biliary Colic (Symptomatic Cholelithiasis):** Postprandial RUQ pain that peaks and **resolves completely within <4–6 hours**; patient is afebrile with normal WBC, normal LFTs, and no gallbladder wall thickening/pericholecystic fluid on US (treated with elective outpatient laparoscopic cholecystectomy).
> - **Choledocholithiasis (Uncomplicated CBD Stone):** RUQ pain >6 hours with **elevated Conjugated Bilirubin, Alkaline Phosphatase, and AST/ALT** + dilated CBD (>6 mm) on US, but **WITHOUT fever or leukocytosis**.
> - **Acute Hepatitis (Viral, Alcoholic, or Drug-Induced):** Markedly elevated AST/ALT (>500–1,000 U/L in viral/ischemic; AST:ALT ≥2:1 in alcoholic), tender hepatomegaly, no impacted cystic duct stone.
> - **Fitz-Hugh-Curtis Syndrome (Perihepatitis):** Acute pleuritic RUQ pain + "violin-string" perihepatic adhesions in a young sexually active woman with **Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (_Chlamydia trachomatis_ or _Neisseria gonorrhoeae_)**; normal gallbladder on US.
>
> **Most Surprising**
>
> - **Mirizzi Syndrome:** Large gallstone impacted in the **cystic duct or Hartmann's pouch** externally **compresses the adjacent Common Hepatic Duct**, causing **obstructive jaundice** without a stone inside the CBD itself (can erode into the bile duct forming a cholecystocholedochal fistula).
> - **Sphincter of Oddi Dysfunction / Biliary Dyskinesia:** Typical biliary colic pain with a completely normal RUQ ultrasound (no stones/sludge); diagnosed by a **HIDA scan with CCK infusion showing a Gallbladder Ejection Fraction (GBEF) <35–40%**.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Hemodynamic resuscitation, pain control, bacterial suppression, and **definitive source control via Early Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy (within 24–72 hours of admission)**.
> - **Secondary Target:** Clear any concomitant common bile duct stones (choledocholithiasis) and prevent gallbladder gangrene/perforation or recurrent biliary events.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Keep NPO, infuse isotonic IV crystalloids (Lactated Ringer's), correct electrolytes, and administer IV Analgesia (Ketorolac 15–30 mg IV [if normal renal function and not immediately going to OR] and/or Fentanyl/Hydromorphone IV) + Ondansetron** immediately upon presentation.
> - **Start Empiric Broad-Spectrum IV Antibiotics within 1 hour of diagnosis** to cover enteric Gram-negative rods (_E. coli, Klebsiella_) and anaerobes:
>   - _Community-Acquired Mild/Moderate (Tokyo Grade I/II):_ **Ceftriaxone (2 g IV daily) + Metronidazole (500 mg IV q8h)**, OR **Cefazolin/Cefuroxime** (if mild Grade I).
>   - _Severe (Tokyo Grade III), Healthcare-Associated, Elderly, or Emphysematous Cholecystitis:_ **Piperacillin-Tazobactam (3.375–4.5 g IV q6h)**, **Cefepime (2 g IV q8h) + Metronidazole**, OR **Meropenem (1 g IV q8h)**.
>
> **Definitive Surgical vs. Percutaneous Management (Stratified by Tokyo Grade & Surgical Fitness)**
>
> - **Perform Early Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy (within 24–72 hours of admission)** as the **gold-standard first-line treatment** for all surgically fit patients with **Tokyo Grade I (Mild) or Grade II (Moderate) Acute Cholecystitis** (including **pregnant patients in ANY trimester**—laparoscopic cholecystectomy is safe and preferred over conservative management throughout pregnancy to prevent maternal-fetal relapse/sepsis!).
>   - _Postoperative Antibiotics:_ **STOP all antibiotics within 24 hours after cholecystectomy** for uncomplicated non-perforated Grade I/II acute cholecystitis! (Continue for **4–7 days** ONLY if gangrene, perforation, emphysematous cholecystitis, or bacteremia is present).
> - **Perform Emergent Cholecystectomy** immediately if the patient has **Emphysematous Cholecystitis**, **Free Gallbladder Perforation / Generalized Peritonitis**, or progressive gangrene.
> - **Perform Ultrasound/CT-Guided Percutaneous Cholecystostomy Tube Drainage** (transhepatic catheter decompression of the gallbladder) + IV antibiotics in **critically ill, hemodynamically unstable Tokyo Grade III patients who are prohibitive surgical risks** AND as the **definitive treatment for Acute Acalculous Cholecystitis** in ICU patients (*Note: In high-risk patients with *calculous* cholecystitis who ARE candidates for anesthesia, the **CHOCOLATE randomized trial** proved that **Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy is superior to Percutaneous Cholecystostomy**, with far fewer major complications and readmissions!*).
>
> **Management of Concomitant Choledocholithiasis**
>
> - **If High Risk for CBD Stone (Total Bilirubin >4 mg/dL + dilated CBD, visualized CBD stone, or Cholangitis):** **Perform Preoperative ERCP with Endoscopic Sphincterotomy and Stone Extraction** FIRST, followed by **Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy during the SAME hospital admission**.
> - **If Intermediate Risk for CBD Stone:** **Perform MRCP or EUS** preoperatively (if positive $\rightarrow$ ERCP then cholecystectomy; if negative $\rightarrow$ proceed directly to cholecystectomy) OR perform **Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy with Intraoperative Cholangiogram (IOC)** and laparoscopic common bile duct exploration.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid delaying Cholecystectomy until a future admission ("cooling off" for 6 weeks) in surgically fit patients**, as 20–30% of patients discharged without surgery return to the ED before their scheduled operation with recurrent cholecystitis, **gallstone pancreatitis**, or **acute cholangitis**.
> - **Avoid Conservative (Non-Operative) Management of Symptomatic Gallstone Disease / Acute Cholecystitis in Pregnant Patients**, as non-operative management carries a >35–50% readmission rate, increased preterm labor, and fetal loss (laparoscopic cholecystectomy is safe in all trimesters).
> - **Avoid Continuing Postoperative Antibiotics beyond 24 hours** after cholecystectomy for uncomplicated (Tokyo Grade I/II) acute cholecystitis (per Tokyo Guidelines and randomized trials).
> - **Avoid Prophylactic Cholecystectomy for Asymptomatic Incidental Gallstones**, as >80% remain asymptomatic for life; **Perform Prophylactic Cholecystectomy for Asymptomatic Gallstones ONLY in high-risk gallbladder cancer / complication scenarios:**
>   1. **Porcelain Gallbladder** (calcified gallbladder wall on CT/X-ray $\rightarrow$ increased gallbladder adenocarcinoma risk),
>   2. **Gallstone >3 cm in diameter**,
>   3. **Gallbladder Polyps ≥10 mm** (or **any size polyp** in a patient with **Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis [PSC]** or concurrent gallstones/sessile morphology),
>   4. **Anomalous pancreaticobiliary ductal union** or **Native American / Pima Indian ancestry**, OR
>   5. **Chronic Hemolytic Anemia (Sickle Cell Disease)** undergoing another abdominal operation.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Gangrenous Cholecystitis & Perforation:** Occurs in ~10–20% (risk factors: older men, diabetes, WBC >15,000/µL, delay in care) $\rightarrow$ **Fundus** is the most common site of perforation:
>   - _Type I:_ Free perforation into peritoneal cavity $\rightarrow$ generalized bile peritonitis (30% mortality).
>   - _Type II (Most common):_ Subacute perforation walled off into a **pericholecystic or subhepatic abscess**.
>   - _Type III:_ Chronic perforation eroding into an adjacent hollow viscus (**duodenum** #1, or hepatic flexure of colon) forming a **cholecystoenteric fistula**.
> - **Gallstone Ileus & Bouveret Syndrome:** A large gallstone (**>2.5 cm**) erodes through a chronically inflamed gallbladder directly into the duodenum (**cholecystoduodenal fistula**) and travels down the small bowel until impacting at the narrowest point—the **ileocecal valve** (**Gallstone Ileus**: causes mechanical **Small Bowel Obstruction** in elderly women) or rarely impacts proximally in the **duodenal bulb/pylorus** causing gastric outlet obstruction (**Bouveret Syndrome**).
>   - **Rigler's Triad on Abdominal X-Ray/CT:** (1) **Pneumobilia** (air in the biliary tree), (2) **Small Bowel Obstruction** (dilated loops/air-fluid levels), and (3) **Ectopic calcified gallstone** in the RLQ/ileum.
>   - _Management:_ **Surgical Enterolithotomy** (longitudinal incision of the ileum to Milk/extract the stone; leave the cholecystoduodenal fistula alone in the acute setting in frail elderly patients!).
> - **Post-Cholecystectomy Bile Duct Injury:** Iatrogenic clipping or transection of the **Common Bile Duct** mistaken for the cystic duct due to failure to achieve the **"Critical View of Safety"** (clearing **Calot's Triangle**—bounded by the **cystic duct, common hepatic duct, and inferior border of the liver**—to identify **only 2 structures** [cystic duct and **cystic artery**] entering the gallbladder before clipping) $\rightarrow$ presents postoperatively with **bile leak (biloma, abdominal pain, peritonitis)** or **progressive obstructive jaundice** $\rightarrow$ diagnose with **HIDA scan / MRCP / ERCP** and refer to hepatobiliary surgery for **Roux-en-Y hepaticojejunostomy**.
> - **Post-Cholecystectomy Syndrome / Bile Acid Diarrhea:** Continuous flow of bile acids into the colon causes secretory diarrhea $\rightarrow$ treat with **Cholestyramine (4 g PO daily)**.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 46-year-old woman with obesity presents with 14 hours of constant, severe RUQ pain radiating to her right scapula that began after eating a heavy dinner, accompanied by nausea and fever. Vitals show T 38.4°C, HR 102 bpm, and BP 126/78 mmHg; exam reveals focal RUQ tenderness with **inspiratory arrest on deep palpation (positive Murphy's sign)**. Labs show WBC 15,200/µL, normal total bilirubin (0.8 mg/dL), normal alkaline phosphatase (85 U/L), and normal lipase (42 U/L); **RUQ Ultrasound** reveals an impacted gallstone in the gallbladder neck, **gallbladder wall thickening of 5 mm, pericholecystic fluid**, and a normal 4-mm CBD (low probability for choledocholithiasis). She is made NPO, started on IV Lactated Ringer's and **IV Ceftriaxone + Metronidazole**, and undergoes an uncomplicated **Early Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy** the next morning (without needing MRCP or ERCP), with antibiotics discontinued immediately postoperatively.
