> [!abstract] Acute Abdomen
> Sudden-onset (<24–48 hours), severe non-traumatic abdominal pain frequently accompanied by **peritoneal irritation** or **hemodynamic instability**, requiring rapid differentiation between **catastrophic surgical/vascular emergencies** and non-surgical medical conditions.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Visceral vs. Parietal Pain Shift (Classic in Appendicitis/Cholecystitis):**
>   - **Visceral pain** (autonomic C-fibers stimulated by stretch/ischemia): **Poorly localized, dull/colicky midline pain** corresponding to embryologic gut origin (**Foregut** [stomach, duodenum, hepatobiliary, pancreas] $\rightarrow$ **Epigastric**; **Midgut** [small bowel, appendix, right colon] $\rightarrow$ **Periumbilical**; **Hindgut** [left colon, rectum] $\rightarrow$ **Suprapubic**).
>   - **Parietal (Somatic) pain** (A-delta fibers in parietal peritoneum): **Sharp, pinpoint, lateralized pain** worsened by movement/coughing, accompanied by reflex abdominal wall muscle spasm (**involuntary guarding / rigidity**).
> - **Referred Pain:** Shared spinal cord dorsal horn segments (**Right shoulder/scapula [C3–C5 phrenic/supraclavicular nerve]** = diaphragm/gallbladder [**Kehr's sign** in splenic rupture; **Boas sign** in cholecystitis]; **Back** = retroperitoneum [pancreas, AAA]; **Groin/testicle/labia** = ureterolithiasis).

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Give IV opioids EARLY:** Administering titrated IV morphine or fentanyl **does NOT mask peritoneal signs** or delay surgical diagnosis; withholding analgesia is outdated and impairs the physical exam.
> - **Pain out of proportion to physical exam = Acute Mesenteric Ischemia (AMI):** Sudden, severe periumbilical pain with a **soft, non-tender abdomen** in a patient with **atrial fibrillation**, heart failure, or atherosclerosis is **Acute Mesenteric Ischemia** until proven otherwise by **CT Angiography (CTA)** (_Note: A normal serum lactate does NOT rule out early mesenteric ischemia—elevated lactate is a LATE sign of transmural bowel necrosis!_).
> - **Unstable + Peritonitis or Free Air = Direct to OR (Skip CT):** Never send a hemodynamically unstable patient with a rigid abdomen or positive FAST/ruptured AAA to the CT scanner ("the doughnut of death").
> - **Always check 3 "hidden" areas on physical exam:** (1) **Inguinal/femoral canals** (incarcerated hernia is a top missed cause of bowel obstruction), (2) **Testicles** (testicular torsion presents as isolated lower abdominal pain in boys/young men), and (3) **Pelvic/pregnancy status** in all reproductive-age females.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Onset & Tempo (Critical Discriminator):**
>   - **Instantaneous / "Thunderclap" (Maximal at 1 second):** **Ruptured** viscus or vascular catastrophe (**Perforated peptic ulcer**, **Ruptured AAA**, **Ruptured ectopic pregnancy**), **Torsion** (**Volvulus**, ovarian/testicular torsion), or **Acute arterial embolus** (SMA embolus).
>   - **Rapidly Progressive (Minutes to 1–2 hours):** **Acute Pancreatitis**, **Biliary Colic**, **Ureteral Colic**, **Strangulated Bowel Obstruction**, **Mesenteric Venous Thrombosis**.
>   - **Gradual / Insidious (Hours to days):** **Acute Appendicitis**, **Acute Cholecystitis**, **Acute Diverticulitis**, **Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID)**.
> - **Associated GI Symptoms:**
>   - _Pain BEFORE vomiting:_ Surgical abdomen (appendicitis, obstruction). _Vomiting BEFORE pain:_ Medical gastroenteritis or Boerhaave syndrome.
>   - _Obstipation_ (inability to pass flatus or stool) + bilious/feculent emesis: **Bowel Obstruction**.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Inspection:** Patient **lying completely motionless** with knees flexed = **Generalized Peritonitis** (movement jars the peritoneum); patient **writhing endlessly, unable to find a comfortable position** = **Visceral Colic** (ureterolithiasis, biliary colic, early mesenteric ischemia); surgical scars (adhesions).
> - **Auscultation & Percussion:** High-pitched **"tinkling" rushes** $\rightarrow$ early mechanical bowel obstruction; **absent bowel sounds** $\rightarrow$ ileus or late peritonitis; **loss of normal liver dullness** (tympany over RUQ) $\rightarrow$ massive pneumoperitoneum.
> - **Palpation (Start farthest away from the site of maximal pain):**
>   - **Involuntary Guarding / "Board-Like" Rigidity:** Reflex spasm of the rectus abdominis that persists during expiration $\rightarrow$ confirms **parietal peritonitis**.
>   - **Peritoneal Signs:** **Cough/Heel-drop tenderness** (more humane and specific than traditional rebound tenderness), **Rovsing's/Psoas/Obturator signs** (appendicitis), **Murphy's sign** (inspiratory arrest on RUQ palpation = acute cholecystitis).
>   - **Pulsatile abdominal mass:** **Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (AAA)**.
> - **Mandatory Hernia, Genitourinary, Pelvic, & Rectal Exam:** Palpate femoral/inguinal orifices; examine scrotum; perform bimanual pelvic exam (cervical motion tenderness) and DRE (fecal impaction, blood, pelvic appendicitis).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Generalized Peritonitis (Clinical Surgical Diagnosis):** Acute abdominal pain accompanied by **involuntary abdominal wall rigidity**, diffuse rebound/percussion tenderness, and systemic inflammatory response (or hemodynamic instability).
> - **Hollow Viscus Perforation:** **Pneumoperitoneum** (free intraperitoneal air beneath the diaphragm on upright CXR or extraluminal gas on CT) OR surgical visualization of a transmural GI defect.
> - **Acute Mesenteric Ischemia (AMI):** Occlusion or critical hypoperfusion of the **Superior Mesenteric Artery (SMA)** (or mesenteric veins) confirmed via **Biphasic CT Angiography (CTA)**, paired with clinical/endoscopic/surgical evidence of bowel ischemia.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs (Order STAT on All Acute Abdomen Patients)**
>
> - **Mandatory Core Panel:**
>   1. **Urine/Serum $\beta$-hCG:** **Mandatory FIRST test** in **ALL females of childbearing age (12–55 y)** regardless of contraception/sexual history to rule out **ectopic pregnancy**.
>   2. **CBC with Differential, CMP, Serum Lipase** (≥3× ULN = acute pancreatitis), **Arterial/Venous Blood Gas + Serum Lactate** (elevated in sepsis, hypoperfusion, or late bowel necrosis/strangulation).
>   3. **Type and Screen / Crossmatch + PT/INR** (preoperative preparation).
>   4. **Urinalysis** (hematuria in nephrolithiasis; pyuria in UTI/pyelonephritis—_Note: sterile pyuria/microscopic hematuria can also occur when an inflamed appendix or diverticulum abuts the ureter/bladder!_).
>   5. **12-Lead ECG + High-Sensitivity Troponin:** Mandatory in **all patients with epigastric pain** (especially >40 y, diabetics, or women) to exclude **Inferior STEMI**.
>   6. **Fingerstick Glucose + Beta-Hydroxybutyrate:** Rule out **Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA)** as a medical mimic of acute abdomen.
>
> **Imaging (Tailored by Quadrant & Hemodynamic Status — ACR Appropriateness Criteria)**
>
> - **Hemodynamically Unstable:** **Bedside POCUS / FAST Exam** (identifies free intraperitoneal fluid in **ruptured ectopic pregnancy** or **ruptured AAA** [aorta >3 cm; typically >5.5 cm when ruptured]) + **Portable Upright Chest X-Ray** (identifies subdiaphragmatic **free air**).
> - **Right Upper Quadrant (RUQ) Pain:** **RUQ Ultrasound** is **first-line** (gallstones, gallbladder wall thickening >3 mm, pericholecystic fluid, sonographic Murphy sign, CBD diameter). If US is equivocal for acute cholecystitis, order a **HIDA Scan (Cholescintigraphy)** (non-visualization of the gallbladder at 4 hours is >95% sensitive/specific for cystic duct obstruction).
> - **Right Lower Quadrant (RLQ), Left Lower Quadrant (LLQ), or Diffuse Pain:** **CT Abdomen and Pelvis with IV Contrast** is the **first-line gold standard** (identifies appendicitis [dilated non-compressible appendix >6 mm with periappendiceal fat stranding], diverticulitis, abscess, bowel obstruction [transition point, closed-loop], or perforation; _oral contrast is NOT needed and delays care_).
>   - _Exception:_ In **pregnant women or children** with suspected appendicitis, start with **Graded-Compression Ultrasound** followed by **Non-Contrast MRI Abdomen/Pelvis** to avoid ionizing radiation.
> - **Suspected Pelvic / Gynecologic Pathology:** **Transvaginal + Pelvic Ultrasound with Color Doppler** (first-line for ectopic pregnancy, tubo-ovarian abscess, or **ovarian torsion**).
> - **Suspected Acute Mesenteric Ischemia:** **CT Angiography (CTA) of the Abdomen/Pelvis** (biphasic arterial and portal venous phases, **NO oral contrast**); standard contrast CT is insufficient!

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Hemodynamic Instability (SBP <90 mmHg, MAP <65 mmHg, Shock Index >1)** or syncope
> - **"Board-like" involuntary abdominal rigidity** or silent abdomen
> - **Pain out of proportion to physical exam** (especially with atrial fibrillation, recent MI, or vascular disease $\rightarrow$ **Acute Mesenteric Ischemia**)
> - **Pulsatile abdominal mass** + hypotension + back/flank pain ($\rightarrow$ **Ruptured AAA**)
> - **Positive $\beta$-hCG** + abdominal pain / vaginal bleeding / syncope ($\rightarrow$ **Ruptured Ectopic Pregnancy**)
> - **Pneumoperitoneum** on upright CXR or **Pneumatosis Intestinalis / Portal Venous Gas** on CT (indicates transmural bowel necrosis)
> - **Fever + Jaundice + RUQ Pain (Charcot Triad)** $\rightarrow$ **Acute Ascending Cholangitis**

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss ("The Deadly Six" Vascular & Perforated Catastrophes)**
>
> - **Ruptured Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (AAA):** Men >60 y, smokers; triad of sudden severe abdominal/back/flank pain, hypotension/syncope, and pulsatile abdominal mass (frequently misdiagnosed as "renal colic" due to flank pain and hematuria!).
> - **Acute Mesenteric Ischemia (AMI):** 4 subtypes: (1) **Arterial Embolism** (50%: atrial fibrillation, mural thrombus after MI; lodges just distal to middle colic artery), (2) **Arterial Thrombosis** (25%: acute-on-chronic atherosclerosis at SMA origin), (3) **Non-Occlusive Mesenteric Ischemia (NOMI)** (20%: ICU patients on high-dose vasopressors/cardiogenic shock/cocaine), (4) **Mesenteric Venous Thrombosis** (5%: hypercoagulable states, cirrhosis, pancreatitis).
> - **Hollow Viscus Perforation:** Perforated peptic ulcer, perforated diverticulitis (**Hinchey III/IV**), perforated appendicitis, Boerhaave syndrome, or toxic megacolon.
> - **Strangulated / Closed-Loop Bowel Obstruction & Volvulus:** **Small Bowel Obstruction (SBO)** (75% from postoperative **adhesions**, 15% from **incarcerated hernias**); **Sigmoid Volvulus** (elderly/institutionalized with chronic constipation; **"coffee-bean" sign** pointing toward RUQ); **Cecal Volvulus** (younger patients; points toward LUQ).
> - **Ruptured Ectopic Pregnancy:** #1 cause of first-trimester maternal mortality; amenorrhea (6–8 weeks), unilateral pelvic pain, vaginal spotting, shoulder-tip pain (hemoperitoneum), hemorrhagic shock.
> - **Acute Ascending Cholangitis:** Biliary obstruction (choledocholithiasis or malignancy) + bacterial superinfection; **Charcot Triad** (Fever, Jaundice, RUQ pain) progressing to **Reynolds Pentad** (+ Hypotension and Altered Mental Status).
>
> **Most Likely (Quadrant-Based Surgical & GI Causes)**
>
> - **RLQ:** **Acute Appendicitis** (#1 surgical emergency worldwide), Meckel diverticulitis, ileocecal Crohn's disease, cecal diverticulitis.
> - **RUQ:** **Acute Cholecystitis**, Biliary colic, Choledocholithiasis, Hepatic abscess, Fitz-Hugh-Curtis syndrome (perihepatitis from PID).
> - **Epigastric:** **Acute Pancreatitis**, Peptic ulcer disease, Gastritis/GERD.
> - **LLQ:** **Acute Sigmoid Diverticulitis**, Ischemic colitis, Infectious/IBD colitis.
> - **Pelvic / Genitourinary:** **Ovarian Torsion** (sudden unilateral adnexal pain + nausea/vomiting due to rotation around infundibulopelvic ligament; often caused by a benign **dermoid cyst ≥5 cm**), **Testicular Torsion**, **Tubo-Ovarian Abscess / PID**, **Ureterolithiasis** (colicky flank-to-groin pain + hematuria).
>
> **Most Surprising ("Non-Surgical Medical Mimics" — Never Operate on These!)**
>
> - **Cardiopulmonary:** **Inferior Myocardial Infarction**, **Basilar Pneumonia / Empyema**, **Pulmonary Embolism**.
> - **Metabolic / Endocrine:** **Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA)** (causes severe abdominal pain, vomiting, and leukocytosis that resolve completely with insulin and IV fluids), **Acute Adrenal Crisis**, **Hypercalcemic Crisis**.
> - **Hematologic / Neurologic / Toxic:** **Acute Intermittent Porphyria (AIP)** (severe neurovisceral abdominal pain with normal exam + dark port-wine urine + peripheral neuropathy/psychosis + hyponatremia; elevated **urine PBG**), **Sickle Cell Vaso-Occlusive Crisis**, **Herpes Zoster** (pre-eruptive dermatomal pain), **Heavy Metal (Lead) Poisoning**, **C1-Esterase Inhibitor Deficiency (Hereditary Angioedema)**.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Rapid hemodynamic resuscitation (**MAP ≥65 mmHg**; or **permissive hypotension SBP 70–90 mmHg** strictly in **ruptured AAA** prior to aortic clamping), early pain relief, and **definitive surgical/endoscopic/endovascular source control within 1–6 hours**.
> - **Secondary Target:** Prevent septic shock, transmural bowel gangrene/short bowel syndrome, and multi-organ failure.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (First 0–30 Minutes)**
>
> - **Keep NPO, place two large-bore IVs (≥16–18G), infuse isotonic crystalloids (Lactated Ringer's), and correct electrolytes ($\text{K}^+, \text{Mg}^{2+}$)** immediately in all patients with suspected surgical abdomen or sepsis (_Exception: Use **permissive hypotension [target SBP 70–90 mmHg]** and immediate blood transfusion in **Ruptured AAA** to avoid popping the retroperitoneal tamponade clot_).
> - **Administer titrated IV Opioid Analgesia (Fentanyl 25–50 mcg IV or Morphine 2–4 mg IV) + IV Antiemetic (Ondansetron 4 mg IV)** immediately upon presentation to relieve severe pain without obscuring the exam.
> - **Administer Broad-Spectrum IV Antibiotics covering Gram-negative rods and Anaerobes within 1 hour** (e.g., **Piperacillin-Tazobactam 3.375–4.5 g IV**, OR **Ceftriaxone 2 g IV + Metronidazole 500 mg IV**; use **Meropenem** or **Cefepime + Metronidazole** if healthcare-associated or septic shock) whenever **perforation, peritonitis, appendicitis, cholecystitis, diverticulitis, cholangitis, or strangulated/ischemic bowel** is diagnosed.
> - **Place a Nasogastric (NG) Tube for gastric decompression** in patients with **Small Bowel Obstruction**, intractable vomiting, paralytic ileus, or hollow viscus perforation to prevent aspiration and reduce intraluminal wall tension.
>
> **Definitive Surgical & Interventional Pathways by Pathology**
>
> - **Hollow Viscus Perforation / Generalized Peritonitis / Ruptured AAA / Ruptured Ectopic Pregnancy:**
>   - **Proceed IMMEDIATELY to the Operating Room for Exploratory Laparotomy or Laparoscopy** (or **Emergent EVAR** for ruptured AAA; **Laparoscopic Salpingectomy** for ruptured ectopic; **Graham Patch** for perforated duodenal ulcer; **Hartmann's Procedure** [sigmoid resection + end colostomy] for perforated feculent diverticulitis [Hinchey IV]).
> - **Acute Mesenteric Ischemia (AMI):**
>   - **Start an immediate unfractionated Heparin IV infusion** (bolus 80 U/kg, then 18 U/kg/hr), **infuse IV crystalloids**, **administer broad-spectrum IV antibiotics**, and **perform Emergent Revascularization** (**Endovascular aspiration embolectomy/stenting** OR **Open Surgical SMA Embolectomy/Bypass + Exploratory Laparotomy** to resect frankly gangrenous bowel, often with a **planned "Second-Look" laparotomy at 24–48 hours** to reassess borderline bowel viability).
> - **Small Bowel Obstruction (SBO) & Volvulus:**
>   - **Perform Emergent Laparotomy** if the patient has **strangulation, peritonitis, closed-loop obstruction, pneumatosis intestinalis, or an incarcerated/strangulated hernia**.
>   - **Manage Uncomplicated Adhesive SBO Conservatively for 48–72 hours** ("drip and suck": NPO, NG tube suction, IV fluids) and administer a **Water-Soluble Contrast (Gastrografin) Challenge** (both diagnostic and therapeutic by drawing edema out of the bowel wall; if contrast reaches the colon on X-ray within 24 hours, >95% resolve without surgery).
>   - **Perform Flexible Sigmoidoscopic Detorsion with Rectal Tube Decompression** as **first-line treatment for uncomplicated Sigmoid Volvulus** (without peritonitis/gangrene), followed by **elective sigmoid colectomy during the same admission** (70% recurrence rate without surgery!). _Note: **Cecal Volvulus** CANNOT be detorsed endoscopically and requires **immediate surgical Right Hemicolectomy**._
> - **Acute Appendicitis, Cholecystitis, & Diverticulitis:**
>   - **Acute Appendicitis:** **Perform Laparoscopic Appendectomy** + IV antibiotics as the gold standard (_Note: **Periappendiceal phlegmon or abscess >3 cm** presenting late [>5 days] is managed FIRST with **IV antibiotics + CT-guided percutaneous drainage**, followed by interval appendectomy/colonoscopy in 6–8 weeks to rule out cecal malignancy_).
>   - **Acute Calculous Cholecystitis:** **Perform Early Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy within 24–72 hours** of admission + IV antibiotics (in critically ill, unstable ICU patients unfit for surgery [or **Acalculous Cholecystitis**], perform **Percutaneous Cholecystostomy Tube drainage**).
>   - **Acute Diverticulitis:** Manage **uncomplicated** cases outpatient (clear liquids; **routine antibiotics are NO LONGER required** in healthy immunocompetent patients with mild uncomplicated diverticulitis per AGA/ASCRS guidelines!). For **complicated diverticulitis with abscess ≥3–4 cm**, perform **CT-guided percutaneous drainage + IV antibiotics**, followed by **colonoscopy 6–8 weeks after resolution** to exclude colon cancer.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid delaying CTA in suspected Acute Mesenteric Ischemia or Ruptured AAA due to elevated Creatinine / Acute Kidney Injury**—life and bowel viability always supersede the risk of contrast nephropathy (100% mortality if AMI goes untreated).
> - **Avoid Oral Contrast (especially Barium) in Acute Abdomen**, as oral contrast delays CT imaging by 2–3 hours, induces vomiting/aspiration in bowel obstruction, obscures mesenteric vessels on CTA, and **Barium causes fatal chemical peritonitis if a perforation is present** (use water-soluble contrast ONLY if specifically required).
> - **Avoid Vasoconstrictors (Alpha-agonists like Phenylephrine or high-dose Norepinephrine) in Non-Occlusive Mesenteric Ischemia (NOMI)** whenever possible, as splanchnic vasoconstriction worsens bowel infarction (optimize cardiac output with fluids/inotropes and consider intra-arterial papaverine).
> - **Avoid attempting Manual Reduction (Taxis) of a Strangulated Hernia** (skin erythema, fever, leukocytosis, acidosis, or peritonitis), as pushing gangrenous/necrotic bowel back into the peritoneal cavity ("reduction en masse") causes fatal missed peritonitis—take directly to the OR!
> - **Avoid Colonoscopy during Acute Diverticulitis** due to the high risk of **colonic perforation** from air insufflation (always wait **6–8 weeks** after acute inflammation resolves to perform colonoscopy).

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Abdominal Compartment Syndrome (ACS):** Sustained **Intra-Abdominal Pressure (IAP) >20 mmHg** (measured via Foley bladder pressure transducer) **with new-onset organ dysfunction** (oliguria/AKI, high peak airway pressures/hypoxia, decreased venous return/hypotension) following massive fluid resuscitation, pancreatitis, or mesenteric ischemia revascularization $\rightarrow$ treat with deep sedation/neuromuscular blockade, NG/rectal decompression, and **emergent Decompressive Laparotomy**.
> - **Short Bowel Syndrome:** Follows extensive small bowel resection (<100–200 cm remaining) for mesenteric ischemia or strangulated volvulus $\rightarrow$ requires long-term **Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN)** and **Teduglutide** (GLP-2 analogue).
> - **Tertiary Peritonitis & Intra-Abdominal Abscess:** Persistent or recurrent intra-abdominal infection (subphrenic, pelvic, or interloop abscess) 5–10 days post-laparotomy/perforation $\rightarrow$ presents with spiking fevers, ileus, and leukocytosis $\rightarrow$ treat with **CT-guided percutaneous catheter drainage** + targeted antibiotics.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 72-year-old woman with a history of atrial fibrillation (recently off apixaban for a dental procedure) presents with 3 hours of sudden, excruciating periumbilical abdominal pain and two episodes of vomiting. Vitals show HR 124 bpm (irregularly irregular) and BP 135/80 mmHg; abdominal exam reveals a **soft, non-distended abdomen with minimal tenderness and no guarding ("pain out of proportion to exam")**, and initial serum lactate is normal at 1.6 mmol/L. Recognizing classic **Acute Mesenteric Ischemia**, the clinician administers IV fentanyl, starts an **unfractionated heparin infusion**, orders a STAT **Biphasic CT Angiography (CTA)** (which confirms an embolus occluding the **Superior Mesenteric Artery**), and takes her immediately for **emergent SMA embolectomy** before transmural bowel necrosis develops.
