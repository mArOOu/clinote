> [!abstract] Abdominal Trauma
> Visceral or vascular injury within the peritoneal cavity, retroperitoneum, or thoracoabdominal transition zone resulting from **Blunt mechanisms** (motor vehicle collisions, falls, handlebar blows: **Spleen [#1, 40–50%] > Liver [#2, 35–45%] > Small Bowel [5–10%]**) or **Penetrating mechanisms**: **Stab Wounds** (**Liver [#1, 40%] > Small Bowel [30%] > Diaphragm [20%]**) vs. **Gunshot Wounds (GSWs)** (**Small Bowel [#1, 50%] > Colon [40%] > Liver [30%]**).

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Blunt Shear & Compression Forces:** Sudden deceleration shears organs at fixed anatomical attachments (**Ligament of Treitz, ileocecal junction, mesenteric root**) or crushes retroperitoneal structures (**duodenum, pancreas**) directly against the rigid **lumbar spine**.
> - **The Lethal Triad / Diamond of Trauma:** Massive hemorrhage $\rightarrow$ **Hypothermia (<35°C)** + **Metabolic Acidosis (pH <7.2 / Base deficit >6)** + **Trauma-Induced Coagulopathy (INR >1.5, Fibrinogen <100)** + **Hypocalcemia** (citrate toxicity from massive blood transfusion binding ionized $\text{Ca}^{2+}$) $\rightarrow$ arrests the coagulation cascade and mandates **Damage Control Surgery**!

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **The Golden Rule of Abdominal Trauma (Hemodynamics Dictate the Pathway!):**
>   - **Blunt Trauma + Hemodynamically UNSTABLE + Positive FAST $\rightarrow$ Immediate Exploratory Laparotomy (NEVER send an unstable patient to the CT scanner!).**
>   - **Blunt Trauma + Hemodynamically STABLE $\rightarrow$ CT Abdomen/Pelvis with IV Contrast** (even if FAST is positive! **>85% of stable blunt splenic and hepatic injuries are managed Non-Operatively** via observation or **IR Angioembolization**).
> - **Gunshot Wound (GSW) vs. Stab Wound to the Anterior Abdomen:**
>   - **Anterior Abdominal GSW:** >90% breach the peritoneum and cause hollow viscus injury $\rightarrow$ **almost ALL go directly to Exploratory Laparotomy**!
>   - **Anterior Abdominal Stab Wound (If Stable, No Peritonitis, No Evisceration):** Perform a **Local Wound Exploration (LWE)** at the bedside; if the **anterior rectus fascia is intact**, discharge the patient! If fascia is violated, perform **Serial Abdominal Exams (q4–6h for 24h) ± CT or Diagnostic Laparoscopy** (because 50% of stab wounds that penetrate the peritoneum cause zero significant injury!).
> - **"Seatbelt Sign" = Small Bowel / Mesenteric Injury + Chance Fracture:** Horizontal lower abdominal ecchymosis carries a **30% risk of hollow viscus/mesenteric injury** (bucket-handle tear causing bowel ischemia) + **L1–L3 flexion-distraction Chance fracture**.
> - **FAST Exam misses the Retroperitoneum and Hollow Viscus:** A negative FAST does **NOT** rule out retroperitoneal hemorrhage (kidneys, pancreas, duodenum, pelvic venous plexus) or early hollow viscus perforation!

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Mechanism & Energy (AMPLE History):** High-speed MVC, unrestrained vs. lap-belt only, steering-wheel/handlebar impact to epigastrium (classic in children $\rightarrow$ **duodenal hematoma or pancreatic transection**), weapon length/caliber/trajectory.
> - **Thoracoabdominal Zone (4th Intercostal Space [Nipples] anteriorly, 6th ICS laterally, 8th ICS [Scapular tips] posteriorly down to the Costal Margin):** Because the diaphragm rises to the 4th ICS during full expiration, **any penetrating wound below the nipples can traverse BOTH the chest and abdomen (Diaphragmatic injury)**!
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Primary Survey (ABCDE):** Tachycardia, narrowed pulse pressure, cool clammy skin, and hypotension (**Hemorrhagic Shock**—remember that in trauma, shock is **hemorrhagic** until proven otherwise; the 5 compartments where a patient can bleed to death are **"Blood on the floor and four more": Chest, Abdomen, Pelvis/Retroperitoneum, and Thighs [femur]**).
> - **Abdominal Inspection & Palpation:**
>   - **Seatbelt Sign:** Linear ecchymosis across the lower abdomen.
>   - **Kehr's Sign:** Referred **left shoulder-tip pain** (especially in Trendelenburg) caused by **splenic subdiaphragmatic hemoperitoneum** irritating the left phrenic nerve (C3–C5).
>   - **Cullen's Sign** (periumbilical ecchymosis) & **Grey Turner's Sign** (flank ecchymosis): Delayed signs (24–48h) of **retroperitoneal hemorrhage** (pancreas, duodenum, kidney, pelvic fracture).
>   - **Evisceration:** Omentum or small bowel protruding through a stab wound ($\rightarrow$ absolute indication for laparotomy).
>   - **Peritonitis (Guarding, rigidity, rebound):** Indicates hollow viscus perforation or hemoperitoneum (_Note: Physical exam is **unreliable** if the patient has an altered sensorium [TBI, intoxication], distracting injury, or spinal cord injury!_).
> - **Pelvic & Genitourinary Exam:** Test pelvic ring stability gently (once only!); inspect for **blood at the urethral meatus, scrotal/perineal butterfly hematoma, or gross hematuria** (renal/bladder/urethral injury).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Hemoperitoneum on eFAST (Extended Focused Assessment with Sonography for Trauma):** Anechoic (black) free fluid in any of the **3 abdominal views**: (1) **RUQ (Morison's Pouch / Hepatorenal recess)** (most sensitive view in supine patient) + right subphrenic/pleural space, (2) **LUQ (Splenorenal recess / Left subphrenic space)** (fluid often collects _above_ the spleen before between spleen and kidney), or (3) **Suprapubic (Pouch of Douglas / Rectovesical pouch)** (_Requires ~200–250 mL of intraperitoneal blood to turn positive_).
> - **Diagnostic Peritoneal Lavage (DPL — Used if eFAST is Equivocal/Unavailable in an Unstable Patient):** Positive if initial catheter aspiration yields **≥10 mL of frank gross blood**, enteric contents/bile, OR lavage effluent contains **>100,000 RBCs/mm³** (blunt), **>500 WBCs/mm³**, or elevated amylase/alkaline phosphatase.
> - **Solid Organ Injury Grading (AAST CT Scale Grades I–V):** Based on subcapsular hematoma size, parenchymal laceration depth (<1 cm, 1–3 cm, >3 cm), devascularization, or **Active Contrast Extravasation ("Blush") / Pseudoaneurysm**.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs (Order STAT on Arrival in Trauma Bay)**
>
> - **Type and Crossmatch (Activate Massive Transfusion Protocol [MTP] if Shock Index >1 or ABC Score ≥2).**
> - **Arterial Blood Gas + Base Deficit & Serum Lactate:** **Base deficit >6 mmol/L** or **Lactate >4 mmol/L** quantifies the severity of occult hemorrhagic shock and guides resuscitation endpoints.
> - **CBC, CMP, Lipase/Amylase, Coagulation Panel (PT/INR, aPTT, Fibrinogen) + Viscoelastic Testing (TEG or ROTEM)**, **Ionized Calcium**, **Urinalysis** (gross or microscopic hematuria), **Urine $\beta$-hCG**, Blood Alcohol/Toxicology.
>   - _Pitfall:_ Initial **Hemoglobin/Hematocrit is often NORMAL** in acute exsanguinating hemorrhage before interstitial fluid shifts occur—never withhold transfusion based on a normal initial Hb!
>
> **Imaging (Strictly Reserved for Hemodynamically STABLE Patients!)**
>
> - **eFAST (Extended FAST) + Portable Supine Chest X-Ray + Pelvic X-Ray:** Performed **within 2–5 minutes at the bedside** in all unstable blunt/penetrating trauma patients (identifies hemoperitoneum, pneumothorax/hemothorax, pericardial tamponade, and open-book pelvic fracture).
> - **CT Abdomen and Pelvis with IV Contrast (Multiphasic: Arterial + Portal Venous Phases; Add Delayed Excretory Phase [CT Cystogram/Urogram] if Hematuria/Pelvic Fracture):**
>   - **Gold standard for ALL hemodynamically STABLE blunt trauma patients** and select stable flank/back penetrating wounds.
>   - _Crucial CT Findings of Hollow Viscus / Mesenteric Injury (Often Subtle!):_ **Unexplained free intraperitoneal fluid WITHOUT a solid organ (spleen/liver) injury** (#1 CT clue of a small bowel/mesenteric tear!), focal bowel wall thickening, mesenteric hematoma/fat stranding, active mesenteric arterial blush, or extraluminal air.
> - **Diagnostic Laparoscopy:** Gold-standard test in **hemodynamically stable patients with Penetrating Left Thoracoabdominal Trauma** to directly visualize and repair **occult Diaphragmatic Lacerations** (CT misses up to 30–50% of small diaphragmatic stab/GSW holes!).

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> **Hard Indications for IMMEDIATE Exploratory Laparotomy (Bypass CT!):**
>
> 1. **Blunt Abdominal Trauma + Hemodynamic Instability + Positive eFAST** (or positive DPL).
> 2. **Penetrating Abdominal Trauma (GSW or Stab) + Hemodynamic Instability.**
> 3. **Gunshot Wound (GSW) traversing the Peritoneal Cavity** (vast majority).
> 4. **Peritonitis** (diffuse rigidity, guarding, rebound) on physical exam after blunt or penetrating trauma.
> 5. **Evisceration** of omentum or bowel through an abdominal wall wound.
> 6. **Pneumoperitoneum (Free Air) or Diaphragmatic Rupture** on Chest/Abdominal X-Ray or CT.
> 7. **Frank GI Bleeding** (bright red blood from NG tube or gross blood per rectum) or **Impalement injury in situ** following penetrating trauma.
> 8. **CT showing Free Fluid WITHOUT Solid Organ Injury** + clinical abdominal tenderness/leukocytosis (indicates **Hollow Viscus Perforation or Mesenteric Bucket-Handle Tear**).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss (Specific Organ & Retroperitoneal Injury Patterns)**
>
> - **Splenic Injury (#1 Blunt Organ Injured):** LUQ/left rib fractures (ribs 9–11), Kehr's sign, hypotension; _Delayed Splenic Rupture:_ Subcapsular hematoma that ruptures freely into the peritoneum **5–14 days** after initial trauma!
> - **Hepatic Injury (#2 Blunt Organ, #1 Stab Organ):** RUQ/right lower rib fractures; severe Grade IV–V central/juxtahepatic venous injuries (**retrohepatic IVC or hepatic veins**) carry >50% mortality.
> - **Duodenal & Pancreatic Injuries (Handlebar / Seatbelt Crush Against L1–L2 Spine):**
>   - **Duodenal Hematoma (Child with Handlebar Blow to Epigastrium):** Intramural blood clot in the 2nd/3rd duodenum causing **complete Gastric Outlet Obstruction (bilious vomiting + "Coiled-Spring" sign on upper GI series)** **48–72 hours after trauma** $\rightarrow$ **Strictly Non-Surgical!** Treat conservatively with **NPO + NG Tube Decompression + TPN for 2–3 weeks** (hematoma resorbs spontaneously!).
>   - **Retroperitoneal Duodenal Perforation:** CT shows **retroperitoneal air/extravasation around the right kidney** $\rightarrow$ **Surgical Emergency** (primary repair / pyloric exclusion).
>   - **Pancreatic Duct Injury:** Determined entirely by the integrity of the **Main Pancreatic Duct** on CT/MRCP/ERCP: if duct is **intact (Grade I–II)** $\rightarrow$ non-operative/drainage; if **main pancreatic duct is transected in the body/tail (Grade III, left of SMV)** $\rightarrow$ **Distal Pancreatectomy**.
> - **Traumatic Diaphragmatic Rupture:**
>   - _Blunt:_ Sudden massive rise in intra-abdominal pressure bursts the diaphragm (**80–90% occur on the LEFT side** because the liver shields the right hemidiaphragm) $\rightarrow$ **stomach and colon herniate into the left chest** (**CXR shows NG tube tip curled inside the left hemithorax**!).
>   - _Penetrating:_ Small 1–2 cm defects in the thoracoabdominal zone; if missed, negative intrathoracic pressure slowly sucks bowel into the chest, presenting years later with **strangulated intrathoracic bowel**.
> - **Retroperitoneal Hematoma (Classified by Anatomical Zones I, II, & III — Master Surgical Rules!):**
>   - **Zone 1 (Central-Medial — Aorta, IVC, Celiac/SMA, Pancreas, Duodenum):** **EXPLORE ALL Zone 1 hematomas** (whether blunt or penetrating!).
>   - **Zone 2 (Lateral / Flank — Kidneys, Renal Vessels, Ascending/Descending Colon):** **Explore Penetrating**; **DO NOT explore Blunt Zone 2** unless the hematoma is **pulsatile, rapidly expanding, or the patient is exsanguinating** (opening Gerota's fascia releases the retroperitoneal tamponade and forces an unnecessary nephrectomy!).
>   - **Zone 3 (Pelvic — Iliac Vessels & Pelvic Venous Plexus from Pelvic Ring Fractures):** **Explore Penetrating**; **NEVER open a Blunt Zone 3 pelvic hematoma during laparotomy** (unroofing a pelvic venous hematoma causes uncontrollable fatal exsanguination! Treat with **Pelvic Binder + Preperitoneal Pelvic Packing + IR Internal Iliac Angioembolization / REBOA**!).

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Rapid hemorrhage control, prevention of the **Lethal Diamond** (maintain **T >36°C, pH >7.25, Ionized $\text{Ca}^{2+} >1.1\text{ mmol/L}$**), and preservation of solid organs (especially the spleen) in hemodynamically stable patients.
> - **Secondary Target:** Early identification and repair of hollow viscus, diaphragmatic, and pancreaticobiliary injuries to prevent delayed peritonitis and sepsis.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (Trauma Bay Hemostatic Resuscitation — ATLS)**
>
> - **Place two large-bore IVs (14–16G), limit crystalloids (<1 L), and initiate Balanced Hemostatic Resuscitation (1 : 1 : 1 ratio of pRBCs : Fresh Frozen Plasma : Platelets)** using **Permissive Hypotension (target SBP 80–90 mmHg or MAP 50–60 mmHg)** (unless concomitant Traumatic Brain Injury [TBI], which requires SBP ≥100–110 mmHg) until surgical/angiographic hemostasis is achieved.
> - **Administer Tranexamic Acid (TXA: 1 g IV over 10 min, then 1 g over 8 hours)** **within 3 hours of injury** (**CRASH-2 trial**) AND **infuse IV Calcium Chloride or Calcium Gluconate** empirically after every 4 units of blood products to prevent citrate-induced hypocalcemia and coagulopathy.
> - **Apply a Circumferential Pelvic Binder centered directly over the Greater Trochanters** (NOT the iliac crests!) if an unstable **"Open-Book" Pelvic Fracture** is contributing to hemorrhagic shock, to close down pelvic venous volume.
> - **Consider Zone 1 or Zone 3 REBOA (Resuscitative Endovascular Balloon Occlusion of the Aorta)** via the common femoral artery as a temporary bridge to the OR/IR suite in patients in extremis from non-compressible torso or pelvic hemorrhage.
>
> **Non-Operative Management (NOM) of Solid Organ Injuries (Spleen, Liver, Kidney — WSES Guidelines)**
>
> - **Eligibility Criterion:** **Hemodynamic Stability is the #1 absolute prerequisite** (regardless of AAST CT Grade I–V!), **PLUS** absence of peritonitis or hollow viscus injury, in a facility with ICU monitoring, serial Hb/abdominal exam capabilities, and immediate OR/IR availability.
> - ** AA ST Grade I–II (Low-Grade) Blunt Spleen / Liver / Kidney Injury WITHOUT Active Blush:**
>   - **Manage with Observation Alone:** Admit to monitored bed/ICU, serial physical exams and **Hemoglobin checks q4–6h for 24–48 hours**, start **pharmacologic VTE prophylaxis (LMWH) within 24–48 hours** once Hb is stable (safe and does not increase bleeding!), and resume diet/activity once stable.
> - **AAST Grade III–V (High-Grade) OR Any Grade with Active Contrast Extravasation ("Blush") / Pseudoaneurysm on CT in a STABLE Patient:**
>   - **Perform Urgent IR Transcatheter Arterial Angioembolization** (coil/gelfoam embolization of the splenic, hepatic, or renal arterial branch), which achieves **>90% organ salvage** without laparotomy!
>   - _Failure of NOM (Proceed Immediately to Laparotomy):_ Development of **hemodynamic instability**, ongoing transfusion requirement (**>2–4 units pRBCs** for solid organ bleeding), or **new peritonitis**.
>
> **Operative Management & Damage Control Laparotomy**
>
> - **Midline Exploratory Laparotomy Technique:** Make a rapid xiphoid-to-pubis incision, evacuate clot, and **pack all 4 quadrants** (RUQ, LUQ, pelvis, paracolic gutters) with laparotomy pads to achieve immediate tamponade while anesthesia catches up; then systematically inspect the **GI tract from the Ligament of Treitz to the peritoneal reflection** and the retroperitoneum.
> - **Organ-Specific Intraoperative Procedures:**
>   - **Spleen:** In an **unstable** patient with severe splenic hemorrhage, **perform an immediate Total Splenectomy** (do NOT waste time attempting splenorrhaphy in a shocked patient!).
>     - _Mandatory Post-Splenectomy Vaccination Rule (Prevent **Overwhelming Post-Splenectomy Infection [OPSI]**):_ Administer vaccines against the **3 Encapsulated Bacteria**—**1. _Streptococcus pneumoniae_ (PCV20)**, **2. _Haemophilus influenzae_ type b (Hib)**, and **3. _Neisseria meningitidis_ (MenACWY + MenB)**—either **14 days AFTER emergent splenectomy** (or prior to hospital discharge if the patient is unreliable for follow-up!).
>   - **Liver (Major Grade IV–V Hemorrhage):**
>     1. **Step 1:** **Manual Compression & Perihepatic Packing** (compressing the liver anteroposteriorly against the diaphragm/spine controls **80% of venous hepatic bleeding**!).
>     2. **Step 2 (Pringle Maneuver — Both Diagnostic & Therapeutic):** Clamp the **Hepatoduodenal Ligament** (containing the **Portal Vein, Hepatic Artery, and Common Bile Duct**) via the foramen of Winslow: if bleeding stops, the source is the **hepatic artery or portal vein**; if **dark venous bleeding continues despite a clamped Pringle maneuver**, the patient has a **Retrohepatic IVC or Major Hepatic Vein Injury** ($\rightarrow$ **re-pack tightly and perform Damage Control closure**!).
>   - **Small Bowel & Colon Perforations:**
>     - **Small Bowel / Nondestructive Colon (<50% circumference):** **Primary Suture Repair**.
>     - **Destructive (>50% circumference) or Devascularized Mesenteric Tear:** **Segmental Resection with Primary Anastomosis** (or **leave in discontinuity** if the patient is in shock/acidotic/hypothermic!).
>     - **Extraperitoneal Rectal Injury:** Perform **Diverting Loop Sigmoid Colostomy** (if destructive or perirectal sepsis).
> - **Damage Control Surgery (Indicated when pH <7.2, Temp <34–35°C, INR >1.5, or Massive Transfusion >10 Units):**
>   - **Abort Definitive Reconstruction:** Control surgical bleeding (splenectomy, perihepatic packing), staple off hollow viscus perforations to stop contamination **without creating anastomoses or stomas**, leave the fascia open with a **Temporary Negative-Pressure Abdominal Closure**, warm and resuscitate the patient in the ICU for **24–48 hours**, and return to the OR for pack removal and bowel anastomosis.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Sending a Hemodynamically Unstable Trauma Patient with a Positive FAST to the CT Scanner** ("The Doughnut of Death")—resuscitation and immediate transport to the Operating Room take absolute priority.
> - **Avoid Opening a Blunt Zone 2 (Renal) or Blunt Zone 3 (Pelvic) Retroperitoneal Hematoma** during laparotomy unless it is pulsatile/rapidly expanding (Zone 2), as incising the retroperitoneal peritoneum releases the natural tamponade and triggers uncontrollable venous hemorrhage (treat blunt pelvic hemorrhage with **preperitoneal packing + IR angioembolization**).
> - **Avoid Operating on an Intramural Duodenal Hematoma** (classic in children after a handlebar injury), as 90% resolve completely within 14–21 days with **NG decompression and TPN**.
> - **Avoid Probing a Penetrating Neck or Abdominal Wound Blindly with a Cotton Swab or Finger without Proper Preparation**, and **NEVER Remove an Impaled Object (knife, rebar) in the Trauma Bay**—all impaled objects must be removed **in the Operating Room under direct surgical vascular control**!
> - **Avoid Closing the Abdominal Wall Fascia Primarily under Tension after Massive Resuscitation or Perihepatic Packing** to prevent fatal **Abdominal Compartment Syndrome (ACS)**.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Abdominal Compartment Syndrome (ACS):** Sustained **Intra-Abdominal Pressure (IAP) >20 mmHg** (measured via Foley bladder transducer) **PLUS new-onset organ failure** (oliguria/AKI, peak airway pressure >40 $\text{cmH}_2\text{O}$, decreased cardiac output/hypotension) $\rightarrow$ requires **emergent surgical Decompressive Laparotomy**.
> - **Missed Hollow Viscus or Mesenteric Injury:** A mesenteric **"bucket-handle" avulsion** tears the mesentery away from a loop of small bowel; the bowel may not perforate immediately on Day 1, but undergoes progressive **ischemic necrosis**, presenting **24–72 hours later** with fever, leukocytosis, lactic acidosis, and delayed peritonitis (or weeks later as an ischemic stricture).
> - **Hepatic Trauma Complications (Bile Leak / Biloma & Hemobilia):**
>   - _Biloma / Bile Leak:_ RUQ pain, fever, and rising bilirubin days after high-grade liver trauma $\rightarrow$ treat with **CT-guided percutaneous drainage + ERCP with biliary stent/sphincterotomy** to lower biliary pressure.
>   - _Hemobilia (Hepatic Artery Pseudoaneurysm rupturing into the biliary tree):_ **Quincke's Triad**: (1) **Upper GI Bleeding (Hematemesis/Melena)**, (2) **RUQ Biliary Pain**, and (3) **Obstructive Jaundice** occurring 1–4 weeks after liver trauma $\rightarrow$ treat with **IR Hepatic Artery Angioembolization**!
> - **Overwhelming Post-Splenectomy Infection (OPSI):** Fulminant, rapidly fatal bacteremia and purpura fulminans (Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome) caused by encapsulated bacteria (**_S. pneumoniae_ [#1]**, _H. influenzae_, _N. meningitidis_) or _Capnocytophaga canimorsus_ (dog bites) / _Babesia_ in asplenic patients $\rightarrow$ prevent with **14-day post-splenectomy vaccinations** and immediate empiric fever action plan (amoxicillin-clavulanate or levofloxacin).

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> **Scenario 1 (Unstable Blunt Trauma):** A 32-year-old man arrives after a high-speed motorcycle crash with HR 136 bpm and BP 76/44 mmHg; he has left lower rib tenderness and a distended abdomen. Massive transfusion (1:1:1 pRBC:FFP:platelets) and **IV Tranexamic Acid (TXA)** are started immediately, and a bedside **eFAST exam** reveals a large anechoic fluid stripe in **Morison's pouch and the splenorenal recess**. Bypassing CT completely due to hemodynamic instability, he is taken **directly to the Operating Room for an emergent Exploratory Laparotomy**, where a shattered spleen (Grade V) is removed (**Total Splenectomy**); he receives **pneumococcal, meningococcal, and Hib vaccinations 14 days postoperatively**.
> **Scenario 2 (Stable High-Grade Splenic Trauma):** A 24-year-old woman presents after a ski collision with LUQ pain and left shoulder pain (**Kehr's sign**); vitals are stable (**HR 88 bpm, BP 124/78 mmHg**) and her abdomen is soft without diffuse peritonitis. Because she is **hemodynamically stable**, she undergoes a **CT Abdomen/Pelvis with IV Contrast**, which demonstrates a **Grade IV splenic laceration with an active arterial contrast blush** and moderate hemoperitoneum. Rather than undergoing laparotomy/splenectomy, she is taken to **Interventional Radiology for selective Splenic Artery Angioembolization** and admitted to the ICU for serial hemoglobin checks q6h, successfully preserving her spleen.
