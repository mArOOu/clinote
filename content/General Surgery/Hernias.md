> [!abstract] Hernias
> Protrusion of a viscus or peritoneal sac through a congenital or acquired anatomical defect in the musculoaponeurotic wall of its containing cavity, classified functionally as **Reducible** (contents return spontaneously or manually into the abdomen), **Incarcerated** (irreducible/trapped due to a narrow neck or adhesions, _without_ vascular compromise), or **Strangulated** (compromised venous/arterial blood supply leading to **ischemia, necrosis, and perforation—a surgical emergency**).

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Anatomical Landmarks of Groin Hernias (Inferior Epigastric Vessels & Inguinal Ligament):**
>   - **Indirect Inguinal Hernia (#1 Groin Hernia in BOTH Men and Women!):** Congenital failure of the **processus vaginalis** to obliterate $\rightarrow$ enters the **deep (internal) inguinal ring LATERAL to the Inferior Epigastric Vessels**, traverses the inguinal canal inside the spermatic cord coverings, and exits the superficial ring into the scrotum/labia majora.
>   - **Direct Inguinal Hernia:** Acquired weakness of the **transversalis fascia** within **Hesselbach's Triangle** (**M**edial to **I**nferior Epigastric Vessels, **L**ateral to **R**ectus Abdominis border, **S**uperior to **I**nguinal Ligament) $\rightarrow$ protrudes directly forward (**L**ow strangulation risk because it has a wide neck).
>   - **Femoral Hernia:** Protrudes **INFERIOR to the Inguinal (Poupart's) Ligament** through the **femoral ring** into the **femoral canal** (**medial to the femoral vein** and **lateral to the sharp, rigid Lacunar [Gimbernat's] Ligament**; **Mnemonic: NAVEL** from lateral to medial: **N**erve, **A**rtery, **V**ein, **E**mpty space [Femoral canal], **L**acunar ligament) $\rightarrow$ **HIGHEST risk of strangulation (30–40%)** due to the unyielding femoral ring!

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Indirect Inguinal vs. Femoral in Women:** **Indirect Inguinal Hernia is STILL the most common groin hernia in women** (as well as men and children); however, **Femoral Hernias** are **4–10× more common in women than men** (due to a wider female pelvis) and **ALL femoral hernias require prompt elective surgical repair** (even if asymptomatic) because 30–40% strangulate!
> - **Watchful Waiting is safe ONLY for Asymptomatic/Minimally Symptomatic Male Inguinal Hernias:** Never "watch and wait" on a **Femoral hernia**, **Symptomatic inguinal hernia**, or **Groin hernia in a non-pregnant female** (high rate of occult femoral hernia).
> - **Pediatric Umbilical vs. Pediatric Indirect Inguinal Hernia (Opposite Management!):**
>   - **Pediatric Umbilical Hernia:** >90% close spontaneously $\rightarrow$ **observe until age 4–5 years** (unless defect >1.5–2 cm or incarcerated).
>   - **Pediatric Indirect Inguinal Hernia:** **NEVER closes spontaneously** and has a high incarceration rate in infants $\rightarrow$ **repair promptly via high ligation of the hernia sac (herniotomy)** shortly after diagnosis!
> - **Never use Synthetic Mesh in a Contaminated / Gangrenous Field:** If a strangulated hernia requires bowel resection or has frank perforation/pus, perform a **primary tissue repair** (e.g., **Bassini, Shouldice, or McVay**) to avoid chronic mesh infection.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Uncomplicated Reducible Hernia:** Asymptomatic or dull, heavy, dragging ache/bulge in the groin or abdominal wall that **worsens with prolonged standing, heavy lifting, coughing, or straining (Valsalva)** and disappears when lying supine.
> - **Incarcerated Hernia:** Previously reducible bulge becomes **acutely stuck (irreducible)** and firm, often accompanied by crampy abdominal pain, nausea, and vomiting if it causes a **mechanical Small Bowel Obstruction (SBO)**.
> - **Strangulated Hernia (Surgical Emergency):** **Constant, severe, crescendo pain** at the hernia site + systemic toxicity (**fever, malaise, obstipation, bilious/feculent vomiting**).
> - **Predisposing Factors (Chronically Elevated Intra-Abdominal Pressure or Collagen Defects):** Chronic cough (**COPD/smoking**), straining from **constipation or BPH**, **ascites** (cirrhosis), **obesity**, pregnancy, heavy lifting, wound infection (#1 risk factor for **Incisional hernia**), and connective tissue disorders (**Ehlers-Danlos, Marfan**).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Examine the Patient BOTH Standing and Supine** (many reducible hernias are only visible/palpable when standing and coughing):
>   - **Inguinal Hernia:** Bulge located **SUPERIOR and MEDIAL** to the pubic tubercle / inguinal ligament; palpate by invaginating the scrotal skin with the index finger into the **superficial inguinal ring** and asking the patient to cough (_Note: Physical exam cannot reliably distinguish Indirect from Direct preoperatively—that distinction is made intraoperatively via the inferior epigastric vessels and does not change surgical management!_).
>   - **Femoral Hernia:** Small, globular bulge located **INFERIOR and LATERAL** to the pubic tubercle (**below the inguinal ligament** in the upper medial thigh); easily missed in obese patients!
> - **Signs of Strangulation (Absolute Contraindication to Manual Reduction!):**
>   1. **Exquisite, severe focal tenderness** over the irreducible mass,
>   2. **Overlying skin erythema, warmth, induration, or bluish discoloration**,
>   3. **Fever and Tachycardia**, AND/OR
>   4. **Generalized Peritonitis** (guarding/rebound) or **Systemic Sepsis**.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Abdominal Wall & Groin Hernias (Clinical Diagnosis):** Palpable or visible fascial defect with protrusion of intra-abdominal contents upon standing/Valsalva (_Note: Imaging is NOT required for clinically obvious groin or ventral hernias_).
> - **Hiatal Hernia (Diagnosed via Barium Esophagram, EGD, or High-Resolution Manometry):**
>   - **Type I (Sliding Hiatal Hernia — 90–95%):** Upward axial displacement of **BOTH the Gastroesophageal Junction (GEJ) and gastric cardia** ≥2 cm above the diaphragmatic hiatus (strongly associated with **GERD**).
>   - **Type II, III, & IV (Paraesophageal Hiatal Hernias — 5–10%):** The **gastric fundus** (Type II: GEJ stays in normal position; **Type III [Most common paraesophageal]: BOTH GEJ and fundus** herniate together) or **other organs (colon, spleen, pancreas = Type IV)** herniate through the hiatus alongside the esophagus into the thorax $\rightarrow$ high risk of **Gastric Volvulus, strangulation, and Cameron ulcers**!

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **Uncomplicated Elective Hernia:** No labs required beyond standard preoperative clearance.
> - **Incarcerated / Suspected Strangulated Hernia:** **STAT CBC** (**WBC >15,000/µL with left shift** suggests strangulation), **BMP**, **Serum Lactate & Venous/Arterial Blood Gas** (**lactic acidosis** indicates bowel ischemia, though **normal lactate does NOT rule out early strangulation!**), PT/INR, and Type and Screen.
>
> **Imaging (Ordered ONLY for Occult Hernias, Obese Patients, Complex Ventral Hernias, or Suspected SBO/Strangulation)**
>
> - **Groin Ultrasound (Dynamic with Valsalva):** **First-line imaging test** when a patient has classic groin pain but an **equivocal or non-palpable exam** (e.g., obese patients or athletes with suspected sports hernia/femoral hernia).
> - **CT Abdomen and Pelvis with IV Contrast:**
>   - **First-line in the Acute/Emergency Setting:** Confirms **Incarceration, Mechanical SBO, Strangulation** (decreased bowel wall enhancement, mesenteric fat stranding/fluid, pneumatosis), and **rare occult pelvic hernias** (**Obturator, Spigelian, Lumbar, or Internal hernias**).
>   - **Preoperative Planning for Complex / Giant Incisional Ventral Hernias:** Measures fascial defect width and **"Loss of Domain"** (when >20–50% of abdominal viscera reside outside the peritoneal cavity inside the hernia sac).
> - **MRI Pelvis:** Gold standard if ultrasound is negative and **Athletic Pubalgia ("Sports Hernia" — core muscle injury of the rectus abdominis/adductor longus aponeurosis WITHOUT a true hernia sac)** or osteitis pubis is suspected.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> **Mandate Immediate Surgical Exploration (Zero Attempt at Bedside Taxis):**
>
> - **Overlying skin erythema, warmth, edema, or ecchymosis** over an irreducible hernia
> - **Exquisite focal tenderness** or **generalized peritoneal signs** (guarding, rebound)
> - **Fever, tachycardia, hypotension**, **Leukocytosis (>15,000/µL)**, or **Elevated Lactate / Metabolic Acidosis**
> - **Incarceration duration >6–8 hours** with severe constant pain
> - **Borchardt's Triad in a Giant Paraesophageal Hiatal Hernia:** (1) **Severe epigastric/retrosternal chest pain**, (2) **Violent retching with inability to vomit**, and (3) **Inability to pass a Nasogastric (NG) tube** $\rightarrow$ diagnostic of **Acute Intrathoracic Gastric Volvulus** (surgical emergency!).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss (Groin/Scrotal & Rare High-Risk Hernias)**
>
> - **Testicular Torsion:** Acute painful hemiscrotal swelling, absent cremasteric reflex, high-riding horizontally oriented testis, negative Prehn sign; rule out with **STAT Color Doppler Scrotal Ultrasound**.
> - **Femoral Artery Pseudoaneurysm / Aneurysm:** Pulsatile groin mass Inferior to the inguinal ligament (especially after cardiac catheterization)—**never incise or attempt to forcefully crush!**
> - **Richter's Hernia:** Only the **antimesenteric wall** (partial circumference) of the small bowel becomes pinched inside a small hernia ring (classic in **femoral hernias or laparoscopic port-site hernias**) $\rightarrow$ **undergoes ischemic strangulation, gangrene, and perforation WITHOUT causing intestinal obstruction (patient continues passing flatus and stool!)**.
> - **Obturator Hernia ("The Little Old Lady's Hernia"):** Elderly, emaciated, multiparous women; bowel herniates through the **obturator canal** compressing the **obturator nerve** $\rightarrow$ presents with **SBO + Howship-Romberg Sign** (lancinating pain radiating down the **medial aspect of the thigh to the knee** worsened by hip extension/internal rotation/abduction); diagnosed by **CT Pelvis**.
> - **Spigelian Hernia:** Protrudes through the **Spigelian fascia** along the **semilunar line** (lateral border of the rectus sheath) just below the **arcuate line**; interpolates _between_ muscular layers (**interparietal**), making it difficult to palpate and prone to strangulation.
> - **Amyand's Hernia & Littre's Hernia:** Inguinal hernia sac containing the **vermiform appendix** (**Amyand's**) or a **Meckel's diverticulum** (**Littre's**).
> - **Pantaloon (Saddlebag) Hernia:** Combined ipsilateral **Direct AND Indirect** inguinal hernia straddling both sides of the inferior epigastric vessels.
> - **Sliding Inguinal Hernia:** An extraperitoneal viscus (**Sigmoid colon** on the left, **Cecum** on the right, or **Urinary Bladder** medially) forms part of the **actual wall of the hernia sac itself** $\rightarrow$ cutting into the posterior wall of the sac accidentally lacerates the colon or bladder!
>
> **Most Likely (Benign Groin & Scrotal Mimics)**
>
> - **Hydrocele (Communicating vs. Non-Communicating):** Painless scrotal swelling that **transilluminates brightly** and allows the examiner to **"get above" the mass** at the spermatic cord (unlike an inguinoscrotal hernia, where you _cannot_ get above the mass at the external ring and it does not transilluminate, except in young infants).
> - **Varicocele:** "Bag of worms" dilation of the **pampiniform venous plexus** (90% **left-sided** because the left gonadal vein drains into the left renal vein at a 90° angle) that collapses when supine (_Warning: An **acute right-sided varicocele or a varicocele that fails to collapse when supine** indicates **retroperitoneal IVC/renal vein obstruction from Renal Cell Carcinoma**!_).
> - **Inguinal Lymphadenopathy or Hidradenitis Suppurativa / Abscess:** Firm, discrete nodes or superficial skin infection.
> - **Diastasis Recti:** Benign midline thinning and widening of the **linea alba** between the rectus abdominis muscles (common in multiparous women and obese men) producing a symmetric midline ridge during a sit-up $\rightarrow$ **NO true fascial defect, ZERO risk of incarceration/strangulation, and NO surgical indication!**

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Tension-free anatomical closure of the fascial defect to eliminate pain, restore abdominal wall integrity, and **prevent visceral incarceration and strangulation**.
> - **Secondary Target:** Minimize chronic post-herniorrhaphy groin pain (inguinodynia) and hernia recurrence (<1–2% with mesh).
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (Incarcerated vs. Strangulated Hernia)**
>
> - **If ANY Signs of Strangulation are Present (Skin erythema, fever, leukocytosis, lactic acidosis, peritonitis, or severe constant pain):**
>   - **DO NOT attempt bedside manual reduction (Taxis)!**
>   - **Keep NPO, infuse IV crystalloids, place an NG tube (if SBO/vomiting), administer Broad-Spectrum IV Antibiotics (Cefoxitin, or Ceftriaxone + Metronidazole), and proceed IMMEDIATELY to the Operating Room for Emergent Surgical Exploration!**
>   - _Intraoperative Repair Choice:_ Inspect the trapped bowel for viability; if the bowel is **pink and viable** (no perforation/contamination), perform a **mesh repair**; if the bowel is **gangrenous/perforated requiring bowel resection**, perform a **Primary Tissue Repair WITHOUT Synthetic Mesh** (e.g., **McVay Cooper's Ligament repair** for femoral/inguinal, or **Bassini/Shouldice repair**) to prevent chronic mesh sepsis!
> - **If Acutely Incarcerated (<6 Hours) WITHOUT Any Signs of Strangulation:**
>   - **Attempt Bedside Manual Reduction (Taxis):** Administer **IV opioid analgesia + conscious sedation (midazolam)** to relax the abdominal wall, place the patient in **Trendelenburg position** (for groin hernias), apply a cold pack for 10–15 minutes to reduce edema, and apply gentle, steady squeezing pressure over the distal hernia while guiding the neck back through the fascial ring (like squeezing toothpaste back into a tube—never push straight down, which "mushrooms" the bowel over the ring edge).
>   - _If Taxis Succeeds:_ Observe the patient in the ED/hospital for several hours to ensure no ischemic bowel was reduced, and schedule **urgent/early elective surgical repair within days to 1–2 weeks**.
>   - _If Taxis Fails:_ **Proceed immediately to Urgent Surgical Repair!**
>
> **Definitive Elective Management of Groin Hernias (HerniaSurge Guidelines)**
>
> - **Watchful Waiting (Observation):** Safe and acceptable **ONLY in Men with Asymptomatic or Minimally Symptomatic Inguinal Hernias** (_Counsel patient that ~70% eventually develop pain and cross over to elective surgery within 5–10 years, though acute strangulation risk is very low [<0.5%/year]_).
> - **Indications for Prompt Elective Surgical Mesh Repair:**
>   1. **ALL Symptomatic Inguinal Hernias in Men**,
>   2. **ALL Groin Hernias in Women** (whether inguinal or femoral, symptomatic or asymptomatic—always repair via **Laparoscopic TEP/TAPP** to cover both the inguinal and femoral orifices simultaneously!), AND
>   3. **ALL Femoral Hernias in Men or Women** (regardless of symptoms, due to the 30–40% strangulation rate!).
> - **Surgical Technique Selection (Tension-Free Mesh Repair is the Gold Standard for Adults):**
>   - **Open Tension-Free Lichtenstein Mesh Repair:** Best for **unilateral primary inguinal hernias in men** (can be performed under local/regional anesthesia in frail patients unfit for general anesthesia).
>   - **Laparoscopic / Robotic Totally Extraperitoneal (TEP) or Transabdominal Preperitoneal (TAPP) Mesh Repair:** **Preferred first-line approach for:** (1) **Bilateral inguinal hernias**, (2) **Recurrent inguinal hernias** after a prior open anterior repair (approaches through virgin posterior planes!), and (3) **All groin hernias in Women** (visualizes and covers the femoral canal); offers less postoperative pain and faster return to work.
>   - **Primary Tissue Repairs (No Mesh — Only if Mesh is Contraindicated/Contaminated):** **Shouldice** (4-layer imbricated transversalis fascia; lowest recurrence among non-mesh repairs), **Bassini** (conjoint tendon sutured to **inguinal ligament**), or **McVay** (conjoint tendon sutured to **Cooper's [pectineal] ligament** $\rightarrow$ **the ONLY tissue repair that closes the Femoral Canal!**).
>
> **Management of Ventral (Umbilical & Incisional) & Hiatal Hernias**
>
> - **Adult Umbilical & Incisional Ventral Hernias:**
>   - **Optimize Modifiable Risk Factors Preoperatively ("Prehabilitation"):** Mandate **smoking cessation ≥4 weeks**, **glycemic control (HbA1c <7.0–7.5%)**, and **weight loss to BMI <30–35** before elective incisional hernia repair to prevent wound breakdown and recurrence!
>   - **Defect <1–2 cm:** Primary suture repair or small mesh patch.
>   - **Defect ≥1–2 cm:** **Mandatory Tension-Free Mesh Repair** (Sublay/Retrorectus [**Rives-Stoppa**] or Laparoscopic/Robotic IPOM/eTEP has the lowest recurrence rate; giant defects >10 cm may require **Component Separation [Transversus Abdominis Release / TAR]**).
>   - _Umbilical Hernia in Decompensated Cirrhosis with Ascites ("Flood Syndrome"):_ **Aggressively control ascites FIRST** (sodium restriction, diuretics, paracentesis, or **TIPS**) _before_ elective repair, because uncontrolled ascites blows out the repair and causes lethal **spontaneous rupture and leaking of ascitic fluid ("Flood syndrome")**!
> - **Hiatal Hernias (SAGES Guidelines):**
>   - **Type I (Sliding Hiatal Hernia):** **Manage Medically with PPIs** (refer to **[[Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease]]**); surgery is NOT indicated unless performing anti-reflux fundoplication for refractory GERD.
>   - **Type II, III, & IV (Paraesophageal Hiatal Hernias):**
>     - _Asymptomatic (especially in elderly/frail):_ Watchful waiting is safe (risk of acute volvulus is ~1%/year).
>     - _Symptomatic (dysphagia, postprandial chest pain, early satiety, regurgitation, or **Cameron Ulcers** [linear gastric mucosal erosions at the diaphragmatic pinch causing chronic **Iron-Deficiency Anemia**]):_ **Perform Laparoscopic Paraesophageal Hernia Repair** (reduction of stomach into abdomen, excision of hernia sac, **primary crural closure**, and **Nissen or Toupet Fundoplication**).

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Attempting Bedside Manual Reduction (Taxis) of a Strangulated Hernia**, as forcefully pushing necrotic or perforated bowel back into the peritoneal cavity (**"Reduction en Masse"**) masks the external bulge while causing **rapidly fatal, undiagnosed generalized feculent peritonitis**!
> - **Avoid Placing Permanent Synthetic Polypropylene Mesh in a Grossly Contaminated Surgical Field** (frank bowel necrosis/resection, enterotomy, or abscess) to prevent chronic **mesh infection, enterocutaneous fistula, and mandatory mesh explantation**.
> - **Avoid Using a Bassini or Lichtenstein Repair alone for a Femoral Hernia**, as sewing to the inguinal ligament does NOT close the femoral ring inferior to it—must use a **preperitoneal mesh (TEP/TAPP), mesh plug, or McVay (Cooper's ligament) repair** (_When incising the medial **Lacunar Ligament** to release a strangulated femoral hernia, beware of the **"Corona Mortis"**—an aberrant anastomotic artery between the obturator and external iliac/inferior epigastric systems present in 25% of patients that causes exsanguinating retroperitoneal hemorrhage if cut!_).
> - **Avoid Operating on Diastasis Recti**, as it is a cosmetic thinning of the linea alba with **zero fascial defect and zero risk of strangulation**.
> - **Avoid Operating Electively on an Umbilical Hernia in a Child <4 Years Old** (unless incarcerated or >2 cm), as >90% close spontaneously.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Chronic Post-Herniorrhaphy Inguinodynia (Nerve Entrapment — ~5–10%):** Severe neuropathic pain or numbness >3 months after surgery due to suture/tack/mesh entrapment of 1 of 3 nerves:
>   1. **Ilioinguinal Nerve (Most common in Open repair):** Pain/paresthesia at the **base of the penis/mons pubis, anterior scrotum/labia majora, and superomedial thigh**.
>   2. **Iliohypogastric Nerve:** Suprapubic pain.
>   3. **Genitofemoral Nerve (Genital branch) & Lateral Femoral Cutaneous Nerve (Most common in Laparoscopic repair — "Triangle of Pain"):** Scrotal/labial pain + loss of cremasteric reflex (genitofemoral) or anterolateral thigh pain (**meralgia paresthetica**).
>   - _Note on Laparoscopic **"Triangle of Doom"**:_ Bounded by the **vas deferens** medially and **gonadal (spermatic) vessels** laterally, containing the **External Iliac Artery and Vein** $\rightarrow$ **NEVER place tacks or staples in the Triangle of Doom or Triangle of Pain!**
> - **Ischemic Orchitis & Testicular Atrophy:** Thrombosis of the delicate **pampiniform venous plexus** inside the spermatic cord from excessive dissection of a large indirect inguinoscrotal hernia sac $\rightarrow$ presents 2–5 days post-op with a **swollen, hard, exquisitely painful testicle and low-grade fever** $\rightarrow$ **Manage Conservatively with NSAIDs, ice, and scrotal support (do NOT re-explore surgically unless Doppler US shows arterial testicular torsion**, as re-exploration worsens testicular loss; ~50% progress to testicular atrophy over months!).
> - **Postoperative Seroma or Hematoma:** Common benign fluid collection in the dead space of a large hernia sac $\rightarrow$ resolves spontaneously over weeks (**do NOT aspirate a seroma with a needle**, as aspiration introduces skin flora and infects the underlying mesh!).

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> **Scenario 1 (Strangulated Femoral Hernia):** A 76-year-old woman presents with 10 hours of severe, constant right groin pain, bilious vomiting, and obstipation. Vitals show T 38.3°C and HR 110 bpm; exam reveals a 3-cm, rock-hard, irreducible, **erythematous and exquisitely tender mass located inferior to the right inguinal ligament** and lateral to the pubic tubercle. Labs show WBC 17,400/µL and lactate 3.1 mmol/L. Recognizing a **Strangulated Right Femoral Hernia**, the team **avoids any attempt at bedside manual reduction**, starts IV fluids and **IV Ceftriaxone + Metronidazole**, and takes her **immediately to the Operating Room**, where a loop of necrotic ileum is resected with primary anastomosis and the femoral ring is closed via a **McVay (Cooper's ligament) tissue repair without synthetic mesh**.
> **Scenario 2 (Elective Male Inguinal Hernia):** A 58-year-old man presents with a 4-month history of an aching right groin bulge that appears when lifting boxes at work and disappears when lying down. Exam while standing confirms a reducible right inguinal hernia above the inguinal ligament. He undergoes an elective **Open Tension-Free Lichtenstein Polypropylene Mesh Repair** under local anesthesia with sedation and is discharged home the same day.
