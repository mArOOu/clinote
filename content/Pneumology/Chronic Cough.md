> [!abstract] Chronic Cough
> A daily cough lasting **>8 weeks in adults** (or **>4 weeks in children**). In non-smokers with a normal Chest X-Ray who are not taking an ACE inhibitor, >90% of cases are caused by four conditions: **Upper Airway Cough Syndrome (UACS)**, **Asthma (Cough-Variant)**, **Non-Asthmatic Eosinophilic Bronchitis (NAEB)**, and **GERD**.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - Stimulation of vagal afferent C-fibers and mechanoreceptors in the larynx, tracheobronchial tree, esophagus, or upper airway (mediated via TRPV1/TRPA1 and ATP-gated P2X3 channels) triggers the brainstem cough center.
> - **Cough Hypersensitivity Syndrome:** Neurogenic inflammation sensitizes peripheral and central cough pathways, causing coughing in response to innocuous stimuli (**hypertussivity**, e.g., cold air, perfumes) or non-tussive stimuli (**allotussia**, e.g., talking, laughing).

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Step 1 Before Any Workup:** **Stop ACE Inhibitors** (causes dry cough in up to 20% via bradykinin/substance P accumulation; can start weeks to _months/years_ after initiation and takes **1–4 weeks [up to 3 months] to resolve**) and **stop smoking**.
> - **The "Big 4" in Immunocompetent Non-Smokers with Normal CXR:** **UACS (Post-Nasal Drip)** is #1, followed by **Cough-Variant Asthma**, **NAEB**, and **GERD**—note that **25–50% of patients have $\ge 2$ simultaneous causes**, so add therapies sequentially rather than stopping a partially effective drug.
> - **NAEB vs. Cough-Variant Asthma:** Both have eosinophilic airway inflammation (elevated FeNO / sputum eosinophils >3%) and **respond to Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS)**, but **NAEB has normal spirometry and a NEGATIVE methacholine challenge** (no bronchial hyperresponsiveness).
> - **Do NOT prescribe empiric PPIs for chronic cough WITHOUT classic reflux symptoms (heartburn/regurgitation):** CHEST/ERS guidelines strongly advise against empiric PPIs for isolated cough without peptic symptoms; use anti-reflux lifestyle measures first.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Duration >8 weeks**; clarify whether **dry/non-productive** (ACEi, asthma, NAEB, GERD, ILD) vs. **productive/purulent** (chronic bronchitis, bronchiectasis, chronic rhinosinusitis, TB).
> - **Specific Etiologic Clues:**
>   - **UACS (Post-Nasal Drip):** Sensation of liquid dripping into the back of the throat, frequent **throat clearing**, nasal congestion, or rhinorrhea (though up to 20% are "silent" without awareness of drip).
>   - **Cough-Variant Asthma:** Nocturnal cough, triggered by exercise, cold air, or allergens; atopic history.
>   - **GERD / Laryngopharyngeal Reflux (LPR):** Heartburn, sour regurgitation, dysphonia, cough worse after meals or when lying supine.
>   - **Post-Infectious (_Bordetella pertussis_):** Paroxysmal coughing fits, inspiratory "whoop", or **post-tussive emesis** (usually resolves by 8 weeks, but can persist longer).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **HEENT:** **"Cobblestone" appearance of the posterior oropharyngeal mucosa** or visible mucopurulent secretions draining down the posterior pharynx (UACS); boggy pale nasal turbinates or nasal polyps; sinus tenderness.
> - **Pulmonary:** Usually **normal** in UACS, Cough-Variant Asthma, NAEB, and GERD; look for **wheezing** (asthma/COPD), **coarse crackles/rhonchi** (bronchiectasis), or **fine bibasilar "Velcro" crackles + digital clubbing** (Interstitial Lung Disease / IPF).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Mandatory Clinical Thresholds:**
>   - **Adult Chronic Cough:** Cough duration **>8 weeks** (Subacute = 3–8 weeks; Acute = <3 weeks).
>   - **Pediatric Chronic Cough:** Cough duration **>4 weeks**.
>   - **Unexplained / Refractory Chronic Cough (UCC / RCC):** Chronic cough >8 weeks that persists despite comprehensive guideline-based investigation and supervised therapeutic trials for UACS, asthma, NAEB, and GERD.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Initial Mandatory Workup (All Patients)**
>
> - **Chest X-Ray (PA & Lateral):** **Mandatory in EVERY patient** with chronic cough to rule out lung cancer, tuberculosis, sarcoidosis, bronchiectasis, or interstitial lung disease.
> - **Spirometry (Pre- and Post-Bronchodilator):** Mandatory to evaluate for asthma (reversible FEV1/FVC <0.70) or COPD (irreversible FEV1/FVC <0.70).
>
> **Second-Tier / Confirmatory Investigations (Normal CXR & Normal Spirometry)**
>
> - **Fractional Exhaled Nitric Oxide (FeNO) or Blood/Induced Sputum Eosinophils:** **FeNO $\ge 25-40$ ppb** or **sputum eosinophils $\ge 3\%$** (blood eosinophils $\ge 150-300/\mu$L) identifies steroid-responsive eosinophilic airway inflammation (Cough-Variant Asthma or NAEB).
> - **Methacholine Bronchoprovocation Challenge:** Order if spirometry is normal to confirm **Cough-Variant Asthma** ($\ge 20\%$ fall in FEV1; negative in NAEB).
> - **Sinus CT or Nasopharyngoscopy:** Order _only_ if UACS fails empiric intranasal therapy or if chronic rhinosinusitis/nasal polyps are suspected.
> - **24-Hour Ambulatory Esophageal pH-Impedance Monitoring:** Order if GERD cough is suspected in the absence of heartburn or if refractory to an 8-week PPI + lifestyle trial.
> - **High-Resolution CT Chest (HRCT) & Bronchoscopy:** Reserve for patients with **red flags**, abnormal CXR, or cough failing all sequential empiric trials (to exclude occult bronchiectasis, early ILD, endobronchial carcinoid/tumor, or retained foreign body).

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Hemoptysis** $\rightarrow$ Urgent CT Chest and bronchoscopy (rule out bronchogenic carcinoma, TB, bronchiectasis).
> - **Smoker >45 years old with a new cough or change in baseline cough** $\rightarrow$ High suspicion for **lung cancer**; order CT Chest even if CXR is normal.
> - **Unexplained weight loss, drenching night sweats, or fevers** $\rightarrow$ Rule out **Tuberculosis**, lymphoma, or lung malignancy.
> - **Hoarseness/dysphonia (>2–4 weeks) or dysphagia** $\rightarrow$ Laryngoscopy/endoscopy to rule out laryngeal or esophageal carcinoma.
> - **Copious daily purulent sputum or recurrent pneumonia** $\rightarrow$ Order HRCT to evaluate for **bronchiectasis** or immunodeficiency.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Bronchogenic Carcinoma:** Smoker, weight loss, hemoptysis, focal wheeze or mass.
> - **Pulmonary Tuberculosis:** Endemic exposure, night sweats, weight loss, apical cavitary infiltrate.
> - **Left Ventricular Heart Failure:** Nocturnal cough with pink frothy sputum, orthopnea, elevated BNP, cardiomegaly.
> - **Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (IPF) / ILD:** Progressive exertional dyspnea, dry hacking cough, bibasilar Velcro crackles, clubbing.
>
> **Most Likely ("The Big 4" + ACEi + Smoking)**
>
> - **Upper Airway Cough Syndrome (UACS / Post-Nasal Drip)** (Most common overall)
> - **Asthma / Cough-Variant Asthma**
> - **Non-Asthmatic Eosinophilic Bronchitis (NAEB)**
> - **Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD) / LPR**
> - **ACE Inhibitor Use or Chronic Smoking Bronchitis**
>
> **Most Surprising**
>
> - **Arnold's Nerve Ear-Cough Reflex:** Chronic dry cough triggered by cerumen impaction or hair irritating the auricular branch of the vagus nerve in the external auditory canal (always do an otoscopic exam!).
>
> **Less Common**
>
> - **Bronchiectasis**
> - **Somatic Cough Syndrome (Habit / Tic Cough):** "Honking" cough that **completely disappears during sleep**.
>

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Complete **resolution of cough** (or significant reduction in cough frequency/severity via Leicester Cough Questionnaire) by systematically eliminating underlying triggers.
> - **Secondary Target:** Early identification of sinister thoracic pathology (malignancy, TB, ILD) and avoidance of inappropriate antibiotic or opioid use.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **N/A — Outpatient condition** (If presenting with massive hemoptysis, stridor, or acute hypoxia, stabilize airway immediately; see [[Massive Hemoptysis]]).
>
> **Step 1: Elimination of Exogenous Triggers (First-Line in All Patients)**
>
> - **Discontinue ACE Inhibitors immediately (switch to an ARB, e.g., Losartan or Valsartan)** in any patient with chronic cough regardless of when the ACEi was started; allow **4 weeks (up to 12 weeks)** for resolution.
> - **Enforce Smoking Cessation** in active smokers; cough improves in >90% within **4 weeks** of quitting.
>
> **Step 2: Sequential & Additive Empiric Trials for "The Big 4" (Normal CXR)**
> _(Note: Because multiple causes frequently coexist, **keep partially effective therapies on board** while adding the next step)._
>
> - **Treat Upper Airway Cough Syndrome (UACS) First (2–4 Week Trial):**
>   - **Allergic Rhinitis:** **Intranasal Corticosteroid (Fluticasone 1–2 sprays/nostril daily)** +/- 2nd-generation antihistamine (Cetirizine/Fexofenadine) or intranasal Azelastine.
>   - **Non-Allergic Rhinitis / Unknown Post-Nasal Drip:** **1st-Generation Antihistamine + Decongestant (e.g., Chlorpheniramine or Brompheniramine + Pseudoephedrine)** for 2–4 weeks (_1st-gen antihistamines work via central anticholinergic drying effects; 2nd-gen non-sedating antihistamines are ineffective for non-allergic cough_).
> - **Treat Cough-Variant Asthma & NAEB Second (2–4 Week Trial):**
>   - **Initiate an Inhaled Corticosteroid (ICS; e.g., Budesonide or Fluticasone BID) or Low-Dose ICS-Formoterol** for 2–4 weeks (both CVA and NAEB respond robustly to ICS; add **Montelukast 10 mg daily** if incomplete response).
> - **Treat Reflux-Related Cough Third (8–12 Week Trial):**
>   - **Implement Diet & Lifestyle Modifications** (avoid meals 3 hours before bedtime, elevate head of bed, weight loss) in **all** suspected reflux-cough patients.
>   - **Add a Proton Pump Inhibitor (Omeprazole 20–40 mg PO BID 30 min before meals)** **ONLY if the patient has concomitant peptic symptoms (heartburn/regurgitation)** or documented acid reflux on pH testing; requires **8–12 weeks** for cough resolution.
>
> **Step 3: Refractory / Unexplained Chronic Cough (Neuromodulation)**
>
> - **Refer for Multimodal Speech Pathology / Behavioral Cough Suppression Therapy** as a safe, effective first-line non-pharmacologic intervention for Refractory Chronic Cough.
> - **Initiate a Central Neuromodulator: Low-Dose Gabapentin (start 300 mg/day, titrate up to 600–900 mg BID as tolerated) OR Pregabalin (75–150 mg BID)** to suppress central cough reflex hypersensitivity (reassess risk/benefit at 6 months).
> - **Consider Low-Dose Slow-Release Morphine (5–10 mg PO BID)** or **Amitriptyline (10 mg PO QHS)** in severe refractory cough unresponsive to gabapentinoids.
>
> **Follow-up**
>
> - **Reassess at 2–4 week intervals** during sequential UACS and ICS trials (and at 8–12 weeks for GERD trials); if no response occurs after completing all trials, order **HRCT Chest** and refer to a **Pulmonologist / Cough Clinic**.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid using 2nd-Generation (Non-Sedating) Antihistamines (Loratadine, Cetirizine) for non-allergic post-nasal drip cough**; they lack the anticholinergic activity required to dry non-histamine-mediated upper airway secretions.
> - **Avoid Empiric Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs) in chronic cough patients who lack heartburn, regurgitation, or objective reflux testing**; randomized trials show no benefit over placebo for non-acid/weakly acidic reflux cough.
> - **Avoid Routine Antibiotics** in chronic cough unless there is clear evidence of protracted bacterial bronchitis (in children), acute bacterial rhinosinusitis, or bronchiectasis exacerbation.
> - **Avoid Codeine and Dextromethorphan in Children** (and avoid long-term codeine in adults due to variable CYP2D6 metabolism, sedation, constipation, and addiction risk).
> - **Avoid Oral Decongestants (Pseudoephedrine)** in patients with uncontrolled hypertension, coronary artery disease, arrhythmias, or benign prostatic hyperplasia (use intranasal ipratropium or intranasal steroids instead).

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Physical Complications of Severe Paroxysms:**
>   - **Cough Syncope:** Transient loss of consciousness due to extreme intrathoracic pressure (>300 mmHg) impeding venous return and cardiac output.
>   - **Musculoskeletal / Thoracic:** **Cough-induced rib fractures** (typically lateral 5th–9th ribs), intercostal muscle strain, abdominal wall hernia, or pneumomediastinum/pneumothorax.
>   - **Genitourinary / Vascular:** **Stress urinary incontinence** (extremely common in women, driving social isolation), subconjunctival hemorrhage, and facial petechiae.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 48-year-old non-smoking female with hypertension (managed on amlodipine) presents with a 10-week history of an irritating dry cough that is worse at night and accompanied by frequent throat clearing; she denies hemoptysis, weight loss, or heartburn. Physical exam reveals a cobblestone posterior pharynx and clear lungs; Chest X-Ray and pre-/post-bronchodilator spirometry are completely normal. Diagnosing likely Upper Airway Cough Syndrome (UACS), you initiate a 3-week empiric trial of a **first-generation antihistamine/decongestant plus intranasal fluticasone**, planning to add an **inhaled corticosteroid (ICS)** or perform FeNO/methacholine testing for Cough-Variant Asthma/NAEB at her 3-week follow-up if the cough only partially improves.
