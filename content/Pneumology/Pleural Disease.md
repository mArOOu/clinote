> [!abstract] Pleural Disease
> Pathologic processes affecting the pleural membranes and space, most commonly manifesting as a **Pleural Effusion** (abnormal accumulation of fluid >15–20 mL in the pleural cavity) or **Pleuritis** (pleural inflammation), classified mechanistically into **Transudates** (systemic hydrostatic/oncotic imbalance) and **Exudates** (local pleural/capillary permeability increase or impaired lymphatic drainage).

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Transudate:** Intact capillary membranes; fluid driven by **increased hydrostatic pressure** (CHF, hepatic hydrothorax) or **decreased oncotic pressure** (cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome).
> - **Exudate:** Damaged/leaky local pleural capillaries or blocked lymphatics due to **infection** (pneumonia/TB), **malignancy**, **pulmonary embolism**, or **autoimmune inflammation** (RA, SLE).

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Diuretic Pitfall in CHF:** Diuresis concentrates pleural protein and LDH, falsely converting ~25% of CHF transudates into "pseudo-exudates" by Light's criteria—calculate the **Serum-to-Pleural Albumin Gradient (>1.2 g/dL)** or **Serum-to-Pleural Protein Gradient (>3.1 g/dL)** or check **pleural NT-proBNP (>1,500 pg/mL)** to confirm a true transudate.
> - **When to Place a Chest Tube IMMEDIATELY (Complicated Parapneumonic Effusion / Empyema):** Any parapneumonic effusion with **frank pus**, **positive Gram stain/culture**, **pleural pH <7.20**, **pleural glucose <40–60 mg/dL**, **pleural LDH >1,000 IU/L**, or **loculations** CANNOT be cured with antibiotics alone and requires immediate tube thoracostomy.
> - **Limit Therapeutic Thoracentesis to $\le 1.5$ Liters at one time** (or stop immediately if chest tightness/cough develops) to prevent **Re-expansion Pulmonary Edema (RPE)**.
> - **Always use Thoracic Ultrasound (POCUS) guidance** for thoracentesis—it increases success rates and cuts iatrogenic pneumothorax rates from ~10% down to <1%.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Progressive dyspnea** (proportional to effusion size and underlying lung reserve), dry non-productive cough, and **pleuritic chest pain** (sharp, worse with inspiration/cough, sometimes radiating to the **ipsilateral shoulder/neck** via phrenic nerve irritation of the diaphragmatic pleura).
> - **Etiologic clues:** Orthopnea/edema (CHF), fever/purulent sputum (parapneumonic/empyema), weight loss/smoking/prior cancer (malignancy), asbestos exposure (mesothelioma/benign asbestos effusion), or recent cardiac surgery/MI (**Dressler syndrome**).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Classic Triad of Pleural Effusion (>300 mL required to detect clinically):**
>   1. **Stony dullness to percussion** over the fluid collection.
>   2. **Decreased or absent breath sounds** over the effusion.
>   3. **Decreased or absent tactile fremitus** and vocal resonance (_distinguishes effusion from lobar consolidation, where fremitus is increased_).
> - **Egophony ("E-to-A" change)** and bronchial breath sounds may be heard strictly at the **superior border** of the effusion (due to compressive atelectasis of the adjacent lung).
> - **Pleural friction rub** (coarse, leathery grating sound on inspiration and expiration) in dry pleuritis; **contralateral tracheal shift** in massive effusion (>1.5–2 L).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Light's Criteria (Diagnostic of an EXUDATE if $\ge 1$ of the following 3 criteria is met; sensitivity 98%):**
>   1. **Pleural fluid Protein / Serum Protein ratio >0.5**
>   2. **Pleural fluid LDH / Serum LDH ratio >0.6**
>   3. **Pleural fluid LDH > $\frac{2}{3}$ (0.67) of the upper limit of normal for Serum LDH**
>      _(If all 3 are negative, the effusion is a **Transudate**)._
> - **Specific Diagnostic Pleural Fluid Cutoffs:**
>   - **Empyema / Complicated Parapneumonic:** Frank pus, positive Gram stain/culture, OR **pH <7.20** (or **Glucose <40–60 mg/dL** / **LDH >1,000 IU/L**).
>   - **Hemothorax:** Pleural fluid **Hematocrit >50% of peripheral blood Hematocrit**.
>   - **Chylothorax** (thoracic duct disruption—lymphoma, trauma/surgery): Milky fluid with **Triglycerides >110 mg/dL** (or presence of chylomicrons).
>   - **Urinothorax** (obstructive uropathy—Only _Transudate_ with pH <7.30): **Pleural fluid Creatinine / Serum Creatinine ratio >1.0**.
>   - **Esophageal Rupture / Pancreatitis:** Elevated **Pleural Amylase** (salivary vs. pancreatic isoform) with very low pH (<7.0 in Boerhaave).

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Indications for Diagnostic Thoracentesis**
>
> - Perform ultrasound-guided diagnostic thoracentesis for **any new, unexplained pleural effusion $\ge 1$ cm thickness** on lateral decubitus CXR, CT, or ultrasound.
> - **When Thoracentesis is NOT required:** Classic **bilateral symmetric effusions in decompensated CHF** without fever, pleuritic chest pain, or marked asymmetry (treat with diuretics first; tap _only_ if unilateral/markedly asymmetric, febrile, pleuritic pain, or fails to resolve after 48–72h of diuresis).
>
> **Pleural Fluid Analysis Panel**
>
> - **Routine (All Taps):** **Total Protein, LDH, pH** (run immediately in blood gas analyzer), **Glucose, Cell Count & Differential**, **Gram Stain & Aerobic/Anaerobic Cultures** (inoculate directly into blood culture bottles), and **Cytology**.
> - **High-Yield Fluid Clues:**
>   - **Neutrophil predominance (>50%):** Acute process (parapneumonic, PE, acute pancreatitis).
>   - **Lymphocyte predominance (>50%):** Chronic process (**Malignancy, Tuberculosis**, lymphoma, chylothorax, rheumatoid pleuritis).
>   - **Very low Glucose (<30 mg/dL) & pH (<7.20):** **Empyema**, **Rheumatoid Effusion**, malignancy, TB, or esophageal rupture.
>   - **Adenosine Deaminase (ADA >40 U/L) + Interferon-gamma / AFB PCR:** Confirms **Tuberculous Pleuritis** in lymphocytic exudates.
>
> **Imaging**
>
> - **Chest X-Ray (Upright PA & Lateral):** Blunting of the lateral costophrenic angle requires **~200 mL** on PA view (**~50–75 mL** on lateral view); classic **meniscus sign**.
> - **Thoracic Ultrasound (POCUS):** Detects as little as **5–20 mL** of fluid, identifies **septations/loculations** (complex septated = exudate/empyema), and guides safe needle placement.
> - **Contrast-Enhanced CT Chest (Pleural Phase):** Order in all undiagnosed exudates; nodular, mediastinal, or circumferential pleural thickening (>1 cm) strongly suggests **malignancy or mesothelioma**.
>
> **Referrals**
>
> - **Pulmonology / Thoracic Surgery:** Undiagnosed lymphocytic exudate after 1–2 negative cytology taps (requires **Medical Thoracoscopy / VATS pleural biopsy**, which has >95% diagnostic yield for malignancy and TB), trapped lung, or loculated empyema.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Massive unilateral effusion with tracheal shift + hypotension/hypoxia** $\rightarrow$ **Tension Hydrothorax/Hemothorax**; requires urgent therapeutic drainage.
> - **Parapneumonic effusion with pH <7.20, glucose <60 mg/dL, or septations on POCUS** $\rightarrow$ **Complicated parapneumonic effusion / Empyema**; place chest tube immediately.
> - **Bloody effusion post-trauma or post-procedure with pleural Hct >50% serum Hct** $\rightarrow$ **Hemothorax**; place large-bore chest tube (28–32 Fr) and consult Thoracic Surgery.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss (Exudates)**
>
> - **Pulmonary Embolism:** Commonly overlooked cause of small, unilateral, often bloody exudative effusion with pleuritic chest pain.
> - **Empyema / Complicated Parapneumonic Effusion:** Persistent sepsis/fever despite pneumonia antibiotics.
> - **Malignant Pleural Effusion:** Lung cancer (#1), breast cancer (#2), lymphoma (#3), or malignant mesothelioma; often massive and recurrent.
> - **Esophageal Rupture (Boerhaave Syndrome):** Left-sided effusion with pH <7.0, high salivary amylase, and food particles.
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Transudates:** **Congestive Heart Failure (#1 cause of all effusions)**, Cirrhosis (**Hepatic Hydrothorax**—usually right-sided due to diaphragmatic defects), Nephrotic Syndrome.
> - **Exudates:** **Uncomplicated Parapneumonic Effusion (#1 cause of exudates)**, Malignancy, Pulmonary Embolism, Tuberculosis.
>
> **Most Surprising**
>
> - **Meigs Syndrome:** Benign ovarian fibroma + ascites + right-sided pleural effusion that resolves completely upon tumor excision.
>
> **Less Common**
>
> - **Autoimmune Pleuritis:** Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE) or Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA; classically low glucose <30 mg/dL, high LDH, high cholesterol crystals).
> - **Drug-Induced Effusion:** Dasatinib, amiodarone, nitrofurantoin, methotrexate, bromocriptine.
>
> **Non-Critical**
>
> - **Viral Pleuritis (Coxsackie B / Bornholm Disease):** Self-limited pleuritic pain with minimal or no effusion.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Rapidly relieve **dyspnea and hypoxemia (SpO2 >92%)**, achieve **complete pleural space drainage and source control** in infected effusions, and establish a definitive tissue/fluid diagnosis.
> - **Secondary Target:** Prevent effusion recurrence in malignant/chronic effusions and prevent fibrothorax or re-expansion pulmonary edema.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Perform Urgent Ultrasound-Guided Therapeutic Thoracentesis or Tube Thoracostomy** in any patient presenting with a **massive effusion causing acute respiratory distress, hypoxemia, or hemodynamic compromise (tension hydrothorax)**, removing up to **1.0–1.5 L initially** to restore ventilation and venous return.
>
> **Management of Transudative Effusions**
>
> - **Treat the Underlying Systemic Condition + Sodium Restriction + Diuretics:**
>   - **CHF:** **IV Loop Diuretics (Furosemide)** + GDMT optimization.
>   - **Hepatic Hydrothorax (Cirrhosis):** **Spironolactone + Furosemide (100:40 mg ratio)** + low-sodium diet; consider **TIPS (Transjugular Intrahepatic Portosystemic Shunt)** if refractory (_strictly avoid long-term chest tube placement_).
>
> **Management of Parapneumonic Effusion & Empyema**
>
> - **Treat Uncomplicated Parapneumonic Effusion** (small/free-flowing, clear fluid, **pH $\ge 7.20$, glucose $\ge 60$ mg/dL**, negative Gram stain) with **systemic CAP antibiotics alone** (no chest tube required unless enlarging).
> - **Insert a Small-Bore Chest Tube (12–14 Fr under ultrasound guidance) PLUS IV Antibiotics (covering anaerobes + strep, e.g., Ceftriaxone + Metronidazole or Piperacillin-Tazobactam for 2–6 weeks)** immediately for any **Complicated Parapneumonic Effusion (pH <7.20 or glucose <40–60 mg/dL) or Empyema (pus/positive culture)**.
> - **Instill Intrapleural Fibrinolytics + DNase (tPA 10 mg + Dornase alfa [DNase] 5 mg via chest tube BID for 3 days — MIST2 regimen)** in **loculated/septated infected effusions** failing simple tube drainage to lyse fibrin septations, decrease viscosity, and avoid surgery.
> - **Refer for Surgical VATS Decortication** if sepsis and residual pleural collection persist despite chest tube drainage and intrapleural tPA + DNase.
>
> **Management of Symptomatic Malignant Pleural Effusion (MPE)**
>
> - **Perform Ultrasound-Guided Therapeutic Thoracentesis (removing $\le 1.5$ L)** first to confirm that **dyspnea improves** after fluid removal and to verify on post-tap CXR that the **lung fully re-expands** (rules out a "trapped/non-expandable lung").
> - **Choose Definitive Palliative Prevention for Recurrent Symptomatic MPE:**
>   - **Expandable Lung:** Offer shared decision-making between **Indwelling Tunneled Pleural Catheter (IPC, e.g., PleurX)** (allows outpatient drainage every 1–2 days; achieves spontaneous autopleurodesis in ~45%) **OR Chemical Talc Pleurodesis** (via chest tube slurry or thoracoscopic poudrage).
>   - **Non-Expandable ("Trapped") Lung:** **Place an Indwelling Tunneled Pleural Catheter (IPC)** as the _only_ effective option (pleurodesis will fail because visceral and parietal pleura cannot appose).
>
> **Management of Hemothorax**
>
> - **Insert a Large-Bore Chest Tube (28–32 Fr)** immediately to evacuate blood and quantify ongoing hemorrhage.
> - **Perform Emergent Thoracotomy / VATS** if initial chest tube output is **$\ge 1,500$ mL immediately** OR ongoing bleeding is **>200 mL/hour for 2–4 consecutive hours**.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid draining >1.5 Liters of pleural fluid in a single thoracentesis** (or draining rapidly if pleural pressure drops below -20 cmH2O) to prevent potentially fatal **Re-expansion Pulmonary Edema (RPE)**.
> - **Avoid placing an Indwelling Chest Tube in Hepatic Hydrothorax (Cirrhosis)**; continuous chest tube drainage causes massive, life-threatening protein/electrolyte depletion, acute kidney injury, secondary bacterial peritonitis/empyema, and fistula formation.
> - **Avoid attempting Chemical Talc Pleurodesis in a patient with a "Trapped Lung"** (visceral pleural peel or endobronchial obstruction preventing lung expansion after thoracentesis); without visceral-parietal pleural apposition, pleurodesis is guaranteed to fail.
> - **Avoid inserting a trocar/chest tube along the inferior border of a rib**; **always insert directly OVER the SUPERIOR margin of the lower rib** to avoid lacerating the intercostal neurovascular bundle (VAN) running in the subcostal groove.
> - **Avoid Intrapleural tPA Monotherapy without DNase** in empyema; the landmark MIST2 trial proved that **tPA + DNase together** is required to clear loculated empyema, whereas tPA alone is ineffective.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Re-expansion Pulmonary Edema (RPE)** $\rightarrow$ Acute severe cough, chest tightness, tachypnea, and unilateral pulmonary edema within 1–24 hours of draining >1.5 L $\rightarrow$ Stop drainage immediately, administer supplemental O2 / CPAP, and provide hemodynamic support.
> - **Iatrogenic Pneumothorax or Hemothorax (Intercostal Artery Laceration)** $\rightarrow$ Post-thoracentesis dyspnea/chest pain $\rightarrow$ Always insert needle **above the rib** under direct **ultrasound guidance** (avoid midline within 6 cm of spine where intercostal artery is exposed).
> - **Fibrothorax ("Trapped Lung")** $\rightarrow$ Untreated empyema or hemothorax organizes into a thick fibrous visceral pleural peel that restricts lung expansion $\rightarrow$ Requires surgical **decortication**.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 62-year-old female hospitalized for right lower lobe pneumonia has persistent fevers (39.1°C), leukocytosis (WBC 19,200/$\mu$L), and worsening dyspnea after 72 hours of IV ceftriaxone and azithromycin; exam reveals stony dullness and absent breath sounds over the lower half of the right hemithorax. Bedside thoracic ultrasound demonstrates a moderate right pleural effusion with echogenic debris and thin septations, and ultrasound-guided diagnostic thoracentesis yields cloudy fluid with **pH 7.08, glucose 28 mg/dL, LDH 1,450 IU/L**, and 92% neutrophils. Diagnosing a **Complicated Parapneumonic Effusion / Empyema**, you immediately place an ultrasound-guided **14 Fr pigtail chest tube**, broaden IV antibiotics to include anaerobic coverage (ceftriaxone + metronidazole), and initiate **intrapleural tPA + DNase (MIST2 protocol)** for 3 days to ensure complete pleural space clearance.
