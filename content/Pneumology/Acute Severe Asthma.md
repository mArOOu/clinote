> [!abstract] Acute Severe Asthma
> Acute, severe, and potentially life-threatening worsening of asthma symptoms refractory to initial standard bronchodilator therapy, characterized by severe airflow obstruction and risk of hypercapnic respiratory failure.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - Trigger-induced airway inflammation causes acute bronchospasm, mucosal edema, and mucus plugging.
> - Incomplete exhalation leads to dynamic hyperinflation ("air trapping") and intrinsic PEEP (auto-PEEP), increasing work of breathing, causing V/Q mismatch, and eventually fatiguing respiratory muscles into hypercapnic failure.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **"Normal" PaCO2 (35–45 mmHg) is a critical red flag** in a tachypneic asthmatic, signaling impending respiratory muscle fatigue and imminent arrest.
> - **Silent chest** reflects severe airflow limitation with insufficient air movement to generate wheezes—prepare for immediate intubation.
> - **Ketamine** is the induction agent of choice for rapid sequence intubation (RSI) due to its bronchodilatory properties.
> - **Permissive hypercapnia** during mechanical ventilation (low RR, prolonged expiratory time) is vital to prevent lethal barotrauma and cardiovascular collapse from auto-PEEP.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - Rapidly progressive dyspnea, cough, and chest tightness unresponsive to rescue SABA.
> - **High-risk history for near-fatal asthma:** Prior intubation/ICU admission, $\ge 2$ hospitalizations or $\ge 3$ ED visits in the past year, SABA overuse (>1 canister/month), or recent systemic corticosteroid withdrawal.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Severe:** Inability to speak in full sentences (speaks in words/phrases), sitting upright/tripod position, accessory muscle use, RR >30/min, HR >120 bpm, diffuse expiratory/inspiratory wheezing, SpO2 <90%.
> - **Life-Threatening / Imminent Arrest:** Altered mental status (drowsiness, confusion, agitation), **silent chest**, paradoxical thoracoabdominal movement, bradycardia, or hemodynamic instability.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Acute Severe Asthma (Clinical Diagnosis + any of the following):**
>   - Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF) or FEV1 <50% of predicted or personal best (<25% indicates life-threatening).
>   - SpO2 <90% on room air.
>   - Inability to complete sentences in one breath, RR >30/min, or HR >120 bpm.
> - **Life-Threatening Asthma:** PEF <25% predicted, PaCO2 $\ge 42$ mmHg (normocapnia/hypercapnia), SpO2 <90% despite oxygen, silent chest, cyanosis, feeble respiratory effort, bradycardia, hypotension, or altered mental status.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **ABG / VBG:** Not routinely required for mild/moderate cases; order in severe/life-threatening exacerbations or non-responders. Early stage shows respiratory alkalosis (low PaCO2); **PaCO2 $\ge 40-42$ mmHg** indicates impending respiratory failure.
> - **Serum Potassium & Glucose:** Monitor during high-dose $\beta_2$-agonist and steroid therapy (risk of hypokalemia and hyperglycemia).
>
> **Imaging**
>
> - **Chest X-Ray (CXR):** Not routinely required; order _only_ if suspecting barotrauma (pneumothorax, pneumomediastinum), pneumonia, congestive heart failure, or if the patient fails to improve / requires intubation.
>
> **Referrals**
>
> - **ICU / Critical Care & Anesthesiology:** Immediate consult for patients with life-threatening features, worsening hypercapnia, or exhaustion.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Altered mental status / drowsiness** $\rightarrow$ hypercapnic narcosis; intubate immediately.
> - **Silent chest** with high work of breathing $\rightarrow$ critical airway obstruction.
> - **Normalizing or rising PaCO2 ($\ge 40$ mmHg)** $\rightarrow$ respiratory muscle fatigue.
> - **Bradycardia or hypotension** $\rightarrow$ imminent cardiopulmonary arrest or severe auto-PEEP/tension pneumothorax.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Anaphylaxis:** Stridor, urticaria/angioedema, hypotension, GI symptoms.
> - **Tension Pneumothorax:** Unilateral absent breath sounds, tracheal deviation, sudden hypotension.
> - **Acute Cardiogenic Pulmonary Edema:** Orthopnea, S3 gallop, bibasilar crackles, elevated BNP.
> - **Pulmonary Embolism:** Sudden onset pleuritic chest pain, risk factors, clear lungs.
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **COPD Exacerbation:** Older age, significant smoking history, chronic productive cough.
> - **Upper Airway Obstruction / Foreign Body:** Inspiratory stridor, sudden onset.
>
> **Most Surprising**
>
> - **Less Common**
>
> - **Vocal Cord Dysfunction (Inducible Laryngeal Obstruction):** Inspiratory stridor localized to neck, normal SpO2, rapid resolution.
>

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Maintain **SpO2 93–95%** (94–98% in pregnant patients or children) and achieve **PEF/FEV1 $\ge 60-80\%$** of predicted or personal best.
> - **Secondary Target:** Rapidly reverse bronchospasm, reduce airway inflammation, and prevent early relapse or mechanical ventilation.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Administer titrated supplemental O2** immediately via nasal cannula or mask if SpO2 <93% to maintain SpO2 93–95% without inducing hyperoxia-driven hypercapnia.
> - **Perform Rapid Sequence Intubation (RSI) using Ketamine (1–2 mg/kg IV)** immediately upon signs of respiratory arrest, coma, severe exhaustion, or rising hypercapnia despite maximal therapy to secure the airway and induce bronchodilation.
>
> **First-Line Targeted Therapy (First Hour)**
>
> - **Administer Inhaled SABA (Albuterol 2.5–5 mg nebulized or 4–10 puffs via MDI with spacer)** every 20 minutes for the first hour (or continuously at 10–15 mg/hr in critical cases) to rapidly reverse acute bronchospasm.
> - **Add Ipratropium Bromide (0.5 mg nebulized or 4–8 puffs via MDI)** every 20 minutes for 3 doses alongside SABA in all severe exacerbations to block vagally mediated bronchoconstriction and reduce hospitalization rates.
> - **Give Systemic Corticosteroids (Prednisone 40–60 mg PO or Methylprednisolone 40–80 mg IV)** within the first hour of presentation to downregulate airway inflammation and prevent relapse (PO is equally effective as IV if tolerated).
>
> **Stepwise Escalation & Rescue**
>
> - **Infuse IV Magnesium Sulfate (2 g IV over 20 minutes)** in patients with severe exacerbations refractory to initial SABA/ipratropium/steroids or with life-threatening features to promote bronchial smooth muscle relaxation via calcium channel blockade.
> - **Administer Epinephrine (0.3–0.5 mg IM of 1 mg/mL) or Terbutaline (0.25 mg SC)** if suspecting anaphylaxis/angioedema or if severe airflow limitation prevents adequate aerosol delivery.
> - **Apply Ventilator Strategy for Permissive Hypercapnia (Low RR 8–12/min, TV 6–8 mL/kg IBW, high inspiratory flow rate 80–100 L/min, I:E ratio 1:4–1:5, minimal PEEP)** immediately post-intubation to allow full exhalation and prevent lethal auto-PEEP and barotrauma.
>
> **Disposition & Follow-up**
>
> - **Admit to ICU** if the patient exhibits PEF <25%, worsening hypercapnia, altered mental status, silent chest, or no improvement after initial continuous bronchodilator and adjunct therapy.
> - **Admit to Medical Ward** if post-treatment (at 1–3 hours) PEF remains 40–60% of predicted with persistent moderate-to-severe symptoms or significant high-risk social/medical factors.
> - **Discharge home** when symptoms have resolved, supplemental O2 is no longer required, and PEF is >60–80% predicted, with a **5–7 day course of oral Prednisone (40–60 mg/day)**, initiation or step-up of an **ICS-containing controller (e.g., ICS-formoterol)**, and PCP follow-up within 2–7 days.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid sedatives/anxiolytics (e.g., benzodiazepines, opioids)** in non-intubated asthmatics to prevent blunting of respiratory drive and precipitating fatal respiratory arrest.
> - **Avoid routine IV aminophylline/theophylline** in acute asthma management due to severe toxicity (arrhythmias, seizures) and lack of efficacy over high-dose SABA.
> - **Avoid high-flow 100% oxygen without titration** in stable patients to prevent worsening V/Q mismatch and hypercapnia (target SpO2 93–95%).
> - **Avoid non-selective beta-blockers and NSAIDs/aspirin** in asthmatic patients to prevent precipitating refractory bronchospasm.
> - **Avoid routine antibiotics** unless there is clear clinical or radiographic evidence of concurrent bacterial pneumonia.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Dynamic Hyperinflation (Auto-PEEP) / Hypotension post-intubation** $\rightarrow$ Sudden drop in BP after starting positive pressure ventilation $\rightarrow$ **Disconnect patient from ventilator** for 30–60 seconds to allow passive exhalation, give IV fluid bolus, and decrease ventilator RR.
> - **Tension Pneumothorax** $\rightarrow$ Sudden hypoxia, hypotension, unilateral breath sounds, high peak inspiratory pressures $\rightarrow$ Immediate needle decompression / tube thoracostomy.
> - **Hypokalemia & Lactic Acidosis (Type B)** $\rightarrow$ Tachycardia, muscle weakness, elevated lactate despite normal perfusion (due to high-dose $\beta_2$-agonist stimulation) $\rightarrow$ Replete potassium; recognize Type B lactic acidosis does not require aggressive fluid resuscitation if euvolemic.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 24-year-old female with poorly controlled asthma presents to the ED in a tripod position, speaking in single words, with RR 34/min, HR 130 bpm, and SpO2 88% on room air. Auscultation reveals diffuse expiratory wheezing; after 1 hour of continuous nebulized albuterol, ipratropium, and IV methylprednisolone, she becomes drowsy with a quiet chest and a VBG showing pH 7.22 and PaCO2 52 mmHg. You immediately administer IV magnesium sulfate, prepare for RSI with ketamine, and configure the ventilator for low respiratory rate and prolonged expiratory time before transferring her to the ICU.
