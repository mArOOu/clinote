> [!abstract] Obstructive Sleep Apnea
> A chronic sleep-related breathing disorder characterized by recurrent partial (hypopnea) or complete (apnea) collapse of the upper airway during sleep despite ongoing inspiratory effort, resulting in intermittent hypoxemia, autonomic surges, and sleep fragmentation.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - Loss of pharyngeal dilator muscle tone (genioglossus) during sleep combined with anatomic narrowing (obesity/parapharyngeal fat pads, retrognathia, tonsillar hypertrophy) causes pharyngeal collapse when intraluminal pressure becomes negative during inspiration.
> - Apnea/hypopnea $\rightarrow$ hypoxia & hypercapnia $\rightarrow$ cortical micro-arousal & **sympathetic catecholamine surge** to restore airway patency $\rightarrow$ daytime somnolence, **resistant systemic hypertension (non-dipping nocturnal BP)**, atrial fibrillation, and hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction (**Group 3 Pulmonary Hypertension / Cor Pulmonale**).

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **OSA is the #1 identifiable secondary cause of Resistant Hypertension** and strongly predisposes to **recurrent Atrial Fibrillation** after cardioversion or catheter ablation.
> - **Home Sleep Apnea Testing (HSAT) CANNOT rule out OSA:** Because HSAT measures recording time rather than true EEG sleep time, it frequently underestimates the AHI; a **negative or inconclusive HSAT in a high-risk patient MANDATES an in-lab Polysomnography (PSG)**.
> - **Screen for Obesity Hypoventilation Syndrome (OHS / Pickwickian Syndrome)** in obese OSA patients (BMI >30): Check a serum **bicarbonate (HCO3- $\ge 27$ mEq/L)** and daytime **ABG (awake PaCO2 >45 mmHg)**.
> - **Tirzepatide (Zepbound; dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist)** is FDA-approved (2024/2025 SURMOUNT-OSA trial) as the **first pharmacotherapy for Moderate-to-Severe OSA in adults with obesity**, reducing AHI by ~50–60% via ~18–20% weight loss.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Nocturnal symptoms:** Loud, habitual **snoring**, **witnessed apneas** (bed partner reports silence followed by a loud snort), **nocturnal gasping/choking**, restless sleep, and nocturia (due to ANP release from right atrial stretch).
> - **Daytime symptoms:** **Excessive Daytime Sleepiness (EDS; Epworth Sleepiness Scale $\ge 10$)**, non-restorative sleep, **morning headaches** (hypercapnic cerebral vasodilation), poor concentration, and drowsy driving/motor vehicle accidents.
> - **Risk factors (STOP-BANG):** **S**noring, **T**iredness, **O**bserved apnea, high **B**P, **B**MI >35 kg/m$^2$, **A**ge >50, **N**eck circumference >40 cm (>17 in men, >16 in women), **G**ender = Male (or postmenopausal female). Score **$\ge 3$** = high risk.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - Obesity (BMI $\ge 30$ kg/m$^2$), large neck circumference, and systemic hypertension.
> - **Crowded Oropharynx:** **Mallampati Score III or IV**, macroglossia (scalloped lateral tongue borders), tonsillar hypertrophy (especially in children), retrognathia/micrognathia, high-arched narrow hard palate, or nasal septal deviation.
> - Signs of **Pulmonary Hypertension / Cor Pulmonale** (loud P2, JVD, lower extremity edema) in severe longstanding cases.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Mandatory Polysomnography (PSG) or Home Sleep Apnea Test (HSAT) Criteria (Either 1 OR 2 Required):**
>   1. **Apnea-Hypopnea Index (AHI) or Respiratory Event Index (REI) $\ge 5$ events/hour** (predominantly obstructive—continued thoracoabdominal effort during absent/reduced airflow) **PLUS $\ge 1$ Clinical Feature:**
>      - Excessive daytime sleepiness, non-restorative sleep, fatigue, or insomnia; OR
>      - Waking with breath-holding, gasping, or choking; OR
>      - Habitual snoring, breathing interruptions, or both noted by a bed partner; OR
>      - **Comorbid condition:** Hypertension, mood disorder, cognitive dysfunction, coronary artery disease, stroke, congestive heart failure, atrial fibrillation, or type 2 diabetes.
>   2. **AHI or REI $\ge 15$ events/hour** of predominantly obstructive events, **EVEN IN THE ABSENCE of any symptoms or comorbidities**.
> - **Adult OSA Severity Thresholds (by AHI):**
>   - **Mild:** **5–14** events/hour
>   - **Moderate:** **15–29** events/hour
>   - **Severe:** **$\ge 30$** events/hour
>   - _(Pediatric threshold: **AHI $\ge 1$ event/hour** is diagnostic of pediatric OSA)._

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **BMP (Serum Bicarbonate / Venous CO2):** If **HCO3- $\ge 27$ mEq/L** in a patient with BMI >30 kg/m$^2$, order a **daytime room-air ABG** to diagnose concurrent **Obesity Hypoventilation Syndrome (PaCO2 >45 mmHg)**.
> - **TSH:** Order only if clinical signs of **hypothyroidism** are present (myxedema causes macroglossia and upper airway narrowing).
>
> **Sleep Testing Selection (PSG vs. HSAT)**
>
> - **Home Sleep Apnea Test (HSAT):** First-line convenience test **ONLY for uncomplicated adults with high pretest probability of Moderate-to-Severe OSA**.
> - **In-Laboratory Attended Polysomnography (PSG — Gold Standard):** **MANDATORY** (HSAT is contraindicated) if the patient has:
>   - **Cardiopulmonary or Neuromuscular Comorbidities:** **HFrEF/HFpEF, moderate-to-severe COPD, Obesity Hypoventilation Syndrome, chronic opioid use, neuromuscular disease, or history of stroke** (high risk of Central Sleep Apnea or nocturnal hypoventilation).
>   - Suspected non-respiratory sleep disorder (narcolepsy, parasomnias, severe insomnia).
>   - **Negative or technically inadequate HSAT** despite persistent clinical suspicion.
>
> **Referrals**
>
> - **Sleep Medicine:** All patients failing CPAP adherence, complex cardiopulmonary comorbidities, or central sleep apnea.
> - **ENT / Maxillofacial Surgery:** Pediatric OSA (for **adenotonsillectomy — first-line in children**), marked tonsillar hypertrophy, or candidates for **Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation** or **Maxillomandibular Advancement**.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Drowsy driving / falling asleep at the wheel or in safety-critical occupations (commercial truck drivers, pilots)** $\rightarrow$ Urgent diagnostic evaluation, immediate CPAP initiation, and counseling against driving while sleepy.
> - **Daytime hypercapnia (PaCO2 >45 mmHg) + BMI >30 + daytime SpO2 <94%** $\rightarrow$ **Obesity Hypoventilation Syndrome (OHS)**; significantly higher mortality and risk of acute-on-chronic hypercapnic respiratory failure.
> - **High burden of Central Apneas (absent thoracoabdominal effort) with crescendo-decrescendo Cheyne-Stokes breathing** $\rightarrow$ Suspect underlying **HFrEF, stroke, or chronic opioid toxicity**.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Obesity Hypoventilation Syndrome (OHS):** BMI >30, daytime awake hypercapnia (PaCO2 >45 mmHg), elevated HCO3- ($\ge 27$ mEq/L), cor pulmonale.
> - **Central Sleep Apnea (CSA) / Cheyne-Stokes Respiration:** Absent inspiratory effort during apneic episodes on PSG; associated with HFrEF, stroke, or opioids.
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Insufficient Sleep Syndrome / Shift Work Disorder:** Most common cause of excessive daytime sleepiness; resolves with sleep extension.
> - **Primary Snoring:** Habitual snoring without apneas, hypopneas, desaturations, or daytime somnolence (AHI <5).
>
> **Most Surprising**
>
> - **Hypothyroidism or Acromegaly:** Endocrine causes of macroglossia and pharyngeal soft-tissue thickening presenting as OSA.
>
> **Less Common**
>
> - **Narcolepsy:** Daytime sleep attacks, **cataplexy** (sudden emotion-triggered loss of muscle tone), hypnagogic hallucinations, sleep paralysis.
> - **Nocturnal Panic Attacks / Nocturnal Asthma / GERD:** Awakenings with choking/dyspnea without obstructive AHI elevation.
>

>
> -

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Normalize **AHI to <5 events/hour**, eliminate nocturnal oxygen desaturations (**maintain SpO2 $\ge 90\%$**), and resolve **Excessive Daytime Sleepiness (ESS <10)**.
> - **Secondary Target:** Achieve **PAP adherence ($\ge 4$ hours/night on $\ge 70\%$ of nights)**, **$\ge 10-15\%$ body weight reduction** (if overweight/obese), and improved blood pressure control.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Counsel immediately against driving or operating heavy machinery when sleepy** in any patient reporting near-miss accidents or severe daytime somnolence until effective therapy is established.
>
> **First-Line Gold-Standard Therapy**
>
> - **Initiate Positive Airway Pressure (Auto-Titrating CPAP [APAP] or Fixed CPAP; typically 5–20 cmH2O) with heated humidification** as first-line therapy for **all patients with Moderate-to-Severe OSA (AHI $\ge 15$)** and **symptomatic Mild OSA (AHI 5–14)** to provide a pneumatic splint that keeps the pharynx patent throughout the respiratory cycle.
> - **Initiate Medical Weight Loss + Lifestyle Modification concurrently in all overweight/obese patients:**
>   - **Prescribe Tirzepatide (Zepbound; titrated to 10–15 mg SC weekly)** alongside diet/exercise in **adults with Moderate-to-Severe OSA and Obesity (BMI $\ge 30$)** to achieve ~18–20% weight loss and a ~50–60% reduction in AHI (remission in up to 40–50% of patients).
>   - **Recommend Positional Therapy (avoiding supine sleep via positional devices)** in patients with supine-predominant OSA, **strict avoidance of evening Alcohol, Opioids, and Benzodiazepines/Z-drugs**, and **Bariatric Surgery referral** if BMI $\ge 35$ kg/m$^2$.
>
> **Second-Line Alternatives & Stepwise Escalation (CPAP Intolerance or Failure)**
>
> - **Prescribe a Custom, Titratable Mandibular Advancement Device (MAD / Oral Appliance)** fitted by a qualified dentist in patients with **Mild-to-Moderate OSA** or **Severe OSA who refuse or cannot tolerate CPAP** to protrude the mandible and tongue anteriorly.
> - **Switch from CPAP to BiPAP (Bilevel PAP)** if the patient cannot tolerate high expiratory pressures (>15 cmH2O) on CPAP or has concurrent **Obesity Hypoventilation Syndrome / COPD overlap** requiring ventilatory pressure support.
> - **Refer for Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation (HNS; e.g., Inspire implant)** in patients with **Moderate-to-Severe OSA (AHI 15–65), BMI <32–35 kg/m$^2$, failed/intolerant to CPAP, and absence of complete concentric collapse at the velopharynx** on drug-induced sleep endoscopy.
> - **Perform Adenotonsillectomy** as **first-line definitive treatment in Pediatric OSA** (or adults with severe tonsillar hypertrophy), or consider **Maxillomandibular Advancement (MMA)** in patients with severe craniofacial retrognathia (_routine Uvulopalatopharyngoplasty [UPPP] has low efficacy and is generally not recommended_).
> - **Add a Wake-Promoting Agent (Modafinil 100–200 mg PO daily, Armodafinil, or Solriamfetol)** ONLY for **persistent Excessive Daytime Sleepiness DESPITE documented optimal CPAP adherence ($\ge 4$ hrs/night)** and exclusion of other causes.
>
> **Follow-up**
>
> - **Review objective CPAP smart-card/cloud data within 30–90 days** of initiation to verify **adherence ($\ge 4$ hrs/night on $\ge 70\%$ of nights)**, **residual AHI (<5/hr)**, and **mask leak**, troubleshooting nasal congestion (add intranasal steroids/heated humidifier) or mask fit early.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Benzodiazepines, Z-drugs, Barbiturates, Opioids, and Alcohol before bedtime** in untreated OSA; they relax pharyngeal dilator muscles, blunt arousal responses to hypoxia, prolong apneas, and can precipitate fatal nocturnal respiratory arrest.
> - **Avoid Adaptive Servo-Ventilation (ASV)** in patients with **Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction (HFrEF; LVEF $\le 45\%$)** and predominant Central Sleep Apnea (SERVE-HF trial showed **increased cardiovascular and all-cause mortality**).
> - **Avoid using Home Sleep Apnea Testing (HSAT) in patients with Heart Failure, COPD, Neuromuscular Disease, Opioid use, or suspected Obesity Hypoventilation**; these patients require in-lab attended Polysomnography (PSG).
> - **Avoid prescribing Modafinil or Solriamfetol as a substitute for CPAP**; wake-promoting agents treat only the symptom of sleepiness and leave the patient exposed to severe nocturnal hypoxia and cardiovascular events.
> - **Avoid Perioperative Opioids and Extubation while still sedated/supine** in surgical patients with OSA; use multimodal non-opioid analgesia, regional anesthesia, extubate fully awake in the semi-upright position, and resume home CPAP immediately in the PACU.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Resistant Systemic Hypertension & Cardiovascular Disease** $\rightarrow$ Nocturnal sympathetic surges cause non-dipping BP, **Atrial Fibrillation**, CAD, HF, and stroke $\rightarrow$ CPAP therapy modestly lowers BP (especially in resistant HTN) and reduces AFib recurrence post-ablation.
> - **Group 3 Pulmonary Hypertension & Cor Pulmonale** $\rightarrow$ Chronic nocturnal alveolar hypoxia causes pulmonary arterial vasoconstriction and right ventricular failure $\rightarrow$ Treat with nocturnal CPAP (+ nocturnal supplemental O2 or BiPAP if concurrent OHS/COPD).
> - **Motor Vehicle & Occupational Accidents** $\rightarrow$ 2- to 7-fold increased risk of crashes due to microsleeps $\rightarrow$ Resolves rapidly with compliant CPAP therapy.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 52-year-old male with resistant hypertension (on amlodipine, lisinopril, and chlorthalidone) and obesity (BMI 36 kg/m$^2$, neck circumference 44 cm) presents with morning headaches, daytime fatigue (Epworth Sleepiness Scale = 14), and loud snoring with witnessed apneic pauses reported by his wife. Because he has no heart failure, COPD, or neuromuscular disease (serum HCO3- is 24 mEq/L), you order a **Home Sleep Apnea Test (HSAT)**, which reveals an obstructive AHI of 38 events/hour (Severe OSA) with nadir SpO2 of 79%. You initiate **Auto-Titrating CPAP (APAP)**, prescribe **Tirzepatide** alongside lifestyle counseling for weight reduction, advise him to avoid alcohol before sleep and not to drive while drowsy, and schedule a 4-week follow-up to check CPAP adherence and residual AHI.
