> [!abstract] Acute Respiratory Failure
> An acute life-threatening inability of the respiratory system to maintain adequate gas exchange—oxygenation (Type 1), CO2 elimination/ventilation (Type 2), or both—developing over minutes to hours and resulting in uncompensated acid-base disturbances and tissue hypoxia.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Type 1 (Hypoxemic / Lung Failure):** Driven by **V/Q mismatch** (responds to O2) or **intrapulmonary shunt** (blood bypasses ventilated alveoli; refractory to 100% FiO2), alongside diffusion limitation, low FiO2, or hypoventilation.
> - **Type 2 (Hypercapnic / Pump Failure):** Driven by **decreased alveolar minute ventilation** ($\dot{V}_A = \text{RR} \times [\text{TV} - V_D]$) or **increased physiological dead space ($V_D$)** from CNS depression, neuromuscular weakness, chest wall restriction, or severe airway obstruction.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Calculate the A-a Gradient** ($\text{PAO}_2 - \text{PaO}_2$; normal $\approx \frac{\text{Age}}{4} + 4$): A **normal A-a gradient** in hypoxemia + hypercapnia indicates pure extrapulmonary hypoventilation (e.g., opioid overdose, neuromuscular crisis); an **elevated A-a gradient** indicates intrinsic lung/vascular pathology (V/Q mismatch, shunt, diffusion defect).
> - **Shunt vs. V/Q Mismatch:** Hypoxemia that **fails to improve with 100% FiO2** is a hallmark of an intrapulmonary shunt (e.g., ARDS, lobar pneumonia, pulmonary edema, intracardiac shunt) and requires positive pressure (PEEP/CPAP) to recruit collapsed alveoli.
> - **ROX Index** ($\frac{\text{SpO}_2 / \text{FiO}_2}{\text{RR}}$) measured at 2, 6, and 12 hours on High-Flow Nasal Cannula (HFNC): A score **<3.85** predicts high risk of HFNC failure and need for immediate intubation; **$\ge 4.88$** predicts success.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - Acute onset of severe dyspnea, orthopnea, air hunger, or inability to speak in full sentences.
> - **Etiologic clues:** Fever/productive cough (pneumonia), orthopnea/edema (ADHF), sudden pleuritic pain (PE/pneumothorax), sedative/opioid use (CNS depression), or progressive weakness/bulbar symptoms (Guillain-Barré, myasthenia gravis).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Hypoxemia signs:** Tachypnea, tachycardia, restlessness/agitation, diaphoresis, central cyanosis (late sign), SpO2 <90%.
> - **Hypercapnia signs:** Somnolence, confusion, morning headache, flushed warm skin, bounding pulses, conjunctival hyperemia, **asterixis** (CO2 narcosis).
> - **Respiratory muscle fatigue (Imminent Arrest):** Paradoxical thoracoabdominal breathing (abdominal inward motion on inspiration), intercostal/supraclavicular retractions, nasal flaring, tripod position, or bradypnea/apnea.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Arterial Blood Gas (ABG) on Room Air (at sea level):**
>   - **Type 1 (Hypoxemic) Respiratory Failure:** **PaO2 <60 mmHg** (or SpO2 <90% / PaO2:FiO2 ratio <300) with normal or low PaCO2 (<45 mmHg).
>   - **Type 2 (Hypercapnic) Respiratory Failure:** **PaCO2 >45–50 mmHg** with **acute respiratory acidosis (pH <7.35)** (in chronic retainers, acute-on-chronic is defined by an acute rise in PaCO2 causing pH <7.35 alongside elevated baseline HCO3-).
> - **Sub-classifications:** **Type 3 (Perioperative)** due to basal atelectasis; **Type 4 (Shock)** due to respiratory muscle hypoperfusion.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **ABG + Lactate:** Confirms Type 1 vs. Type 2 failure, quantifies pH/acidemia, allows calculation of **A-a gradient** and **PaO2/FiO2 (P/F) ratio** (Mild ARDS: 200–300; Moderate: 100–200; Severe: $\le 100$).
> - **CBC, BMP, BNP, Troponin, D-dimer, Procalcitonin:** Differentiates infectious, cardiogenic, and thromboembolic etiologies.
>
> **Imaging**
>
> - **Point-of-Care Lung Ultrasound (POCUS / BLUE Protocol):** Rapid bedside differentiation:
>   - _Absent lung sliding + barcode sign:_ **Pneumothorax**.
>   - _Diffuse B-lines + smooth pleura:_ **Cardiogenic pulmonary edema** (vs. irregular pleura/subpleural consolidations in **ARDS**).
>   - _Focal B-lines / hepatization / dynamic air bronchograms:_ **Pneumonia**.
>   - _Normal A-lines + positive DVT scan:_ **Pulmonary Embolism**.
> - **Chest X-Ray (CXR):** Mandatory initial screen (clear lungs = PE, asthma/COPD, extrapulmonary pump failure; diffuse bilateral opacities = ADHF vs. ARDS; unilateral opacity = pneumonia, effusion, atelectasis).
> - **Bedside Spirometry (NIF / FVC):** Essential in suspected neuromuscular weakness (Guillain-Barré, Myasthenia Gravis). **Negative Inspiratory Force (NIF) worse than -20 to -30 cmH2O** or **Vital Capacity (VC) <15–20 mL/kg** indicates need for elective intubation.
>
> **Referrals**
>
> - **ICU / Critical Care:** Immediate consultation for any patient requiring NIV, HFNC, or invasive mechanical ventilation.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Bradypnea, agonal breathing, or paradoxical abdominal motion** $\rightarrow$ Complete diaphragm exhaustion; arrest is minutes away.
> - **Altered mental status (coma, severe agitation) or inability to protect airway** $\rightarrow$ Absolute contraindication to NIV; intubate immediately.
> - **PaO2 <60 mmHg despite 100% FiO2** $\rightarrow$ Severe intrapulmonary shunt requiring PEEP / invasive ventilation.
> - **pH <7.25 or rapidly rising PaCO2** $\rightarrow$ Decompensated ventilatory failure.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Type 1 (Shunt / V/Q Mismatch):** ARDS, Acute Cardiogenic Pulmonary Edema, Pulmonary Embolism, Tension Pneumothorax, Severe Pneumonia.
> - **Type 2 (Pump / Airway Failure):** Acute Severe Asthma / AECOPD, Opioid/Sedative Overdose, Myasthenic Crisis / Guillain-Barré Syndrome, Cervical Spinal Cord Injury.
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Community-Acquired Pneumonia / Sepsis**
> - **Decompensated Heart Failure**
> - **COPD / Asthma Exacerbation**
>
> **Most Surprising**
>
> - **Methemoglobinemia / Carbon Monoxide Poisoning:** Tissue hypoxia with normal PaO2 on ABG (saturation gap on co-oximetry).
>
> **Less Common**
>
> - **Diffuse Alveolar Hemorrhage / Acute Interstitial Pneumonia**
>

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Maintain **SpO2 92–96%** (PaO2 60–80 mmHg) in standard Type 1 failure, or **SpO2 88–92%** in patients at risk for Type 2 hypercapnic failure (e.g., COPD, obesity hypoventilation, neuromuscular disease), while restoring **pH >7.30–7.35**.
> - **Secondary Target:** Reverse the underlying etiology, unload fatigued respiratory muscles, and prevent ventilator-induced lung injury (VILI).
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Open airway (head-tilt/chin-lift, jaw thrust, suctioning) and administer Naloxone (0.4–2 mg IV/IM/IN)** immediately if opioid-induced hypoventilation (pinpoint pupils, bradypnea) is suspected to rapidly restore spontaneous ventilation.
> - **Perform Immediate Rapid Sequence Intubation (RSI)** in patients presenting with apnea, coma (GCS $\le 8$), inability to protect the airway, hemodynamic instability, or refractory hypoxemia/acidosis despite non-invasive support.
>
> **Non-Invasive Respiratory Support (Modality Selection)**
>
> - **Initiate High-Flow Nasal Cannula (HFNC; 40–60 L/min, FiO2 titrated)** as first-line therapy in **acute hypoxemic respiratory failure (Type 1, e.g., pneumonia, early ARDS)** without hypercapnia to provide heated/humidified O2, dead-space washout, and low-level PEEP (reduce intubation and mortality vs. standard O2).
> - **Initiate Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP; 5–10 cmH2O) or BiPAP** as first-line therapy in **Acute Cardiogenic Pulmonary Edema** to recruit flooded alveoli, reduce LV preload, and decrease LV afterload.
> - **Initiate Bilevel Positive Airway Pressure (BiPAP / NIV; IPAP 10–15 cmH2O, EPAP 4–5 cmH2O)** as first-line therapy in **acute hypercapnic respiratory failure (Type 2, pH $\le 7.35$, PaCO2 $>45$ mmHg, e.g., AECOPD)** to augment tidal volume (via pressure support = IPAP minus EPAP) and decrease work of breathing.
>
> **Invasive Mechanical Ventilation & Rescue**
>
> - **Apply Lung-Protective Ventilation (Tidal Volume 4–8 mL/kg predicted body weight, Plateau Pressure $\le 30$ cmH2O, PEEP 5–20 cmH2O)** in all intubated patients with Type 1 failure/ARDS to prevent volutrauma and atelectrauma.
> - **Implement Prone Positioning for >12–16 hours/day** in intubated patients with **moderate-to-severe ARDS (PaO2/FiO2 <150)** to optimize dorsal lung recruitment, improve V/Q matching, and reduce mortality.
> - **Optimize Minute Ventilation (adjust RR and TV while allowing long expiratory time I:E 1:3–1:4 in obstructive disease)** in intubated Type 2 failure patients to gradually correct pH to >7.30 without causing auto-PEEP or post-hypercapnic metabolic alkalosis.
>
> **Disposition & Follow-up**
>
> - **Reassess ABG and clinical trajectory at 1–2 hours** after initiating NIV or HFNC; **proceed to immediate intubation** if there is no improvement in pH/PaCO2, worsening tachypnea, ROX index <3.85, or declining mental status.
> - **Admit all patients with Acute Respiratory Failure to the ICU or Step-Down Unit** for continuous pulse oximetry, capnography, and hemodynamic monitoring.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid delaying intubation** in patients failing NIV or HFNC after 1–2 hours; prolonged failed trials of NIV/HFNC significantly increase ICU mortality.
> - **Avoid NIV (BiPAP/CPAP)** in patients with **altered mental status, inability to clear copious secretions, active emesis/GI bleed, recent upper GI/airway surgery, facial trauma, or untreated pneumothorax** to prevent fatal aspiration or tension pneumothorax.
> - **Avoid BiPAP as a bridge in rapidly progressive neuromuscular crisis (Guillain-Barré / Myasthenia)** once bulbar dysfunction or NIF worse than -20 cmH2O develops; proceed directly to elective intubation before hypercapnia ensues.
> - **Avoid rapid normalization of PaCO2** in patients with chronic hypercapnia (high baseline HCO3-) on mechanical ventilation to prevent severe **post-hypercapnic metabolic alkalosis**, cerebral vasoconstriction, and seizures.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Ventilator-Induced Lung Injury (VILI) / Barotrauma** $\rightarrow$ Pneumothorax or alveolar damage from excessive tidal volumes or plateau pressures >30 cmH2O $\rightarrow$ Strictly maintain TV 6 mL/kg PBW and Pplat $\le 30$ cmH2O.
> - **Hemodynamic Collapse on Positive Pressure** $\rightarrow$ Increased intrathoracic pressure decreases venous return (preload), causing acute hypotension after NIV or intubation $\rightarrow$ Administer IV fluid bolus or vasopressors (norepinephrine) peri-intubation.
> - **Post-Hypercapnic Alkalosis** $\rightarrow$ Rapidly blowing off CO2 in a chronic retainer leaves unbuffered elevated kidneys HCO3-, causing pH >7.55, arrhythmias, and seizures $\rightarrow$ Target normal pH (7.35–7.40), not normal PaCO2.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 58-year-old male with severe right lower lobe pneumonia develops worsening tachypnea (RR 34/min) and SpO2 84% despite a non-rebreather mask at 15 L/min; he is alert and speaking in short phrases. ABG reveals pH 7.46, PaCO2 31 mmHg, and PaO2 52 mmHg (Type 1 hypoxemic failure with elevated A-a gradient). You transfer him to the ICU, initiate High-Flow Nasal Cannula (HFNC) at 50 L/min with FiO2 0.70, start empiric IV antibiotics, and calculate a ROX index at 2 hours to monitor for early intubation triggers.
