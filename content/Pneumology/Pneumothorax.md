> [!abstract] Pneumothorax
> Accumulation of air within the pleural space between the visceral and parietal pleura, leading to loss of negative intrapleural pressure and partial or complete lung collapse.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Spontaneous:** Rupture of apical subpleural blebs in healthy lungs (**Primary / PSP**) or rupture of damaged lung parenchyma/bullae in underlying disease (**Secondary / SSP**, e.g., COPD, CF, _Pneumocystis_ pneumonia, Marfan, lymphangioleiomyomatosis).
> - **Tension PTX:** A one-way valve defect traps air in the pleural space during inspiration, progressively increasing intrapleural pressure, compressing the vena cava/mediastinum, and causing **obstructive shock** via impaired venous return.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Tension Pneumothorax is a strictly clinical diagnosis:** NEVER delay needle decompression to obtain a Chest X-Ray in a hypotensive or rapidly deteriorating patient.
> - **5th Intercostal Space (ICS) at the Anterior/Mid-Axillary Line** is now the ATLS first-line site for adult needle decompression (thinner chest wall than the 2nd ICS mid-clavicular line).
> - **Conservative management (observation alone)** is now endorsed as a safe option for minimally symptomatic **Primary Spontaneous Pneumothorax (PSP)** regardless of size on CXR, provided close follow-up is guaranteed.
> - **Supplemental high-flow O2 increases PTX reabsorption rate by 3- to 4-fold** by lowering the partial pressure of nitrogen in pleural capillaries (nitrogen washout).

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Sudden-onset unilateral pleuritic chest pain** and acute dyspnea (often occurs at rest).
> - **PSP risk factors:** Tall, thin young males (ages 15–35), **smoking** (increases risk 20x), Marfan syndrome.
> - **SSP risk factors:** Age >50, COPD/emphysema, cystic fibrosis, asthma, interstitial lung disease, thoracic endometriosis (catamenial PTX within 72h of menses).
> - **Traumatic / Iatrogenic:** Recent central venous catheter (subclavian > IJ), thoracentesis, lung biopsy, barotrauma from mechanical ventilation, or chest trauma.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Uncomplicated PTX:** Ipsilateral **decreased or absent breath sounds**, **hyperresonance to percussion**, decreased tactile fremitus, and asymmetric chest expansion.
> - **Tension PTX:** Severe respiratory distress/cyanosis, **hypotension (obstructive shock)**, tachycardia, **jugular venous distension (JVD)**, and **contralateral tracheal deviation** (late sign).
> - **Subcutaneous emphysema** (crepitus over chest/neck) or **Hamman's sign** (precordial crunching synchronous with heartbeat).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Tension Pneumothorax (Clinical Criteria):** Unilateral absent breath sounds/hyperresonance PLUS **hemodynamic instability (SBP <90 mmHg)** or **severe acute hypoxia/respiratory distress**.
> - **Imaging Confirmation (Stable Patients):**
>   - **Upright Inspiratory CXR:** Visible **visceral pleural line** with absence of lung markings peripheral to the line (or **deep sulcus sign** on supine CXR).
>   - **Point-of-Care Ultrasound (POCUS):** Absence of lung sliding, absence of B-lines/comet-tail artifacts, **"Barcode / Stratosphere sign"** on M-mode, and presence of a **"Lung Point"** (100% specific transition point between normal sliding lung and PTX).
> - **Size Thresholds (for intervention planning):**
>   - **BTS:** **Large** = $\ge 2$ cm rim from chest wall to lung margin at the level of the hilum.
>   - **ACCP:** **Large** = $\ge 3$ cm distance from lung apex to ipsilateral cupola.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **ABG:** Not routinely needed; shows hypoxemia and respiratory alkalosis (or hypercapnia in severe SSP/COPD).
>
> **Imaging**
>
> - **POCUS (Extended FAST):** First-line rapid bedside test in trauma/instability; higher sensitivity (86–98%) than supine CXR.
> - **Upright Inspiratory PA Chest X-Ray:** First-line confirmatory test in hemodynamically stable patients (_routine expiratory films are NOT recommended_ as they do not improve diagnostic yield).
> - **CT Chest (without contrast):** Gold standard; order _only_ for uncertain CXR (e.g., differentiating giant emphysematous bullae from SSP), malpositioned chest tube, persistent air leak, or pre-operative VATS planning.
>
> **Referrals**
>
> - **Thoracic Surgery / Interventional Pulmonology:** Refer if **persistent air leak >3–5 days**, failure of lung re-expansion, **recurrent ipsilateral PTX**, **first contralateral PTX**, **bilateral PTX**, **hemopneumothorax**, or high-risk professions (pilots, scuba divers).

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Hypotension, JVD, or tracheal shift** $\rightarrow$ Tension pneumothorax; decompress immediately.
> - **Rapid desaturation or high peak pressures on mechanical ventilation** $\rightarrow$ Tension PTX from barotrauma; place chest tube immediately.
> - **Bilateral absent breath sounds** $\rightarrow$ Bilateral tension PTX; immediate bilateral decompression.
> - **Fluid level on CXR (Hydropneumothorax / Hemopneumothorax)** $\rightarrow$ Risk of massive internal hemorrhage; requires large-bore chest tube (28–32 Fr).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Pulmonary Embolism:** Pleuritic pain, dyspnea, hypoxia, but _normal breath sounds_ and clear CXR.
> - **Acute Coronary Syndrome / Aortic Dissection:** Crushing/tearing chest pain, ECG changes, normal lung sliding.
> - **Cardiac Tamponade:** Hypotension + JVD, but _equal bilateral breath sounds_ and muffled heart sounds.
> - **Esophageal Rupture (Boerhaave Syndrome):** Severe retching, chest pain, subcutaneous emphysema, left pleural effusion/PTX.
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Acute Pericarditis / Pleuritis:** Positional sharp pain, friction rub, diffuse ST elevations.
> - **Musculoskeletal / Rib Fracture:** Focal chest wall tenderness, normal lung sliding.
>
> **Most Surprising**
>
> - **Giant Emphysematous Bulla:** Mimics large PTX on CXR in COPD patients; **CT Chest is mandatory** before placing a chest tube to avoid puncturing a bulla and creating a bronchopleural fistula.
>
> **Less Common**
>
> - **Diaphragmatic Hernia:** Bowel sounds in chest, NG tube tip in hemithorax on CXR.
>

>
> -

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Immediate restoration of **hemodynamic stability (SBP >90 mmHg)** and **oxygenation (SpO2 >92–94%; 88–92% in COPD)**, followed by complete **lung re-expansion**.
> - **Secondary Target:** Prevent recurrence (pleurodesis when indicated) and avoid unnecessary invasive procedures in stable PSP.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (Tension Pneumothorax)**
>
> - **Perform Immediate Needle Decompression (14–16G angiocatheter, 5–8 cm length)** in the **5th ICS at the anterior/mid-axillary line** (or 2nd ICS mid-clavicular line) upon clinical suspicion of Tension PTX without waiting for CXR to relieve obstructive shock.
> - **Perform Finger Thoracostomy followed by Tube Thoracostomy** immediately after needle decompression (or as primary decompression in trauma) to provide definitive pleural evacuation.
> - **Administer High-Flow Supplemental Oxygen (10–15 L/min; titrate to 88–92% if COPD)** in symptomatic or hospitalized patients to correct hypoxia and accelerate pleural air resorption by 4-fold.
>
> **Primary Spontaneous Pneumothorax (PSP — Stable, No Underlying Lung Disease)**
>
> - **Observe Conservatively (Outpatient or ED observation for 4 hours with repeat CXR)** in patients with **small (<2 cm at hilum / <3 cm at apex) OR minimally symptomatic/asymptomatic large PSP** with reliable follow-up to avoid invasive intervention.
> - **Perform Simple Needle Aspiration (14–16G catheter, aspirating up to 2.5 L of air) OR insert a Small-Bore Ambulatory Pigtail Catheter (8–14 Fr with Heimlich valve)** in **symptomatic PSP** (significant dyspnea) to rapidly relieve symptoms and allow outpatient management if lung re-expands.
> - **Insert a Small-Bore Chest Tube (8–14 Fr) attached to a water-seal device** if simple aspiration fails (>2.5 L aspirated without expansion) or the patient cannot be safely managed with an ambulatory valve.
>
> **Secondary Spontaneous (SSP) & Traumatic Pneumothorax**
>
> - **Admit and Insert a Small-Bore Chest Tube (8–14 Fr)** in all **symptomatic SSP** or **SSP $\ge 2$ cm at hilum** due to poor cardiopulmonary reserve and high risk of persistent air leak (small <1–2 cm asymptomatic SSP may be observed inpatient for 24h on O2).
> - **Insert a Chest Tube (14 Fr pigtail for simple traumatic PTX; 24–32 Fr surgical tube if concurrent Hemothorax or mechanical ventilation)** in **Traumatic Pneumothorax** (>20% volume, symptomatic, or requiring positive-pressure ventilation) to prevent progression to tension PTX.
>
> **Stepwise Escalation & Definitive Surgical Management**
>
> - **Apply Low-Volume Thoracic Wall Suction (-10 to -20 cmH2O)** if the lung fails to fully re-expand after 24–48 hours of water-seal drainage alone.
> - **Perform VATS (Video-Assisted Thoracoscopic Surgery) with Bleb/Bulla Resection and Chemical/Mechanical Pleurodesis (e.g., Talc poudrage or pleural abrasion)** if there is a **persistent air leak >3–5 days**, recurrent PTX, bilateral PTX, hemopneumothorax, or high-risk occupation (pilots/divers).
>
> **Disposition & Follow-up**
>
> - **Discharge stable PSP patients** once repeat CXR at 4 hours shows a stable/improving PTX and the patient can walk comfortably; schedule **repeat outpatient CXR in 2–4 weeks** to confirm complete resolution.
> - **Counsel on strict precautions:** **Absolute lifelong prohibition of scuba diving** (unless bilateral surgical pleurectomy performed), **avoid air travel until $\ge 1-2$ weeks after complete radiographic resolution**, and **mandatory smoking/cannabis cessation** (reduces recurrence risk from ~50% to ~20%).

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid delaying needle decompression for a Chest X-Ray** in a patient with suspected Tension Pneumothorax; this is a fatal error.
> - **Avoid initiating Positive-Pressure Ventilation (BiPAP, CPAP, or Intubation)** in a patient with an untreated pneumothorax _before_ placing a chest tube, as positive pressure rapidly converts a simple PTX into a lethal Tension PTX.
> - **Avoid clamping a bubbling chest tube** (indicates an active air leak/bronchopleural fistula) to prevent immediate iatrogenic Tension Pneumothorax.
> - **Avoid blind chest tube insertion in severe bullous emphysema (COPD)** without CT confirmation if the patient is stable, to prevent placing a tube directly into a giant bulla.
> - **Avoid rapid evacuation or high suction immediately after draining a large chronic PTX (>72 hours duration)** to prevent **Re-expansion Pulmonary Edema (RPE)**; start on water seal without suction.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Tension Pneumothorax** $\rightarrow$ Hypotension, JVD, hypoxia, absent unilateral breath sounds $\rightarrow$ Immediate needle decompression (5th ICS mid-axillary) followed by chest tube.
> - **Re-expansion Pulmonary Edema (RPE)** $\rightarrow$ Acute onset cough, chest tightness, frothy sputum, and hypoxia immediately after rapid drainage of a large/prolonged PTX $\rightarrow$ Stop suction (leave on water seal), administer supplemental O2/CPAP, and provide supportive care.
> - **Bronchopleural Fistula (Persistent Air Leak)** $\rightarrow$ Continuous bubbling in the water-seal chamber lasting >3–5 days $\rightarrow$ Thoracic surgery consult for VATS or endobronchial valve placement.
> - **Hemopneumothorax** $\rightarrow$ Disruption of vascular pleural adhesions during lung collapse causing shock and air-fluid level on CXR $\rightarrow$ Large-bore chest tube (28–32 Fr) and VATS if bleeding >1,500 mL initially or >200 mL/hr for 2–4 hours.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A tall, thin 22-year-old male smoker presents with sudden-onset right-sided pleuritic chest pain and mild dyspnea that began while watching television; vitals are BP 122/76 mmHg, HR 88 bpm, RR 18/min, and SpO2 98% on room air. Exam reveals decreased right apical breath sounds, and an upright CXR confirms a 2.5 cm right primary spontaneous pneumothorax without mediastinal shift. Because he is hemodynamically stable and minimally symptomatic, you discuss shared decision-making between conservative observation versus ambulatory pigtail catheter/needle aspiration, counsel him on smoking cessation and avoiding air travel, and arrange repeat CXR follow-up.
