> [!abstract] Acute COPD Exacerbation
> An acute event characterized by worsening of dyspnea, cough, and/or sputum production over <14 days, often accompanied by tachypnea and/or tachycardia, caused by increased local and systemic inflammation from airway infection, pollution, or other insults.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - Viral or bacterial triggers amplify small airway inflammation, bronchospasm, and mucus hypersecretion, worsening expiratory flow limitation.
> - Incomplete alveolar emptying causes dynamic hyperinflation (auto-PEEP), increasing work of breathing and worsening V/Q mismatch, leading to acute-on-chronic hypercapnic and hypoxemic respiratory failure.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Target SpO2 88–92% strictly:** Over-oxygenation causes hyperoxia-induced hypercapnia (primarily via Haldane effect and worsening V/Q mismatch) and increases mortality.
> - **Early BiPAP (NIV) is the gold-standard life-saving intervention** for acute respiratory acidosis (pH $\le 7.35$, PaCO2 $>45$ mmHg), significantly reducing intubation and mortality.
> - **Sputum purulence** is the single most reliable clinical indicator for initiating empiric antibiotics.
> - **"5 days of 40 mg Prednisone"** (REDUCE trial) is non-inferior to 14 days and minimizes steroid toxicity; PO is equivalent to IV if the patient can swallow.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Cardinal symptoms (Anthonisen Criteria):** Worsening dyspnea, increased sputum volume, and increased sputum purulence over <14 days.
> - **Triggers:** Upper respiratory viral infections (most common), bacterial infection (_H. influenzae, S. pneumoniae, M. catarrhalis, P. aeruginosa_), environmental pollutants, or medication non-adherence.
> - **High-risk history:** Prior intubation/NIV, $\ge 2$ exacerbations or $\ge 1$ hospitalization in the past year, baseline home oxygen use, or severe baseline airflow obstruction.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Moderate-to-Severe:** Tachypnea (RR >24/min), pursed-lip breathing, tripod positioning, use of accessory muscles, diffuse expiratory wheezing, prolonged expiratory phase, or coarse rhonchi.
> - **Life-Threatening / Acute Respiratory Failure:** Altered mental status (somnolence, confusion, asterixis from CO2 narcosis), paradoxical abdominal breathing, cyanosis, **silent chest**, or hemodynamic instability.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Clinical Diagnosis:** Acute worsening of respiratory symptoms (dyspnea, cough, and/or sputum) over <14 days after excluding acute mimics (CHF, PE, pneumonia, pneumothorax).
> - **GOLD Rome Severity Classification:**
>   - **Mild:** Dyspnea VAS <5, RR <24/min, HR <95 bpm, resting SpO2 $\ge 92\%$ (or <3% drop from baseline), CRP <10 mg/L (treated with SABA only).
>   - **Moderate:** Meets $\ge 3$ of 5: Dyspnea VAS $\ge 5$, RR $\ge 24$/min, HR $\ge 95$ bpm, resting SpO2 $<92\%$ (or $\ge 3\%$ drop), CRP $\ge 10$ mg/L; ABG shows PaO2 $>60$ mmHg and/or SaO2 $>90\%$ without acidosis (treated with SABA + steroids/antibiotics).
>   - **Severe:** Meets Moderate criteria PLUS **ABG showing hypercapnic respiratory acidosis (PaCO2 $>45$ mmHg and pH $<7.35$)** or new/worsening hypoxemia (PaO2 $\le 60$ mmHg).

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **ABG / VBG:** Essential in moderate-to-severe cases or SpO2 <92%. **pH $\le 7.35$ with PaCO2 $>45$ mmHg** confirms acute (or acute-on-chronic) respiratory acidosis and triggers immediate BiPAP.
> - **CBC, BMP, CRP, BNP, Troponin:** Check elevated HCO3- (indicates baseline chronic CO2 retention); BNP and Troponin rule out concurrent heart failure or NSTEMI.
> - **Respiratory Viral Panel & Sputum Culture:** Order sputum culture _only_ in hospitalized patients with purulent sputum, severe exacerbations, or risk factors for _Pseudomonas_ (frequent antibiotics, bronchiectasis, severe FEV1 <30%).
> - **Spirometry:** **NOT recommended** during an acute exacerbation (unreliable and poorly tolerated).
>
> **Imaging**
>
> - **Chest X-Ray (CXR):** Mandatory in all ED/hospitalized patients to rule out pneumonia, pneumothorax, pleural effusion, or pulmonary edema.
> - **CT Pulmonary Angiogram (CTPA):** Order if severe sudden dyspnea, chest pain, DVT signs, or failure to respond to standard therapy suggests Pulmonary Embolism.
>
> **Referrals**
>
> - **ICU / Pulmonology:** Immediate consult for pH <7.25, failure of NIV, hemodynamic instability, or need for invasive mechanical ventilation.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Somnolence, confusion, or asterixis** $\rightarrow$ Severe hypercapnic encephalopathy (CO2 narcosis).
> - **pH <7.25 despite NIV** $\rightarrow$ High risk of NIV failure; prepare for immediate intubation.
> - **Silent chest or paradoxical thoracoabdominal motion** $\rightarrow$ Respiratory muscle exhaustion and imminent arrest.
> - **New-onset arrhythmia or hypotension** $\rightarrow$ Severe hypoxia, auto-PEEP, or concurrent ACS/PE.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Pulmonary Embolism:** Up to 16% prevalence in unexplained AECOPD; suspect if sudden onset, pleuritic pain, or lack of infectious prodrome.
> - **Acute Decompensated Heart Failure (ADHF):** Orthopnea, elevated JVP, S3, peripheral edema, elevated BNP, cardiomegaly/edema on CXR.
> - **Tension Pneumothorax:** Sudden deterioration, unilateral absent breath sounds, hyperresonance, tracheal shift.
> - **Acute Coronary Syndrome:** Chest pressure, diaphoresis, ECG changes, dynamic troponin elevation.
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Community-Acquired Pneumonia:** Fever, focal crackles/consolidation on CXR, leukocytosis.
> - **Acute Severe Asthma:** Younger age, atopic history, reversible airflow obstruction.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Maintain **SpO2 88–92%** and normalize arterial pH to **>7.35** (resolving acute respiratory acidosis without attempting to force PaCO2 to normal in chronic retainers).
> - **Secondary Target:** Relieve acute airflow obstruction, eradicate bacterial triggers, prevent invasive mechanical ventilation, and prevent early relapse.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Administer controlled supplemental O2 via Venturi mask (24–28% FiO2) or nasal cannula (1–2 L/min)** immediately upon presentation if hypoxemic to achieve **SpO2 88–92%** while preventing hyperoxia-induced hypercapnic coma.
> - **Initiate Non-Invasive Ventilation (BiPAP; initial IPAP 10–15 cmH2O, EPAP 4–5 cmH2O)** immediately in patients with **acute respiratory acidosis (pH $\le 7.35$ and PaCO2 $>45$ mmHg)**, severe dyspnea with muscle fatigue, or persistent hypoxemia despite supplemental O2 to unload respiratory muscles and reduce intubation rates.
> - **Perform Rapid Sequence Intubation (RSI)** immediately if the patient has respiratory arrest, NIV contraindications (coma, inability to protect airway, copious secretions, facial trauma, hemodynamic instability), or **worsening acidosis (pH <7.25) after 1–2 hours of BiPAP**.
>
> **First-Line Targeted Therapy**
>
> - **Administer Inhaled SABA (Albuterol 2.5 mg nebulized or 4–8 puffs via MDI+spacer) PLUS SAMA (Ipratropium 0.5 mg nebulized or 4–8 puffs)** every 20–60 minutes initially, then every 4–6 hours to achieve maximal bronchodilation.
> - **Give Systemic Corticosteroids (Prednisone 40 mg PO daily for 5 days, or Methylprednisolone 40 mg IV if NPO)** within the first hour to shorten recovery time, improve FEV1 and oxygenation, and reduce treatment failure.
> - **Start Empiric Antibiotics for 5 days** when the patient presents with **all 3 Anthonisen cardinal symptoms** (dyspnea, sputum volume, sputum purulence), **2 symptoms if one is increased sputum purulence**, or requires **mechanical ventilation (NIV or invasive)**:
>   - **Outpatient / Simple:** **Amoxicillin-clavulanate (875/125 mg PO BID)**, **Doxycycline (100 mg PO BID)**, or **Azithromycin (500 mg PO day 1, then 250 mg days 2–5)**.
>   - **Hospitalized (No _Pseudomonas_ risk):** **Ceftriaxone (1–2 g IV daily)** or **Levofloxacin (750 mg PO/IV daily)**.
>   - **High _Pseudomonas_ Risk:** **Cefepime (2 g IV q8h)** or **Piperacillin-tazobactam (4.5 g IV q6h)**.
>
> **Stepwise Escalation & Ventilator Management**
>
> - **Consider IV Magnesium Sulfate (2 g IV over 20 minutes)** in severe exacerbations refractory to initial bronchodilators and steroids to provide adjunct smooth muscle relaxation.
> - **Set Invasive Mechanical Ventilation for prolonged expiration (RR 10–14/min, TV 6–8 mL/kg IBW, I:E ratio 1:3 to 1:4, PEEP 5–8 cmH2O)** post-intubation to prevent dynamic hyperinflation (auto-PEEP), barotrauma, and hypotension.
>
> **Disposition & Follow-up**
>
> - **Admit to ICU** if the patient presents with pH <7.25, severe dyspnea unresponsive to initial therapy, altered mental status, hemodynamic instability, or need for invasive ventilation.
> - **Admit to Medical Ward** if the patient has severe baseline COPD, acute respiratory acidosis requiring BiPAP (if step-down capable), new hypoxemia/cor pulmonale, serious comorbidities, or inadequate home support.
> - **Discharge with Maintenance Optimization** once clinically stable for 24–48 hours; initiate or optimize long-acting bronchodilators (**LAMA + LABA**, adding **ICS** if blood eosinophils $\ge 300$ cells/$\mu$L), refer to **Pulmonary Rehabilitation** (start within 3–4 weeks post-discharge), ensure smoking cessation/vaccinations (Influenza, Pneumococcal, COVID-19, RSV), and schedule outpatient follow-up within 1–4 weeks.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid high-flow untitrated oxygen (e.g., non-rebreather at 15 L/min)** in COPD patients to prevent blunting hypoxic vasoconstriction (increasing dead space) and the Haldane effect, which precipitates lethal hypercapnic acidosis.
> - **Avoid methylxanthines (theophylline / aminophylline)** due to a narrow therapeutic window, high toxicity (arrhythmias, seizures), and lack of clinical benefit.
> - **Avoid BiPAP (NIV)** in patients with altered mental status/coma, inability to clear copious secretions, active vomiting, or hemodynamic instability to prevent aspiration and delayed intubation.
> - **Avoid nebulizers driven by continuous high-flow oxygen** in hypercapnic patients; use **compressed air-driven nebulizers** with supplemental nasal cannula oxygen titrated to keep SpO2 88–92%.
> - **Avoid systemic corticosteroid courses >5–7 days** to prevent hyperglycemia, myopathy, and secondary pneumonia without added respiratory benefit.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Hypercapnic Coma (CO2 Narcosis)** $\rightarrow$ Somnolence, asterixis, bounding pulses, pH <7.25 after excessive O2 administration $\rightarrow$ Immediately titrate O2 down to target SpO2 88–92% and initiate BiPAP or intubate.
> - **Auto-PEEP / Dynamic Hyperinflation** $\rightarrow$ Sudden hypotension and high peak/plateau pressures on mechanical ventilation $\rightarrow$ Disconnect ventilator tubing for 30–60 seconds to allow trapped air to escape, bolus IV fluids, and reduce respiratory rate.
> - **Secondary Pneumothorax** $\rightarrow$ Rupture of emphysematous bullae causing sudden hypoxia and unilateral absent breath sounds $\rightarrow$ Immediate tube thoracostomy.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 68-year-old male with a 50-pack-year smoking history presents with 4 days of worsening dyspnea and thick green sputum; vitals show RR 30/min, HR 110 bpm, and SpO2 82% on room air. ABG reveals pH 7.28, PaCO2 68 mmHg, PaO2 52 mmHg, and HCO3- 31 mEq/L, with hyperinflation on CXR. You immediately start controlled oxygen via Venturi mask targeting SpO2 88–92%, initiate BiPAP, and administer nebulized albuterol/ipratropium, oral prednisone 40 mg, and IV ceftriaxone before admitting him to a monitored step-down unit.
