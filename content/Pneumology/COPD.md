> [!abstract] COPD
> A heterogeneous chronic lung condition characterized by persistent, progressive, and largely irreversible airflow limitation caused by abnormalities of the airways (bronchitis/bronchiolitis) and/or alveoli (emphysema), usually resulting from significant exposure to noxious particles or gases.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - Noxious exposure (cigarette smoke, biomass fuel) triggers chronic **neutrophilic, CD8+ T-cell, and macrophage** inflammation plus protease-antiprotease imbalance (excess elastase).
> - **Chronic Bronchitis** (goblet cell hyperplasia, mucus hypersecretion, small airway fibrosis) + **Emphysema** (destruction of alveolar walls/capillary beds $\rightarrow$ loss of elastic recoil, reduced DLCO, dynamic hyperinflation/air trapping).

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Only Interventions Proven to Reduce Mortality in COPD:** **Smoking cessation**, **Long-Term Oxygen Therapy (LTOT)** in severe resting hypoxemia (PaO2 $\le 55$ mmHg or SpO2 $\le 88\%$), **Single-Inhaler Triple Therapy (LABA+LAMA+ICS)** in frequent exacerbators (Group E with high eosinophils), **Non-Invasive Ventilation** in stable severe hypercapnia, and **Lung Volume Reduction Surgery** in upper-lobe emphysema.
> - **GOLD ABE Assessment Tool (C and D merged into Group E):** Pharmacotherapy is guided by symptoms (**mMRC / CAT**) and exacerbation history (**Group E** = $\ge 2$ moderate exacerbations or $\ge 1$ hospitalization/year), NOT by FEV1% alone.
> - **Blood Eosinophil Count guides ICS use:** Add an Inhaled Corticosteroid (ICS) as **Triple Therapy (LABA+LAMA+ICS)** _only_ when blood eosinophils are **$\ge 300$ cells/$\mu$L** (or $\ge 100$ cells/$\mu$L with recurrent exacerbations on LABA+LAMA); never use ICS monotherapy.
> - **Screen EVERY COPD patient once for Alpha-1 Antitrypsin (AAT) Deficiency** (especially if age <45, minimal smoking, **panacinar basilar emphysema**, or unexplained liver disease).

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Progressive, persistent exertional dyspnea**, chronic cough, and regular sputum production.
> - **Chronic Bronchitis (Clinical Definition):** Productive cough for **$\ge 3$ months per year for $\ge 2$ consecutive years** in the absence of other causes.
> - **Risk factors:** Cigarette smoking (typically >10–20 pack-years), occupational dusts/fumes, indoor biomass fuel smoke, or AAT deficiency.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Hyperinflation signs:** Barrel chest (increased AP diameter), hyperresonance to percussion, decreased breath sounds, depressed flattened hemidiaphragms, and **Hoover's sign** (paradoxical inward movement of the lower lateral rib cage during inspiration).
> - **Airflow obstruction signs:** Prolonged expiratory phase, pursed-lip breathing, tripod positioning, wheezing/rhonchi.
> - **Cor Pulmonale (Right Heart Failure from Group 3 PH):** Elevated JVP, right ventricular heave, loud P2, hepatomegaly, and bilateral pitting peripheral edema.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Mandatory Spirometric Confirmation:**
>   - **Post-Bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ratio <0.70** (measured 15–30 minutes after 400 mcg inhaled albuterol) in the appropriate clinical context.
> - **GOLD Airflow Obstruction Severity (Post-BD FEV1 % Predicted):**
>   - **GOLD 1 (Mild):** FEV1 $\ge 80\%$ predicted
>   - **GOLD 2 (Moderate):** $50\% \le$ FEV1 $< 80\%$ predicted
>   - **GOLD 3 (Severe):** $30\% \le$ FEV1 $< 50\%$ predicted
>   - **GOLD 4 (Very Severe):** FEV1 $< 30\%$ predicted
> - **GOLD ABE Clinical Grouping (for Initial Therapy):**
>   - **Group A:** 0–1 moderate exacerbations (no hospitalizations) AND **mMRC 0–1 / CAT <10**.
>   - **Group B:** 0–1 moderate exacerbations (no hospitalizations) AND **mMRC $\ge 2$ / CAT $\ge 10$**.
>   - **Group E (Exacerbators):** **$\ge 2$ moderate exacerbations OR $\ge 1$ exacerbation leading to hospitalization** in the past year (regardless of symptom score).

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **CBC with Differential:** Mandatory to check **Blood Eosinophil Count** (threshold $\ge 300$ cells/$\mu$L for initial ICS; <100 cells/$\mu$L predicts no ICS benefit and high pneumonia risk) and screen for secondary polycythemia (Hct >55%).
> - **Alpha-1 Antitrypsin (AAT) Level & Phenotype:** One-time screen recommended by WHO/GOLD for **all** patients diagnosed with COPD.
> - **ABG:** Order if resting SpO2 <92% or FEV1 <50% to evaluate for chronic hypercapnia and **Long-Term Oxygen Therapy (LTOT)** eligibility.
>
> **Imaging**
>
> - **Chest X-Ray (CXR):** Shows hyperinflation (flattened diaphragms, increased retrosternal airspace, hyperlucent lungs, narrow "water-bottle/teardrop" heart shadow) and excludes lung cancer, bronchiectasis, or heart failure.
> - **Low-Dose CT Chest (LDCT):** Recommended annually for **Lung Cancer Screening** in adults aged **50–80 with a $\ge 20$ pack-year smoking history** (current smokers or quit within 15 years); also used pre-intervention for Lung Volume Reduction.
> - **Lung Volumes & DLCO:** Plethysmography shows **increased TLC, RV, and FRC** (air trapping); **DLCO is reduced (<80%)** in emphysema due to alveolar-capillary destruction (normal in pure asthma).
>
> **Referrals**
>
> - **Pulmonology / Thoracic Surgery:** Severe COPD (GOLD 3–4), frequent exacerbations despite Triple Therapy, suspected AAT deficiency, or evaluation for **Endobronchial Valves / Lung Volume Reduction Surgery (LVRS)** / Lung Transplantation.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Unexplained weight loss, hemoptysis, or new focal chest pain/mass** $\rightarrow$ High suspicion for **bronchogenic carcinoma**; order CT Chest immediately.
> - **Resting SpO2 $\le 88\%$** $\rightarrow$ Severe chronic hypoxemia; check ABG and prescribe Long-Term Oxygen Therapy (LTOT) to prevent cor pulmonale and mortality.
> - **Morning headaches, daytime somnolence, or ankle edema** $\rightarrow$ Chronic hypercapnic respiratory failure and **Cor Pulmonale** (Group 3 pulmonary hypertension).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Bronchogenic Carcinoma:** Shared smoking risk factor; weight loss, hemoptysis, focal opacity.
> - **Congestive Heart Failure:** Orthopnea, PND, bibasilar crackles, elevated BNP, cardiomegaly, restrictive or normal spirometry.
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Asthma:** Younger onset, atopic history, diurnal symptom variability, complete spirometric reversibility, normal DLCO.
> - **Bronchiectasis:** Large volumes of daily purulent sputum, recurrent _Pseudomonas_ infections, bronchial dilation on HRCT.
>
> **Most Surprising**
>
> - **Tuberculosis:** Chronic productive cough, night sweats, apical cavitary lesions in endemic areas.
>
> **Less Common**
>
> - **Obliterative Bronchiolitis:** Younger non-smokers, history of rheumatoid arthritis, bone marrow/lung transplant, or fume exposure.
>

>
> -

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Reduce symptom burden (**CAT <10, mMRC <2**), improve exercise tolerance, and prevent **acute exacerbations**.
> - **Secondary Target:** Halt accelerated FEV1 decline, prevent cor pulmonale, and **reduce all-cause mortality**.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **N/A — Chronic outpatient management** (For acute worsening of dyspnea/sputum, treat with **controlled O2 [SpO2 88–92%], SABA+SAMA, Prednisone 40 mg x 5d, Antibiotics, and BiPAP** if pH $\le 7.35$; see [[Acute COPD Exacerbation]]).
>
> **First-Line Maintenance Pharmacotherapy (By GOLD ABE Group)**
>
> - **Prescribe a Short-Acting Bronchodilator PRN (SABA or SAMA) PLUS a single Long-Acting Bronchodilator (LABA or LAMA)** for **Group A** patients to relieve mild intermittent dyspnea.
> - **Initiate Dual Long-Acting Bronchodilator Therapy (LABA + LAMA in a single inhaler, e.g., Umeclidinium-Vilanterol or Tiotropium-Olodaterol)** as first-line therapy for **Group B** and **Group E** patients (with blood eosinophils <300 cells/$\mu$L) to maximize bronchodilation and reduce exacerbations.
> - **Initiate Single-Inhaler Triple Therapy (LABA + LAMA + ICS, e.g., Fluticasone-Umeclidinium-Vilanterol or Budesonide-Glycopyrrolate-Formoterol)** as first-line therapy in **Group E** patients if **blood eosinophils are $\ge 300$ cells/$\mu$L** (or if concomitant asthma is present) to reduce exacerbations and all-cause mortality.
>
> **Stepwise Escalation (Persistent Exacerbations on Dual/Triple Therapy)**
>
> - **Escalate from LABA+LAMA to Triple Therapy (LABA+LAMA+ICS)** in patients with persistent exacerbations if **blood eosinophils are $\ge 100$ cells/$\mu$L**.
> - **Add Dupilumab (300 mg SC q2w; Anti-IL-4R$\alpha$)** in patients with **persistent exacerbations despite maximal Triple Therapy, chronic bronchitis phenotype, AND blood eosinophils $\ge 300$ cells/$\mu$L** to significantly reduce moderate-to-severe exacerbations and improve FEV1 (FDA/GOLD approved 2024/2025).
> - **Add Ensifentrine (3 mg nebulized BID; Dual PDE3/PDE4 inhibitor)** as an add-on maintenance bronchodilator/anti-inflammatory in patients with persistent dyspnea/exacerbations.
> - **Add Roflumilast (500 mcg PO daily; PDE4 inhibitor)** if **blood eosinophils are <100 cells/$\mu$L** (or failing Triple Therapy) in patients with **FEV1 <50% predicted and Chronic Bronchitis phenotype** with prior hospitalization.
> - **Add Azithromycin (250 mg PO daily or 500 mg 3x/week)** in **former smokers** with persistent exacerbations despite Triple Therapy (screen for QTc prolongation, hearing loss, and nontuberculous mycobacteria first).
>
> **Disease-Modifying, Oxygen & Interventional Therapy**
>
> - **Enforce Smoking Cessation (Varenicline, Bupropion, or Nicotine Replacement + counseling)** and prescribe **Vaccines (Annual Influenza, PCV20 or PCV15+PPSV23, COVID-19, RSV [age $\ge 60$], and Tdap)** in all patients.
> - **Refer to Pulmonary Rehabilitation** for all **Group B and Group E** patients (improves exercise capacity, dyspnea, and hospitalizations).
> - **Prescribe Long-Term Oxygen Therapy (LTOT; $\ge 15$ hours/day titrated to SpO2 $\ge 90\%$)** to improve survival if:
>   - **Resting PaO2 $\le 55$ mmHg OR SpO2 $\le 88\%$** (confirmed twice over 3 weeks), OR
>   - **Resting PaO2 56–59 mmHg OR SpO2 89%** PLUS **Cor Pulmonale, right heart failure, or Polycythemia (Hct >55%)**.
> - **Prescribe Nocturnal Non-Invasive Positive Pressure Ventilation (Home BiPAP)** in patients with **persistent severe daytime hypercapnia (PaCO2 $\ge 52$ mmHg)** 2–4 weeks after an acute respiratory failure hospitalization.
>
> **Follow-up**
>
> - **Monitor inhaler technique, adherence, symptom burden (CAT/mMRC), and exacerbation frequency** every 3–6 months, with **annual spirometry** to track FEV1 trajectory.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid ICS Monotherapy** in COPD; it is ineffective at halting FEV1 decline or mortality Alone and increases pneumonia risk.
> - **Avoid adding or continuing ICS** in patients with **recurrent bacterial pneumonia, mycobacterial infection, or blood eosinophils <100 cells/$\mu$L** (withdraw ICS and step down to LABA+LAMA).
> - **Avoid combining a SAMA (Ipratropium) with a maintenance LAMA (Tiotropium/Umeclidinium)** to prevent additive systemic anticholinergic toxicity (acute urinary retention in BPH, narrow-angle glaucoma, severe dry mouth, tachycardia).
> - **Avoid Long-Term Oxygen Therapy in active smokers** without strict fire-safety education due to the high risk of fatal facial/airway thermal burns.
> - **Avoid withholding cardioselective $\beta_1$-blockers (Metoprolol, Bisoprolol)** when COPD patients have a clear cardiovascular indication (HFrEF, post-MI, AFib); cardioselective $\beta_1$-blockers are safe and reduce mortality in COPD with CVD.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Cor Pulmonale (Group 3 Pulmonary Hypertension)** $\rightarrow$ Chronic hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction causing RV hypertrophy/dilation, JVD, hepatomegaly, and peripheral edema $\rightarrow$ Treat underlying hypoxia with **LTOT** (primary therapy) and cautious diuretics for volume overload (pulmonary vasodilators like sildenafil are generally _not_ recommended).
> - **Secondary Spontaneous Pneumothorax** $\rightarrow$ Sudden severe dyspnea and unilateral absent breath sounds from ruptured emphysematous bullae $\rightarrow$ High mortality; requires immediate tube thoracostomy.
> - **Secondary Polycythemia** $\rightarrow$ Chronic hypoxia stimulates EPO $\rightarrow$ Hct >55% $\rightarrow$ Resolves with **LTOT** $\ge 15$ hrs/day.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 64-year-old male with a 45-pack-year smoking history presents with progressive exertional dyspnea (he stops for breath after walking 100 yards on level ground; mMRC = 3) and two ED visits for exacerbations requiring oral steroids in the past year. Post-bronchodilator spirometry shows FEV1/FVC of 0.54 and FEV1 48% predicted (GOLD 3, Group E); CBC shows a blood eosinophil count of 380 cells/$\mu$L, and resting SpO2 is 93%. You counsel him on smoking cessation with varenicline, initiate **Single-Inhaler Triple Therapy (LABA + LAMA + ICS)**, refer him to **Pulmonary Rehabilitation**, order a **Low-Dose CT Chest** for lung cancer screening and a one-time **Alpha-1 Antitrypsin level**, and administer pneumococcal, influenza, and RSV vaccines.
