> [!abstract] Community-Acquired Pneumonia
> An acute infection of the pulmonary parenchyma acquired outside of a hospital or long-term care setting (or manifesting <48 hours after hospital admission).

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - Microaspiration of oropharyngeal pathogens (_S. pneumoniae_ [most common], _H. influenzae_, _M. catarrhalis_) or inhalation of aerosols (_Mycoplasma, Chlamydia, Legionella_, respiratory viruses) overwhelms mucociliary clearance and alveolar macrophages.
> - Local cytokine release triggers capillary leak and neutrophil recruitment, filling alveoli with exudate (**consolidation**), causing **intrapulmonary shunting** and V/Q mismatch $\rightarrow$ Type 1 hypoxemic respiratory failure.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Do NOT routinely add anaerobic coverage (Metronidazole or Clindamycin) for suspected Aspiration Pneumonia** unless a **lung abscess or empyema** is present on imaging (ATS/IDSA guideline).
> - **Sputum Gram stain/culture and Blood Cultures are NOT recommended** for outpatients or non-severe routine inpatients; order them **ONLY in Severe CAP** or when empirically covering **MRSA or _Pseudomonas_** (prior isolation, hospitalization + IV antibiotics in last 90 days).
> - **Adjunctive IV Hydrocortisone (200 mg/day for 4–7 days + taper)** reduces 28-day mortality and intubation in **Severe ICU CAP** (CAPE COD trial; especially if CRP >150 mg/L, excluding influenza/fungal pneumonia).
> - **Clinical stability for discharge** requires 48–72 hours of improvement; **routine follow-up Chest X-Ray is NOT needed** if symptoms resolve within 5–7 days, _except_ in patients aged **$\ge 50$ with significant smoking history** (repeat CXR in 6–12 weeks to rule out an underlying obstructing bronchogenic carcinoma).

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - Acute onset of **fever/chills**, **productive cough** (purulent or rust-colored sputum), **pleuritic chest pain**, and **dyspnea**.
> - **Atypical / Pathogen Clues:**
>   - **Older / Immunocompromised:** May present **without fever or cough**, exhibiting only **acute confusion/delirium, falls, tachypnea, or decompensated comorbidity**.
>   - **_Legionella pneumophila_:** High fever, **GI symptoms (watery diarrhea)**, CNS confusion, **hyponatremia (<130 mEq/L)**, contaminated water/AC exposure.
>   - **_Mycoplasma pneumoniae_:** Young adults, subacute dry cough, headache, **bullous myringitis**, erythema multiforme, cold agglutinin hemolytic anemia.
>   - **_Staphylococcus aureus_:** Rapidly progressive cavitating/necrotizing pneumonia **post-influenza**.
>   - **_Klebsiella pneumoniae_:** Alcohol use disorder, diabetes, **"currant-jelly" sputum**, bulging interlobar fissure.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - Fever (>38°C), tachycardia, tachypnea (RR >20/min; most sensitive sign in elderly), SpO2 <92%.
> - **Signs of Consolidation:** Focal **bronchial breath sounds**, **inspiratory crackles (rales)**, **dullness to percussion**, **increased tactile fremitus**, and **egophony ("E-to-A" changes)** (_Note: decreased fremitus and absent breath sounds indicate a parapneumonic pleural effusion_).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Mandatory for Confirmed Diagnosis (Both 1 and 2 Required):**
>   1. **Clinical Signs/Symptoms of Acute Lower Respiratory Tract Infection:** $\ge 1$ of cough, sputum production, dyspnea, or pleuritic chest pain PLUS $\ge 1$ systemic feature (fever >38°C, hypothermia, diaphoresis, or leukocytosis/leukopenia).
>   2. **Demonstrable New Pulmonary Infiltrate on Imaging:** Lobar consolidation, interstitial infiltrate, or cavitation on **Chest X-Ray (CXR)**, **CT Chest**, or **Lung Ultrasound**.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **Outpatient:** **No routine labs or cultures required** (pulse oximetry + CXR only).
> - **Inpatient (Moderate/Severe):** **CBC, BMP (BUN, Cr, Na+, Glucose), Lactate, ABG** (if hypoxic/septic).
> - **Microbiology (ONLY in Severe CAP or MRSA/_Pseudomonas_ risk/coverage):**
>   - **2 sets of Blood Cultures** & **Sputum Gram stain/culture** prior to antibiotics.
>   - **Urinary Antigen Tests:** _Streptococcus pneumoniae_ and _Legionella pneumophila_ (serogroup 1).
>   - **MRSA Nasal Swab PCR:** High negative predictive value (>95%); **a negative swab allows immediate de-escalation of empiric vancomycin/linezolid**.
> - **Viral Testing:** **Nasopharyngeal PCR for Influenza and SARS-CoV-2** in all hospitalized patients (or when circulating in community).
> - **Procalcitonin:** Do _not_ use to withhold initial antibiotics in radiographically confirmed CAP; useful at 48–72h to guide **early antibiotic discontinuation**.
>
> **Imaging**
>
> - **Chest X-Ray (PA & Lateral):** Mandatory to confirm infiltrate and detect parapneumonic effusion or cavitation.
> - **Thoracic Ultrasound / Chest CT:** Order if parapneumonic effusion is suspected on CXR (>1 cm layering fluid on lateral decubitus or POCUS requires **diagnostic thoracentesis** to rule out empyema).
>
> **Severity & Risk Stratification (Admission vs. ICU Decision)**
>
> - **CURB-65 Score (1 point each):** **C**onfusion, **U**rea (BUN >19 mg/dL or >7 mmol/L), **R**espiratory rate $\ge 30$/min, **B**lood pressure (SBP <90 or DBP $\le 60$ mmHg), Age **$\ge 65$** years.
>   - _0–1:_ Outpatient; _2:_ Inpatient Ward; _$\ge 3$:_ Severe CAP (consider ICU).
> - **ATS/IDSA Severe CAP Criteria (Triggers ICU Admission):**
>   - **1 Major Criterion:** Septic shock requiring **vasopressors** OR respiratory failure requiring **invasive mechanical ventilation**.
>   - **OR $\ge 3$ Minor Criteria:** RR $\ge 30$/min, PaO2/FiO2 $\le 250$, Multilobar infiltrates, Confusion/disorientation, Uremia (BUN $\ge 20$ mg/dL), Leukopenia (WBC <4,000), Thrombocytopenia (<100,000), Hypothermia (<36°C), Hypotension requiring aggressive fluid resuscitation.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Hypotension refractory to fluids or Lactate >2 mmol/L** $\rightarrow$ Septic shock; start vasopressors and broad-spectrum IV antibiotics within 1 hour.
> - **PaO2/FiO2 $\le 250$ or RR $\ge 30$/min** $\rightarrow$ Impending respiratory failure; initiate High-Flow Nasal Cannula (HFNC) and transfer to ICU.
> - **Persistent fever/leukocytosis after 72 hours of appropriate antibiotics + dullness at lung base** $\rightarrow$ Suspect **complicated parapneumonic effusion / empyema**; perform immediate POCUS and diagnostic thoracentesis.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Pulmonary Embolism with Infarction:** Sudden pleuritic pain, hemoptysis, wedge-shaped infiltrate (Hampton hump), lack of infectious prodrome.
> - **Acute Cardiogenic Pulmonary Edema:** Bilateral symmetric perihilar opacities, elevated BNP, S3 gallop, rapid response to diuretics.
> - **Obstructing Bronchogenic Carcinoma:** Post-obstructive pneumonia that fails to clear or recurs in the same lobe in a smoker.
> - **Pulmonary Tuberculosis:** Chronic productive cough (>2–3 weeks), night sweats, weight loss, upper lobe cavitary infiltrate.
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Acute Bronchitis:** Acute cough <3 weeks with **normal vital signs and clear Chest X-Ray** (no antibiotics indicated).
> - **Acute COPD or Asthma Exacerbation:** Diffuse wheezing without focal consolidation on CXR.
>
> **Most Surprising**
>
> - **Cryptogenic Organizing Pneumonia (COP) or Acute Eosinophilic Pneumonia:** Subacute "non-resolving pneumonia" failing multiple courses of antibiotics.
>
> **Less Common**
>
> - **Hypersensitivity Pneumonitis / Drug-Induced Pneumonitis** (e.g., amiodarone, nitrofurantoin, checkpoint inhibitors).
>

>

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Achieve **Clinical Stability within 48–72 hours** (Temperature $\le 37.8^\circ$C, HR $\le 100$ bpm, RR $\le 24$/min, SBP $\ge 90$ mmHg, **SpO2 $\ge 90\%$ on room air**, normal mental status, and ability to maintain oral intake).
> - **Secondary Target:** Eradicate the causative pathogen with a **short 5-day antibiotic course**, prevent progression to sepsis/ARDS, and drain any complicated pleural space infection.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Administer Empiric IV Antibiotics within 1 hour, 30 mL/kg IV Crystalloid Bolus (for hypotension/lactate $\ge 4$), and Norepinephrine** in patients presenting with **Severe CAP + Septic Shock** to prevent multiorgan failure and death.
> - **Initiate High-Flow Nasal Cannula (HFNC) or Invasive Mechanical Ventilation** immediately if SpO2 <90% despite standard oxygen (target SpO2 92–96%, or 88–92% if concurrent COPD).
>
> **Outpatient Empiric Therapy (CURB-65 0–1 / PSI I–II; Duration: Minimum 5 Days)**
>
> - **Prescribe High-Dose Amoxicillin (1 g PO TID) OR Doxycycline (100 mg PO BID)** as first-line monotherapy in **healthy outpatients without comorbidities** (_Azithromycin 500 mg day 1 then 250 mg daily is used ONLY if local pneumococcal macrolide resistance is <25%_).
> - **Prescribe Combination Therapy with Amoxicillin-Clavulanate (875/125 mg PO BID) or Cefpodoxime PLUS Azithromycin or Doxycycline — OR — Respiratory Fluoroquinolone Monotherapy (Levofloxacin 750 mg PO daily or Moxifloxacin 400 mg PO daily)** in **outpatients WITH comorbidities** (chronic heart, lung, liver, or renal disease; diabetes; alcoholism; malignancy; asplenia) or antibiotic use in the past 3 months.
>
> **Inpatient Non-Severe Empiric Therapy (CURB-65 = 2 / PSI III–IV)**
>
> - **Administer a $\beta$-Lactam (Ceftriaxone 1–2 g IV daily OR Ampicillin-Sulbactam 1.5–3 g IV q6h) PLUS a Macrolide (Azithromycin 500 mg IV/PO daily) — OR — Monotherapy with a Respiratory Fluoroquinolone (Levofloxacin 750 mg IV/PO daily)** upon admission.
>
> **Severe ICU CAP & MDR Risk Factors**
>
> - **Administer a $\beta$-Lactam (Ceftriaxone 2 g IV daily or Ampicillin-Sulbactam) PLUS EITHER Azithromycin (500 mg IV daily) OR Levofloxacin (750 mg IV daily)** for **all Severe ICU CAP** (_Fluoroquinolone monotherapy is contraindicated in ICU CAP_).
> - **Add Anti-MRSA Coverage (Vancomycin 15–20 mg/kg IV q8–12h OR Linezolid 600 mg IV/PO q12h)** ONLY if the patient has **prior respiratory isolation of MRSA** or **hospitalization + parenteral antibiotics within the last 90 days** (obtain MRSA nasal swab PCR to de-escalate at 48h if negative).
> - **Swap the $\beta$-Lactam to an Antipseudomonal Agent (Piperacillin-Tazobactam 4.5 g IV q6h, Cefepime 2 g IV q8h, or Meropenem 1 g IV q8h)** ONLY if the patient has **prior respiratory isolation of _Pseudomonas_**, severe structural lung disease (bronchiectasis), or **hospitalization + IV antibiotics in the last 90 days**.
> - **Add Oseltamivir (75 mg PO BID for 5 days)** immediately if testing is positive for **Influenza** (regardless of symptom duration >48h in hospitalized patients).
> - **Administer Adjunctive IV Hydrocortisone (200 mg/day continuous infusion or 50 mg IV q6h for 4–7 days, then taper)** in **Severe ICU CAP** (especially with septic shock or high inflammatory markers, provided Influenza and _Aspergillus_ are excluded) to reduce mortality and need for intubation.
>
> **Transition, Duration & Follow-up**
>
> - **Switch from IV to Oral Antibiotics** as soon as the patient is hemodynamically stable, clinically improving, afebrile, and able to swallow medications.
> - **Stop Antibiotics after 5 Days Total** in patients who have been afebrile and clinically stable for $\ge 48-72$ hours (_extend to 7 days only for confirmed MRSA or Pseudomonas, or 4–6 weeks for lung abscess/empyema_).
> - **Order a Follow-up Chest X-Ray in 6–12 weeks** in patients aged **$\ge 50$ years (especially current/former smokers)** to ensure complete radiographic resolution and exclude an occult obstructing lung malignancy.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Fluoroquinolone Monotherapy in Severe ICU CAP**; combination therapy with a $\beta$-lactam + macrolide (or $\beta$-lactam + fluoroquinolone) is mandatory to reduce ICU mortality.
> - **Avoid Macrolide Monotherapy (Azithromycin alone)** in regions where _S. pneumoniae_ macrolide resistance is **$\ge 25\%$** (which includes most of the US and Europe) or in any patient with comorbidities.
> - **Avoid routine Anaerobic Coverage (Metronidazole or Clindamycin)** for suspected aspiration pneumonia without lung abscess or empyema; it adds no clinical benefit and increases _Clostridioides difficile_ colitis risk.
> - **Avoid Systemic Corticosteroids in Influenza Pneumonia or Fungal Pneumonia**, as steroids increase mortality and viral shedding in influenza.
> - **Avoid using the outdated "HCAP" (Healthcare-Associated Pneumonia) category** to reflexively prescribe vancomycin + piperacillin-tazobactam to nursing home or dialysis patients without specific MRSA/_Pseudomonas_ risk factors.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Complicated Parapneumonic Effusion / Empyema** $\rightarrow$ Persistent fever/leukocytosis despite 72h of antibiotics; diagnostic thoracentesis shows **frank pus, positive Gram stain/culture, pleural pH <7.20, glucose <40–60 mg/dL, or LDH >1,000 IU/L** $\rightarrow$ Requires **immediate Tube Thoracostomy (chest tube drainage)** +/- intrapleural tPA/DNase, NOT antibiotics alone.
> - **Lung Abscess / Necrotizing Pneumonia** $\rightarrow$ Cavitary lesion with an air-fluid level on CXR/CT (often _S. aureus_, _Klebsiella_, or polymicrobial anaerobes) $\rightarrow$ Treat with prolonged antibiotics (3–6 weeks of Ampicillin-Sulbactam or Carbapenem); **avoid percutaneous drainage** unless medical therapy fails.
> - **Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) & Septic Shock** $\rightarrow$ Rapidly worsening bilateral infiltrates and P/F ratio <300 $\rightarrow$ Lung-protective ventilation (TV 6 mL/kg PBW) and vasopressor support.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 72-year-old male with hypertension and type 2 diabetes presents to the ED with 3 days of fever (38.9°C), productive rust-colored cough, and right-sided pleuritic chest pain. Vitals show BP 118/72 mmHg, HR 104 bpm, RR 26/min, and SpO2 90% on room air; he is alert and oriented, labs show BUN 24 mg/dL and WBC 15,400/$\mu$L, and CXR confirms a right lower lobe consolidation without effusion (CURB-65 score = 2). You admit him to the medical ward, administer supplemental O2 to maintain SpO2 >92%, and initiate **Ceftriaxone 1 g IV daily plus Azithromycin 500 mg PO daily** (without routine blood/sputum cultures or anaerobic coverage), planning to transition to oral therapy for a total 5-day course once he is afebrile and stable for 48 hours.
