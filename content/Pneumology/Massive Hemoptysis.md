> [!abstract] Massive Hemoptysis
> Expectoration of a rapidly life-threatening volume of blood from the lower respiratory tract that causes acute airway obstruction, asphyxiation, or hemodynamic instability.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **>90% originates from the high-pressure Bronchial Artery system** (systemic pressure) due to chronic airway inflammation/neovascularization (bronchiectasis, TB, malignancy, cystic fibrosis, aspergilloma); <10% arises from the low-pressure pulmonary artery system (e.g., **Rasmussen aneurysm** in cavitary TB, swan-ganz perforation).
> - **Patients die from asphyxiation (clot obstructing the conducting airways; anatomical dead space is only ~150 mL), NOT exsanguination.**

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **"Bleeding Side Down":** Immediately place the patient in the **lateral decubitus position with the bleeding lung dependent (down)** to prevent gravity-driven blood spillage and asphyxiation of the healthy, non-bleeding lung.
> - **Intubate with a Large-Bore Endotracheal Tube ($\ge 8.0-8.5$ mm ID):** A standard 7.0–7.5 mm ETT is too narrow to permit passage of a therapeutic flexible bronchoscope or bronchial blocker alongside ventilation.
> - **Bronchial Artery Embolization (BAE) is the definitive first-line intervention** (>85–90% immediate success rate); emergency surgery is strictly a last resort due to ~20–40% mortality.
> - **Inhaled Tranexamic Acid (TXA; 500 mg nebulized)** is a safe, rapid bedside temporizing measure that significantly reduces bleeding volume and need for invasive procedures.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - Coughing up bright red, frothy blood or large blood clots.
> - **Etiologic clues:** History of **bronchiectasis**, **tuberculosis**, **bronchogenic carcinoma**, **mycetoma/aspergilloma** in a prior cavity, cystic fibrosis, lung abscess, or anticoagulant/antiplatelet use.
> - **True Hemoptysis vs. Pseudohemoptysis:** Bright red, frothy, alkaline pH, mixed with sputum/macrophages, preceded by cough (vs. **Hematemesis**: dark red/coffee-ground, acidic pH, mixed with food, preceded by nausea/vomiting; or **Epistaxis**: posterior pharyngeal drip).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - Tachypnea, hypoxia, anxiety, gurgling/stridor over central airways, and reduced breath sounds or unilateral coarse crackles/rhonchi over the bleeding lobe.
> - **Signs of impending asphyxiation:** Cyanosis, inability to clear secretions, rapid desaturation, and bradycardia.
> - **Signs of autoimmune/vasculitis (Diffuse Alveolar Hemorrhage):** Hematuria/glomerulonephritis, sinusitis, palpable purpura (**Goodpasture/anti-GBM**, **GPA/Wegener's**, **Microscopic Polyangiitis**).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Clinical & Quantitative Thresholds (Any of the following):**
>   - **Functional Definition (Life-Threatening Hemoptysis):** _Any_ volume of expectorated blood causing **airway obstruction, hypoxemic respiratory failure, or hemodynamic instability**.
>   - **Volume Cutoffs:** **$\ge 100-150$ mL in a single expectoration** (or **>100 mL/hour**), OR **$\ge 300-600$ mL over 24 hours**.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **Type and Crossmatch (2–4 units pRBCs), CBC, PT/INR, aPTT, Fibrinogen:** Identify and correct coagulopathy or thrombocytopenia immediately.
> - **ABG, CMP, Urinalysis:** Check renal function and hematuria/red cell casts (pulmonary-renal syndrome).
> - **Sputum AFB, Fungal Culture, ANCA, Anti-GBM:** Order once stabilized to identify infectious or vasculitic etiology.
>
> **Imaging**
>
> - **Portable Chest X-Ray (CXR):** Rapid bedside screen to **lateralize the bleeding side** (shows cavitary lesion, mass, or unilateral alveolar infiltrate in ~50–60% of cases; note that aspirated blood can cause bilateral infiltrates).
> - **CT Chest Angiography (CTA; bronchial artery protocol):** **Gold-standard diagnostic and localization test in stable patients** (identifies bleeding site in >80–90% of cases and maps aberrant bronchial/non-bronchial arteries for IR embolization).
> - **Flexible / Rigid Bronchoscopy:** Diagnostic _and_ therapeutic; mandatory at bedside if the patient is **too unstable for CT transport** to clear airway clots, lateralize bleeding, and place an endobronchial blocker.
>
> **Referrals**
>
> - **Interventional Radiology (IR), Pulmonology/Interventional Pulmonology, and Thoracic Surgery:** Activate a multidisciplinary massive hemoptysis alert immediately upon presentation.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Difficulty speaking, stridor, or rapid desaturation** $\rightarrow$ Central airway clot occlusion; requires immediate intubation and bronchoscopic suctioning.
> - **Hemoptysis in a patient with a pulmonary artery catheter (Swan-Ganz)** $\rightarrow$ **Pulmonary artery rupture** (50% mortality); immediately **deflate balloon, do NOT pull catheter back completely, pull back slightly and re-inflate balloon to tamponade**, place bleeding side down, and call IR/Thoracic Surgery.
> - **Tracheostomy patient with sudden brisk bleeding around/through cannula** $\rightarrow$ **Tracheoinnominate Artery Fistula (TIF)**; immediately **hyperinflate tracheostomy cuff** (or intubate orally and perform **Utley maneuver**: digital compression of the innominate artery anteriorly against the manubrium via the tracheostomy stoma) and go emergently to the OR.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Massive Upper GI Bleed (Hematemesis) with Aspiration:** History of cirrhosis/ulcers, coffee-ground emesis, melena, normal CXR initially.
> - **Pulmonary Embolism with Infarction:** Pleuritic chest pain, tachycardia, usually mild-to-moderate hemoptysis (rarely massive unless receiving thrombolytics).
> - **Diffuse Alveolar Hemorrhage (DAH):** Triad of hemoptysis (absent in 1/3), diffuse bilateral alveolar infiltrates, and dropping hemoglobin; treated with **high-dose IV Methylprednisolone + Plasmapheresis/Rituximab**, NOT embolization.
> - **Aortobronchial Fistula:** History of thoracic aortic aneurysm or prior aortic graft repair; universally fatal without immediate endovascular stent-grafting (TEVAR).
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Bronchiectasis / Cystic Fibrosis Exacerbation**
> - **Active or Post-Tubercular Cavitary Disease / Aspergilloma**
> - **Bronchogenic Carcinoma** (especially squamous cell carcinoma)
>
> **Most Surprising**
>
> - **Mitral Stenosis:** Rupture of dilated bronchial veins from severe left atrial hypertension.
>
> **Less Common**
>
> - **Catamenial Hemoptysis:** Thoracic endometriosis causing cyclical hemoptysis during menses.
>
> **Non-Critical**
>
> - **Severe Epistaxis / Pharyngolaryngeal Bleeding:** Identified on nasopharyngeal exam.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** **Prevent asphyxiation** by protecting the non-bleeding lung and maintaining **SpO2 >92%**, while achieving rapid **definitive hemostasis**.
> - **Secondary Target:** Reverse coagulopathy, maintain hemodynamic stability (MAP $\ge 65$ mmHg, Hb >7–8 g/dL), and treat the underlying etiology.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (First 0–15 Minutes)**
>
> - **Position the patient in the Lateral Decubitus Position with the bleeding lung DOWN** immediately once the bleeding side is known or suspected to prevent blood from spilling into and drowning the healthy contralateral lung.
> - **Intubate with a Single-Lumen Large-Bore Endotracheal Tube ($\ge 8.0-8.5$ mm ID)** if the patient has severe hypoxia, poor cough/exhaustion, or ongoing brisk bleeding to allow large-bore suctioning and passage of a therapeutic flexible bronchoscope.
> - **Perform Selective Mainstem Intubation (advance ETT into the non-bleeding mainstem bronchus)** or **place an Endobronchial Blocker (e.g., Arndt or Fogarty balloon catheter)** under bronchoscopic guidance if brisk bleeding threatens the healthy lung to isolate and ventilate the unaffected lung.
>
> **Medical & Bronchoscopic Temporizing Therapy**
>
> - **Administer Nebulized Tranexamic Acid (TXA; 500 mg in 5 mL normal saline q8h) OR IV TXA (1 g IV over 10 min)** immediately at bedside to inhibit local fibrinolysis and reduce bleeding volume.
> - **Reverse Anticoagulation and Coagulopathy STAT:** Give **4-factor PCC + Vitamin K** for warfarin, **Andexanet alfa / PCC** for factor Xa inhibitors, **Idarucizumab** for dabigatran, **Protamine** for heparin, **Platelets** if <50,000/$\mu$L, and **Cryoprecipitate** if fibrinogen <150 mg/dL.
> - **Perform Therapeutic Bronchoscopy (Rigid or Flexible)** in unstable patients to suction obstructive airway clots, apply **iced normal saline lavage (50 mL aliquots)**, instill **topical Epinephrine (1:10,000 or 1:20,000) or cold TXA**, and tamponade the bleeding segment with a balloon blocker.
>
> **Definitive Intervention & Escalation**
>
> - **Perform CT Chest Angiography (CTA) followed by Bronchial Artery Embolization (BAE)** via Interventional Radiology as the **definitive first-line treatment** for all localized massive hemoptysis once the airway is secured.
> - **Perform Emergency Surgical Lung Resection (Lobectomy)** _only_ if BAE fails or is technically impossible, the bleeding source is definitively localized to a single resectable lobe (e.g., localized cavitary aspergilloma, trauma, iatrogenic PA rupture), and the patient has adequate baseline pulmonary reserve.
>
> **Disposition & Follow-up**
>
> - **Admit all patients with Massive Hemoptysis to the ICU** for continuous airway monitoring, serial hemoglobin checks, and observation for early re-bleeding (occurs in 10–20% post-BAE).

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid placing the bleeding side UP**; gravity will drain blood directly into the healthy dependent lung, causing rapid fatal asphyxiation.
> - **Avoid routine initial use of Double-Lumen Endotracheal Tubes (DLT)** in emergencies unless placed by an experienced thoracic anesthesiologist; DLTs are difficult to place in a bloody airway, easily malposition, and their narrow lumens clot off quickly and **cannot accommodate a standard therapeutic bronchoscope**.
> - **Avoid cough suppressants (codeine/opioids) and sedatives** in non-intubated patients, as an intact cough reflex is essential to clear blood clots from the trachea.
> - **Avoid Bronchial Artery Embolization if the Anterior Spinal Artery (Artery of Adamkiewicz; classic "hairpin loop" on angiography) originates from the target bronchial artery** to prevent catastrophic **anterior spinal cord infarction / paraplegia**.
> - **Avoid emergency surgical lobectomy in patients with diffuse bilateral disease, active pulmonary tuberculosis, or non-localized bleeding** due to prohibitive (>40%) perioperative mortality.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Asphyxiation / Airway Obstruction** $\rightarrow$ Sudden hypoxia, silent chest, high peak ventilator pressures,PEA arrest from central blood clot $\rightarrow$ Immediate rigid/flexible bronchoscopic clot extraction or cryotherapy clot removal (cryoprobe freezes to clot and pulls it out _en bloc_).
> - **Anterior Spinal Cord Infarction (Post-BAE)** $\rightarrow$ Acute bilateral flaccid paraplegia and loss of pain/temperature sensation with preserved proprioception (<1–6% risk after BAE) $\rightarrow$ Prevent via super-selective microcatheter embolization distal to spinal branches.
> - **Recurrent Hemoptysis** $\rightarrow$ Early re-bleeding (<1 month) due to incomplete embolization or non-bronchial systemic collaterals (e.g., internal mammary, intercostal, inferior phrenic arteries); late re-bleeding (>1 month) due to disease progression (e.g., aspergilloma) $\rightarrow$ Repeat CTA/BAE or elective surgical resection.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 54-year-old male with a history of prior cavitary right upper lobe tuberculosis presents coughing up >250 mL of bright red blood over the past hour; his SpO2 is 86% on room air, HR is 118 bpm, and right upper lobe rhonchi are audible. You immediately place him in the **right lateral decubitus position (bleeding side down)**, administer nebulized TXA (500 mg) and high-flow O2, and intubate him with an 8.5 mm endotracheal tube. After stabilizing his airway and confirming a right upper lobe mycetoma with active bronchial extravasation on CTA, Interventional Radiology performs successful Bronchial Artery Embolization (BAE) and he is admitted to the ICU.
