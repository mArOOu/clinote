> [!abstract] Asthma
> A heterogeneous chronic airway disorder characterized by chronic airway inflammation, bronchial hyperresponsiveness, and variable, reversible expiratory airflow limitation.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - Predominantly **Type 2 (T2-high) inflammation** driven by Th2/ILC2 cells (IL-4 $\rightarrow$ IgE class-switching; IL-5 $\rightarrow$ eosinophil recruitment; IL-13 $\rightarrow$ mucus hypersecretion & hyperresponsiveness).
> - Exposure to triggers causes acute bronchospasm, mucosal edema, and mucus plugging; chronic untreated inflammation leads to irreversible **airway remodeling** (subepithelial fibrosis, smooth muscle hypertrophy).

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **NEVER treat asthma with SABA monotherapy (GINA Track 1):** Every teen/adult with asthma must receive an **ICS-containing inhaler** (preferred: as-needed low-dose **ICS-Formoterol** as both controller and reliever [MART]) to prevent severe exacerbations and asthma deaths.
> - **Normal spirometry between episodes does NOT rule out asthma:** If clinical suspicion is high and baseline spirometry is normal, order a **Methacholine Bronchoprovocation Challenge** (negative test rules out asthma) or measure **FeNO**.
> - **Samter's Triad (AERD):** Asthma + Chronic Rhinosinusitis with Nasal Polyps + acute bronchospasm/nasal congestion within 30–180 minutes of ingesting **Aspirin/NSAIDs** (mediated by COX-1 inhibition shifting arachidonic acid to leukotrienes; treat with **Montelukast**).
> - **Check inhaler technique and adherence FIRST** before stepping up pharmacotherapy; up to 70–80% of patients use their inhalers incorrectly.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Episodic and variable** wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness, and cough (classically **worse at night or early morning**).
> - **Triggers:** Viral URIs, allergens (dust mites, pollen, pets), cold/dry air, exercise, occupational irritants, beta-blockers, or aspirin/NSAIDs.
> - **Atopic triad:** Personal or family history of **allergic rhinitis** and **atopic dermatitis (eczema)**.
> - **Atypical presentations:** **Cough-Variant Asthma** (chronic dry cough as the sole symptom, prominent at night; normal exam).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - Often **completely normal** when stable between episodes.
> - **During symptomatic periods:** Diffuse polyphonic **expiratory wheezing** and prolonged expiratory phase.
> - **Associated atopic signs:** Pale, boggy nasal turbinates, **nasal polyps** (suspect AERD), or flexural lichenified eczema.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Mandatory for Confirmed Diagnosis (Both 1 and 2 required, ideally prior to starting ICS):**
>   1. **History of variable respiratory symptoms** (wheeze, shortness of breath, chest tightness, and/or cough that vary over time and in intensity).
>   2. **Confirmed variable expiratory airflow limitation** via baseline **reduced FEV1/FVC ratio (<0.75–0.80 in adults or <LLN)** PLUS at least ONE of the following:
>      - **Positive Bronchodilator Responsiveness (Reversibility):** Increase in **FEV1 or FVC of $\ge 12\%$ AND $\ge 200$ mL** from baseline 15 minutes after 200–400 mcg albuterol (_or $\ge 10\%$ of predicted FEV1/FVC per 2021 ERS/ATS criteria_).
>      - **Excessive PEF Variability:** Average daily diurnal PEF variability **>10%** over 2 weeks.
>      - **Significant Response to 4 Weeks of ICS Controller Therapy:** Increase in **FEV1 by $\ge 12\%$ and $\ge 200$ mL** from baseline.
>      - **Positive Exercise Challenge:** Fall in **FEV1 of $>10\%$ AND $>200$ mL** from baseline.
>      - **Positive Bronchial Challenge Test (Methacholine / Histamine):** Fall in **FEV1 of $\ge 20\%$** ($\text{PC}_{20} \le 8$ mg/mL) from baseline.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **Fractional Exhaled Nitric Oxide (FeNO):** Surrogate marker of Type 2 eosinophilic airway inflammation (**$\ge 40-50$ ppb** supports T2-high asthma and predicts strong ICS response; useful for monitoring adherence).
> - **CBC with Differential (Blood Eosinophils) & Total/Allergen-Specific IgE:** Mandatory workup in **Severe Asthma (Step 5)** to phenotype for targeted biologic therapy (**Eosinophils $\ge 150-300$ cells/$\mu$L** indicates eosinophilic phenotype).
> - **DLCO (Diffusing Capacity):** Normal or slightly elevated in asthma (distinguishes from emphysema/COPD where DLCO is reduced).
>
> **Imaging**
>
> - **Chest X-Ray (CXR):** Not routinely required for diagnosis; order at baseline only to exclude mimics (CHF, mass, bronchiectasis) or if atypical features (hemoptysis, weight loss, persistent focal wheeze) are present.
>
> **Referrals**
>
> - **Pulmonology / Allergy & Immunology:** Unconfirmed diagnosis after workup, suspected occupational asthma, **Severe Asthma (Step 4/5)** uncontrolled on medium/high-dose ICS-LABA, $\ge 2$ oral steroid bursts/year, or history of near-fatal asthma.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **SABA overuse ($\ge 3$ canisters/year or $\ge 1$ canister/month)** $\rightarrow$ Directly associated with severe exacerbations and asthma-related death.
> - **Unilateral / Fixed Monophonic Wheeze** $\rightarrow$ Indicates focal bronchial obstruction (foreign body, endobronchial tumor); requires bronchoscopy/CT.
> - **Asthma + peripheral eosinophilia (>1,000/$\mu$L) + sinusitis + neuropathy/purpura** $\rightarrow$ Suspect **Eosinophilic Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis (EGPA / Churg-Strauss)**.
> - **Asthma + central bronchiectasis + fleeting pulmonary infiltrates + brown mucus plugs** $\rightarrow$ Suspect **Allergic Bronchopulmonary Aspergillosis (ABPA)** (check total IgE >1,000 IU/mL and _Aspergillus_-specific IgE).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Acute Decompensated Heart Failure ("Cardiac Asthma"):** Orthopnea, PND, elevated JVP, bibasilar crackles, elevated BNP.
> - **Central Airway Obstruction / Tumor / Foreign Body:** Stridor or fixed monophonic wheeze, flattened inspiratory/expiratory flow-volume loops.
> - **Pulmonary Embolism:** Acute dyspnea, tachycardia, pleuritic pain, risk factors.
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **COPD:** Age >40, >10–20 pack-year smoking history, incomplete reversibility (post-BD FEV1/FVC <0.70), reduced DLCO.
> - **Upper Airway Cough Syndrome (Post-Nasal Drip) or GERD:** Chronic cough without airflow obstruction on spirometry.
> - **Inducible Laryngeal Obstruction (Vocal Cord Dysfunction):** Inspiratory stridor localized to the throat, abrupt onset/resolution, normal SpO2, flattened inspiratory loop on spirometry.
>
> **Most Surprising**
>
> - **EGPA (Churg-Strauss Syndrome) or ABPA:** Refractory "asthma" with systemic vasculitis or bronchiectasis.
>
> **Less Common**
>
> - **Bronchiectasis:** Daily copious purulent sputum, tram-track/signet-ring sign on High-Resolution CT.
>

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Achieve **Symptom Control** (daytime symptoms $\le 2$ days/week, reliever needed $\le 2$ days/week, **zero nocturnal awakenings**, and **zero activity limitation**).
> - **Secondary Target:** **Risk Reduction** (prevent exacerbations, avoid oral corticosteroid toxicity, preserve normal lung function/FEV1, and prevent airway remodeling).
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **N/A — Chronic outpatient condition** (For acute flares, administer **SABA + Ipratropium + Systemic Prednisone 40–60 mg PO for 5–7 days**; see [[Acute Severe Asthma]]).
>
> **First-Line Chronic Therapy — GINA Track 1 (Preferred: ICS-Formoterol MART Regimen)**
>
> - **Prescribe As-Needed Low-Dose ICS-Formoterol (Budesonide-Formoterol 160/4.5 mcg, 1–2 inhalations PRN symptoms)** for **Steps 1 & 2** (symptoms <4–5 days/week) to provide rapid bronchodilation while simultaneously treating airway inflammation and cutting severe exacerbations by >60% vs. SABA alone.
> - **Step Up to Low-Dose ICS-Formoterol Maintenance AND Reliever Therapy (MART; 1 inhalation BID/QD maintenance + PRN reliever)** at **Step 3** when symptoms occur most days or cause nocturnal awakening $\ge 1$/week.
> - **Step Up to Medium-Dose ICS-Formoterol Maintenance (2 inhalations BID) + Low-Dose ICS-Formoterol PRN Reliever** at **Step 4** for patients with daily uncontrolled symptoms or low lung function despite Step 3 therapy.
> - _Alternative (GINA Track 2 — if ICS-Formoterol unavailable):_ **Step 1:** Take low-dose ICS whenever SABA is taken; **Step 2:** Daily low-dose ICS + PRN SABA; **Step 3:** Daily low-dose ICS-LABA + PRN SABA; **Step 4:** Medium/high-dose ICS-LABA + PRN SABA.
>
> **Adjuncts & Step 5 Escalation (Severe Asthma)**
>
> - **Add a LAMA (Tiotropium 2.5–5 mcg daily or Triple-Inhaler ICS-LABA-LAMA)** at **Step 4/5** if asthma remains uncontrolled on medium/high-dose ICS-LABA to improve FEV1 and increase time to exacerbation.
> - **Add a Leukotriene Receptor Antagonist (Montelukast 10 mg PO daily)** as an adjunct or alternative in patients with **Aspirin-Exacerbated Respiratory Disease (AERD)**, exercise-induced asthma, or concurrent allergic rhinitis.
> - **Initiate Targeted Biologic Therapy at Step 5** based on inflammatory phenotype for severe exacerbations despite high-dose ICS-LABA-LAMA:
>   - **Allergic Asthma (Elevated IgE + perennial aeroallergen sensitization):** **Omalizumab** (Anti-IgE).
>   - **Severe Eosinophilic Asthma (Blood Eosinophils $\ge 150-300/\mu$L):** **Mepolizumab / Reslizumab** (Anti-IL-5) or **Benralizumab** (Anti-IL-5R$\alpha$).
>   - **Type 2 Asthma (Eosinophils $\ge 150/\mu$L OR FeNO $\ge 25$ ppb) or OCS-dependent / Nasal Polyps / Eczema:** **Dupilumab** (Anti-IL-4R$\alpha$ blocking IL-4/IL-13).
>   - **Broad Severe Asthma (including T2-low / Non-Eosinophilic):** **Tezepelumab** (Anti-TSLP).
>
> **Non-Pharmacological & Follow-up**
>
> - **Provide a Written Asthma Action Plan** and verify **inhaler + spacer technique** and adherence at every clinical visit.
> - **Reassess symptom control in 1–3 months** after starting or stepping up treatment; **consider stepping down ICS dose by 25–50%** once good asthma control has been maintained for **$\ge 3$ months**.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid LABA Monotherapy (e.g., Salmeterol or Formoterol without an ICS)** in asthma; unopposed LABA masks worsening airway inflammation and **increases the risk of asthma-related death (FDA Black Box Warning)**.
> - **Avoid SABA Monotherapy (e.g., Albuterol PRN alone)**; regular SABA use downregulates $\beta_2$-receptors, increases bronchial hyperresponsiveness, and increases fatal exacerbation risk.
> - **Avoid Non-Selective Beta-Blockers (e.g., Propranolol, Nadolol, Timolol eye drops)** in asthmatics to prevent life-threatening bronchospasm (cardioselective $\beta_1$-blockers like metoprolol/bisoprolol may be used cautiously if strongly indicated for HFrEF/MI).
> - **Avoid Aspirin and NSAIDs** in patients with nasal polyps or history of AERD to prevent severe acute bronchospasm.
> - **Avoid forgetting to counsel on Montelukast neuropsychiatric risks (FDA Black Box Warning):** Screen for depression, agitation, vivid nightmares, and suicidal ideation when prescribing leukotriene modifiers.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Inhaled Corticosteroid (ICS) Local Side Effects** $\rightarrow$ Oral candidiasis (thrush) and dysphonia/hoarseness (vocal cord myopathy) $\rightarrow$ Prevent by **using a valved holding chamber (spacer)** with MDIs and **rinsing mouth with water ("swish and spit")** after every use.
> - **Airway Remodeling** $\rightarrow$ Chronic untreated airway inflammation causing fixed, irreversible airflow obstruction (FEV1/FVC persistently <0.70) $\rightarrow$ Prevent with early, consistent ICS therapy.
> - **Allergic Bronchopulmonary Aspergillosis (ABPA)** $\rightarrow$ Worsening asthma, brownish mucus plugs, central bronchiectasis, Total IgE >1,000 IU/mL $\rightarrow$ Treat with systemic corticosteroids (prednisone taper) +/- itraconazole/voriconazole.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 26-year-old female with a history of seasonal allergic rhinitis presents with episodic wheezing, chest tightness, and a dry cough that wakes her from sleep twice a week; she currently uses an albuterol inhaler 4 times per week. Spirometry reveals an FEV1/FVC of 0.68, with a 15% and 310 mL increase in FEV1 following inhaled albuterol, confirming asthma. Following GINA Track 1 guidelines, you discontinue SABA-only therapy and initiate **low-dose Budesonide-Formoterol (ICS-Formoterol) as a daily maintenance and reliever therapy (MART; Step 3)**, verify her inhaler technique, provide a written Asthma Action Plan, and schedule follow-up in 6 weeks.
