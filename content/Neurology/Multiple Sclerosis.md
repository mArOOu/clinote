> [!abstract] Multiple Sclerosis
> Chronic, immune-mediated inflammatory neurodegenerative disease of the central nervous system (CNS) characterized by **perivenular autoimmune demyelination**, reactive astrocytic gliosis ("sclerotic plaques"), and progressive axonal loss, separated in **time and anatomical space**.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Autoimmune Demyelination:** Autoreactive myelin-specific $\text{CD4}^+$ ($\text{T}_\text{H}1/\text{T}_\text{H}17$) and $\text{CD8}^+$ T-cells and B-cells cross the blood-brain barrier $\rightarrow$ target myelin basic protein and oligodendrocytes $\rightarrow$ complement activation and macrophage-mediated myelin stripping.
> - **Conduction Failure:** Loss of myelin exposes internodal $\text{K}^+$ channels, preventing saltatory conduction; chronic denudation leads to calcium-mediated mitochondrial failure and irreversible **axonal transection**.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Internuclear Ophthalmoplegia (INO):** Demyelination of the **Medial Longitudinal Fasciculus (MLF)**; on horizontal gaze, the ipsilateral eye **fails to adduct**, while the contralateral abducting eye exhibits horizontal nystagmus (convergence is spared!). **Bilateral INO in a young adult is MS until proven otherwise.**
> - **Uhthoff Phenomenon (Pseudo-Relapse):** Transient, reversible worsening of pre-existing neurological deficits triggered by **elevated core body temperature** (hot showers, exercise, fever/UTI) due to heat-induced conduction block in demyelinated axons; **do NOT give steroids—treat the fever/infection!**
> - **Lhermitte Sign:** Sudden, electric shock-like sensation radiating down the spine into the limbs elicited by **neck flexion** (indicates cervical posterior column demyelinating plaque).
> - **Rule Out NMOSD Before Starting DMTs:** Never start MS disease-modifying therapies without checking **Anti-Aquaporin-4 (AQP4-IgG)**; giving MS drugs (especially Natalizumab, Fingolimod, or Interferon-beta) to a patient with Neuromyelitis Optica causes **catastrophic, fulminant visual and spinal cord necrosis**!
> - **Check JC Virus (JCV) Antibody Index:** Mandatory every 6 months for patients on **Natalizumab** to stratify risk of fatal **Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML)**.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - Demographic: Young adults (ages 20–40), females > males (3:1).
> - **Classic Clinical Syndromes (Focal deficits evolving over 24–48 hours, lasting weeks, then partially/fully remitting):**
>   - **Optic Neuritis (Initial presentation in ~20%):** Unilateral painful vision loss, retro-orbital pain worse with eye movement, impaired visual acuity, and **color desaturation (especially red desaturation)**.
>   - **Brainstem / Cerebellar Syndrome:** Diplopia (INO, CN VI palsy), vertigo, scanning speech, intention tremor, limb/gait ataxia.
>   - **Spinal Cord (Transverse Myelitis):** Asymmetric sensory loss, band-like trunk sensation, Lhermitte sign, spastic paraparesis, and **neurogenic bladder dysfunction** (urinary urgency, urge incontinence, or retention).
> - **Pervasive Symptoms:** Disabling central **fatigue** (worse in afternoons/heat), heat sensitivity, depression, and cognitive slowing.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Afferent Visual Defect:** **Relative Afferent Pupillary Defect (RAPD / Marcus Gunn pupil)**; fundoscopy shows papillitis (in 1/3) or normal disc acutely (retrobulbar neuritis in 2/3), followed by **temporal optic disc pallor**.
> - **Ocular Motility:** Internuclear Ophthalmoplegia (unilateral or bilateral).
> - **Upper Motor Neuron Signs:** Spasticity, hyperreflexia, clonus, and positive **Babinski sign**.
> - **Posterior Column Signs:** Decreased vibration and joint position sense, positive **Romberg sign**.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> **2017 McDonald Diagnostic Criteria (Requires Dissemination in Space AND Dissemination in Time):**
>
> - **Dissemination in Space (DIS):** $\ge 1$ T2-hyperintense lesion ($\ge 3$ mm) in **$\ge 2$ of 4 typical CNS anatomical regions**:
>   1. **Periventricular** (classic perpendicular ovoid "Dawson's fingers").
>   2. **Cortical / Juxtacortical** (touching or within cerebral cortex).
>   3. **Infratentorial** (brainstem, cerebellar peduncles).
>   4. **Spinal Cord**.
> - **Dissemination in Time (DIT) — Fulfilled by ANY 1 of the following:**
>   1. Simultaneous presence of **gadolinium-enhancing AND non-enhancing lesions** on a single baseline MRI scan.
>   2. A new T2 or gadolinium-enhancing lesion on a follow-up scan compared to baseline.
>   3. **Presence of CSF-specific Oligoclonal Bands (OCBs)** on lumbar puncture (substitutes for radiologic DIT in patients with proven DIS!).

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Initial / Gold Standard**
>
> - **MRI Brain and Cervical/Thoracic Spinal Cord with and without IV Gadolinium:**
>   - **Brain MRI:** Multiple ovoid hyperintense T2/FLAIR lesions perpendicular to lateral ventricles (Dawson's fingers), corpus callosum lesions, and active punctate/ring **gadolinium enhancement** (active blood-brain barrier breakdown lasting 4–8 weeks).
>   - **Spine MRI:** Focal, eccentric, short-segment plaques (**<2 vertebral segments in length**, occupying <50% of cord cross-section) in the cervical/thoracic cord.
>
> **Confirmatory / Lumbar Puncture**
>
> - **CSF Analysis:**
>   - **Oligoclonal Bands (OCBs):** Presence of **$\ge 2$ unique CSF-specific bands** (absent in paired serum) via isoelectric focusing (positive in $>85–90\%$ of MS).
>   - **IgG Index:** Elevated ($>0.7$, reflecting intrathecal IgG synthesis).
>   - **Cell Count:** Mild lymphocytic pleocytosis ($<50\text{ cells/}\mu\text{L}$; if $>50$ cells/$\mu$L, suspect infection, sarcoidosis, or NMOSD).
>
> **Rule-Out Serologies (Mandatory Prior to Therapy)**
>
> - **Serum Anti-AQP4-IgG and Anti-MOG-IgG:** Rule out **Neuromyelitis Optica Spectrum Disorder (NMOSD)** and MOG antibody disease.
> - **Anti-JC Virus (JCV) Antibody with Index:** Baseline stratification prior to disease-modifying therapy.
> - **Systemic Panel:** Vitamin B12, TSH, ANA, ESR, Treponemal antibodies (Syphilis), Lyme serology, Chest X-ray (Sarcoidosis).
>
> **Adjunctive Neurophysiology**
>
> - **Visual Evoked Potentials (VEP):** Prolonged **P100 latency** with preserved waveform morphology (demonstrates subclinical optic nerve demyelination).

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Hyperacute "stroke-like" onset (maximal within minutes to hours)** (suggests ischemia, not MS).
> - **Longitudinally Extensive Transverse Myelitis (LETM):** Continuous spinal cord lesion extending across **$\ge 3$ complete vertebral bodies** on sagittal MRI (hallmark of **NMOSD** or MOGAD; rare in MS).
> - **Bilateral simultaneous severe optic neuritis** with poor visual recovery (suggests NMOSD).
> - Encephalopathy, high fever, or meningismus (suggests ADEM, vasculitis, or infectious meningoencephalitis).
> - Complete absence of brain lesions on high-resolution MRI.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Neuromyelitis Optica Spectrum Disorder (NMOSD):** Autoimmune attack on astrocyte aquaporin-4 water channels; severe bilateral optic neuritis, LETM, and **area postrema syndrome** (intractable nausea, vomiting, or hiccups lasting days); serum **AQP4-IgG positive**; **worsened by MS DMTs!**
> - **MOG Antibody-Associated Disease (MOGAD):** Pediatric or adult demyelination; prominent optic disc edema and bilateral optic neuritis; serum **MOG-IgG positive**.
> - **Acute Disseminated Encephalomyelitis (ADEM):** Monophasic, post-infectious/post-vaccinal demyelination with prominent **encephalopathy (altered mental status, coma)** and bilateral fluffy white matter lesions in children.
> - **Primary CNS Lymphoma or Glioblastoma:** Mass lesions with vasogenic edema.
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Migraine with White Matter Hyperintensities:** Non-specific subcortical/deep white matter T2 spots in chronic migraineurs (lack periventricular/corpus callosum distribution; no Dawson's fingers; normal CSF).
> - **Small Vessel Ischemic Disease (Microvascular Leukoaraiosis):** Punctate white matter changes in older patients with hypertension/diabetes; sparing of corpus callosum and spinal cord.
> - **Vitamin B12 Deficiency:** Subacute combined degeneration (bilateral dorsal/lateral column dysfunction); megaloblastic anemia, low B12, elevated methylmalonic acid.
>
> **Less Common**
>
> - **Neurosarcoidosis:** Cranial neuropathies (CN VII), basilar leptomeningeal enhancement on MRI, elevated CSF ACE.
> - **CNS Vasculitis or Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE).**

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Accelerate recovery from acute relapses, achieve **NEDA (No Evidence of Disease Activity: zero clinical relapses, zero new/enlarging T2 lesions, zero gadolinium-enhancing lesions, and zero confirmed disability progression)**.
> - **Secondary Target:** Slow irreversible brain atrophy and secondary progression, preserve ambulation and employment, and manage spasticity, bladder, and fatigue.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (Acute Disabling Relapse)**
>
> - **Rule out systemic infection (UTI/fever)** prior to immunosuppression to exclude a pseudo-relapse (Uhthoff phenomenon).
> - **Administer High-Dose Corticosteroids (IV Methylprednisolone 1,000 mg IV daily for 3 to 5 consecutive days** [or Oral Prednisone 1,250 mg daily]) immediately for acute relapses causing functional visual, motor, or cerebellar impairment to accelerate recovery time (note: steroids do _not_ alter long-term disease progression).
> - **Initiate Plasma Exchange (PLEX: 5 to 7 sessions on alternate days)** as second-line rescue therapy for severe, disabling acute attacks (severe paraparesis, blindness) that fail to improve within 1–2 weeks of high-dose corticosteroids.
>
> **Disease-Modifying Therapy (DMT) for Relapsing-Remitting MS (RRMS)**
>
> - _Practice Standard (Early High-Efficacy Strategy):_ Initiating high-efficacy therapy early prevents permanent axonal loss and disability accumulation compared to historical slow-escalation models.
> - **High-Efficacy Monoclonal Antibodies (First-Line):**
>   - **Anti-CD20 B-Cell Depleting Therapies:** **Ocrelizumab (600 mg IV q6 months)**, **Ofatumumab (20 mg SC monthly)**, or **Ublituximab (IV q6m)** (profound reduction in relapses and MRI lesions; **Ocrelizumab is also the ONLY approved DMT for Primary Progressive MS [PPMS]**; monitor for hypogammaglobulinemia and HBV reactivation).
>   - **Natalizumab (Tysabri: 300 mg IV q4 weeks):** Monoclonal antibody against $\alpha_4\beta_1$-integrin (blocks leukocyte adhesion and CNS extravasation; **highest efficacy for rapid inflammatory control**; restricted to **JCV antibody-negative** or low-index patients to prevent PML).
> - **Oral Sphingosine-1-Phosphate (S1P) Receptor Modulators (Moderate-to-High Efficacy):**
>   - **Fingolimod (0.5 mg PO daily)**, **Siponimod**, **Ozanimod**, or **Ponesimod** (sequesters lymphocytes inside lymph nodes; mandates baseline ECG/first-dose observation for bradycardia and baseline dilated eye exam to rule out **macular edema**).
> - **Oral / Injectable Platform Therapies (Moderate Efficacy):**
>   - **Dimethyl Fumarate (Tecfidera: 240 mg PO BID)** (activates Nrf2 antioxidant pathway; monitor for lymphopenia and flushing).
>   - **Glatiramer Acetate (Copaxone)** or **Interferon-beta (1a/1b)** (safest long-term safety profile; preferred options if pregnancy is actively planned).
>
> **Symptomatic Management**
>
> - **Spasticity:** **Baclofen (10–20 mg PO TID)** or **Tizanidine (2–4 mg PO TID)** as first-line agents; physical therapy and stretching.
> - **Gait Impairment / Walking Speed:** **Dalfampridine (4-Aminopyridine: 10 mg PO BID)** (voltage-gated $\text{K}^+$ channel blocker that improves action potential conduction in demyelinated axons; contra: seizures and renal impairment).
> - **Neurogenic Bladder:**
>   - _Detrusor Hyperreflexia (Urge Incontinence / Frequency):_ **Oxybutynin** or **Mirabegron** ($\beta_3$-agonist).
>   - _Urinary Retention / Detrusor-Sphincter Dyssynergia:_ Clean intermittent self-catheterization (CIC) $\pm$ **Tamsulosin**.
> - **Fatigue:** Amantadine (100 mg BID) or Modafinil (100–200 mg daily).
> - **Neuropathic Pain & Trigeminal Neuralgia:** Gabapentin, Pregabalin, or **Carbamazepine** (first-line for MS-associated trigeminal neuralgia).

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid High-Dose Corticosteroids for Pseudo-Relapses (Uhthoff Phenomenon)**; always screen for and treat underlying **urinary tract infections** or viral illnesses before diagnosing an acute relapse.
> - **Avoid MS Disease-Modifying Therapies (Natalizumab, Fingolimod, Interferon-beta) in Neuromyelitis Optica Spectrum Disorder (NMOSD)**, as these agents trigger **catastrophic disease exacerbations and permanent quadriplegia/blindness**!
> - **Avoid Natalizumab in Patients with High Anti-JC Virus (JCV) Antibody Index (>1.5) and Prior Immunosuppressant Use** to prevent fatal **Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML)** (opportunistic brain infection by JC polyomavirus infecting oligodendrocytes).
> - **Avoid S1P Receptor Modulators (Fingolimod, Siponimod) in Patients with Pre-existing Cardiac Conduction Blocks (Second/Third-degree AV block, Sick Sinus Syndrome) or Baseline Macular Edema** to prevent severe bradyarrhythmias and irreversible visual loss.
> - **Avoid Teriflunomide in Men or Women Considering Pregnancy** without undergoing an accelerated **Cholestyramine Washout Protocol (8 g TID for 11 days)** to eliminate the drug (remains in plasma for up to 2 years due to enterohepatic recirculation; Category X teratogen).
> - **Avoid Dalfampridine (4-Aminopyridine) in Patients with a History of Seizures or Moderate-to-Severe Renal Impairment ($\text{CrCl} \le 50\text{ mL/min}$)** due to high risk of triggering intractable seizures.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML):** Opportunistic lytic JC virus infection of oligodendrocytes in patients on **Natalizumab** (or fingolimod/dimethyl fumarate) $\rightarrow$ presents with progressive subacute cognitive, visual, or motor decline $\rightarrow$ stop drug immediately, perform brain MRI (subcortical asymmetric T2 lesions sparing cortex) + CSF JC virus PCR, and perform **plasma exchange** to clear natalizumab.
> - **Neurogenic Bladder Sepsis & Urosepsis:** High post-void residuals $\rightarrow$ recurrent UTIs $\rightarrow$ common cause of pseudo-relapses and secondary hospitalization.
> - **Aspiration Pneumonia & Decubitus Ulcers:** Late-stage bedbound complications in secondary or primary progressive disease.
> - **Severe Depression & Suicide:** Rates of clinical depression exceed 40–50%; high risk of suicide $\rightarrow$ routine annual depression screening.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 28-year-old woman presents with 3 days of progressive, painful blurred vision and loss of red color vision in her left eye, accompanied by pain with eye movement. Examination demonstrates visual acuity of 20/100 in the left eye, a left **Relative Afferent Pupillary Defect (RAPD)**, and a normal fundus (**Retrobulbar Optic Neuritis**). Brain MRI with IV gadolinium reveals an enhancing left optic nerve, **five ovoid periventricular T2 hyperintensities oriented perpendicularly to the ventricles (Dawson's fingers)**, and one non-enhancing juxtacortical lesion (**Dissemination in Space**); lumbar puncture demonstrates **4 CSF-specific Oligoclonal Bands** (**fulfilling Dissemination in Time** per 2017 McDonald Criteria). Serum anti-AQP4 antibodies are negative; she receives **IV Methylprednisolone (1 g daily for 3 days)** with rapid visual recovery and is initiated on high-efficacy first-line disease-modifying therapy with **Ocrelizumab**.
