> [!abstract] Guillain-Barré Syndrome
> Acute, immune-mediated peripheral polyradiculoneuropathy classically presenting as rapidly progressive, symmetric, **ascending flaccid paralysis** and **areflexia**, reaching maximal clinical nadir within 4 weeks (most common cause of acute non-traumatic flaccid paralysis in developed countries).

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Molecular Mimicry:** Preceding infection (predominantly **_Campylobacter jejuni_**, also CMV, EBV, Zika) generates cross-reactive antibodies targeting peripheral nerve gangliosides (GM1, GD1a, GQ1b).
> - **Complement-Mediated Lysis:** Autoantibodies trigger complement activation and macrophage infiltration leading to **Schwann cell demyelination** (**AIDP**) or direct **axolemmal destruction** (**AMAN/AMSAN**), arresting saltatory conduction.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **"20/30/40 Rule" for Impending Respiratory Failure:** Intubate electively if **Forced Vital Capacity (FVC) <20 mL/kg**, **Negative Inspiratory Force (NIF) < -30 $\text{cmH}_2\text{O}$**, or **Maximum Expiratory Pressure (MEP) <40 $\text{cmH}_2\text{O}$** (do NOT wait for hypoxia or hypercapnia!).
> - **Corticosteroids are INEFFECTIVE / HARMFUL:** Multiple randomized trials prove steroids do not speed recovery and may delay motor improvement; **never give steroids for GBS**!
> - **Albuminocytological Dissociation:** High CSF protein with normal CSF WBC count (<5–10 cells/µL); **often normal in the first week** (present in only ~50% at day 1–3, rising to >80% by week 2)—normal CSF does _not_ rule out GBS.
> - **Miller Fisher Syndrome (MFS) Triad:** **Ophthalmoplegia + Ataxia + Areflexia** (spares limb strength; associated with **anti-GQ1b antibodies** in >90%).
> - **Never use Succinylcholine:** Denervated motor endplates upregulate extrajunctional acetylcholine receptors $\rightarrow$ succinylcholine causes **fatal hyperkalemic cardiac arrest**.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - Antecedent respiratory infection or gastrointestinal illness (_Campylobacter_ enteritis) **1 to 4 weeks prior** (or immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy).
> - Progressive, symmetric weakness ascending from lower extremities to arms and trunk over days.
> - Distal paresthesias ("pins and needles" in toes/fingers) typically precede weakness; objective sensory loss is usually mild/minimal.
> - Severe aching, radicular, or neuropathic pain in the lower back, hips, or thighs (present in up to 70–80%).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Symmetric flaccid quadriparesis:** Proximal and distal muscles involved; legs weaker than arms.
> - **Hyporeflexia or Areflexia:** Mandatory hallmark (loss of patellar and Achilles reflexes occurs early).
> - **Cranial nerve palsies (~50%):** **Bilateral facial nerve palsy (facial diplegia)**, bulbar weakness (dysphagia, dysarthria, weak cough).
> - **Autonomic Instability (Dysautonomia in ~60%):** Labile blood pressure (severe hypertension or orthostatic hypotension), fluctuating tachycardia, **life-threatening sinus bradycardia/asystole**, ileus, urinary retention.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> **Brighton Criteria (Mandatory Core Features for Diagnosis):**
>
> - **Bilateral and flaccid weakness** of the limbs.
> - **Decreased or absent deep tendon reflexes (hyporeflexia/areflexia)** in weak limbs.
> - **Monophasic illness pattern** with clinical nadir reached between **12 hours and 28 days (4 weeks)** (progression >8 weeks defines CIDP).
> - **Absence of an alternative identifiable cause** for weakness.
> - _High Certainty Criteria (Level 1/2):_ Supported by **Albuminocytological Dissociation** on CSF analysis AND **Nerve Conduction Studies (NCS/EMG)** showing demyelination or axonal motor neuropathy.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Initial**
>
> - **Bedside Pulmonary Function Testing:** Serial **Forced Vital Capacity (FVC)** and **Negative Inspiratory Force (NIF)** q4–6 hours (or "single breath count" <15–20 if spirometry unavailable).
> - **Continuous Cardiac Telemetry:** Mandatory for continuous arrhythmia and autonomic monitoring.
>
> **Confirmatory**
>
> - **Lumbar Puncture (CSF Analysis):** Demonstrates **Albuminocytological Dissociation** (elevated CSF protein [>45 mg/dL] with **normal CSF WBC count <5–10 cells/µL**; if WBC >50/µL, suspect HIV, Lyme, CMV, or poliomyelitis).
> - **Nerve Conduction Studies / Electromyography (NCS/EMG):** Shows prolonged distal latencies, conduction slowing, temporal dispersion, and absent F-waves (AIDP) or decreased CMAP amplitudes (AMAN). _May be normal in the first 7–10 days._
>
> **Serology**
>
> - **Anti-GQ1b IgG:** Confirms **Miller Fisher Syndrome** (>90% sensitivity).
> - **Anti-GM1 / Anti-GD1a:** Associated with acute motor axonal neuropathy (AMAN).

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **FVC <20 mL/kg, NIF < -30 $\text{cmH}_2\text{O}$**, or Single Breath Count <15 (impending neuromuscular respiratory arrest).
> - Bulbar failure (dysphagia, pooling of secretions, absent gag/cough $\rightarrow$ aspiration risk).
> - Severe dysautonomia (hemodynamic instability, wide blood pressure swings, bradyarrhythmias, sinus pauses).
> - Rapid motor progression to flaccid quadriplegia within <24–48 hours.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Myasthenia Gravis Crisis:** Fatigable ptosis/diplopia (pupils spared), preserved deep tendon reflexes, no sensory symptoms; positive anti-AChR/MuSK.
> - **Botulism:** Descending paralysis (starts with cranial nerves and fixed dilated pupils $\rightarrow$ limbs), prominent dry mouth, unreactive pupils.
> - **Spinal Cord Compression / Transverse Myelitis:** Distinct sensory level, early bladder/bowel incontinence, hyperreflexia/Babinski (after acute spinal shock phase).
> - **Tick Paralysis:** Rapid ascending paralysis mimicking GBS; resolves rapidly upon finding and removing the tick (_Dermacentor_).
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Chronic Inflammatory Demyelinating Polyradiculoneuropathy (CIDP):** Motor/sensory deficits progressing or relapsing for **>8 weeks** (steroid-responsive!).
> - **Acute Hypokalemic Periodic Paralysis:** Flaccid quadriparesis with serum $\text{K}^+ <2.5\text{ mEq/L}$; reverses quickly with potassium repletion.
>
> **Less Common**
>
> - **Acute Intermittent Porphyria:** Motor neuropathy + severe colicky abdominal pain + psychosis/confusion + hyponatremia.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Prevent fatal neuromuscular respiratory arrest, halt immune-mediated nerve injury, and stabilize autonomic fluctuations.
> - **Secondary Target:** Accelerate motor recovery and prevent immobility complications (DVT/PE, decubiti, contractures).
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Admit to ICU or Step-Down Unit immediately** upon suspicion of GBS for continuous cardiac telemetry and serial respiratory mechanics monitoring.
> - **Intubate electively via Rapid Sequence Intubation (using Rocuronium)** when **FVC <20 mL/kg**, **NIF < -30 $\text{cmH}_2\text{O}$**, or bulbar weakness prevents secretion clearance to avoid emergency hypoxic arrest.
> - **Avoid Succinylcholine entirely** during intubation to prevent fatal hyperkalemic cardiac arrest from denervated extrajunctional receptors.
>
> **First-Line Immunotherapy (Equal Efficacy — Start within First 2–4 Weeks)**
>
> - **Administer IV Immune Globulin (IVIG: 0.4 g/kg/day IV for 5 consecutive days [total dose 2.0 g/kg])** as preferred first-line therapy (easier administration, superior hemodynamic stability in dysautonomia) to neutralize pathogenic antibodies.
> - **Perform Plasma Exchange (PLEX: 4 to 5 sessions of 200–250 mL/kg plasma volume exchange over 7–14 days)** as an equivalent first-line alternative (especially in severe axonal variants or if IVIG is contraindicated).
>
> **Autonomic & Supportive Management**
>
> - **Treat autonomic blood pressure swings with short-acting titratable IV agents** (IV Esmolol or Nicardipine for severe hypertension; IV crystalloids and low-dose vasopressors for hypotension; **Atropine or temporary transcutaneous pacing** for severe bradycardia/asystole).
> - **Start Subcutaneous LMWH (Enoxaparin) + Intermittent Pneumatic Compression (IPC) devices** upon admission in non-ambulatory patients to prevent fatal pulmonary embolism.
> - **Prescribe multimodal neuropathic analgesia (Gabapentin, Pregabalin, or Duloxetine)** for severe aching and radicular pain.
>
> **Disposition & Follow-up**
>
> - **Initiate early multidisciplinary rehabilitation** (PT/OT/Speech) to prevent joint contractures and pressure sores.
> - **Re-treat with a second course of IVIG or PLEX** if the patient develops **Treatment-Related Fluctuation (TRF)** (occurs in ~10% within 8 weeks); if weakness continues to progress beyond 8 weeks, reclassify and treat as **CIDP**.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Corticosteroids (IV Methylprednisolone or Oral Prednisone)** in GBS to prevent worsening functional recovery and delayed motor improvement.
> - **Avoid Combining IVIG and Plasma Exchange (PLEX)** (or giving PLEX immediately after IVIG) to prevent washing out expensive IVIG without clinical benefit.
> - **Avoid Succinylcholine** in GBS to prevent life-threatening **hyperkalemic cardiac arrest**.
> - **Avoid Waiting for Hypoxia ($\text{SpO}_2 <90\%$) or Hypercapnia on ABG before Intubating**, as diaphragmatic exhaustion leads to sudden catastrophic arrest; rely strictly on **FVC, NIF, and bulbar signs**.
> - **Avoid Long-Acting Antihypertensives** for dysautonomia-induced hypertension to prevent refractory, precipitate hypotension during sudden autonomic swings.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Neuromuscular Respiratory Failure:** Diaphragmatic weakness $\rightarrow$ FVC <20 mL/kg, paradoxical abdominal breathing $\rightarrow$ elective endotracheal intubation.
> - **Lethal Cardiac Dysautonomia:** Severe bradycardia, sinus pauses, or asystole $\rightarrow$ continuous telemetry, atropine, temporary pacemaker.
> - **Deep Vein Thrombosis & Pulmonary Embolism:** Prolonged flaccid immobilization $\rightarrow$ prophylactic LMWH + IPC sleeves.
> - **Severe Neuropathic Pain:** Aching, burning pain in back and legs $\rightarrow$ gabapentin/pregabalin/short-term opioids.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 34-year-old man presents with progressive numbness in his feet and difficulty climbing stairs 2 weeks after recovering from a diarrheal illness (_C. jejuni_). Exam reveals symmetric 3/5 lower extremity weakness, 4/5 upper extremity weakness, and complete **absence of deep tendon reflexes (areflexia)** bilaterally. Bedside FVC is 28 mL/kg and LP shows **elevated protein (98 mg/dL) with 2 WBCs/µL** (albuminocytological dissociation); he is admitted to the ICU, started on **IVIG 0.4 g/kg/day for 5 days**, and serial FVC/NIF measurements are tracked q4h without administering steroids.
