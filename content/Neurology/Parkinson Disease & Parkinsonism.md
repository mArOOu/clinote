> [!abstract] Parkinson Disease & Parkinsonism
> **Parkinsonism:** Clinical syndrome characterized by **Bradykinesia** plus either resting tremor, rigidity, or both.
> **Parkinson Disease (PD):** Progressive neurodegenerative disorder caused by loss of dopaminergic neurons in the **substantia nigra pars compacta (SNpc)** and intracellular aggregation of misfolded **$\alpha$-synuclein (Lewy bodies)**, leading to classic asymmetric motor symptoms and pervasive non-motor manifestations.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Striatal Dopamine Depletion:** Degeneration of the nigrostriatal pathway reduces direct pathway activation ($\text{D}_1$) and disinhibits the indirect pathway ($\text{D}_2$), causing excessive subthalamic nucleus and globus pallidus internus firing $\rightarrow$ net **over-inhibition of the thalamus** and motor cortex.
> - **$\alpha$-Synuclein Pathology:** Lewy bodies spread caudally to rostrally (Braak staging), originating in the dorsal motor nucleus of vagus/olfactory bulb (accounting for early non-motor features: **anosmia, constipation, REM sleep behavior disorder**) before ascending to the midbrain and neocortex.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Asymmetry is the Key:** Idiopathic PD begins **asymmetrically** (one limb/side) and maintains an asymmetric asymmetry throughout its course; symmetric onset with early falls within the 1st year strongly indicates an **Atypical Parkinsonian Syndrome (PSP or MSA)**.
> - **Dopamine Agonist Impulse Control Warning:** Non-ergot dopamine agonists (**Pramipexole, Ropinirole**) stimulate limbic $\text{D}_3$ receptors, triggering severe **Impulse Control Disorders** (pathologic gambling, hypersexuality, compulsive shopping) in up to 15–20% of patients!
> - **Antipsychotic Rule in PD:** If a PD patient develops hallucinations/psychosis, **avoid typical antipsychotics and most atypicals (Haloperidol, Risperidone, Olanzapine)**, which block striatal $\text{D}_2$ receptors and paralyze motor function; use **Pimavanserin** (5-$\text{HT}_\text{2A}$ inverse agonist, zero $\text{D}_2$ affinity), low-dose **Quetiapine**, or **Clozapine**.
> - **Parkinsonism-Hyperpyrexia Syndrome:** Abrupt cessation of levodopa or dopamine agonists triggers a life-threatening state indistinguishable from **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS)** with hyperthermia, severe rigidity, autonomic instability, and elevated CK; treat by **re-administering dopaminergic agents immediately**!

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Cardinal Motor Features (TRAP):**
>   - **T**remor: 4–6 Hz resting "pill-rolling" tremor (starts unilaterally in a hand or foot, worse during rest/distraction, suppressed during voluntary movement).
>   - **R**igidity: Limb stiffness, shoulder/neck tightness, difficulty turning in bed.
>   - **A**kinesia / **B**radykinesia (**Mandatory**): Slowness of movement, progressive **micrographia** (handwriting gets smaller/cramped), soft muffled speech (hypophonia).
>   - **P**ostural Instability: Late feature in idiopathic PD (if early $\rightarrow$ suspect PSP).
> - **Premotor / Non-Motor Features (Precede motor onset by 5–20 years):**
>   - **REM Sleep Behavior Disorder (RBD):** Dream enactment behavior (punching, kicking, yelling during sleep; strong predictor of synucleinopathy).
>   - **Hyposmia / Anosmia** (loss of smell) and chronic **constipation**.
>   - Depression, anxiety, and symptomatic **orthostatic hypotension**.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Bradykinesia Testing (Essential):** Rapid alternating finger tapping, hand opening/closing, and foot tapping show progressive **reduction in amplitude and/or speed** (fatiguing/arrest).
> - **Cogwheel Rigidity:** Rhythmic, ratchety resistance to passive flexion/extension of the wrist or elbow (superimposed tremor on rigidity; enhanced by voluntary movement of the opposite limb = **Froment maneuver**).
> - **Gait & Posture:** Stooped posture, **shuffling steps**, reduced unilateral arm swing, **en bloc turning** (turning like a statue with multiple small steps), **festination** (involuntary speeding up of steps with forward-leaning center of gravity), and freezing of gait at doorways.
> - **Cranial Exam:** Masked facies (hypomimia), decreased blink rate, and **glabellar reflex (Myerson sign)** (inability to suppress blinking upon repeated tapping between the eyebrows).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> **MDS (Movement Disorder Society) Clinical Diagnostic Criteria for Parkinson's Disease:**
>
> - **Step 1: Mandatory Core Diagnosis of Parkinsonism:**
>   - **Bradykinesia** (slowness AND progressive reduction in speed or amplitude on repetitive movements), **PLUS AT LEAST ONE of**:
>     1. **Muscular Rigidity** (cogwheel or lead-pipe).
>     2. **4–6 Hz Resting Tremor** (unilateral extremity).
> - **Step 2: Exclusion of Secondary or Atypical Causes:**
>   - Absence of absolute exclusion criteria: Prominent cerebellar signs, downward vertical supranuclear gaze palsy, severe autonomic failure within 5 years, behavioral frontotemporal dementia within 5 years, treatment with dopamine receptor blockers (e.g., Metoclopramide, Haloperidol).
> - **Step 3: Documenting Supportive Criteria ($\ge 2$ Required):**
>   - **Clear, dramatic response to dopaminergic therapy (Levodopa)**.
>   - Presence of **Levodopa-Induced Dyskinesia**.
>   - Resting tremor of an extremity documented on exam.
>   - Loss of smell (**hyposmia**) on objective testing or cardiac sympathetic denervation on MIBG scintigraphy.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Initial**
>
> - **Clinical Evaluation:** PD is primarily a **clinical diagnosis**; routine imaging is not required for classic, asymmetric, levodopa-responsive presentation in older adults.
> - **Serum Ceruloplasmin + Slit-Lamp Exam (Kayser-Fleischer rings):** Mandatory in any patient presenting with parkinsonism, tremor, or dystonia **aged <40–50 years** to rule out **Wilson Disease**!
>
> **Imaging & Diagnostic Adjuncts (When Presentation is Atypical)**
>
> - **Brain MRI with/without Contrast:** Indicated to exclude normal pressure hydrocephalus (NPH), vascular multi-infarct parkinsonism, structural lesions, or distinctive atypical parkinsonian signs:
>   - **"Hummingbird sign" / "Penguin sign"** (midbrain tegmentum atrophy) $\rightarrow$ **Progressive Supranuclear Palsy (PSP)**.
>   - **"Hot cross bun sign"** (cruciform T2 pontine hyperintensity) $\rightarrow$ **Multiple System Atrophy (MSA)**.
> - **Dopamine Transporter SPECT (DaTscan — I-123 Ioflupane):** Demonstrates loss of striatal dopamine transporters (asymmetric comma-shaped putaminal signal loss); **differentiates neurodegenerative parkinsonism from Essential Tremor or Drug-Induced Parkinsonism** (both of which have a completely _normal_ DaTscan).
> - **Formal Levodopa Challenge Test:** Objective measurement of motor scores (UPDRS) before and after an acute dose of levodopa (a **$\ge 30\%$ improvement** confirms levodopa responsiveness).

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> **MDS "Red Flags" Suggesting Atypical Parkinsonian Syndromes (NOT Idiopathic PD):**
>
> - **Early rapid progression to wheelchair dependence within 5 years** of onset.
> - **Early severe bulbar dysfunction** (prominent dysphonia, anarthria, or severe dysphagia within 5 years).
> - **Inspiratory respiratory stridor** (classic in **MSA**).
> - **Severe autonomic failure within 5 years** (severe symptomatic orthostatic hypotension or bladder incontinence/retention).
> - **Recurrent unexplained falls within the first 1–3 years** (classic in **PSP**).
> - **Downward vertical supranuclear gaze palsy** (slow/restricted downward saccades = pathognomonic for **PSP**).
> - **Complete lack of response to high-dose Levodopa ($\ge 1,000$ mg/day)**.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Secondary Parkinsonism**
>
> - **Wilson Disease:** Autosomal recessive ATP7B mutation $\rightarrow$ copper accumulation in basal ganglia; young patient (<40 y), psychiatric symptoms, transaminitis, low ceruloplasmin, Kayser-Fleischer rings.
> - **Drug-Induced Parkinsonism (DIP):** Antipsychotics (typical > atypical) or antiemetics (**Metoclopramide, Prochlorperazine**); **symmetric onset**, prominent rigidity/bradykinesia, rare resting tremor, normal DaTscan.
> - **Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH):** Triad of **"Wet, Wacky, and Wobbly"** (early urinary incontinence, cognitive impairment, and **magnetic / apraxic gait** with feet glued to the floor); ventriculomegaly disproportionate to sulcal enlargement on MRI.
>
> **Most Likely (Benign Tremor Mimic)**
>
> - **Essential Tremor:** **Action / Postural 8–12 Hz tremor** (worse with arms outstretched, writing, or holding a cup), **bilateral and symmetric**, improves temporarily with alcohol, family history common; **lacks bradykinesia, rigidity, and gait impairment**; normal DaTscan.
>
> **Atypical Parkinsonian Syndromes ("Parkinson-Plus")**
>
> - **Progressive Supranuclear Palsy (PSP — Tauopathy):** Symmetrical parkinsonism, **early backward falls**, **vertical supranuclear gaze palsy**, retrocollis (neck extension), axial rigidity > limb rigidity, poor levodopa response.
> - **Multiple System Atrophy (MSA — Synucleinopathy):** Severe early **autonomic failure** (orthostatic syncope, neurogenic bladder) + cerebellar ataxia (MSA-C) or parkinsonism (MSA-P).
> - **Corticobasal Degeneration (CBD — Tauopathy):** Extreme asymmetric parkinsonism, limb **apraxia**, **alien limb phenomenon**, cortical sensory loss, and myoclonus.
> - **Dementia with Lewy Bodies (DLB):** Cognitive decline and **vivid visual hallucinations occur BEFORE or CONCURRENTLY (within 1 year)** of motor parkinsonism (in PD, dementia occurs late, typically $>5–10$ years after motor onset!).

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Optimize motor function and activities of daily living (restore mobility and dexterity), minimize motor complications (**"wearing-off" fluctuations and peak-dose dyskinesia**).
> - **Secondary Target:** Control debilitating non-motor symptoms (psychosis, orthostasis, depression, RBD, constipation) and maintain independence.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Parkinsonism-Hyperpyrexia Syndrome (Acute Dopamine Withdrawal):** **Immediately restart Carbidopa/Levodopa (or give IV/SC Apomorphine or transdermal Rotigotine)** alongside aggressive cooling, IV hydration, and ICU admission if a PD patient abruptly stops medications and develops acute rigidity, fever, altered mental status, and autonomic collapse.
>
> **First-Line Symptomatic Motor Therapy**
>
> - **Levodopa/Carbidopa (Sinemet: initiate 25/100 mg PO TID with meals/water):** The **most potent and effective symptomatic medication** for PD; carbidopa (peripheral DOPA decarboxylase inhibitor) must be at least **$\ge 75–100$ mg/day** to prevent peripheral conversion to dopamine (nausea, orthostatic hypotension).
> - **Non-Ergot Dopamine Receptor Agonists (Pramipexole 0.125 mg PO TID, Ropinirole 0.25 mg TID, or Rotigotine patch):** Alternative initial monotherapy in **younger patients (<60 years)** to delay the onset of levodopa motor fluctuations; screen strictly for **impulse control disorders, excessive daytime sleepiness, and hallucinations**.
> - **Monoamine Oxidase-B (MAO-B) Inhibitors (Rasagiline 0.5–1 mg PO daily or Selegiline 5 mg PO BID):** Monotherapy for very mild early disease; inhibits central dopamine catabolism.
>
> **Management of Advanced Motor Complications**
>
> - **"Wearing-Off" Fluctuations (Levodopa effect ends before the next scheduled dose):**
>   - **Increase Levodopa dosing frequency** (shorten dosing intervals from q4h to q3h).
>   - **Add a COMT Inhibitor (Entacapone 200 mg PO administered WITH each Levodopa dose)** to extend levodopa plasma half-life (turns urine orange-brown).
>   - **Add an MAO-B Inhibitor (Rasagiline) or Dopamine Agonist**.
> - **Peak-Dose Dyskinesia (Involuntary choreic/writhing movements during maximum drug levels):**
>   - **Reduce individual Levodopa doses** (while increasing frequency to maintain "on" time).
>   - **Add Amantadine (100 mg PO BID–TID or ER QHS)** (NMDA receptor antagonist with specific anti-dyskinetic properties; monitor for livedo reticularis and peripheral edema).
> - **Freezing of Gait (FOG):** Physical therapy with **sensory cueing** (visual laser lines, rhythmic auditory metronome beats).
>
> **Surgical & Device-Assisted Advanced Therapies**
>
> - **Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS):** **Bilateral Subthalamic Nucleus (STN) or Globus Pallidus Internus (GPi) DBS** is indicated for idiopathic PD patients with severe motor fluctuations, disabling dyskinesias, or medication-refractory tremor who **REMAIN responsive to Levodopa** and have **NO significant cognitive impairment (dementia) or untreated severe depression**.
> - **Continuous Enteral Levodopa/Carbidopa Gel (Duopa):** Continuous jejunal pump infusion via PEG-J tube to eliminate gastric emptying delays and smooth out motor fluctuations.
>
> **Targeted Management of Non-Motor Symptoms**
>
> - **Psychosis / Hallucinations:** **Prescribe Pimavanserin (34 mg PO daily)** or **Quetiapine (12.5–50 mg PO QHS)** (reduces hallucinations without worsening motor parkinsonism).
> - **REM Sleep Behavior Disorder (RBD):** **Melatonin (3–15 mg PO QHS)** as first-line (or Clonazepam 0.25–1 mg QHS; watch for fall/sedation risk).
> - **Symptomatic Orthostatic Hypotension:** Elevate head of bed, waist-high compression stockings, increase fluid/salt intake; add **Midodrine (2.5–10 mg PO TID)**, **Droxidopa (100–600 mg TID)**, or **Fludrocortisone**.
> - **Constipation:** Daily **Polyethylene Glycol (PEG 3350)** and hydration.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Typical Antipsychotics (Haloperidol, Fluphenazine) and Common Atypical Antipsychotics (Risperidone, Olanzapine, Aripiprazole)** in PD to prevent catastrophic blockade of remaining striatal dopamine receptors, precipitating acute immobility and NMS.
> - **Avoid Antiemetic Dopamine Receptor Antagonists (Metoclopramide, Prochlorperazine, Promethazine)** for nausea in PD (prescribe **Trimethobenzamide** or **Ondansetron** instead!).
> - **Avoid Abrupt Discontinuation or Rapid Tapering of Levodopa or Dopamine Agonists** to prevent **Parkinsonism-Hyperpyrexia Syndrome** (lethal NMS-like hyperthermia).
> - **Avoid Dopamine Agonists in Patients Aged $\ge 70$ Years, Patients with Pre-existing Cognitive Decline, or History of Addictive Behaviors** to prevent precipitating delirium, severe hallucinations, and destructive impulse control disorders.
> - **Avoid Combining MAO-B Inhibitors (Selegiline, Rasagiline) with Meperidine (Demerol) or Tramadol** to prevent severe **Serotonin Syndrome** and autonomic collapse.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Levodopa-Induced Motor Fluctuations & Dyskinesias:** Develops in $>50\%$ of patients within 5 years of levodopa therapy due to pulsatile stimulation of denervated striatal receptors $\rightarrow$ managed by adding COMT/MAO-B inhibitors, amantadine, or DBS.
> - **Parkinson's Disease Dementia (PDD):** Cognitive decline developing $>1$ year (typically years/decades) after established motor PD $\rightarrow$ treat with **Cholinesterase Inhibitors (Rivastigmine, Donepezil)**.
> - **Aspiration Pneumonia:** Progressive dysphagia and pharyngeal dysmotility in advanced disease $\rightarrow$ leading cause of death in Parkinson's disease $\rightarrow$ swallow evaluation, modified food textures, and speech therapy.
> - **Injurious Falls & Hip Fractures:** Resulting from retropulsion, postural instability, freezing of gait, and orthostatic syncope $\rightarrow$ physical therapy, home safety evaluations, and mobility assist devices.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 66-year-old right-handed man presents with a 9-month history of an intermittent, resting "pill-rolling" tremor in his right thumb, progressive slowness when buttoning his shirts, and micrographic handwriting. His wife notes he has been acting out violent dreams during sleep for the past 5 years (**RBD**). Physical exam reveals an asymmetric 4-Hz right resting hand tremor that suppresses when reaching for an object, right wrist cogwheel rigidity, masked facial expression, and progressive slowing and decrement of right finger-tapping (**Bradykinesia**); sensory exam, eye movements, and gait stability are intact. He is diagnosed with **Idiopathic Parkinson Disease**; after discussing therapy, he is initiated on **Carbidopa/Levodopa (25/100 mg PO TID with meals)**, resulting in dramatic resolution of his tremor and functional stiffness at his 6-week follow-up.
