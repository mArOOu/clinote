> [!abstract] Acute Ischemic Stroke & TIA
> **Acute Ischemic Stroke (AIS):** Sudden-onset focal neurological deficit caused by thromboembolic or hemodynamic occlusion of a cerebral artery resulting in **permanent central nervous system infarction** (~87% of all strokes).
> **Transient Ischemic Attack (TIA):** Tissue-based definition—transient focal neurological dysfunction caused by focal brain, spinal cord, or retinal ischemia **WITHOUT acute infarction on diffusion-weighted MRI (DWI)** (symptoms typically resolve within <1 hour; carries a **10–15% risk of completed stroke within 90 days**, highest in the first **48 hours**).

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Ischemic Core vs. Penumbra:** Arterial occlusion drops cerebral blood flow (CBF) below **10 mL/100g/min** in the **Ischemic Core** ($\rightarrow$ ATP depletion, $\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+$ pump failure, **cytotoxic edema**, and irreversible neuronal necrosis within minutes), surrounded by the **Ischemic Penumbra** (CBF **10–20 mL/100g/min**: electrically silent/stunned neurons kept alive by collateral pial vessels that will die over hours unless reperfused).
> - **TOAST Etiologic Mechanisms:** (1) **Cardioembolism (25–30%):** Atrial fibrillation, LV thrombus, endocarditis (sudden maximal deficit at onset, hemorrhagic transformation, cortical territories in multiple vascular beds); (2) **Large-Artery Atherosclerosis (20%):** Carotid bifurcation or intracranial stenosis (stuttering/crescendo TIAs, watershed infarcts); (3) **Small-Vessel Lipohyalinosis / Lacunar (20%):** Chronic hypertension/diabetes damaging deep penetrating lenticulostriate arteries (<15 mm infarcts, no cortical signs); (4) **Other Determined (<5%):** Cervical artery dissection (young patient + neck pain/Horner's), hypercoagulable states; (5) **Cryptogenic / ESUS (25%).**

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **"Last Known Well" (LKW) is the clock:** If a patient wakes up with stroke symptoms (**Wake-Up Stroke**), the time of onset is when they went to sleep normal—**NOT** when they woke up! (Though Wake-Up Strokes can still receive IV thrombolysis if **MRI shows a DWI-FLAIR mismatch** [bright on DWI, negative on FLAIR = <4.5 hours old] or **Endovascular Thrombectomy up to 24 hours**!).
> - **Only ONE lab is required BEFORE giving IV Alteplase / Tenecteplase:** **Fingerstick Blood Glucose!** Do NOT delay IV thrombolysis waiting for CBC, INR, or creatinine unless the patient is on warfarin/DOACs or has a known bleeding diathesis.
> - **Tenecteplase (TNK) vs. Alteplase (tPA):** Single-bolus **IV Tenecteplase (0.25 mg/kg, max 25 mg)** over 5 seconds is non-inferior/superior to 60-minute Alteplase infusion (especially for Large Vessel Occlusions bridging to thrombectomy) and is now a primary guideline-endorsed thrombolytic.
> - **High-Risk TIA / Minor Stroke (NIHSS ≤3–5, ABCD² ≥4) = Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (DAPT) for 21 Days ONLY:** Once hemorrhage is ruled out on CT (and no tPA was given), load immediately with **Aspirin (325 mg) + Clopidogrel (300–600 mg)** (or Ticagrelor), continue **DAPT for 21 days**, then drop to **monotherapy** to prevent major hemorrhage (**CHANCE / POINT trials**).

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History & Vascular Territory Localization (Crucial for Rounds)**
>
> - **Anterior Circulation (Internal Carotid Artery $\rightarrow$ ACA & MCA):**
>   - **Ophthalmic Artery (ICA branch):** **Amaurosis Fugax** (painless, transient monocular blindness described as a "curtain coming down over one eye").
>   - **Anterior Cerebral Artery (ACA):** **Contralateral leg > arm weakness and sensory loss**, urinary incontinence, abulia/akinetic mutism, primitive grasp/suck reflexes.
>   - **Middle Cerebral Artery (MCA — Most Common):** **Contralateral face and arm > leg weakness/sensory loss**, **contralateral homonymous hemianopia**, and **ipsilateral gaze deviation ("eyes look TOWARD the ischemic lesion"** and away from the weak side) **PLUS Cortical Signs:**
>     - _Dominant (Left) Hemisphere:_ **Aphasia** (Superior division = **Broca's expressive**; Inferior division = **Wernicke's receptive**) or **Gerstmann Syndrome** (angular gyrus: agraphia, acalculia, finger agnosia, left-right disorientation).
>     - _Non-Dominant (Right) Hemisphere:_ **Contralateral Hemispatial Neglect**, anosognosia (denial of deficit), aprosodia.
> - **Posterior Circulation (Vertebrobasilar $\rightarrow$ PCA, Cerebellar, & Brainstem "Crossed Syndromes"):**
>   - **Posterior Cerebral Artery (PCA):** **Contralateral homonymous hemianopia with macular sparing**, alexia without agraphia (left PCA + splenium of corpus callosum), or thalamic pain (Dejerine-Roussy).
>   - **Lateral Medullary (Wallenberg) Syndrome (PICA / Vertebral Artery — Most Common Brainstem Stroke):** **Vertigo/nystagmus, dysphagia, hoarseness, absent gag reflex (CN IX/X — nucleus ambiguus), intractable hiccups**, **Ipsilateral facial pain/temperature loss (CN V)**, **Ipsilateral Horner's Syndrome** (ptosis, miosis, anhidrosis), **Ipsilateral ataxia**, AND **Contralateral body pain/temperature loss** (spinothalamic tract) (_Crucial: Motor strength is SPARED!_).
>   - **Basilar Artery Occlusion ("Locked-In Syndrome" — Ventral Pons):** **Quadriplegia, bilateral facial/bulbar palsy, and horizontal gaze palsy** with **preserved consciousness and vertical eye movements/blinking** (reticular activating system and midbrain are spared).
> - **Lacunar Syndromes (Deep Penetrating Arteries — Strictly LACK Cortical Signs [No aphasia, no neglect, no visual field cut!]):**
>   1. **Pure Motor Hemiparesis (#1):** Face = Arm = Leg (**Posterior Limb of Internal Capsule** or basis pontis).
>   2. **Pure Sensory Stroke:** (**Ventral Posterolateral [VPL] Thalamus**).
>   3. **Ataxic Hemiparesis** (Internal capsule / pons) or **Dysarthria–Clumsy Hand Syndrome** (Basis pontis / genu of internal capsule).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Calculate the NIH Stroke Scale (NIHSS) Immediately (0–42 points):** Quantifies deficit severity (**NIHSS ≥6** strongly predicts a **Large Vessel Occlusion [LVO]**).
> - **Cardiovascular / Neck Exam:** Pulse check for **atrial fibrillation**, carotid bruits, unequal arm blood pressures (**aortic dissection** causing carotid occlusion!).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Acute Ischemic Stroke:** Acute focal neurological deficit in a defined arterial territory **PLUS** exclusion of intracranial hemorrhage on **Non-Contrast Head CT** (confirmed definitively by **hyperintensity on DWI with corresponding hypointensity on ADC map on Brain MRI**, or persistence of deficit ≥24 hours).
> - **Transient Ischemic Attack (TIA):** Acute transient focal neurological or monocular deficit of vascular origin with **complete clinical resolution AND absence of restricted diffusion on DWI-MRI**.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Immediate Hyperacute Imaging ("Time is Brain" — Door-to-CT <20 Minutes)**
>
> - **1. Non-Contrast Head CT (NCCT):** **Mandatory FIRST test in 100% of suspected strokes** to rule out **Intracranial Hemorrhage** (absolute contraindication to thrombolysis/antiplatelets).
>   - _Note:_ NCCT is typically **normal in the first 3–6 hours** of ischemic stroke! Early subtle signs of a massive MCA stroke include the **"Hyperdense MCA Sign"** (bright clot inside M1 stem), **loss of insular ribbon**, **obscuration of the lentiform nucleus**, and effacement of cortical sulci (**ASPECTS Score ≥6** indicates a small-to-moderate core suitable for thrombectomy).
> - **2. CT Angiography (CTA) of the Head and Neck:** Perform **immediately alongside the initial NCCT** (do NOT delay IV tPA/TNK to wait for CTA reconstruction, and **do NOT wait for serum creatinine!**) to detect a proximal **Large Vessel Occlusion (LVO: Intracranial ICA, M1/proximal M2 MCA, or Basilar Artery)** for Endovascular Thrombectomy.
> - **3. CT Perfusion (CTP) or MRI DWI/PWI (or DWI-FLAIR):** Indicated in the **Late Window (6 to 24 hours from Last Known Well)** or **Wake-Up Strokes** to identify salvageable penumbra vs. core mismatch.
>
> **Labs & Secondary Etiologic Workup (Post-Stabilization)**
>
> - **Point-of-Care Fingerstick Blood Glucose:** **ONLY mandatory lab prior to IV thrombolysis** (must rule out hypoglycemia <50 mg/dL or extreme hyperglycemia >400 mg/dL, which mimic focal stroke!).
> - **Routine Labs:** CBC (platelets), PT/INR & aPTT, Troponin, Fasting Lipid Panel, HbA1c, TSH, Urine toxicology (cocaine/amphetamines in young patients).
> - **Etiologic "Stroke Workup" (To Determine Secondary Prevention):**
>   1. **Vascular Imaging of Carotid Bifurcation:** CTA Head/Neck, MRA, or **Carotid Duplex Ultrasound** (measures degree of symptomatic extracranial ICA stenosis: **70–99%** or **50–69%**).
>   2. **Cardiac Rhythm Monitoring:** **12-lead ECG + ≥24–72 hours of Inpatient Telemetry**, followed by **30-day Outpatient Ambulatory Holter/Event Monitor or Implantable Loop Recorder** in cryptogenic/embolic strokes to capture occult **Paroxysmal Atrial Fibrillation**.
>   3. **Echocardiography:** **Transthoracic Echocardiogram (TTE) with Agitated Saline "Bubble Study"** (or **Transesophageal Echo [TEE]** in patients <60 y with cryptogenic stroke to detect **Patent Foramen Ovale [PFO]**, left atrial appendage thrombus, or endocarditis).

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Rapid decline in consciousness (GCS drop ≥2 points), new Cushing's triad (hypertension + bradycardia), or unilateral blown pupil:** Indicates **Hemorrhagic Transformation** (especially within 24h of tPA) OR **Malignant MCA / Cerebellar Edema with Herniation** $\rightarrow$ stop tPA infusion immediately and get **STAT Non-Contrast Head CT**!
> - **Cerebellar Stroke (Vertigo, ataxia, vomiting):** High risk of rapid **edematous swelling in the tight posterior fossa** within 24–72 hours, compressing the **4th ventricle (acute obstructive hydrocephalus)** and causing fatal **upward transtentorial or downward tonsillar brainstem herniation**!
> - **Acute Stroke + Tearing Chest/Back Pain or Unequal Arm Blood Pressures:** **Type A Aortic Dissection extending into the Carotid Artery** $\rightarrow$ **Giving IV tPA is 100% FATAL (causes hemopericardium/rupture)!**
> - **ABCD² Score ≥4 in TIA:** (**A**ge ≥60 [1], **B**P ≥140/90 [1], **C**linical: unilateral weakness [2] or speech disturbance without weakness [1], **D**uration: ≥60 min [2] or 10–59 min [1], **D**iabetes [1]) $\rightarrow$ high 48-hour stroke risk; requires inpatient admission or rapid-access stroke clinic within 24h.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss ("Stroke Mimics" — Do NOT Give tPA to Hazardous Mimics!)**
>
> - **Intracerebral Hemorrhage (ICH) or Subdural/Subarachnoid Hemorrhage:** Clinically indistinguishable from ischemic stroke (though more often has headache, vomiting, and severe BP elevation); excluded immediately by **Non-Contrast Head CT**.
> - **Hypoglycemia (<50 mg/dL):** Can produce **focal hemiparesis and aphasia** identical to an MCA stroke; resolves instantly with **IV Dextrose (D50W)**.
> - **Todd's Paralysis (Postictal Paresis):** Transient focal unilateral weakness lasting hours after a focal or generalized seizure (witnessed convulsion, tongue biting, urinary incontinence).
> - **Type A Aortic Dissection or Bacterial Endocarditis (Septic Emboli):** Both are **absolute contraindications to IV thrombolysis** (endocarditis septic emboli have a massive rate of mycotic aneurysm rupture and intracranial hemorrhage with tPA!).
>
> **Most Likely (Benign Mimics)**
>
> - **Hemiplegic / Complex Migraine with Aura:** Marching sensory/visual/motor symptoms evolving gradually over 15–30 minutes (unlike sudden 1-second vascular stroke onset) followed by throbbing headache in a young patient.
> - **Bell's Palsy (Peripheral CN VII Palsy) vs. Central Stroke Facial Palsy:**
>   - **Bell's Palsy (LMN):** Paralyzes the **ENTIRE ipsilateral half of the face (forehead CANNOT wrinkle**, eye cannot close).
>   - **Stroke (UMN):** **Spares the forehead** (due to bilateral corticobulbar innervation of the upper face) and weakens only the **contralateral lower face**!
> - **Peripheral Vestibular Neuronitis / BPPV vs. Posterior Circulation Stroke (Use the HINTS Exam!):** In acute continuous vertigo, a **Central Brainstem/Cerebellar Stroke** is indicated by **ANY 1 of the "INFARCT" HINTS findings**: **I**mpulse test (Head Impulse) **N**ormal, **F**ast-phase **A**lternating (direction-changing) nystagmus, or **R**efixation on **C**over **T**est (vertical **Skew deviation**)!
> - **Functional Neurological Disorder (Conversion Disorder) or Multiple Sclerosis Flare.**

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Rapid arterial recanalization (**Door-to-Needle <45–60 min** for IV thrombolysis; **Door-to-Groin Puncture <60–90 min** for Thrombectomy) to rescue the ischemic penumbra.
> - **Secondary Target:** Maintain **Permissive Hypertension** (if not lysed) or **Strict BP Control (<180/105 mmHg)** (if lysed) to prevent hemorrhagic transformation, prevent malignant cerebral edema, and prevent recurrent stroke.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (Hyperacute Reperfusion Therapy & Blood Pressure Targets)**
>
> - **Stabilize ABCs, check Fingerstick Glucose, maintain $\text{SpO}_2 \ge 94\%$, treat fever ($\text{T} <37.5^\circ\text{C}$ with Acetaminophen), and target Euglycemia (BG 140–180 mg/dL)** (both fever and hyperglycemia directly expand infarct size!).
> - **Manage Blood Pressure Strictly According to Thrombolysis Status (Crucial Exam & Point-of-Care Rule!):**
>   - **IF Eligible for IV Thrombolysis (tPA / TNK):** Blood pressure **MUST be lowered to <185/110 mmHg BEFORE starting the bolus** (using **IV Labetalol 10–20 mg IV push** or **IV Nicardipine infusion 5–15 mg/hr** or **Clevidipine**), and **maintained strictly <180/105 mmHg for the first 24 hours AFTER thrombolysis** to prevent fatal intracerebral hemorrhage!
>   - **IF NOT Receiving IV Thrombolysis (and No Thrombectomy Reperfusion):** **Maintain "Permissive Hypertension" for the first 24–48 hours!** **Do NOT lower blood pressure unless BP is >220/120 mmHg** (or if there is a concomitant medical emergency requiring acute BP lowering: **acute MI, aortic dissection, acute pulmonary edema, or hypertensive encephalopathy**), because high systemic BP is required to perfuse the ischemic penumbra via collateral vessels! (If >220/120 mmHg, lower gently by only 15% over 24 hours).
> - **1. Administer IV Thrombolysis (Within 0 to 4.5 Hours of Last Known Well — AHA/ASA Guidelines):**
>   - **Agents:** **IV Alteplase (tPA: 0.9 mg/kg, max 90 mg**; give **10% as a 1-minute IV bolus** and infuse the remaining **90% over 60 minutes**) **OR** **IV Tenecteplase (TNK: 0.25 mg/kg, max 25 mg as a single 5-second IV bolus)** in patients with disabling stroke presenting **within ≤3 hours (or 3 to 4.5 hours)** of LKW (or Wake-Up Stroke with **MRI DWI-FLAIR mismatch**).
>   - **Absolute Rule After IV Thrombolysis:** **Withhold ALL Antiplatelets (Aspirin/Clopidogrel) and Anticoagulants (including DVT prophylaxis heparin!) for 24 hours after tPA/TNK**, and obtain a **repeat Non-Contrast Head CT at 24 hours** to confirm absence of hemorrhage before starting antiplatelets!
> - **2. Perform Endovascular Mechanical Thrombectomy (EVT — Stent-Retriever / Aspiration):**
>   - **Standard Early Window (0 to 6 Hours from LKW):** Perform EVT in all patients with an **Anterior Circulation Large Vessel Occlusion (Intracranial ICA or M1 MCA)** on CTA, **NIHSS ≥6**, and **ASPECTS ≥6** (and **up to 24 hours in Basilar Artery Occlusion**). _Crucial Rule: If the patient is also within the 4.5-hour IV thrombolysis window, **give IV tPA/TNK FIRST and bridge immediately to Thrombectomy** (never skip tPA to wait for thrombectomy, and never delay thrombectomy to see if tPA works!)._
>   - **Extended Late Window (6 to 24 Hours from LKW — DAWN & DEFUSE-3 Trials; PLUS Large-Core Strokes [ASPECTS 3–5] per SELECT2 / ANGEL-ASPECT Trials):** Perform EVT up to **24 hours** from LKW if CTA confirms an anterior LVO and CT Perfusion/MRI shows salvageable brain tissue (clinical-core or perfusion mismatch) or even in large ischemic cores (**ASPECTS 3–5**)!
>
> **Management of Acute Complications (tPA Bleeding & Malignant Edema)**
>
> - **Symptomatic Intracranial Hemorrhage After tPA/TNK (Occurs in ~6% within 24–36h):**
>   - **STOP the tPA infusion immediately**, order a **STAT Non-Contrast Head CT**, check CBC/PT/aPTT/Fibrinogen, and **administer 10 units of Cryoprecipitate IV** (to raise fibrinogen >150 mg/dL) **PLUS Tranexamic Acid (TXA 1,000 mg IV) or Aminocaproic Acid**!
> - **Malignant MCA Infarction Edema or Space-Occupying Cerebellar Infarction (Peaks at 48–96 Hours):**
>   - **Elevate head of bed to 30°, administer Hyperosmolar Therapy (Hypertonic Saline [3% or 23.4% NaCl] or IV Mannitol)** as a temporary bridge, and **consult Neurosurgery STAT for Emergent Decompressive Hemicraniectomy (within <48 hours in patients ≤60 years with Malignant MCA edema)** OR **Suboccipital Decompressive Craniectomy + EVD** (for **Cerebellar Infarction causing brainstem compression or hydrocephalus**—life-saving!).
>
> **Secondary Stroke Prevention (Tailored by Etiology)**
>
> - **Mandatory Bedside Dysphagia Screen BEFORE Any Oral Intake (Water, Food, or Oral Aspirin!):** Aspiration pneumonia is a leading cause of stroke death; if the patient fails the swallow screen, give **Rectal Aspirin (300 mg suppository)** or administer via NG tube.
> - **Non-Cardioembolic Stroke / TIA (Atherosclerotic, Lacunar, or Cryptogenic):**
>   - **If No tPA/TNK Given:**
>     - _Minor Stroke (NIHSS ≤3–5) or High-Risk TIA ($\text{ABCD}^2 \ge 4$) Presenting Within 24–72 Hours:_ **Initiate Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (DAPT): Load with Aspirin (160–325 mg) + Clopidogrel (300–600 mg)** (or Ticagrelor), continue **Aspirin 81 mg + Clopidogrel 75 mg daily for 21 DAYS ONLY**, then transition to **lifelong Single Antiplatelet Therapy (Aspirin 81 mg daily, Clopidogrel 75 mg daily, or Aspirin/Dipyridamole)** (**CHANCE / POINT trials**).
>     - _Moderate-to-Severe Stroke (NIHSS >5):_ Start **Aspirin (160–325 mg PO/PR)** within 24–48 hours (after 24h post-tPA CT if lysed).
>   - **Symptomatic Extracranial Internal Carotid Artery (ICA) Stenosis (Ipsilateral to TIA/Non-Disabling Stroke):**
>     - **70–99% Stenosis (or 50–69% in select Men):** **Perform Carotid Endarterectomy (CEA) (preferred in patients ≥70 years) or Carotid Artery Stenting (CAS) WITHIN 2 WEEKS (48 hours to 14 days) of the index stroke/TIA** (**NASCET / CREST trials**; delay >2 weeks loses most of the preventative benefit!).
>     - **<50% Stenosis OR 100% Complete Carotid Occlusion:** **Medical Management ONLY!** (**Never operate on a 100% completely occluded carotid artery**—there is zero lumen to open and surgery only embolizes distal clot!).
>   - **Symptomatic Intracranial Atherosclerotic Stenosis (70–99% of Intracranial ICA, MCA, or Basilar):** **Treat with Aggressive Medical Management ONLY (DAPT with Aspirin + Clopidogrel for 90 days + High-Intensity Statin + Target BP <140/90 mmHg)** (**SAMMPRIS trial** proved that intracranial stenting doubles stroke/death rates compared to medical therapy!).
> - **Cardioembolic Stroke (Atrial Fibrillation):**
>   - **Initiate Lifelong Oral Anticoagulation with a Direct Oral Anticoagulant (DOAC: Apixaban, Rivaroxaban, Dabigatran, or Edoxaban — preferred over Warfarin unless moderate-to-severe rheumatic mitral stenosis or mechanical heart valve)** (_Timing of Initiation to Avoid Hemorrhagic Transformation ["1 – 3 – 6 – 12 Day Rule" or **ELAN Trial**]:_ Start **Day 1–2** for TIA/minor stroke; **Day 3–6** for moderate stroke; **Day 6–14** [after repeat CT rules out hemorrhagic transformation] for large territorial infarcts!).
> - **Universal Risk Factor Modification (All Ischemic Strokes):**
>   - **High-Intensity Statin:** **Atorvastatin 80 mg PO daily** or **Rosuvastatin 20–40 mg PO daily** (target **LDL <70 mg/dL** per **SPARCL / Treat Stroke to Target trials**).
>   - **Chronic Blood Pressure Lowering (Start >48–72 hours post-stroke once stable):** Target **BP <130/80 mmHg** using an **ACE Inhibitor/ARB + Thiazide Diuretic or CCB**.
>   - **Patent Foramen Ovale (PFO) Closure:** Perform **percutaneous PFO device closure + antiplatelet therapy** ONLY in patients aged **18–60 years with a Cryptogenic non-lacunar stroke** and a high-risk PFO (large shunt or atrial septal aneurysm) after ruling out all other causes.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
> **Key Absolute Contraindications to IV Thrombolysis (Alteplase / Tenecteplase — Must Memorize!):**
>
> - **Any Intracranial Hemorrhage on Non-Contrast Head CT** or history of **ANY prior Intracranial Hemorrhage**.
> - **Severe Uncontrolled Hypertension (BP >185/110 mmHg)** that cannot be lowered safely prior to bolus.
> - **Prior Ischemic Stroke, Severe Head Trauma, or Intracranial/Intraspinal Surgery within the past 3 MONTHS.**
> - **Intra-axial Intracranial Neoplasm**, **Arteriovenous Malformation (AVM)**, **Infective Endocarditis**, or **Suspected Aortic Dissection**.
> - **Active Internal Bleeding** or **GI Malignancy / GI Bleed within 21 days**.
> - **Coagulopathy / Anticoagulants:** **Platelets <100,000/µL**, **INR >1.7**, **aPTT >40s**, therapeutic LMWH within 24 hours, or **DOAC (Apixaban/Rivaroxaban/Dabigatran) taken within the past 48 hours** (unless normal thrombin time/anti-Xa or reversed with Idarucizumab for dabigatran).
>
> **Other Critical Management Pitfalls:**
>
> - **Avoid Continuing Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (Aspirin + Clopidogrel) Long-Term (>21–90 Days) for Secondary Stroke Prevention** (outside of recent stenting), as long-term DAPT significantly increases **major and fatal intracranial hemorrhage** without reducing recurrent stroke (**MATCH / CHARISMA trials**).
> - **Avoid Aggressive Acute Blood Pressure Lowering (<180/100 mmHg) in the first 24–48 hours in Non-Thrombolysed Stroke Patients**, as dropping systemic BP collapses collateral perfusion and **converts the salvageable penumbra into dead core**!
> - **Avoid Hypotonic IV Fluids (0.45% NaCl or D5W)** in acute stroke, as free water enters ischemic brain tissue and **worsens cerebral edema** (use **isotonic 0.9% Normal Saline** only).

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Hemorrhagic Transformation (Petechial vs. Parenchymal Hematoma):** Spontaneous or tPA-induced bleeding into necrotic ischemic tissue (highest risk in **large cardioembolic strokes** and **uncontrolled BP >180/105 mmHg**) $\rightarrow$ presents with acute neurological deterioration and headache $\rightarrow$ reverse tPA with **Cryoprecipitate (10 U) + TXA**.
> - **Malignant Cerebral / Cerebellar Edema & Herniation:** Cytotoxic and vasogenic edema peaks at **Days 3 to 5** after a large MCA or cerebellar stroke $\rightarrow$ requires **early Decompressive Hemicraniectomy (<48h)** or **Suboccipital Craniectomy**.
> - **Aspiration Pneumonia (#1 Medical Cause of Death Post-Stroke):** Bulbar dysfunction / impaired gag reflex $\rightarrow$ prevented by strict **NPO until bedside swallow evaluation** is passed.
> - **Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) & Pulmonary Embolism:** Paralyzed lower extremity $\rightarrow$ apply **Intermittent Pneumatic Compression (IPC) sleeves on Day 1** (superior to graduated compression stockings, which cause skin necrosis per **CLOTS trial**) and start **subcutaneous LMWH** (wait until **24 hours after tPA** and repeat CT!).
> - **Post-Stroke Seizures & Post-Stroke Depression (~30%):** Do **NOT** give prophylactic antiepileptic drugs; treat only if clinical seizures occur (prefer **Levetiracetam** or **Lamotrigine**). Screen all patients for post-stroke depression and treat with **SSRIs**.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> **Scenario 1 (Hyperacute LVO Stroke):** A 68-year-old woman with hypertension and atrial fibrillation (off anticoagulation) develops sudden right-sided face and arm paralysis, global aphasia, and left gaze preference (**NIHSS = 18**) witnessed by her husband **75 minutes ago**. Fingerstick glucose is 118 mg/dL and BP is 194/108 mmHg. **Non-Contrast Head CT** shows no hemorrhage (ASPECTS = 9), and concurrent **CTA Head/Neck** demonstrates an **acute Left M1 Middle Cerebral Artery occlusion**. Her BP is rapidly lowered to **176/98 mmHg (<185/110) with IV Labetalol 10 mg**, she receives an immediate **IV Tenecteplase (0.25 mg/kg) bolus** at 35 minutes from arrival, and is transferred directly to the angiosuite for **Mechanical Thrombectomy**, achieving complete recanalization (TICI 3). Aspirin and heparin are held for 24 hours until a repeat Head CT confirms no hemorrhage, and **Apixaban** is initiated on Day 4.
> **Scenario 2 (High-Risk TIA):** A 62-year-old man with diabetes and smoking history presents 3 hours after a 45-minute episode of slurred speech and left hand weakness that has now completely resolved (**NIHSS = 0, $\text{ABCD}^2\text{ Score} = 5$**). Non-Contrast Head CT is negative for blood and DWI-MRI shows no acute infarct. He is loaded immediately with **Aspirin 325 mg + Clopidogrel 300 mg**, placed on **Atorvastatin 80 mg daily**, and undergoes a **CTA Neck**, which reveals an **80% right internal carotid artery stenosis**; he undergoes a successful **right Carotid Endarterectomy (CEA) 4 days later** and transitions from DAPT to single antiplatelet therapy at Day 21.
