> [!abstract] Peripheral Neuropathies
> Heterogeneous group of disorders characterized by damage to the peripheral motor, sensory, or autonomic nerves, broadly categorized by pattern: **Length-Dependent Polyneuropathy** (symmetric "stocking-glove"), **Mononeuropathy Multiplex** (patchy, asymmetric, multifocal), or **Focal Mononeuropathy** (single nerve entrapment/ischemia).

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Axonal Degeneration (#1 Pattern — 80%):** Length-dependent "dying back" of the longest axons from metabolic, toxic, or ischemic insults (distal lower extremities affected first; normal conduction velocity with **markedly reduced CMAP/SNAP amplitudes**).
> - **Segmental Demyelination:** Immune-mediated or genetic disruption of Schwann cells and myelin sheaths (uniform in CMT1; patchy with **conduction block and severe velocity slowing** in CIDP).
> - **Vasa Nervorum Ischemia:** Epineurial arteriole necrotizing vasculitis causing focal nerve infarction (**Mononeuropathy Multiplex**).

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Mononeuropathy Multiplex = Systemic Vasculitis until proven otherwise:** Acute/subacute, painful, asymmetric sensory and motor loss in $\ge 2$ separate named peripheral nerves (e.g., right foot drop [peroneal] + left wrist drop [radial]) is a **rheumatologic/neurologic emergency** requiring immediate **nerve/muscle biopsy and high-dose steroids**!
> - **Normal EMG/NCS with Severe Burning Feet = Small Fiber Neuropathy:** Routine EMG/NCS measures _only_ large myelinated A-alpha/beta fibers; small unmyelinated C-fibers (pain/temperature/autonomic) are electrically silent on NCS $\rightarrow$ diagnose via **Skin Punch Biopsy** showing reduced **Intraepidermal Nerve Fiber Density (IENFD)**!
> - **Diabetic Pupillary Sparing Rule:** Microvascular ischemic **Diabetic CN III Palsy** causes severe ptosis and "down-and-out" diplopia with **completely preserved (reactive) pupillary light reflexes** (ischemia affects the central somatic core, sparing superficial parasympathetic pupillomotor fibers; contrast with **PCom aneurysm**, which compresses the pupil!).
> - **Beware Vitamin B6 Toxicity:** Excessive ingestion of **Pyridoxine (Vitamin B6 >100–200 mg/day)** in health supplements causes a **severe, irreversible sensory ataxic ganglionopathy**!
> - **Copper Deficiency Mimics B12:** Subacute combined degeneration (myeloneuropathy) with normal B12 levels is often **Copper Deficiency** (frequently caused by zinc overload from dental adhesives competing for intestinal absorption).

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Sensory Symptoms (Usually First):** Length-dependent symmetric paresthesias ("pins and needles"), dysesthesias, burning pain, allodynia, or "walking on crumpled paper/pebbles"; starts in toes, ascends to knees, then appears in fingers (**"stocking-glove" distribution**).
> - **Motor Symptoms:** Distal weakness, tripping over curbs (foot drop), difficulty unscrewing jars or buttoning shirts.
> - **Autonomic Symptoms:** Postural lightheadedness (orthostatic hypotension), gastroparesis, alternating diarrhea/constipation, erectile dysfunction, neurogenic bladder, or anhidrosis.
> - **Etiologic Clues:** Diabetes mellitus (most common), alcohol excess, neurotoxic chemotherapy (Paclitaxel, Oxaliplatin, Vincristine, Bortezomib), bariatric surgery (B12, thiamine, copper), family history (high arches, claw toes = **Charcot-Marie-Tooth**).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Small-Fiber Exam:** Impaired pinprick and temperature sensation, hyperalgesia/allodynia.
> - **Large-Fiber Exam:** Impaired **128-Hz tuning fork vibration sense** at the great toe (earliest and most sensitive physical finding!), impaired joint position sense (proprioception), and a **positive Romberg sign** (sensory ataxia).
> - **Reflexes:** **Loss of Achilles deep tendon reflexes (ankle jerks)** is the universal early physical hallmark of length-dependent polyneuropathy.
> - **Motor & Musculoskeletal Exam:** Distal muscle wasting (extensor digitorum brevis, thenar/interossei); pes cavus (high arched feet) and hammer toes (**Charcot-Marie-Tooth Disease**).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Distal Symmetric Sensorimotor Polyneuropathy (DSPN):** Bilateral, symmetrical, length-dependent sensory and/or motor symptoms and signs **PLUS electrodiagnostic confirmation (NCS/EMG)** demonstrating axonal loss or demyelination.
> - **Small Fiber Neuropathy (SFN):** Characteristic burning distal sensory symptoms + normal strength, normal reflexes, and **normal large-fiber NCS/EMG**, CONFIRMED by **Punch Skin Biopsy of the distal leg (10 cm above lateral malleolus)** showing **reduced Intraepidermal Nerve Fiber Density (IENFD)** below the 5th percentile.
> - **Chronic Inflammatory Demyelinating Polyradiculoneuropathy (CIDP — EAN/PNS Criteria):**
>   - Progressive, symmetric or asymmetric, proximal AND distal weakness evolving over **$>8$ weeks**, **generalized hyporeflexia/areflexia**, AND
>   - Electrodiagnostic proof of **peripheral demyelination in $\ge 2$ nerves** (motor conduction velocity slowing $<70\%$, prolonged distal latencies $>150\%$, temporal dispersion, or **partial motor conduction block**).

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Tier 1 (Mandatory Laboratory Screen for All Unexplained Polyneuropathies)**
>
> - **1. Fasting Plasma Glucose & HbA1c** (or 2-hour Oral Glucose Tolerance Test; impaired glucose tolerance / prediabetes is a leading cause of painful small fiber neuropathy!).
> - **2. Serum Vitamin B12 + Methylmalonic Acid (MMA):** Serum MMA is mandatory because up to 30% of functional, neuro-damaging B12 deficiencies have "normal" borderline serum B12 levels (200–400 pg/mL).
> - **3. Serum Protein Electrophoresis with Immunofixation (SPEP / IFE) & Serum Free Light Chains (sFLC):** Screens for **Monoclonal Gammopathy of Undetermined Significance (MGUS)**, Multiple Myeloma, and AL Amyloidosis (routine SPEP alone misses up to 40% of light chain pathology!).
> - **4. Comprehensive Metabolic Panel (BUN/Cr, LFTs) & TSH.**
>
> **Tier 2 (Targeted Electrodiagnostics & Specialized Workup)**
>
> - **Nerve Conduction Studies & Needle Electromyography (NCS/EMG):**
>   - **Axonal vs. Demyelinating:** Axonal shows reduced amplitudes with preserved velocities; demyelinating shows conduction velocity slowing, prolonged distal latencies, and conduction block.
>   - **Symmetric Length-Dependent vs. Multifocal:** Distinguishes distal polyneuropathy from **Mononeuropathy Multiplex**, radiculopathy, or plexopathy.
> - **Systemic Vasculitis / Autoimmune Panel (Indicated for Rapidly Progressive or Asymmetric Neuropathy):** ANA, ANCA (p-ANCA/c-ANCA), Rheumatoid Factor, **Cryoglobulins**, ESR/CRP, Hepatitis B/C serologies, and HIV.
> - **Superficial Peroneal Nerve / Sural Nerve & Muscle Biopsy:** Gold standard for confirming **systemic necrotizing vasculitic neuropathy** (transmural fibrinoid necrosis of epineurial vessels) or **Amyloid Neuropathy** (Congo red staining with apple-green birefringence).

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Acute or subacute asymmetric, patchy multifocal motor/sensory loss (Mononeuropathy Multiplex)** $\rightarrow$ systemic necrotizing vasculitis (requires immediate steroids/immunosuppression to prevent permanent limb paralysis).
> - **Motor-predominant weakness with proximal AND distal involvement** (suggests CIDP, GBS, or ALS).
> - **Rapid progression of numbness and weakness over days to weeks.**
> - Prominent early autonomic failure with non-diabetic weight loss and nephrotic-range proteinuria (suspect **AL Amyloidosis**).
> - Neuropathy accompanied by systemic "B-symptoms" (fever, night sweats, palpable purpura).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Systemic Vasculitic Neuropathy (Polyarteritis Nodosa, Microscopic Polyangiitis, EGPA, Cryoglobulinemia):** Painful mononeuropathy multiplex with systemic organ involvement.
> - **AL / Hereditary Amyloid Neuropathy:** Severe painful small-fiber neuropathy + early autonomic failure + cardiomyopathy.
> - **Paraneoplastic Sensory Neuronopathy (Anti-Hu / ANNA-1):** Small Cell Lung Cancer presenting with severe, rapid, asymmetric, non-length-dependent sensory ataxia (dorsal root ganglionitis).
> - **Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS) / CIDP:** Acute (<4 weeks) vs. chronic (>8 weeks) symmetric ascending paralysis with areflexia.
>
> **Most Likely (Etiologies of Symmetric Sensorimotor Polyneuropathy)**
>
> - **Diabetic Sensorimotor Polyneuropathy (DSPN):** #1 cause in developed nations; length-dependent stocking-glove sensory loss.
> - **Alcoholic & Nutritional Neuropathy:** Direct alcohol neurotoxicity combined with thiamine (B1) and folate deficiency.
> - **Uremic Neuropathy:** Advanced renal failure; improves with hemodialysis or renal transplantation.
> - **Chemotherapy-Induced Peripheral Neuropathy (CIPN):** Platinum agents, taxanes, vinca alkaloids.
>
> **Less Common**
>
> - **Charcot-Marie-Tooth Disease (CMT1A):** Autosomal dominant duplication of **PMP22**; presents in childhood/adolescence with distal motor weakness, foot drop, pes cavus, and hammer toes.
> - **Multifocal Motor Neuropathy (MMN):** Pure asymmetric painless motor weakness in named nerve distributions (e.g., wrist drop) with **high-titer anti-GM1 antibodies** and conduction block on NCS; **worsens with steroids**; responds to **IVIG**.
> - **Leprosy (Hansen Disease):** _Mycobacterium leprae_; hypopigmented anesthetic skin macules with palpable, visibly enlarged peripheral nerves (greater auricular, ulnar, common peroneal).

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Identify and treat the reversible underlying etiology (glycemic control, nutritional repletion, immunosuppression for vasculitis/CIDP) to halt axonal loss.
> - **Secondary Target:** Achieve meaningful neuropathic pain relief (**$\ge 30–50\%$ pain reduction**), prevent diabetic foot ulcerations/amputations, and restore functional mobility.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Initiate High-Dose IV Corticosteroids (Methylprednisolone 1,000 mg IV daily for 3 to 5 days) PLUS Cyclophosphamide or Rituximab** immediately upon clinical suspicion of **Systemic Vasculitic Neuropathy (Mononeuropathy Multiplex)** to prevent irreversible ischemic nerve infarction.
> - **Initiate Induction Immunotherapy for CIDP** using **IV Immune Globulin (IVIG: 2 g/kg over 2–5 days)**, **Plasma Exchange (PLEX)**, or **Oral Prednisone (60 mg daily)** (all three have proven equivalent efficacy; maintenance IVIG or subcutaneous IG is standard).
>
> **First-Line Pharmacotherapy for Neuropathic Pain (AAN Guidelines)**
>
> - **Gabapentinoids (First-Line):**
>   - **Initiate Gabapentin (start 300 mg PO QHS, titrating up to 900–1,200 mg PO TID; max 3,600 mg/day)** OR **Pregabalin (start 75 mg PO BID, titrating to 150–300 mg PO BID)** (binds voltage-gated $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channel $\alpha_2\delta$ subunits; adjust dose for renal impairment; monitor for sedation and peripheral edema).
> - **Serotonin-Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitors (SNRIs — First-Line):**
>   - **Initiate Duloxetine (start 30 mg PO daily for 1 week, then 60 mg PO daily; max 120 mg/day)** (especially advantageous if comorbid depression or anxiety; avoid in severe renal or hepatic impairment).
> - **Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs — First-Line):**
>   - **Initiate Amitriptyline or Nortriptyline (10 to 25 mg PO QHS, titrating up to 50–75 mg QHS)** (prefer Nortriptyline in elderly due to lower anticholinergic and orthostatic side-effect burden; obtain baseline ECG to rule out QTc prolongation or heart block).
> - **Topical Adjuncts for Focal Neuropathic Pain:**
>   - **Apply Topical Lidocaine 5% Patch** (12 hours on, 12 hours off) or **Capsaicin 8% Topical Patch (Qutenza)** for localized, painful, allodynic skin areas.
>
> **Etiology-Specific Disease Modification**
>
> - **Diabetic Neuropathy:** Optimize glycemic control (target HbA1c 7.0–7.5%; lifestyle, metformin, SGLT2i/GLP-1a) to slow neuropathy progression.
> - **Vitamin B12 Deficiency:** **Administer Cyanocobalamin (1,000 mcg IM daily for 1 week, then weekly for 4 weeks, then monthly for life** [or 1,000–2,000 mcg PO daily if adherence confirmed]) to arrest neurologic decline (paresthesias improve, but deep sensory loss may be permanent).
> - **Neurotoxic Chemotherapy:** Dose-reduce or discontinue the offending agent upon early sensory signs to prevent permanent axonal injury.
>
> **Preventative Diabetic Foot Care**
>
> - **Perform annual 10-g Semmes-Weinstein Monofilament Sensory Testing** to identify the "insensate foot."
> - **Enforce strict daily patient foot self-inspection**, professional podiatry nail care, and fitted diabetic protective footwear to prevent unperceived pressure ulcers, osteomyelitis, and amputation.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Opioids (Oxycodone, Morphine, Fentanyl) as Routine First-Line Neuropathic Pain Therapy** to prevent substance dependence, tolerance, hyperalgesia, and sedation with minimal functional improvement.
> - **Avoid Corticosteroids in Multifocal Motor Neuropathy (MMN)**, as steroids cause paradoxical worsening of motor weakness (MMN is treated with **IVIG**, never steroids!).
> - **Avoid Chronic Pyridoxine (Vitamin B6) Supplementation $>100\text{ mg/day}$**, as excess B6 is a direct neurotoxin that destroys dorsal root ganglion cell bodies, producing irreversible **sensory neuronopathy**.
> - **Avoid Dismissing Mononeuropathy Multiplex as Simple "Multiple Entrapment Neuropathies"** (e.g., carpal tunnel plus peroneal palsy), as delaying systemic immunosuppression in vasculitis leads to permanent limb paralysis and fatal visceral ischemia.
> - **Avoid TCAs in Patients with Cardiac Conduction Abnormalities (AV block, long QTc), Severe Glaucoma, or Severe Urinary Retention** due to dangerous anticholinergic and quinidine-like cardiotoxic effects.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Diabetic Foot Ulceration & Lower Extremity Amputation:** Unperceived mechanical trauma on an insensate foot $\rightarrow$ painless ulcer over metatarsal heads $\rightarrow$ deep tissue infection $\rightarrow$ **osteomyelitis** $\rightarrow$ surgical amputation.
> - **Charcot Neuroarthropathy (Charcot Foot):** Loss of pain sensation and autonomic vasomotor control $\rightarrow$ repetitive microtrauma $\rightarrow$ joint subluxation, fractures, and collapse of the plantar arch (**"rocker-bottom foot"**).
> - **Sensory Ataxia & Severe Falls:** Loss of lower extremity proprioception $\rightarrow$ high fall risk in darkness $\rightarrow$ require cane/walker and physical therapy.
> - **Cardiac Autonomic Neuropathy (CAN):** Resting tachycardia, fixed heart rate, orthostatic syncope, and **silent (painless) myocardial infarction**.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 58-year-old man with a 10-year history of poorly controlled Type 2 Diabetes (HbA1c 9.4%) presents with 1 year of progressive burning, tingling pain in both feet that keeps him awake at night and feels like "walking on needles." Physical examination demonstrates absent vibration sensation at the bilateral great toes, reduced pinprick sensation ascending to the mid-shins in a **symmetric stocking distribution**, completely **absent Achilles deep tendon reflexes**, and normal motor strength (**Diabetic Sensorimotor Polyneuropathy**). Tier 1 labs confirm normal B12, TSH, and SPEP; he is counselled on daily visual foot inspections and glycemic optimization, and is initiated on **Duloxetine (30 mg PO daily for 1 week, then 60 mg PO daily)**, achieving a $>50\%$ reduction in burning pain and restful sleep within 4 weeks.
