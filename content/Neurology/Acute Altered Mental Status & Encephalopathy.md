> [!abstract] Acute Altered Mental Status / Encephalopathy
> Acute disturbance (developing over hours to days) in **arousal (level of consciousness: lethargy, stupor, coma)** and/or **cognition and attention (content of consciousness: Delirium / Acute Encephalopathy)** caused by global cerebral dysfunction—most commonly **toxic, metabolic, or infectious** insults (~75–85%) rather than primary structural brain lesions (~15–25%).

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Anatomic Basis of Consciousness (Requires Failure of 1 of 2 Systems):**
>   1. **Ascending Reticular Activating System (ARAS)** in the **midbrain and upper pontine tegmentum** (even a tiny focal brainstem stroke/hemorrhage or uncal herniation compressing the ARAS causes immediate coma!).
>   2. **Bilateral Cerebral Hemispheres / Thalami** (**Crucial Rule:** A _unilateral_ cerebral hemisphere lesion [e.g., standard MCA stroke] **DOES NOT cause coma or diffuse encephalopathy** unless it is so massive that it causes midline shift/compression of the contralateral hemisphere or brainstem!).
> - **Neurotransmitter Imbalance in Delirium:** **Central Acetylcholine (ACh) deficiency** + **Dopamine excess** (plus GABA/glutamate dysregulation and neuroinflammation from systemic cytokines crossing the blood-brain barrier).

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Hypoactive Delirium is the most common AND most dangerous subtype:** Elderly hospitalized patients who are quiet, withdrawn, and "just sleeping" (**Hypoactive Delirium, >50–70%**) have a **higher mortality rate** than agitated **Hyperactive Delirium** because their sepsis, hypoxia, or metabolic failure goes unrecognized!
> - **Always give IV Thiamine (500 mg) BEFORE or WITH Glucose:** Administering a dextrose bolus to a malnourished, alcoholic, or hyperemetic patient without thiamine depletes their last remaining Vitamin B1 (cofactor for pyruvate dehydrogenase, $\alpha$-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, and transketolase) and precipitates irreversible **Wernicke-Korsakoff Syndrome**!
> - **Delirium vs. Dementia (3 Key Discriminators):**
>   - **Delirium:** **Acute onset** (hours–days), **Fluctuating course** (waxing/waning, "sundowning"), **Impaired Attention** (inability to recite months of the year backward = **#1 hallmark**!), and **Altered level of consciousness** (_Reversible_).
>   - **Dementia:** **Insidious onset** (months–years), steady progression, **Preserved attention and alertness** until very late stages (_Irreversible_).
> - **Check the Bladder and the Med List in Every Elderly Patient:** **Acute Urinary Retention ("Cystocerebral Syndrome")**, **Fecal Impaction**, and **Anticholinergics / Benzodiazepines / Opioids / Diphenhydramine** are the top reversible bedside culprits of acute inpatient delirium!

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History (Obtain from Family, Caregivers, EMS, & Medication Administration Record)**
>
> - **Establish True Baseline:** Cognitive status 2 weeks ago vs. 2 hours ago; exact time of onset (instantaneous = vascular/seizure; hours–days = infectious/metabolic/toxic).
> - **High-Risk Delirium Precipitants ("AEIOU TIPS" & "DELIRIUM"):**
>   - **A**lcohol (Intoxication or **Delirium Tremens** [48–96h after last drink]), **A**cidosis, **A**mmonia (Hepatic encephalopathy).
>   - **E**lectrolytes (**Hyponatremia/Hypernatremia, Hypercalcemia**), **E**ndocrine (**Hypoglycemia**, HHS/DKA, **Myxedema Coma**, Thyroid Storm, Adrenal Crisis), **E**pilepsy (**Nonconvulsive Status Epilepticus [NCSE]** or postictal state).
>   - **I**nfection (#1 cause in elderly: **UTI, Pneumonia, Sepsis, Meningitis, HSV Encephalitis**).
>   - **O**xygen (**Hypoxia** or **Hypercapnia [$\text{CO}_2$ narcosis]** in COPD/OSA/opioids), **O**verdose (**Opioids, Benzodiazepines, Anticholinergics, Serotonin Syndrome, NMS, Salicylates, Lithium, Cefepime, Baclofen**).
>   - **U**remia (Renal failure).
>   - **T**rauma / **T**emperature (**Subdural Hematoma** in elderly after minor/unwitnessed fall! Hypothermia/Heat stroke), **T**hiamine (**Wernicke Encephalopathy**).
>   - **I**nsulin / **P**sychiatric / **P**orphyria / **S**troke / **S**hock / **S**pace-occupying lesion.
>
> **Physical (Focused Rapid Neurological & Toxidrome Exam)**
>
> - **Vital Signs (Immediate Etiologic Clues):**
>   - _Hyperthermia (>39–40°C):_ Meningitis/sepsis, **Serotonin Syndrome** (clonus!), **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome** (lead-pipe rigidity!), **Anticholinergic toxicity**, **Thyroid storm**, or **Sympathomimetic/Salicylate overdose**.
>   - _Hypothermia (<35°C):_ **Myxedema Coma**, Sepsis, Hypoglycemia, Alcohol/Sedative exposure.
>   - _Severe Hypertension (>180/120 mmHg):_ **Hypertensive Encephalopathy / PRES**, Intracerebral/Subarachnoid Hemorrhage, or Cushing's triad (with bradycardia).
> - **Pupils & Eye Movements (Localize Structural vs. Toxic):**
>   - **Pinpoint (1 mm) Pupils:** **Opioid overdose**, **Organophosphates/Cholinergics**, or **Pontine Hemorrhage**.
>   - **Dilated (Mydriatic) Pupils:** **Anticholinergics** ("blind as a bat, mad as a hatter, red as a beet, hot as a hare, dry as a bone"), **Sympathomimetics**, or **Post-anoxic encephalopathy**.
>   - **Unilateral Fixed Dilated ("Blown") Pupil:** **Uncal Transtentorial Herniation** compressing the ipsilateral **CN III** (parasympathetic fibers run on the _outer_ perimeter of CN III) or **PCom aneurysm**.
>   - **Bilateral CN VI Palsy / Nystagmus / Conjugate Gaze Palsy:** **Wernicke Encephalopathy** (Classic Triad [present in only 33%]: **Encephalopathy + Oculomotor dysfunction + Gait Ataxia**).
> - **Motor & Reflex Clues:**
>   - **Asterixis (Negative Myoclonus):** **Hepatic Encephalopathy**, **Uremia**, or **$\text{CO}_2$ Retention (Hypercapnia)**!
>   - **Lower-Extremity Hyperreflexia & Inducible Ankle Clonus:** **Serotonin Syndrome** (vs. **Lead-Pipe Rigidity & Hyporeflexia** in **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome**).
>   - **Nuchal Rigidity (Kernig/Brudzinski):** **Bacterial Meningitis** or **Subarachnoid Hemorrhage**.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> **Delirium — DSM-5 & CAM (Confusion Assessment Method) Criteria:**
>
> - **CAM Diagnosis Requires Feature 1 + Feature 2 + EITHER Feature 3 OR Feature 4:**
>   1. **Acute Onset and Fluctuating Course** (change from baseline mental status that fluctuates in severity over the course of the day), **AND**
>   2. **Inattention** (difficulty focusing, sustaining, or shifting attention; e.g., unable to recite days of the week or months of the year backward, or DIGIT span failure), **PLUS EITHER:**
>   3. **Disorganized Thinking** (rambling, incoherent, or illogical flow of ideas), **OR**
>   4. **Altered Level of Consciousness** (anything other than normal "alert": hypervigilant, lethargic, stuporous, or comatose).
> - **Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS — 3 to 15 Points):** **Eye Opening (1–4)** + **Verbal Response (1–5)** + **Motor Response (1–6)** (_Threshold:_ **GCS ≤8 = Coma** $\rightarrow$ "Less than 8, intubate!").

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Immediate Bedside & Tier 1 STAT Labs (Perform in ALL Patients)**
>
> - **1. Point-of-Care Fingerstick Blood Glucose + Pulse Oximetry ($\text{SpO}_2$):** Mandatory at **Minute 0**.
> - **2. Basic Metabolic Panel + Calcium, Magnesium, Phosphorus:** Screens for **Hyponatremia/Hypernatremia**, **Hypercalcemia** (malignancy/hyperparathyroidism), **Uremia (BUN)**, and AKI.
> - **3. Arterial or Venous Blood Gas (ABG/VBG) + Co-Oximetry (Carboxyhemoglobin) + Lactate:** Essential to detect **Acute Hypercapnic Respiratory Failure ($\text{PaCO}_2 >50\text{–}60\text{ mmHg}$ — $\text{CO}_2$ narcosis)** in lethargic patients whose $\text{SpO}_2$ looks normal on supplemental oxygen, as well as **Carbon Monoxide Poisoning** (headache/confusion in winter/house fire).
> - **4. CBC with Differential, Hepatic Panel, Urinalysis + Urine Culture, Chest X-Ray, & 12-Lead ECG:** Screens for occult infection (UTI, pneumonia), liver failure, and silent MI/QT prolongation.
> - **5. Urine & Serum Toxicology Screen:** **Acetaminophen and Salicylate levels** (mandatory in any suspected overdose), ethanol, serum osmolal gap (toxic alcohols), and drug levels (**Lithium, Digoxin, Phenytoin, Valproate, Carbamazepine**; _Note: **Valproate** can cause severe **hyperammonemic encephalopathy** even with normal LFTs and therapeutic valproate levels!_).
> - **6. Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone (TSH) & Free T4, Vitamin B12, Cortisol, & Blood Cultures (if febrile/hypothermic).**
> - **7. Post-Void Residual (PVR) Bladder Scan:** Perform at the bedside in confused elderly patients to rule out **acute urinary retention**.
>
> **Neuroimaging, LP, & EEG (Targeted Tier 2 Workup)**
>
> - **Non-Contrast Head CT (NCCT):**
>   - _Not required_ in every elderly patient with a clear, mild, reversible medical delirium (e.g., simple UTI/dehydration that rapidly clears).
>   - **MANDATORY STAT** if ANY of the following are present: (1) **Focal neurological deficit**, (2) **History of recent fall/head trauma**, (3) **Active Anticoagulation or Coagulopathy** (high risk of occult **Subdural Hematoma**!), (4) **GCS ≤12 / Stupor / Coma**, (5) **Sudden-onset headache**, (6) **Fever/meningismus** (prior to LP), or (7) **Failure to improve after 24–48 hours** of treating the suspected metabolic cause.
> - **Brain MRI (with DWI and FLAIR):** Order if Head CT is negative and suspicion exists for **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES)** (symmetric vasogenic edema in parieto-occipital lobes triggered by severe hypertension, eclampsia, tacrolimus/cyclosporine, or chemotherapy), **Wernicke Encephalopathy** (symmetric T2/FLAIR hyperintensity in the **mammillary bodies, dorsomedial thalami, and periaqueductal gray**), **HSV-1 Encephalitis** (temporal lobe hemorrhage/edema), or **Basilar Artery Stroke**.
> - **Lumbar Puncture (LP):** Perform immediately (after Head CT if immunocompromised, age ≥60, CNS lesion, seizure, papilledema, or altered consciousness) in ANY patient with **unexplained fever + altered mental status** to rule out **Bacterial Meningitis or Viral (HSV) Encephalitis**.
> - **Electroencephalography (EEG / Continuous cEEG):** Mandatory to rule out **Nonconvulsive Status Epilepticus (NCSE)** in patients with unexplained stupor/coma, subtle eye/facial twitching, or prior seizures (\*Diffuse metabolic/hepatic/uremic encephalopathy classically shows generalized slowing with **Triphasic Waves\***).

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **GCS ≤8**, loss of airway protective reflexes, or rapidly declining consciousness ($\rightarrow$ **Intubate immediately** and get STAT Head CT).
> - **Unilateral dilated non-reactive pupil, Cushing's triad (hypertension + bradycardia + irregular respirations), or decorticate/decerebrate posturing:** Signals **Acute Brain Herniation** ($\rightarrow$ elevate HOB 30°, hyperventilate briefly to $\text{PaCO}_2\text{ 30–35 mmHg}$, give **23.4% or 3% Hypertonic Saline / Mannitol**, and call Neurosurgery STAT).
> - **Fever + Neck Stiffness / Petechial Rash + Altered Mental Status:** **Acute Bacterial Meningitis or HSV Encephalitis** ($\rightarrow$ **NEVER delay IV Vancomycin + Ceftriaxone + Ampicillin [if >50 y] + Acyclovir + Dexamethasone** while waiting for Head CT or LP!).
> - **Acute AMS in an Anticoagulated or Elderly Patient (Even WITHOUT Reported Trauma):** **Acute or Chronic Subdural Hematoma** (bridging vein tear) until proven otherwise by Head CT.
> - **Confusion + Oculomotor Palsy (Nystagmus / CN VI Palsy) + Ataxia:** **Wernicke Encephalopathy** ($\rightarrow$ give **high-dose IV Thiamine 500 mg TID** immediately).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss ("Structural & Vascular Mimics of Delirium")**
>
> - **Non-Dominant (Right) MCA or PCA Inferior Division Stroke:** Can present with **acute agitated delirium, confusion, and hemispatial neglect** without obvious motor paralysis!
> - **Wernicke's (Fluent / Receptive) Aphasia (Left Superior Temporal Gyrus Stroke):** Patient produces fluent, nonsensical **"word salad"** and cannot follow commands; **frequently misdiagnosed as "acute psychosis" or "delirium"** by non-neurologists! (Clue: Patient is alert, attentive, and often has a **right superior quadrantanopia ["pie in the sky"]**).
> - **Subdural Hematoma, Subarachnoid Hemorrhage, or Basilar Artery Thrombosis.**
> - **Nonconvulsive Status Epilepticus (NCSE):** Accounts for ~8–10% of unexplained coma/stupor in the ICU.
> - **Central Nervous System Infections:** Bacterial meningitis, **HSV-1 temporal lobe encephalitis**, cerebral malaria, rocky mountain spotted fever, or neurosyphilis/cryptococcal meningitis.
> - **Life-Threatening Drug Toxidromes:**
>   - **Serotonin Syndrome:** SSRI/SNRI + Tramadol, Linezolid, MAOI, Dextromethorphan, Fentanyl, or St. John's Wort $\rightarrow$ **Rapid onset (<24h)** of **Triad: Mental status change + Autonomic hyperactivity (fever, tachycardia, diaphoresis, diarrhea) + Neuromuscular excitation (HYPERREFLEXIA and Inducible/Ocular CLONUS)** $\rightarrow$ treat by stopping agents, benzodiazepines, and **Cyproheptadine** (5-HT2A antagonist).
>   - **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Dopamine antagonists (Haloperidol, antipsychotics, metoclopramide) or abrupt withdrawal of Parkinson's levodopa $\rightarrow$ **Gradual onset (1–3 days)** of **Tetrad: Fever + Encephalopathy + Autonomic instability + "LEAD-PIPE" Muscle Rigidity with HYPOREFLEXIA** (+ elevated CK >1,000–100,000 U/L) $\rightarrow$ treat by stopping antipsychotic, cooling, IV fluids, and **Dantrolene** (ryanodine receptor antagonist) or **Bromocriptine**.
>
> **Most Likely (Common Inpatient Delirium Triggers)**
>
> - **Infection (UTI, Pneumonia, Cellulitis, Intra-abdominal Sepsis) & Dehydration / Prerenal AKI.**
> - **Polypharmacy & Medication Toxicity:** **Anticholinergics (Diphenhydramine, Oxybutynin, Hydroxyzine, TCAs)**, **Benzodiazepines**, **Opioids**, **Z-drugs (Zolpidem)**, **Corticosteroids**, **Fluoroquinolones**, **Cefepime** (causes nonconvulsive status epilepticus in renal failure!), and **H2-blockers (Famotidine)** in CKD.
> - **Alcohol Withdrawal Delirium (Delirium Tremens) or Benzodiazepine Withdrawal.**
> - **Uncontrolled Pain, Acute Urinary Retention, or Fecal Impaction.**
>
> **Most Surprising**
>
> - **Transient Global Amnesia (TGA):** Sudden, benign episode in middle-aged/older adults (often triggered by physical exertion, emotional stress, or cold water immersion) of **profound anterograde amnesia (repeatedly asking "Where am I? What happened?" every 60 seconds)** with **completely preserved personal identity, alertness, attention, and normal neurological exam**; resolves spontaneously within **<24 hours** (typically 4–8 hours; **NOT a TIA**—requires reassurance only!).
> - **Hashimoto Encephalopathy (SREAT):** Steroid-responsive encephalopathy with high anti-TPO antibodies, myoclonus, and seizures despite normal TSH.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Immediate stabilization of ABCs, **reversal of life-threatening hypoglycemia/hypoxia/opioid/thiamine emergencies within <5 minutes**, and identification/treatment of the underlying medical or structural cause.
> - **Secondary Target:** Restore baseline cognitive function using **multicomponent non-pharmacologic delirium bundles** while **minimizing physical restraints, antipsychotics, and benzodiazepines**.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (First 0–15 Minutes — The "Coma Cocktail" & Stabilization)**
>
> - **1. Airway & Oxygenation:** Administer supplemental $\text{O}_2$ if $\text{SpO}_2 <92\%$, and **intubate immediately** if **GCS ≤8**, inability to protect airway, or severe hypercapnic acidosis.
> - **2. Administer Targeted Empirical "Coma Cocktail" Interventions (Tailored to Bedside Assessment):**
>   - **Dextrose:** Check fingerstick glucose immediately; if **<60–70 mg/dL**, administer **25–50 g IV Dextrose (50–100 mL of D50W)**!
>   - **Thiamine (Vitamin B1):** Administer **High-Dose IV Thiamine (500 mg IV infused over 30 min TID for 3–5 days, followed by 250 mg IV/IM daily for 3–5 days)** **BEFORE or concurrently with Dextrose** in any patient with **alcohol use disorder, malnutrition, bariatric surgery, hyperemesis gravidarum, or suspected Wernicke Encephalopathy** (_Note: Standard 100 mg PO/IV doses are insufficient to cross the blood-brain barrier and rescue acute Wernicke Encephalopathy!_).
>   - **Naloxone (Opioid Antagonist):** Administer **IV/IN/IM Naloxone (0.04 to 0.4 mg IV, titrating up to 2–10 mg)** **ONLY if Opioid Toxicity is suspected** (triad of **CNS depression + Respiratory depression [RR <12/min] + Pinpoint Miosis**) (_Start with a low 0.04–0.1 mg dose in chronic opioid users who are breathing adequately to reverse apnea without precipitating violent acute opioid withdrawal and pulmonary edema!_).
> - **3. Treat Time-Critical CNS Infections & Hypertensive Encephalopathy Immediately:**
>   - **Suspected Bacterial Meningitis / HSV Encephalitis:** Administer **IV Ceftriaxone (2 g q12h) + IV Vancomycin + IV Ampicillin (2 g q4h if age >50, pregnant, or immunocompromised for _Listeria_) + IV Dexamethasone (0.15 mg/kg q6h given BEFORE/WITH first antibiotic dose) + IV Acyclovir (10 mg/kg q8h)** immediately after drawing blood cultures (do NOT wait for CT/LP!).
>   - **Hypertensive Encephalopathy / PRES:** Lower Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) smoothly by **20–25% within the first hour** using an **IV Nicardipine, Clevidipine, or Labetalol infusion**.
>
> **First-Line Management of Inpatient Delirium (Multicomponent Non-Pharmacologic Bundle — Gold Standard!)**
>
> - **1. Identify and Reverse the Underlying Precipitant:** Treat infection (UTI/pneumonia), rehydrate with isotonic fluids, correct electrolytes ($\text{Na}^+, \text{Ca}^{2+}$), relieve **urinary retention** (remove unnecessary indwelling Foley catheters—or straight-cath if retaining!), treat **constipation/fecal impaction**, and adequately treat **acute pain** (using scheduled acetaminophen or regional nerve blocks to spare opioids).
> - **2. Perform an Immediate "Medication Deprescribing Sweep" (Beers Criteria):** **STOP all Anticholinergics (diphenhydramine, hydroxyzine, oxybutynin, cyclobenzaprine), Benzodiazepines (lorazepam, alprazolam), Z-drugs (zolpidem), Meperidine, H2-blockers, and unnecessary Opioids/Steroids!**
> - **3. Implement the HELP (Hospital Elder Life Program) / ABCDEF ICU Bundle:**
>   - **Frequent Reorientation:** Visible clocks, calendars, whiteboards, and family presence at the bedside.
>   - **Sensory Optimization:** Ensure the patient is wearing their **eyeglasses and hearing aids** (sensory deprivation strongly drives delirium and hallucinations!).
>   - **Sleep-Wake Cycle Preservation:** Open blinds/bright light and **early mobilization/physical therapy during the day**; dark, quiet room, earplugs, and **zero unnecessary vital sign checks or blood draws between 10 PM and 6 AM**!
>   - **Remove Tethers:** Remove unnecessary IV lines, telemetry wires, Foley catheters, and **strictly avoid physical restraints** (tethers and restraints worsen agitation, delirium duration, and strangulation/fall injuries!).
>
> **Pharmacologic Rescue (Strictly Reserved ONLY for Severe Agitation Posing Imminent Physical Danger!)**
>
> - _Crucial Guideline Rule:_ **Antipsychotics DO NOT prevent delirium and DO NOT shorten the duration or mortality of delirium!** Use them **ONLY** as a last resort when severe hyperactive agitation or psychosis causes **imminent physical danger to the patient or staff** (e.g., pulling out an endotracheal tube or arterial line) or prevents life-saving medical care:
>   - **First-Line Rescue (Non-Parkinson's / Normal QTc):** **Low-Dose Haloperidol (0.5 to 1.0 mg PO, IM, or IV)** OR an **Atypical Antipsychotic—Quetiapine (12.5–25 mg PO), Olanzapine (2.5–5 mg PO/IM), or Risperidone (0.5 mg PO)**—at the **lowest effective dose for the shortest possible duration** (reassess daily to discontinue!).
>   - **If Patient Has Parkinson's Disease or Lewy Body Dementia:** **Strictly Avoid Haloperidol, Risperidone, and Olanzapine** (potent $\text{D}_2$ blockade causes catastrophic, irreversible Parkinsonian motor rigidity and NMS!); use **Quetiapine (12.5–25 mg PO)** or **Pimavanserin** ONLY!
>   - **Mechanically Ventilated ICU Delirium:** Switch sedative infusion from Lorazepam/Midazolam to **Dexmedetomidine (Precedex — central $\alpha_2$-agonist)**, which provides cooperative "arousable" sedation without respiratory depression and significantly reduces ICU delirium compared to benzodiazepines (**MENDS / SEDCOM trials**).

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Administering Benzodiazepines (Lorazepam, Diazepam, Midazolam) for Medical / Elderly Delirium**, as benzodiazepines are **deliriogenic** and paradoxically **worsen confusion, agitation, respiratory depression, and falls** (_ONLY 3 Indications for Benzodiazepines in Agitated Delirium:_ **1. Alcohol Withdrawal [Delirium Tremens]**, **2. Benzodiazepine/Barbiturate Withdrawal**, or **3. Sympathomimetic/Cocaine/Serotonin Toxicity or Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome**!).
> - **Avoid Routine Flumazenil (GABA-A Antagonist) in Coma of Unknown Etiology or Unknown Overdose**, as Flumazenil precipitates **intractable, benzodiazepine-refractory Status Epilepticus and fatal cardiac arrhythmias** in patients who are chronically dependent on benzodiazepines or who co-ingested **Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs)**!
> - **Avoid Rapid Correction of Chronic Hyponatremia (>48 hours duration) by >8 mEq/L in any 24-Hour Period**, as rapid osmotic shifts shrink astrocytes and cause **Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome (Central Pontine Myelinolysis)** (presenting 2–6 days later with irreversible **"Locked-In Syndrome"** / spastic quadriplegia and pseudobulbar palsy!).
> - **Avoid Haloperidol or Metoclopramide in Patients with Parkinson's Disease, Dementia with Lewy Bodies, Prolonged QTc (>500 ms — risk of Torsades de Pointes!), or Anticholinergic Overdose** (check an ECG before giving IV Haloperidol!).
> - **Avoid Diagnosing a New "Psychiatric Disorder" or "New Dementia" During an Acute Hospitalization**—any acute behavioral change in a medical patient (especially age >50 with no prior psychiatric history) is **organic Delirium / Encephalopathy** until proven otherwise, and dementia cannot be accurately diagnosed until acute delirium has fully resolved!

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Wernicke-Korsakoff Syndrome:** Untreated Wernicke encephalopathy progresses to irreversible **Korsakoff Amnestic Syndrome** (destruction of the **mammillary bodies and anterior/dorsomedial thalamic nuclei** [Papez circuit]) $\rightarrow$ **permanent anterograde and retrograde amnesia + confabulation** (less than 20% ever recover!).
> - **Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome (ODS):** Iatrogenic overcorrection of chronic hyponatremia (>8–10 mEq/L per 24h; highest risk in alcoholics, cirrhotics, malnourished, and hypokalemic patients) $\rightarrow$ if overcorrection occurs, immediately administer **D5W + Desmopressin (DDAVP)** to re-lower serum sodium!
> - **Aspiration Pneumonia, Pressure Ulcers, Injurious Falls, & Long-Term Cognitive Decline:** Inpatient delirium is an independent predictor of 2–3× increased 1-year mortality, institutionalization, and accelerated progression to permanent **Alzheimer's/vascular dementia**.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> **Scenario 1 (Hypoactive/Mixed Inpatient Delirium in the Elderly):** An 82-year-old man with mild BPH is postoperative Day 2 from hip fracture repair and becomes acutely disoriented, inattentive (unable to recite the days of the week backward), picking at his bedsheets, and seeing "bugs on the wall" overnight (**CAM-positive Delirium**). Vitals are stable (afebrile, $\text{SpO}_2$ 97%, glucose 115 mg/dL) and neurological exam is non-focal. Review of his MAR reveals he received **IV diphenhydramine** for itching and **scheduled oxycodone**, and a bedside bladder scan reveals **850 mL of acute urinary retention**. Diphenhydramine is discontinued, oxycodone is transitioned to scheduled acetaminophen, a straight catheter drains his bladder, his eyeglasses and hearing aids are placed on him with family reorientation, and **benzodiazepines/haloperidol are avoided**, resulting in complete resolution of his delirium by the next morning.
> **Scenario 2 (Wernicke Encephalopathy):** A 49-year-old man with chronic alcohol use disorder is brought to the ED confused and unsteady on his feet. Vitals show HR 96 bpm, BP 110/70 mmHg, and fingerstick glucose 58 mg/dL; exam reveals disorientation, **bilateral lateral rectus (CN VI) palsies, horizontal nystagmus**, and a wide-based **ataxic gait**. Recognizing **Wernicke Encephalopathy**, the clinician administers **High-Dose IV Thiamine (500 mg IV) FIRST**, followed immediately by **IV Dextrose (D50W)**, magnesium repletion, and admission for a 5-day high-dose IV thiamine protocol, preventing progression to permanent Korsakoff syndrome.
