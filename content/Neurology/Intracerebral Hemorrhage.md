> [!abstract] Intracerebral Hemorrhage
> Non-traumatic spontaneous bleeding directly into the **brain parenchyma** (often extending into the ventricles), accounting for ~10–15% of all strokes and carrying a **30–40% 30-day mortality rate** (driven by **hematoma expansion in the first 3–6 hours**, mass effect, and brainstem herniation).

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Deep Perforating Arteriole Rupture (#1 Cause — Chronic Hypertension):** Chronic hypertension induces **lipohyalinosis** and **Charcot-Bouchard microaneurysms** in small, unbranched deep penetrating arteries (**lenticulostriate** [putamen/globus pallidus], **thalamoperforating** [thalamus], **paramedian branches of basilar artery** [pons], and **superior cerebellar artery branches** [dentate nucleus of cerebellum]).
> - **Superficial Lobar Vessel Rupture (#2 Cause in Elderly — Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy [CAA]):** Deposition of **$\beta$-amyloid ($A\beta_{40}$)** in the tunica media/adventitia of small-to-medium **cortical and leptomeningeal arteries** (apple-green birefringence on **Congo red** stain) $\rightarrow$ brittle vessel walls $\rightarrow$ **recurrent superficial lobar hemorrhages** (parieto-occipital > frontal/temporal) in normotensive patients **>60–65 years** (often with Alzheimer's dementia / ApoE $\varepsilon2/\varepsilon4$).

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Location tells you the Etiology (Deep vs. Lobar):**
>   - **Deep Subcortical (Putamen/Basal Ganglia [50%], Thalamus [15%], Pons [10%], Deep Cerebellum [10%]) = Chronic Hypertension.**
>   - **Lobar (Cortical/Subcortical Junction) = Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy (if >60 y)** OR **Arteriovenous Malformation (AVM) / Cavernoma / Cocaine / Venous Thrombosis / Tumor (if <50 y)**.
> - **Smoothly lower SBP to 130–140 mmHg within 1 hour (INTERACT2 / ATACH-2 / INTERACT3 Care Bundle):** Unlike ischemic stroke (where you allow permissive hypertension up to 220/120), in acute ICH with SBP 150–220 mmHg, **immediately and smoothly lower SBP to a target of 130–150 mmHg (specifically ~140 mmHg)** to halt active hematoma expansion—_while strictly avoiding lowering SBP <130 mmHg or dropping SBP by >60 mmHg in 1 hour, which causes acute kidney injury and cerebral ischemia!_
> - **Cerebellar Hemorrhage >3 cm = Immediate Surgical Evacuation:** Any cerebellar hematoma **>3 cm in diameter** (or causing **brainstem compression or obstructive hydrocephalus**) is a **Neurosurgical Emergency** requiring immediate **Suboccipital Craniectomy and Hematoma Evacuation** (do NOT place an EVD alone without decompressing the posterior fossa, as upward transtentorial herniation can occur!).
> - **Never give Platelets for Aspirin/Clopidogrel-associated ICH (PATCH Trial):** Unless the patient is undergoing an open neurosurgical craniotomy, **platelet transfusion in antiplatelet-associated ICH INCREASES death and disability**!

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Progressive "Smooth" Worsening over 30–120 Minutes (Unlike Embolic Stroke):** While ischemic embolic stroke is maximal at 1 second, ICH typically begins during **waking active hours** with a focal deficit that **steadily worsens over minutes to hours** as the hematoma expands and raises Intracranial Pressure (ICP).
> - **Elevated ICP Symptoms (Much more common than in Ischemic Stroke):** **Severe headache**, **projectile vomiting**, **acute hypertension**, and **rapidly declining level of consciousness (stupor/coma)**.
> - **Risk Factor Screen:** Uncontrolled hypertension, **warfarin or DOAC use**, dual antiplatelet therapy, recent thrombolysis (tPA), **sympathomimetic abuse (cocaine, methamphetamine, phenylpropanolamine)**, heavy alcohol use, cirrhosis/thrombocytopenia, or metastatic cancer (**Melanoma, Choriocarcinoma, Renal Cell Carcinoma, Thyroid, Lung**—**Mnemonic:MR. CT Lung** bleed into brain metastases!).
>
> **Physical — Classic Anatomic Localization Syndromes (High-Yield Exam Triads!)**
>
> - **1. Putamen / Internal Capsule (Most Common Site — 35–50%):**
>   - **Contralateral dense hemiplegia** (face, arm, leg) + **contralateral hemisensory loss** + **contralateral homonymous hemianopia**, AND
>   - **Eyes deviate TOWARD the hematoma** (away from the paralyzed side: **"looks at the lesion"**).
> - **2. Thalamus (10–15%):**
>   - **Contralateral hemisensory loss > motor hemiparesis**, AND
>   - **Classic Ocular Signs (Midbrain Tectum Compression):** **Eyes deviate DOWNWARD and INWARD ("peering at the tip of the nose" / Parinaud syndrome)**, **small sluggish/non-reactive pupils**, and convergence retraction nystagmus; very high rate of **intraventricular extension (IVH)** into the 3rd ventricle causing acute hydrocephalus.
> - **3. Pons (5–10% — Often Catastrophic):**
>   - **Rapid onset of deep coma** within minutes (reticular activating system destruction) + **Quadriplegia / Decerebrate posturing**,
>   - **Pinpoint (1-mm) reactive pupils** (sympathetic descending tract destruction with intact midbrain CN III parasympathetics; **indistinguishable from opioid overdose!**), AND
>   - **Absent horizontal eye movements** (absent oculocephalic ["doll's eyes"] and cold caloric reflexes) + **ocular bobbing** + hyperthermia.
> - **4. Cerebellum (10% — Posterior Fossa Emergency):**
>   - **Sudden inability to stand or walk (truncal/gait ataxia)**, severe occipital headache, **repeated vomiting**, dizziness/vertigo, **ipsilateral limb ataxia**, and **ipsilateral facial palsy or 6th nerve/conjugate gaze palsy**—initially **WITHOUT hemiparesis**! (Rapidly progresses over hours to **obstructive hydrocephalus, brainstem compression, coma, and death** if not surgically evacuated).
> - **5. Lobar (Cortical — 20–25%):**
>   - **Seizures at onset** (much more common in lobar ICH [~15–20%] than deep ICH!) + focal cortical signs (**Frontal**: contralateral arm/face weakness, expressive aphasia; **Parietal**: hemisensory loss, neglect; **Occipital**: contralateral homonymous hemianopia; **Temporal**: fluent Wernicke aphasia).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Non-Contrast Head CT (NCCT — Gold Standard, ~100% Sensitivity):** Acute intraparenchymal hemorrhage appears immediately as a **well-demarcated, hyperdense (bright white) mass** within the brain parenchyma, surrounded by a hypodense rim of perihematomal edema, ± mass effect, midline shift, or **Intraventricular Hemorrhage (IVH)**.
> - **Hematoma Volume Calculation ($\text{ABC}/2$ Formula on Head CT — Critical Prognostic Metric):**
>   $$\text{Volume (mL)} = \frac{A \times B \times C}{2}$$
>   - **$A$:** Largest hemorrhage diameter (cm) on the CT slice with the biggest clot area.
>   - **$B$:** Largest diameter (cm) **perpendicular ($90^\circ$)** to $A$ on that same slice.
>   - **$C$:** Number of CT slices containing hemorrhage multiplied by the **slice thickness (cm)** (e.g., 0.5 cm).
>   - _Prognostic Threshold:_ **Volume >30 mL** (or **>60 mL** with GCS <8) carries >80–90% mortality without intervention.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Immediate Labs**
>
> - **STAT Coagulation & Hematology Panel:** **PT/INR, aPTT, CBC (Platelet count), Fibrinogen**, Type and Screen, CMP, **Troponin + ECG** (neurogenic stunned myocardium), **Fingerstick Glucose**, and **Urine Toxicology Screen (Cocaine / Amphetamines)** in all patients <55 years or without known hypertension.
> - **Calculate the ICH Score (0 to 6 Points — Predicts 30-Day Mortality, though Do NOT use it to prematurely withdraw life support in the first 24–48h!):**
>   1. **GCS Score:** 3–4 (**2 pts**), 5–12 (**1 pt**), 13–15 (**0 pts**).
>   2. **ICH Volume ($\text{ABC}/2$):** **≥30 mL** (**1 pt**), <30 mL (**0 pts**).
>   3. **Intraventricular Hemorrhage (IVH):** Yes (**1 pt**), No (**0 pts**).
>   4. **Infratentorial (Cerebellar/Pontine) Origin:** Yes (**1 pt**), No (**0 pts**).
>   5. **Age:** **≥80 years** (**1 pt**), <80 years (**0 pts**).
>
> **Vascular & Etiologic Imaging**
>
> - **CT Angiography (CTA) of the Head (Performed with the Initial NCCT):**
>   - **1. Identifies the "Spot Sign" (Contrast extravasation focus inside the hematoma):** Strongly predicts **ongoing active bleeding, massive hematoma expansion, and mortality**!
>   - **2. Screens for Underlying Macrovascular Lesions (AVM, Saccular/Mycotic Aneurysm, Dural AV Fistula, Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis [CVST], or Moyamoya):** Mandatory in all patients **<65 years**, **lobar ICH without CAA**, **isolated IVH**, or **absence of hypertension**.
> - **Repeat Stability Non-Contrast Head CT at 6 to 24 Hours (or Immediately if Neurologic Decline):** Mandatory to evaluate for **hematoma expansion** (which occurs in ~30% of patients within the first 3–6 hours) or developing **hydrocephalus**.
> - **Brain MRI with Gradient-Echo (GRE) or Susceptibility-Weighted Imaging (SWI):**
>   - Identifies **chronic hemosiderin microbleeds** (black dots): **strictly lobar/cortical microbleeds + cortical superficial siderosis** confirms **Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy (CAA)** (Boston Criteria 2.0), whereas **deep basal ganglia/brainstem microbleeds** confirm **hypertensive angiopathy**.
>   - Repeat contrast MRI at **4–8 weeks** after hematoma resorption in unexplained lobar ICH to uncover an occult **cavernous malformation or hemorrhagic brain tumor** that was compressed/masked by the acute clot!

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Cerebellar ICH >3 cm**, or any cerebellar ICH with **4th ventricular compression / obstructive hydrocephalus**.
> - **Anticoagulant-Associated ICH (Warfarin, Apixaban, Rivaroxaban, Dabigatran, Heparin, or Post-tPA):** Hematoma expansion continues for up to 24 hours with >50% mortality unless **reversed within <30–60 minutes** of arrival!
> - **Intraventricular Extension (IVH) with Acute Obstructive Hydrocephalus** (stupor, downward gaze palsy).
> - **CTA "Spot Sign"** or **Hematoma Volume ≥30 mL**.
> - **Cushing's Triad (Widened pulse pressure hypertension, bradycardia, irregular Cheyne-Stokes/apneic respirations) + Unilateral Dilated Non-Reactive Pupil (CN III compression):** Signals impending **Uncal Transtentorial Herniation**.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss (Secondary Causes of ICH Requiring Specific Therapy)**
>
> - **Arteriovenous Malformation (AVM) or Cavernous Malformation:** #1 cause of spontaneous ICH in **children and young adults (<40 years)**; AVM shows a "bag of worms" nidus with early draining veins on CTA/DSA.
> - **Ruptured Saccular Berry Aneurysm (e.g., MCA Bifurcation or Anterior Communicating Artery):** Can jet directly into the adjacent temporal/frontal lobe parenchyma, mimicking a primary ICH alongside Subarachnoid Hemorrhage.
> - **Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis (CVST):** Headache, papilledema, seizures, and **parasagittal/temporal hemorrhagic venous infarcts** that do not respect arterial territories (classic in pregnancy/postpartum, OCPs, prothrombotic states); **uniquely treated WITH therapeutic Anticoagulation (Heparin) EVEN in the presence of intracerebral hemorrhage!**
> - **Hemorrhagic Transformation of an Ischemic Stroke or Hemorrhagic Brain Metastasis / Glioblastoma:** Metastases (Melanoma, Renal Cell, Choriocarcinoma, Lung, Thyroid) show **disproportionate massive vasogenic edema**, multiple lesions, or ring enhancement.
> - **Infective Endocarditis (Ruptured Mycotic Aneurysm):** Fever, murmur, bacteremia + distal MCA cortical ICH.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals (2022 AHA/ASA Guidelines & INTERACT3 Care Bundle)**
>
> - **Primary Target ("Time is Brain" in ICH Too!):** **Halt hematoma expansion** within the first **30–60 minutes** via **1. Immediate Anticoagulation Reversal** AND **2. Rapid, Smooth Blood Pressure Lowering to SBP 130–140 mmHg** (range 130–150 mmHg), while maintaining **Cerebral Perfusion Pressure ($\text{CPP} = \text{MAP} - \text{ICP}$) ≥60–70 mmHg**.
> - **Secondary Target:** Prevent cerebral herniation, evacuate life-threatening cerebellar/lobar clots, and prevent secondary brain injury (maintain **Euglycemia [BG 110–180 mg/dL]**, **Normothermia [$\text{T} <37.5^\circ\text{C}$]**, and **Sodium 140–150 mEq/L**).
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (First 0–60 Minutes — The "Golden Hour" of ICH)**
>
> - **1. Airway & ICP Stabilization:** **Intubate for airway protection** if **GCS ≤8**, loss of gag/cough reflexes, or severe respiratory distress (use **Etomidate or Propofol + Rocuronium** to blunt the sympathetic ICP surge during laryngoscopy); **elevate Head of Bed to 30°** with the neck in midline neutral alignment to maximize jugular venous drainage.
> - **2. Acute Blood Pressure Lowering (Start within 15–30 min, achieve target within 1 Hour):**
>   - **If Presenting SBP is 150 to 220 mmHg:** **Immediately and smoothly lower SBP to a target of 140 mmHg (acceptable range 130–150 mmHg)** using a continuous titratable IV infusion—**Nicardipine (5–15 mg/hr IV)**, **Clevidipine (1–2 mg/hr IV)**, or **Labetalol (10–20 mg IV bolus / infusion)**—to minimize variability and halt hematoma growth.
>   - **Safety Floor (Crucial Guideline Update):** **NEVER lower SBP below <130 mmHg**, and **avoid dropping SBP by >60 mmHg within 1 hour** (in patients presenting with extreme hypertension >220 mmHg), as over-aggressive drops precipitate **acute renal failure and perihematomal cerebral ischemia** (**ATACH-2 trial**).
> - **3. STAT Reversal of Anticoagulation & Coagulopathy (Administer Immediately After NCCT — Do NOT Wait for INR/Lab Results if History confirms Anticoagulant Use!):**
>   - **Warfarin (Vitamin K Antagonist with INR ≥1.4):** **Administer 4-Factor Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (4F-PCC [Kcentra]: 25–50 units/kg IV over 10–15 min) PLUS Vitamin K (10 mg slow IV infusion)** (**4F-PCC is mandatory first-line over FFP** because it normalizes the INR within **15 minutes** without causing the massive 1–2 L volume overload of FFP!).
>   - **Direct Factor Xa Inhibitors (Apixaban, Rivaroxaban, Edoxaban taken within 18–24h):** **Administer Andexanet alfa (Andexxa)** (or **4-Factor PCC 50 units/kg IV** if Andexanet is unavailable).
>   - **Direct Thrombin Inhibitor (Dabigatran):** **Administer Idarucizumab (Praxbind: 5 g IV [two 2.5-g vials])** (orPCC + urgent Hemodialysis if unavailable).
>   - **Unfractionated Heparin or LMWH (Enoxaparin):** **Administer IV Protamine Sulfate** (1 mg per 100 units of UFH given in past 2–3 hours; max 50 mg).
>   - **Thrombolytic-Related ICH (Alteplase / Tenecteplase within 24–36h):** **Administer Cryoprecipitate (10 units IV)** + **Tranexamic Acid (1 g IV)**.
>   - **Severe Thrombocytopenia (Platelets <50,000/µL):** **Transfuse 1 unit of Platelets** (_Crucial Exception:_ If platelets are >100,000/µL and the patient is simply taking **Aspirin or Clopidogrel**, **DO NOT transfuse platelets** unless going to the OR!).
>
> **Neurocritical Care & Neurosurgical Intervention**
>
> - **Treat Elevated ICP / Impending Herniation:**
>   - **Administer Hyperosmolar Therapy:** **Hypertonic Saline (3% NaCl bolus 250 mL or 23.4% NaCl 30 mL via central line)** (preferred if hypotensive/hypovolemic) OR **IV Mannitol (0.5–1.0 g/kg bolus)** (monitor serum osmolality and osmolal gap <20).
>   - **Place an External Ventricular Drain (EVD) (Ventriculostomy):** Indicated in any patient with **GCS ≤8**, clinical transtentorial herniation, OR **Intraventricular Hemorrhage (IVH) causing Acute Obstructive Hydrocephalus** (allows both continuous CSF drainage and real-time ICP monitoring to maintain **CPP 60–70 mmHg**).
> - **Surgical Hematoma Evacuation Indications (Infratentorial vs. Supratentorial):**
>   - **Cerebellar ICH (Class I Life-Saving Indication):** **Perform Emergent Surgical Suboccipital Decompressive Craniectomy and Hematoma Evacuation** for ANY cerebellar hemorrhage **>3 cm in diameter**, **volume ≥15 mL**, OR causing **brainstem compression or hydrocephalus**.
>   - **Supratentorial Lobar ICH (10–30 mm from Cortical Surface):** Routine open craniotomy for deep basal ganglia/thalamic ICH does _not_ improve functional outcomes (**STICH I/II trials**); HOWEVER, **Minimally Invasive Surgery (MIS) with Stereotactic/Endoscopic Clot Evacuation (within 24 hours)** significantly improves functional outcomes in **Superficial Lobar ICH (30–80 mL)** (**2024 ENRICH trial**!), and **Decompressive Craniectomy ± Evacuation** is life-saving for deteriorating patients with large supratentorial ICH and refractory midline shift.
>
> **Seizure Management, VTE Prophylaxis, & Long-Term Secondary Prevention**
>
> - **Seizures:** **Do NOT prescribe prophylactic Antiepileptic Drugs (AEDs)** (especially Phenytoin, which worsens cognitive outcomes!). Treat **clinical seizures** or **electrographic seizures on continuous EEG** (order cEEG in any patient with depressed mental status out of proportion to CT injury) with **IV Levetiracetam**.
> - **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE) Prophylaxis:** Apply **Intermittent Pneumatic Compression (IPC) sleeves immediately on Admission Hour 1**; once a repeat Head CT at **24 to 48 hours** confirms **hematoma stability (no expansion)**, initiate **pharmacologic low-dose subcutaneous LMWH or Unfractionated Heparin**!
> - **Long-Term Prevention of Recurrent ICH:**
>   - **Strict Long-Term Blood Pressure Control (<130/80 mmHg):** Single most effective intervention to prevent recurrent deep and lobar ICH.
>   - **Resuming Antiplatelets / Anticoagulants:**
>     - _Antiplatelets:_ Safe to resume for strong vascular indications (CAD, prior ischemic stroke) (**RESTART trial** showed no increase in recurrent ICH!).
>     - _Anticoagulation (Atrial Fibrillation):_ If the patient had a **Deep Hypertensive ICH**, resuming a **DOAC** after **4–8 weeks** is generally reasonable if BP is strictly controlled; if the patient has **Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy (Lobar ICH)**, recurrent lobar bleeding risk on anticoagulants is very high (~10%/year)—strongly consider **Left Atrial Appendage Occlusion (Watchman device)** instead of lifelong anticoagulation!

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Platelet Transfusions in patients taking Aspirin or Clopidogrel who are NOT undergoing Open Neurosurgery** (**PATCH trial** proved platelet transfusions increase thromboembolism and mortality in non-operative antiplatelet-ICH).
> - **Avoid Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP) as First-Line Warfarin Reversal** when **4-Factor PCC** is available, as FFP requires ABO thawing, takes 6–12 hours to normalize the INR (allowing fatal hematoma expansion), and causes **Transfusion-Associated Circulatory Overload (TACO)**.
> - **Avoid Corticosteroids (Dexamethasone) for Perihematomal Edema in ICH**—unlike vasogenic tumor edema, ICH edema does NOT benefit from steroids, and randomized trials prove steroids increase infectious mortality and hyperglycemia without reducing ICP!
> - **Avoid Placing an External Ventricular Drain (EVD) Alone for Hydrocephalus from a Large Cerebellar Hemorrhage (>3 cm) WITHOUT Performing Surgical Posterior Fossa Decompression**, as draining CSF from above relieves counter-pressure and causes **fatal Upward Transtentorial Herniation of the cerebellum through the tentorial incisura**!
> - **Avoid Over-Aggressive Blood Pressure Reduction to SBP <130 mmHg** in acute ICH to prevent cerebral hypoperfusion and acute kidney injury.
> - **Avoid Early Do-Not-Resuscitate (DNR) Orders or Withdrawal of Life-Sustaining Therapy within the First 24–48 Hours** based solely on initial GCS or ICH Score, as this creates a "self-fulfilling prophecy" of mortality in patients who may otherwise recover significantly.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Early Hematoma Expansion (~30–40%):** Occurs predominantly within the **first 3 to 6 hours** of symptom onset $\rightarrow$ single strongest modifiable determinant of death and permanent disability $\rightarrow$ prevented by immediate BP control (130–140 mmHg) and <60-minute anticoagulant reversal.
> - **Intraventricular Hemorrhage (IVH) & Acute Obstructive (Non-Communicating) Hydrocephalus:** Blood ruptures from the thalamus or caudate/putamen into the lateral/3rd/4th ventricles, where blood clots obstruct the **Foramen of Monro or Cerebral Aqueduct of Sylvius** $\rightarrow$ requires urgent **External Ventricular Drain (EVD)** placement (± intraventricular thrombolysis via the EVD to keep the catheter patent per **CLEAR III trial**).
> - **Perihematomal Edema & Brain Herniation (Uncal, Central Transtentorial, or Tonsillar):** Thrombin and hemoglobin breakdown products (iron) trigger intense secondary inflammatory edema peaking at **Days 3 to 7**.
> - **Neurogenic Stunned Myocardium / Takotsubo Cardiomyopathy & Neurogenic Pulmonary Edema:** Catecholamine surge from acute ICP spike causes deep symmetric **"cerebral T-wave inversions"**, QT prolongation, troponin elevation, and apical ballooning.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> **Scenario 1 (Anticoagulant-Associated Deep Hypertensive ICH):** A 71-year-old man with hypertension and atrial fibrillation on **warfarin** develops sudden left-sided hemiplegia, slurred speech, severe headache, and vomiting 45 minutes ago. On arrival, BP is 206/112 mmHg, HR is 64 bpm, and he has dense left face/arm/leg paralysis with **rightward gaze deviation ("looking at the lesion")**. **Non-Contrast Head CT** reveals a **25-mL right putaminal (basal ganglia) hemorrhage** without intraventricular extension, and bedside point-of-care **INR is 3.1**. Within 20 minutes of arrival, he is started on an **IV Nicardipine infusion** titrated smoothly to a target **SBP of 140 mmHg** and receives **4-Factor Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (4F-PCC) + 10 mg IV Vitamin K**, normalizing his INR to 1.2 within 15 minutes and preventing hematoma expansion on his 6-hour repeat Head CT.
> **Scenario 2 (Cerebellar Hemorrhage Emergency):** A 66-year-old woman with poorly controlled hypertension presents with sudden occipital headache, intractable vomiting, vertigo, and complete **inability to stand or walk**, though limb motor strength is 5/5 bilaterally. Over the next hour in the ED, she becomes progressively stuporous (GCS drops from 14 to 9). **Non-Contrast Head CT** demonstrates a **3.8-cm right cerebellar hemisphere hemorrhage** compressing the 4th ventricle with early **obstructive hydrocephalus**. Her BP is lowered to 140 mmHg with IV clevidipine, she is intubated, given a bolus of **3% Hypertonic Saline**, and taken **immediately to the Operating Room for an emergent Suboccipital Decompressive Craniectomy and Hematoma Evacuation + EVD**, achieving a full neurological recovery.
