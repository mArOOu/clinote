> [!abstract] Neuromuscular Junction Disorders
> Spectrum of acquired autoimmune, toxic, or paraneoplastic disorders impairing synaptic transmission between motor nerve terminals and skeletal muscle endplates, categorized by **Post-Synaptic failure (Myasthenia Gravis)** vs. **Pre-Synaptic failure (Lambert-Eaton Myasthenic Syndrome, Botulism)**.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Myasthenia Gravis (Post-Synaptic):** Autoantibodies against **AChR (~85%)**, **MuSK (~5–8%)**, or **LRP4** trigger complement-mediated destruction of post-synaptic junctional folds, reducing the number of functional acetylcholine receptors.
> - **Lambert-Eaton Myasthenic Syndrome (Pre-Synaptic):** Autoantibodies against **P/Q-type Voltage-Gated Calcium Channels (VGCC)** block pre-synaptic $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ influx, preventing exocytic release of Acetylcholine (ACh); **~50–60% associated with Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC)**.
> - **Botulism (Pre-Synaptic):** _Clostridium botulinum_ neurotoxin cleaves **SNARE proteins** (synaptobrevin, SNAP-25), irreversibly preventing vesicular ACh exocytosis.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Repetitive Nerve Stimulation (RNS) Electrodiagnostic Triple Rule:**
>   - _Myasthenia Gravis:_ **Decremental response (>10%)** at low-frequency (2–3 Hz) stimulation; no change with exercise.
>   - _Lambert-Eaton (LEMS):_ Low baseline CMAP amplitude $\rightarrow$ marked **Incremental facilitation (>60–100%)** after 10 seconds of maximal exercise or high-frequency (20–50 Hz) stimulation.
>   - _Botulism:_ Low baseline CMAP $\rightarrow$ mild-to-moderate **Incremental response (30–100%)** at high frequency, accompanied by **fixed, unreactive pupils**!
> - **Reflexes & Autonomics Differentiate MG vs. LEMS vs. Botulism:**
>   - _MG:_ DTRs **normal**; autonomics normal; pupils **normal**.
>   - _LEMS:_ DTRs **depressed/absent** but **temporarily return after 10 seconds of isometric exercise (Lambert sign)**; prominent autonomic dysfunction (**dry mouth #1**, erectile dysfunction); pupils normal/sluggish.
>   - _Botulism:_ DTRs **absent**; severe autonomic failure (dry mouth, ileus, urinary retention); **pupils FIXED and DILATED**!
> - **Amifampridine (3,4-DAP) is First-Line for LEMS:** Blocks pre-synaptic voltage-gated potassium channels, prolonging action potential depolarization to enhance $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ entry and ACh release.
> - **Screen for SCLC in LEMS:** Any patient diagnosed with LEMS requires **CT Chest/Abdomen/Pelvis or Whole-Body FDG-PET/CT** immediately, repeated every 6 months for at least 2 years.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Myasthenia Gravis:** Fluctuating, fatigable muscle weakness worsening with sustained activity and toward the end of the day; **ocular symptoms first (ptosis, binocular diplopia in >80%)**, progressing to bulbar (dysarthria, dysphagia) and proximal limb weakness.
> - **Lambert-Eaton (LEMS):** Progressive proximal lower extremity weakness (difficulty climbing stairs, rising from chairs) that **temporarily improves after brief exercise**; systemic autonomic symptoms (**dry mouth**, constipation, erectile dysfunction); heavy smoking history.
> - **Botulism:** **Acute symmetric descending flaccid paralysis** presenting with cranial nerve palsies (**"4 D's": Diplopia, Dysarthria, Dysphagia, Dyspnea**) and blurred vision, progressing rapidly to flaccid quadriplegia; history of home-canned food, black-tar heroin wound, or infant ingesting raw honey.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Ocular Exam:** Fatigable ptosis on sustained upgaze for 60 seconds (MG); **fixed dilated unreactive pupils** (Botulism); spared pupils (MG and LEMS).
> - **Deep Tendon Reflexes (DTRs):** Completely preserved (MG); absent at rest, with brisk temporary return after 10 seconds of maximum voluntary muscle contraction = **Lambert sign** (LEMS); absent/hypoactive (Botulism).
> - **Motor Strength:** Asymmetric proximal weakness; neck flexion weaker than extension (MG); symmetric pelvic girdle weakness (LEMS).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Myasthenia Gravis (MG):** Documented fatigable skeletal muscle weakness **PLUS** serologic confirmation (**Anti-AChR**, **Anti-MuSK**, or **Anti-LRP4 IgG**) OR **$\ge 10\%$ decremental response** on low-frequency (2–3 Hz) Repetitive Nerve Stimulation (RNS) / increased jitter on Single-Fiber EMG.
> - **Lambert-Eaton Myasthenic Syndrome (LEMS):** Proximal limb weakness with hyporeflexia/autonomic dysfunction **PLUS** positive **P/Q-type Voltage-Gated Calcium Channel (VGCC) Antibodies** OR **$\ge 60–100\%$ incremental CMAP facilitation** on high-frequency (20–50 Hz) RNS or immediately post-10-second exercise.
> - **Botulism:** Acute descending flaccid paralysis with pupillary/autonomic involvement **PLUS** laboratory isolation of **botulinum neurotoxin** in serum, stool, gastric aspirate, or suspected food item via mouse bioassay or PCR/ELISA.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Initial / Bedside**
>
> - **Bedside Pulmonary Mechanics:** Serial **Forced Vital Capacity (FVC)** and **Negative Inspiratory Force (NIF)** q4–6 hours (mandatory in any NMJ presentation to detect impending neuromuscular respiratory failure).
> - **Ice Pack Test:** Application of an ice pack over a ptotic eyelid for 2 minutes; **improvement of ptosis by $\ge 2$ mm** is $>90\%$ sensitive/specific for ocular MG (cold temperature inhibits acetylcholinesterase).
>
> **Confirmatory Serology & Electrodiagnostics**
>
> - **Serology Panel:** Anti-AChR binding/blocking/modulating antibodies; if negative, send Anti-MuSK and Anti-LRP4. Send **Anti-P/Q-type VGCC antibodies** for suspected LEMS (>95% sensitive in SCLC-LEMS).
> - **Repetitive Nerve Stimulation (RNS) & Single-Fiber EMG (SFEMG):** Perform low-frequency (2–3 Hz) and high-frequency (20–50 Hz) testing; SFEMG showing increased **jitter and impulse blocking** is the most sensitive diagnostic test for all NMJ transmission defects (>95–99% sensitive).
>
> **Oncologic & Mediastinal Screening**
>
> - **Chest CT without IV contrast:** Mandatory in all MG patients to screen for **Thymoma** (present in 10–15%; thymic hyperplasia in 65%).
> - **Chest CT with IV contrast OR Whole-Body FDG-PET/CT:** Mandatory in all LEMS patients to detect occult **Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC)**.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **FVC <20 mL/kg, NIF < -30 $\text{cmH}_2\text{O}$**, or Single Breath Count <15 (impending neuromuscular respiratory arrest).
> - **Paradoxical inward abdominal breathing** during inspiration (diaphragmatic paralysis).
> - Rapidly progressive descending paralysis with **fixed dilated pupils** (Botulism).
> - Severe bulbar dysfunction with inability to swallow secretions (aspiration risk).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS):** Ascending flaccid paralysis with areflexia, sensory paresthesias, and CSF albuminocytological dissociation; spares extraocular muscles (except Miller Fisher).
> - **Cholinergic Crisis:** Excessive acetylcholinesterase inhibitor therapy resulting in depolarization block $\rightarrow$ flaccid weakness PLUS **SLUDGE/DUMBELS** toxidrome (salivation, lacrimation, urination, defecation, GI cramps, emesis, miosis, bradycardia, fasciculations).
> - **Tick Paralysis:** Rapidly ascending flaccid paralysis with normal pupils and absent reflexes; cured within hours of removing the tick (_Dermacentor_).
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Polymyositis / Dermatomyositis:** Symmetric proximal muscle weakness without ocular/bulbar involvement; elevated serum Creatine Kinase (CK) and abnormal muscle biopsy.
> - **Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS):** Combined upper motor neuron (hyperreflexia, spasticity, Babinski) and lower motor neuron (atrophy, fasciculations) findings; spares extraocular motility.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Maintain ventilatory capacity (**FVC >20 mL/kg, NIF > -30 $\text{cmH}_2\text{O}$**), achieve functional motor recovery, and prevent aspiration.
> - **Secondary Target:** Eradicate underlying oncologic triggers (**Thymoma resection in MG; Chemoradiation for SCLC in LEMS**) and transition to long-term steroid-sparing immunosuppression.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Intubate electively via Rapid Sequence Intubation (avoiding succinylcholine)** when **FVC <15–20 mL/kg**, **NIF < -30 $\text{cmH}_2\text{O}$**, or when bulbar weakness impairs airway clearance (refer to **[[Myasthenic Crisis]]**).
> - **Administer Equine Botulinum Heptavalent Antitoxin (HBAT: single IV vial)** immediately upon clinical suspicion of **Foodborne or Wound Botulism** (or **BabyBIG [Human Botulism Immune Globulin]** for infant botulism) without waiting for laboratory confirmation, to neutralize circulating free neurotoxin.
> - **Perform surgical debridement and administer IV Penicillin G or Metronidazole** for **Wound Botulism** (_AFTER antitoxin has been infused to prevent toxin-surge release_).
>
> **Targeted Chronic Therapy: Myasthenia Gravis (MG)**
>
> - **Initiate Oral Pyridostigmine (Mestinon: 30 to 60 mg PO TID–QID, titrating up to 120 mg q4h)** as first-line symptomatic therapy for generalized or ocular AChR+ MG (_Note: Pyridostigmine is often ineffective or poorly tolerated in **Anti-MuSK MG**!_).
> - **Initiate Oral Prednisone (starting 15–20 mg/day, titrating up to 60 mg/day)** combined with **Azathioprine (2–3 mg/kg/day)** or **Mycophenolate Mofetil (1,000 mg PO BID)** for patients with inadequate symptom control on pyridostigmine alone (bridge with PLEX/IVIG if starting high-dose steroids to avoid the steroid dip).
> - **Initiate Rituximab (375 mg/m² IV weekly × 4 doses or 1,000 mg IV q6 months)** as the **preferred first-line immunotherapy for Anti-MuSK MG** and refractory generalized AChR+ MG.
> - **Perform Complete Transsternal or Robotic Thymectomy** in all patients with **Thymoma** and in all patients aged **18 to 50/65 years with generalized AChR+ MG** (even without thymoma per MGTX trial) to increase long-term remission rates.
>
> **Targeted Therapy: Lambert-Eaton Myasthenic Syndrome (LEMS)**
>
> - **Initiate Amifampridine (3,4-Diaminopyridine / 3,4-DAP: 15 to 60 mg/day PO divided TID–QID, max 80 mg/day)** as the **first-line symptomatic treatment of choice** to increase pre-synaptic ACh release (screen for seizure history; monitor for paresthesias).
> - **Add Oral Pyridostigmine (30–60 mg PO TID)** as an adjunct to enhance acetylcholine longevity at the synapse.
> - **Treat the Underlying Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC) with Platinum-based Chemoradiation** immediately, as successful cancer eradication provides the greatest likelihood of sustained LEMS remission.
> - **Administer IVIG (2 g/kg over 2–5 days) or Plasma Exchange (PLEX)** for severe, debilitating LEMS weakness or autoimmune non-tumor LEMS refractory to amifampridine.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Aminoglycosides (Gentamicin, Tobramycin), Fluoroquinolones (Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin), and Macrolides (Azithromycin)** in ALL patients with NMJ disorders, as they block pre-synaptic $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ entry and post-synaptic receptors, directly triggering **fatal respiratory arrest**.
> - **Avoid IV Magnesium, Beta-Blockers, Calcium Channel Blockers, and Procainamide** in NMJ disorders to prevent acute uncoupling of synaptic transmission.
> - **Avoid Succinylcholine in Myasthenia Gravis (where patients are resistant and require massive doses with erratic recovery) AND avoid in LEMS/Botulism (where patients are hypersensitive)**; if paralytics are mandatory for intubation, use **reduced-dose Rocuronium (1/3 to 1/2 normal dose)** with **Sugammadex** immediately available.
> - **Avoid Delaying Botulism Antitoxin while waiting for laboratory confirmation**, as antitoxin neutralizes _only_ circulating unbound neurotoxin and cannot reverse established intracellular paralysis.
> - **Avoid Continuing Pyridostigmine in Mechanically Ventilated / Crisis Patients** to prevent copious airway bronchorrhea and diagnostic confusion with cholinergic crisis.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Myasthenic Crisis / Ventilatory Arrest:** Acute respiratory muscle failure $\rightarrow$ FVC <20 mL/kg, NIF < -30 $\rightarrow$ emergent intubation + PLEX/IVIG.
> - **Cholinergic Crisis:** Anticholinesterase overdose $\rightarrow$ muscle fasciculations, flaccid weakness, miosis, bradycardia, profuse secretions $\rightarrow$ stop pyridostigmine, administer **IV Atropine (0.5–1.0 mg)**.
> - **Seizures Induced by Amifampridine (3,4-DAP):** High doses (>80–100 mg/day) cross the blood-brain barrier to block central $\text{K}^+$ channels $\rightarrow$ dose-dependent epileptic seizures.
> - **Aspiration Pneumonia:** Severe bulbar failure with absent gag reflex $\rightarrow$ chemical and bacterial pneumonitis.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 61-year-old man with a 40-pack-year smoking history presents with a 4-month history of progressive difficulty rising from a chair and a persistent dry mouth. Neurological examination reveals bilateral 3/5 proximal hip flexor weakness and completely **absent patellar and Achilles deep tendon reflexes**; however, after performing **10 seconds of maximum isometric quadriceps contraction, his patellar reflexes become briskly 2+ (positive Lambert sign)** and his motor strength temporarily improves to 4+/5. Repetitive nerve stimulation shows a low baseline CMAP that increases by **180% following exercise (incremental facilitation)**, and serum **anti-P/Q-type VGCC antibodies** are strongly positive (**Lambert-Eaton Myasthenic Syndrome**). He is initiated on **Amifampridine (3,4-DAP: 15 mg PO TID)** with dramatic restoration of strength, and a contrast-enhanced Chest CT reveals a **2.5-cm hilar mass with mediastinal adenopathy**, confirming biopsy-proven **Small Cell Lung Cancer**.
