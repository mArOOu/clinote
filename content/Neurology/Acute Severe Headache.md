> [!abstract] Acute Severe Headache
> Abrupt, high-intensity headache ("worst headache of life" or peak intensity within **<1 minute = Thunderclap Headache**) requiring rapid differentiation between benign primary headaches (migraine, cluster) and catastrophic secondary intracranial or vascular emergencies.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Meningeal & Vascular Nociception:** Pain arises from mechanical stretch, chemical irritation, or inflammation of pain-sensitive structures: **dura mater, large intracranial arteries, venous sinuses, and cranial nerves (V, IX, X)**; brain parenchyma itself is insensate.
> - **Intracranial Pressure (ICP) Alterations:** Raised ICP (mass effect, hydrocephalus, venous thrombosis) strains the dural attachment fibers; low ICP (spontaneous CSF leak) causes brain sag and traction on bridging veins and sensory nerves.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **The "6-Hour Rule" for SAH:** A non-contrast head CT (NCCT) performed on a modern third-generation scanner **within 6 hours of headache onset** has a **>99% sensitivity (negative predictive value ~99.9%)** for subarachnoid hemorrhage when interpreted by a qualified radiologist; if negative within 6 hours, lumbar puncture can be safely omitted in low-risk patients.
> - **LP Timing for Xanthochromia:** If CT is obtained **>6 hours** from onset or is equivocal, lumbar puncture is mandatory; wait **≥2 to 12 hours from headache onset** before tapping to allow enzymatic conversion of RBCs into **bilirubin (xanthochromia on spectrophotometry)**, which differentiates true SAH from a traumatic tap.
> - **Pupil-Involving CN III Palsy = PCom Aneurysm:** Ptosis and "down-and-out" eye deviation with a **dilated, unreactive pupil** is an expanding **Posterior Communicating Artery (PCom) aneurysm** compressing parasympathetic pupillary fibers until proven otherwise $\rightarrow$ emergent CTA!
> - **GCA: Treat BEFORE Biopsy:** If Giant Cell (Temporal) Arteritis is suspected (age >50 + elevated ESR/CRP + new headache/jaw claudication), start **high-dose systemic corticosteroids immediately** to prevent irreversible anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (AION) and permanent blindness; biopsy remains diagnostic for up to 2–4 weeks.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History (Screen with SNOOP10 Red Flags)**
>
> - **Onset & Tempo:** Sudden "thunderclap" onset (peaking in <1 minute) strongly suggests **Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH)**, **Reversible Cerebral Vasoconstriction Syndrome (RCVS)**, or cervical artery dissection.
> - **Systemic symptoms:** Fever, chills, weight loss (meningitis, brain abscess, temporal arteritis).
> - **Neurologic deficits:** Altered mental status, seizure, focal weakness, numbness, or visual field deficits.
> - **Associated Features:**
>   - _Jaw claudication, scalp tenderness, vision changes in patient >50 years:_ **Giant Cell Arteritis**.
>   - _Neck/head trauma, neck pain, Horner syndrome (ptosis + miosis):_ **Carotid or Vertebral Artery Dissection**.
>   - _Pregnancy / Postpartum / OCPs + progressive headache + seizure:_ **Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis (CVST)**.
>   - _Unilateral severe eye pain, blurred vision, colored halos, nausea:_ **Acute Angle-Closure Glaucoma**.
>   - _Positional:_ Worsened by upright posture / relieved supine (Intracranial Hypotension); worsened by lying flat / coughing / Valsalva (Raised ICP / Mass).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Vital signs:** Fever (infection), severe malignant hypertension (hypertensive encephalopathy, PRES).
> - **Fundoscopy:** **Papilledema** (raised ICP, mass, CVST, IIH); loss of spontaneous venous pulsations.
> - **Meningeal signs:** Nuchal rigidity, **Kernig's sign**, **Brudzinski's sign** (SAH, meningitis).
> - **Neurological exam:** Cranial nerve palsies (CN III palsy in PCom aneurysm; CN VI palsy as a false-localizing sign of raised ICP), focal motor/sensory deficits, pronator drift.
> - **Targeted regional exam:** Tender, pulseless, nodular temporal arteries (GCA); red eye with a mid-dilated fixed pupil and "rock-hard" globe on palpation (Glaucoma).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Ottawa Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH) Rule (Applies to alert patients ≥15 y with new severe non-traumatic headache peaking within 1 hour):**
>   - Investigate with **immediate neuroimaging** if **ANY** of the following 6 criteria are present (100% sensitivity for SAH):
>     1. Age **≥40 years**
>     2. **Neck pain or stiffness**
>     3. Witnessed **loss of consciousness**
>     4. Onset during **exertion**
>     5. **Thunderclap** headache (instant peak pain)
>     6. Limited **neck flexion** on examination
> - **Giant Cell Arteritis (GCA — 2022 ACR/EULAR Criteria):** Age $\ge 50$ + high ESR ($\ge 50$ mm/hr) or CRP ($\ge 10$ mg/L) + temporal artery tenderness/claudication, confirmed by **temporal artery biopsy** (transmural mononuclear infiltrate, giant cells) or **halo sign on temporal artery duplex ultrasound**.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Initial**
>
> - **Non-Contrast Head CT (NCCT):** **Mandatory first-line imaging test** (detects acute SAH, intraparenchymal hemorrhage, hydrocephalus, or large mass lesions).
> - **ESR and CRP:** STAT in all patients **≥50 years** with a new headache to evaluate for Giant Cell Arteritis.
>
> **Confirmatory / Stepwise Protocol**
>
> - **Lumbar Puncture (LP):** Mandatory if NCCT is negative/equivocal but high clinical suspicion for SAH remains (or if CT obtained >6 hours from onset).
>   - _CSF Analysis:_ Measure **Opening Pressure** (normal 10–20 $\text{cmH}_2\text{O}$); send tubes 1 and 4 for **cell count** (clearing RBCs indicates traumatic tap; persistent RBCs favor SAH); evaluate for **Xanthochromia** via spectrophotometry (confirms SAH).
>   - _Meningitis workup:_ Send for CSF Gram stain, culture, glucose (CSF:serum <0.6), protein, and viral/bacterial PCR.
> - **CT Angiography (CTA) of the Head and Neck:** Identifies ruptured intracranial saccular aneurysms, **cervical internal carotid/vertebral artery dissection** (intramural hematoma, "flame-shaped" taper), or "string of beads" vasospasm in **RCVS**.
> - **Brain MRI with MR Venography (MRV):** Gold standard for suspected **Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis (CVST)** (filling defect in transverse/superior sagittal sinus) and **Pituitary Apoplexy** (enlarged, hemorrhagic sella).

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Thunderclap onset** (maximal within <60 seconds $\rightarrow$ SAH, RCVS).
> - New focal neurological deficits, altered mental status, or seizures.
> - New-onset headache in patients **≥50 years old** (GCA, mass lesion).
> - Headache in patients with **cancer or HIV/immunosuppression** (metastasis, cryptococcal meningitis, toxoplasmosis).
> - **Papilledema** or postural/positional headache.
> - Fever + nuchal rigidity + purpuric rash (bacterial meningitis).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Aneurysmal Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH):** Ruptured saccular/berry aneurysm; thunderclap headache + meningismus.
> - **Acute Bacterial Meningitis:** Fever, nuchal rigidity, AMS, petechial rash.
> - **Cervical Artery Dissection (Carotid or Vertebral):** Neck pain + headache + unilateral Horner syndrome (carotid) or posterior circulation ischemia (vertebral).
> - **Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis (CVST):** Hypercoagulable/postpartum female with progressive headache, papilledema, seizures, and venous infarcts.
> - **Acute Angle-Closure Glaucoma:** Unilateral ocular pain, fixed mid-dilated pupil, elevated intraocular pressure (>30–40 mmHg).
> - **Pituitary Apoplexy:** Sudden hemorrhage into a pituitary macroadenoma; severe headache, bilateral vision loss (bitemporal hemianopia), ophthalmoplegia, acute adrenal crisis.
>
> **Most Likely (Primary Headaches)**
>
> - **Migraine:** Unilateral, pulsating, moderate-to-severe headache lasting 4–72 hours with photophobia, phonophobia, and nausea $\pm$ aura.
> - **Tension-Type Headache:** Bilateral, dull, band-like pressing pain without photophobia/phonophobia or nausea.
> - **Cluster Headache:** Unilateral, excruciating periorbital/temporal pain lasting 15–180 minutes with ipsilateral autonomic features (lacrimation, rhinorrhea, miosis, ptosis).
>
> **Most Surprising**
>
> - **Reversible Cerebral Vasoconstriction Syndrome (RCVS):** Recurrent thunderclap headaches over 1–2 weeks, often triggered by postpartum state, SSRIs, triptans, or sympathomimetics; CTA shows multifocal "string of beads" segmental vasoconstriction.
> - **Spontaneous Intracranial Hypotension (SIH):** Low CSF volume from a spinal dural leak; severe orthostatic headache (worse upright, completely relieved supine).

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Rule out and treat underlying vascular/infectious catastrophes, control elevated ICP, preserve vision (GCA/Glaucoma), and eliminate pain.
> - **Secondary Target:** Avoid medication-overuse headache and prevent stroke (dissection/RCVS).
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (Life-Threatening Secondary Causes)**
>
> - **Manage Aneurysmal SAH immediately** by titrating an **IV Nicardipine infusion (target SBP <140 mmHg)**, administering **Oral Nimodipine (60 mg q4h)** to prevent secondary delayed ischemic neurological deficits (vasospasm), and obtaining **STAT Neurosurgery / Neurointerventional consult for aneurysm securement (coiling or clipping within 24 hours)**.
> - **Administer IV Dexamethasone (10 mg IV) + Ceftriaxone (2 g IV) + Vancomycin (15–20 mg/kg IV) + Ampicillin (2 g IV if age >50)** immediately upon suspicion of **Bacterial Meningitis** (do NOT delay antibiotics for CT or LP!).
> - **Administer High-Dose Corticosteroids (IV Methylprednisolone 1 g/day for 3 days if vision loss; or Oral Prednisone 1 mg/kg/day [max 60 mg/day] if no vision loss)** immediately upon suspicion of **Giant Cell Arteritis** to preserve eyesight.
> - **Start Therapeutic Anticoagulation (IV Unfractionated Heparin or Subcutaneous LMWH)** immediately upon confirmation of **Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis (CVST)** (anticoagulation is indicated **EVEN IF a secondary hemorrhagic venous infarct is present**!).
> - **Administer Topical Timolol 0.5% + Apraclonidine 1% + Pilocarpine 2% + IV Acetazolamide (500 mg IV)** immediately upon diagnosing **Acute Angle-Closure Glaucoma** to reduce intraocular pressure.
>
> **Acute Targeted Therapy for Severe Primary Headaches (Status Migrainosus)**
>
> - **Administer an IV Migraine Cocktail** for acute refractory severe migraine:
>   - **IV Dopamine Antagonist:** **Metoclopramide (10 mg IV)** OR **Prochlorperazine (10 mg IV)** (give with **Diphenhydramine 25 mg IV** to prevent akathisia/dystonic reactions), **PLUS**
>   - **IV NSAID:** **Ketorolac (15–30 mg IV)**, **PLUS**
>   - **IV Hydration:** 1 L Normal Saline bolus.
> - **Add Dexamethasone (10 mg IV/PO single dose)** to the acute migraine regimen to prevent 72-hour headache recurrence.
> - **Administer 100% High-Flow Oxygen via non-rebreather mask (12–15 L/min for 15 minutes) + Subcutaneous Sumatriptan (6 mg SC)** immediately for an acute **Cluster Headache** attack.
>
> **Disposition & Follow-up**
>
> - **Admit to Neuro-ICU:** All patients with confirmed SAH, CVST, dissection with ischemia, or bacterial meningitis.
> - **Discharge Home with Outpatient Follow-up:** Patients with normal NCCT, normal LP (if indicated), resolution of primary headache symptoms, and no red-flag features.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Performing Lumbar Puncture BEFORE Neuroimaging** in patients with focal neurologic deficits, altered mental status, papilledema, seizures, or severe immunocompromise to prevent fatal **brain herniation**.
> - **Avoid Triptans (Sumatriptan) and Ergotamines** in patients with suspected **Subarachnoid Hemorrhage, Cervical Artery Dissection, RCVS, uncontrolled hypertension, or ischemic heart disease/stroke** due to potent vasoconstrictive properties.
> - **Avoid Delaying Corticosteroids in Suspected Giant Cell Arteritis** while waiting for a temporal artery biopsy to prevent permanent, irreversible **blindness**.
> - **Avoid Opioids (Morphine, Hydromorphone, Oxycodone)** for routine treatment of primary headaches (migraine/tension) to prevent **medication-overuse headache (rebound)**, hyperalgesia, and dependency.
> - **Avoid Withholding Anticoagulation in Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis (CVST) solely due to Intracranial Hemorrhage**, as the hemorrhage is venous/hypertensive in origin and will worsen unless venous outflow is restored with heparin.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Aneurysmal Rebleeding & Delayed Cerebral Ischemia (Vasospasm):** Peak vasospasm occurs at **Days 4 to 14** post-SAH $\rightarrow$ monitor with daily Transcranial Doppler (TCD), maintain euvolemia, and treat with **oral Nimodipine**.
> - **Permanent Anterior Ischemic Optic Neuropathy (AION):** Irreversible vascular occlusion of posterior ciliary arteries in untreated GCA $\rightarrow$ high-dose steroids prevent contralateral eye involvement.
> - **Medication-Overuse Headache (MOH):** Chronic daily headache from using simple analgesics $\ge 15$ days/month or triptans/opioids/combination analgesics $\ge 10$ days/month for $>3$ months $\rightarrow$ requires complete withdrawal of the offending agent.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 44-year-old woman presents to the ED with a sudden, excruciating headache that peaked in intensity in less than 30 seconds while lifting weights at the gym ("thunderclap onset"), accompanied by nausea and neck stiffness. A non-contrast head CT performed 2 hours after onset reveals hyperdense blood layering within the basal cisterns and the anterior interhemispheric fissure (**Subarachnoid Hemorrhage**). An immediate **IV Nicardipine infusion** is started targeting SBP <140 mmHg, oral **Nimodipine** is administered, and an emergent **CTA Head** demonstrates a 6-mm **Anterior Communicating Artery (ACom) saccular aneurysm**, which is successfully treated with **endovascular coiling** within 12 hours.
