> [!abstract] Tremor & Common Movement Disorders
> Group of neurological conditions characterized by abnormal involuntary movements, broadly classified into **Hyperkinetic Disorders** (Tremor, Chorea, Dystonia, Tics, Myoclonus) and **Sensorimotor Disorders (Restless Legs Syndrome)**, resulting from dysfunction of the basal ganglia, thalamus, or cerebellothalamocortical loops.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Essential Tremor:** Pacemaker oscillations in the **cerebellothalamocortical pathway** driven by Purkinje cell loss and altered $\text{GABA}_\text{A}$ receptor transmission in the dentate nucleus.
> - **Huntington Disease (Chorea):** Autosomal dominant **CAG trinucleotide repeat expansion ($\ge 40$ repeats)** on the _HTT_ gene (chromosome 4p) $\rightarrow$ toxic huntingtin protein accumulation $\rightarrow$ selective degeneration of striatal GABAergic medium spiny neurons in the **caudate nucleus and putamen** (disinhibits thalamus $\rightarrow$ excessive motor output).
> - **Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS):** Central **iron deficiency** in the substantia nigra impairs dopamine synthesis, producing circadian sensorimotor hyperarousal.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **The 3-Type Tremor Discriminator:**
>   - _Rest Tremor (4–6 Hz):_ Present when limb is fully supported against gravity; suppresses during voluntary movement (**Parkinson's Disease**).
>   - _Action / Postural Tremor (8–12 Hz):_ Present when holding arms outstretched against gravity; worsens with motor action (**Essential Tremor, Enhanced Physiologic**).
>   - _Intention / Kinetic Tremor (3–4 Hz):_ Tremor amplitude exponentially increases as the limb approaches a visual target (**Cerebellar Outflow Disease: MS, stroke, ataxia**).
> - **Alcohol Responsiveness in Essential Tremor:** Dramatic, temporary cessation of tremor after a single glass of wine is pathognomonic for **Essential Tremor** (>50–70% report relief due to central GABAergic cerebellar inhibition).
> - **Rule Out Wilson Disease Under Age 50:** Any patient aged **<40–50 years** presenting with a new movement disorder (tremor ["wing-beating"], dystonia, chorea, or parkinsonism) requires **serum Ceruloplasmin and a Slit-Lamp Exam for Kayser-Fleischer rings**!
> - **RLS: Check Ferritin First!** If **serum Ferritin <75 ng/mL**, oral or IV iron repletion can cure RLS without starting dopaminergic drugs.
> - **Anticholinergic Paradox in Movement Disorders:** Anticholinergics (**Benztropine, Diphenhydramine**) _cure_ acute drug-induced dystonia, but **SEVERELY WORSEN Tardive Dyskinesia**!

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Essential Tremor (ET):** Bilateral, symmetric action tremor of the hands/arms during active tasks (drinking from a cup, using a spoon, writing); head tremor ("yes-yes" vertical or "no-no" horizontal titubation); positive family history (autosomal dominant in ~50%); aggravated by stress, caffeine, fatigue.
> - **Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS — URGE Mnemonic):**
>   - **U**rge to move legs, usually accompanied by uncomfortable creeping/crawling dysesthesias deep in calves.
>   - **R**est / inactivity induces symptoms.
>   - **G**etting active (walking, stretching) provides immediate relief.
>   - **E**vening or nocturnal worsening (triggers severe insomnia; associated with **Periodic Limb Movements of Sleep [PLMS]**).
> - **Huntington Disease:** Insidious onset in 30s–40s of triad: **Chorea** (involuntary, rapid, jerky, non-stereotyped dance-like movements) + **Psychiatric changes** (depression, irritability, psychosis, high suicide risk) + **Dementia/Executive decline**.
> - **Dystonia (Focal):** Sustained muscle contractions causing abnormal twisting postures (e.g., **Cervical Dystonia / Torticollis**, **Blepharospasm** [forced eyelid closure], **Writer's Cramp**); relieved by a **sensory trick ("geste antagoniste"**—light touch to the chin or cheek temporarily aborts the spasm).
> - **Tardive Dyskinesia:** Involuntary, stereotypic, repetitive choreoathetoid movements of the face and mouth (**lip-smacking, tongue protrusion / "fly-catcher tongue", chewing, grimacing**) occurring after **$\ge 3$ months of dopamine-blocking antipsychotics/antiemetics**.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Tremor Testing:**
>   - _Postural:_ Tremor emerges holding arms outstretched.
>   - _Kinetic:_ Tremor on finger-to-nose testing, pouring water between cups, or drawing an **Archimedes spiral**.
>   - _Rest:_ Arms resting relaxed in lap (suppresses when moving).
> - **Motor Chorea Testing:** "Milkmaid's grip" (inability to maintain sustained hand grip), "serpentine tongue" (inability to keep tongue protruded for 10 seconds), and dance-like gait.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Essential Tremor (MDS Consensus Criteria):**
>   - Isolated bilateral upper limb action tremor (postural or kinetic),
>   - **At least 3 years' duration**,
>   - With or without tremor in other locations (head, voice, lower limbs), AND
>   - **Absence of other neurologic signs** (no bradykinesia, rigidity, ataxia, or dystonia).
> - **Restless Legs Syndrome (IRLSSG Criteria — Requires ALL 5):**
>   1. Urge to move the legs, usually accompanied by uncomfortable sensations.
>   2. Urge begins or worsens during periods of rest or inactivity.
>   3. Urge is partially or totally relieved by movement (walking, stretching).
>   4. Urge occurs exclusively or predominantly in the evening or at night.
>   5. Symptoms cannot be solely accounted for by another medical/behavioral condition (e.g., leg cramps, venous stasis, positional discomfort).
> - **Huntington Disease:** Confirmed manifest motor chorea **PLUS $\ge 36–40$ CAG repeats** on _HTT_ gene genetic testing.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Initial Screening Panel**
>
> - **Essential Tremor:** Primary clinical diagnosis. Check **TSH, Free T4** (rule out hyperthyroidism), electrolytes, and review medications (screen for **Enhanced Physiologic Tremor triggers**: caffeine, albuterol, SSRIs, lithium, valproate, amiodarone, theophylline).
> - **Restless Legs Syndrome:** **Serum Ferritin and Total Iron Binding Capacity (TIBC):** Mandatory in all patients (**serum Ferritin $<75\text{ ng/mL}$ indicates central brain iron deficiency requiring repletion**).
> - **Suspected Wilson Disease (Age <50 with tremor, dystonia, or psychiatric signs):** **Serum Ceruloplasmin** (low), **24-hour urinary copper excretion** (elevated), and **slit-lamp exam** (Kayser-Fleischer rings).
>
> **Imaging & Advanced Diagnostics**
>
> - **DaTscan (I-123 Ioflupane SPECT):** Normal striatal uptake in Essential Tremor; **reduced uptake in Parkinson's Disease** (useful when distinguishing severe ET from tremor-dominant PD).
> - **Brain MRI with/without contrast:** Indicated in intention/cerebellar tremor (screens for MS plaques, cerebellar stroke, or tumor) and Huntington's disease (demonstrates **bilateral atrophy of the caudate nuclei** with enlargement of the frontal horns of the lateral ventricles).

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Sudden or rapidly progressive onset of tremor or chorea** (suggests acute stroke, drug toxicity, or autoimmune encephalitis).
> - Tremor accompanied by **gait ataxia, dysmetria, and nystagmus** (cerebellar mass, stroke, or multiple sclerosis).
> - Tremor or chorea accompanied by cognitive decline or psychiatric symptoms in a patient **$<50$ years old** (Wilson disease, Huntington disease).
> - **Laryngeal dystonia** or severe oculogyric crisis following antiemetic/antipsychotic administration (acute dystonic reaction).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Secondary Causes**
>
> - **Wilson Disease:** Autosomal recessive copper overload; young patient, "wing-beating" coarse postural tremor, dystonia, psychiatric decline, liver disease.
> - **Acute Drug-Induced Dystonic Reaction:** Hours to days after starting dopamine receptor blockers (**Haloperidol, Metoclopramide, Prochlorperazine**); torticollis, oculogyric crisis (upward gaze fixation), trismus.
> - **Hemiballismus:** Sudden, violent, flinging, large-amplitude movements of one limb/side caused by a **lacunar stroke in the contralateral Subthalamic Nucleus (STN)**.
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Enhanced Physiologic Tremor:** High-frequency, low-amplitude postural tremor visible when holding paper; unmasked by anxiety, hyperthyroidism, caffeine, hypoglycemia, or drugs.
> - **Parkinsonian Rest Tremor:** Asymmetric 4–6 Hz "pill-rolling" tremor present at rest with cogwheel rigidity and bradykinesia.
>
> **Less Common**
>
> - **Tourette Syndrome:** Multiple motor tics AND at least one vocal tic persisting for **$>1$ year**, with onset **before age 18**; preceded by a premonitory urge and temporarily suppressible.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Suppress disabling tremor and involuntary hyperkinetic movements to restore functional activities of daily living (eating, writing, drinking), replete central iron in RLS, and preserve independence.
> - **Secondary Target:** Avoid medication-induced complications (e.g., dopaminergic augmentation in RLS; sedation or depression with VMAT2 inhibitors).
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Administer IV or IM Diphenhydramine (25–50 mg) OR IV/IM Benztropine (1–2 mg)** immediately for an **Acute Drug-Induced Dystonic Reaction** (laryngeal spasm, oculogyric crisis, torticollis post-antipsychotic/antiemetic) to restore the striatal dopamine-acetylcholine balance within 10–20 minutes.
> - **Administer a second-generation antipsychotic or VMAT2 inhibitor** short-term for **Acute Hemiballismus** (while optimizing stroke secondary prevention) to prevent severe exhaustion and rhabdomyolysis.
>
> **Targeted Therapy: Essential Tremor (ET)**
>
> - **First-Line Pharmacotherapy (Equal Efficacy — Select Based on Comorbidities):**
>   - **Initiate Propranolol (start 40 mg PO daily, titrating to 80–240 mg/day PO divided BID–TID)** (preferred in young, hypertensive, or anxious patients; reduces tremor amplitude by ~50%).
>   - **Initiate Primidone (start 12.5–25 mg PO QHS, titrating slowly to 250 mg TID)** (preferred if asthma or bradycardia contraindicates beta-blockers; metabolized to phenobarbital; monitor for acute first-dose vertigo/ataxia).
> - **Second-Line Pharmacotherapy:** Combine **Propranolol + Primidone** (synergistic), or trial **Topiramate (100–400 mg/day)** or **Gabapentin (1,200–2,400 mg/day)**.
> - **Refractory Disabling Essential Tremor:**
>   - **Refer for Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS) of the Ventral Intermediate (VIM) Nucleus of the Thalamus** (provides >80% tremor suppression).
>   - **Perform MR-Guided Focused Ultrasound (MRgFUS) Unilateral Thalamotomy** as a non-invasive, incisionless ablative alternative.
>
> **Targeted Therapy: Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS)**
>
> - **Replete Iron First:** **Administer Oral Ferrous Sulfate (325 mg with Vitamin C every other day)** if **serum Ferritin is $<75\text{ ng/mL}$** (or administer **IV Ferric Carboxymaltose** if oral iron is poorly tolerated or ferritin $<30\text{ ng/mL}$).
> - **First-Line Pharmacotherapy (Non-Dopaminergic Alpha-2-Delta Ligands Preferred):**
>   - **Initiate Gabapentin (300 to 900 mg PO taken 2 hours before bedtime)** OR **Pregabalin (75 to 150 mg PO at bedtime)** as **first-line therapy** (avoids the risk of dopaminergic augmentation!).
> - **Second-Line / Intermittent Therapy (Dopamine Agonists):**
>   - Use low-dose **Pramipexole (0.125–0.5 mg PO)**, **Ropinirole (0.25–2 mg PO)**, or **Rotigotine transdermal patch** ONLY for intermittent/refractory symptoms (_Counsel patient that chronic daily use carries a $>50\%$ risk of **Augmentation**!_).
>
> **Targeted Therapy: Dystonia, Chorea, & Tardive Dyskinesia**
>
> - **Focal Dystonia (Cervical Torticollis, Blepharospasm):** **Administer Botulinum Toxin Injections (Botox / Dysport)** directly into overactive muscles every 10–12 weeks (**first-line gold standard**).
> - **Huntington Disease Chorea & Tardive Dyskinesia:**
>   - **Initiate a Vesicular Monoamine Transporter 2 (VMAT2) Inhibitor—Deutetrabenazine (Austedo: 6 mg PO BID, titrating to 48 mg/day) OR Valbenazine (Ingrezza: 40–80 mg PO daily)**—to deplete presynaptic dopamine release and suppress hyperkinetic movements (**first-line for both conditions**; screen for depression and suicidal ideation).
>   - _For Tardive Dyskinesia:_ Gradually taper and discontinue the offending first-generation antipsychotic or switch to a low-risk atypical (**Quetiapine or Clozapine**).

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Anticholinergics (Diphenhydramine, Benztropine, Trihexyphenidyl) in Tardive Dyskinesia**, as blocking acetylcholine worsens neuroleptic-induced dopamine receptor supersensitivity and **severely exacerbates Tardive Dyskinesia** (use **VMAT2 inhibitors** instead!).
> - **Avoid Long-Term Daily High-Dose Dopamine Agonists (Pramipexole, Ropinirole) as First-Line Therapy for Restless Legs Syndrome** to prevent **Augmentation** (an iatrogenic nightmare where RLS symptoms progressively worsen, start earlier in the afternoon, and spread to the arms and trunk; always use **alpha-2-delta ligands [Gabapentin/Pregabalin] first**!).
> - **Avoid Beta-Blockers (Propranolol) in Essential Tremor Patients with Severe Asthma, Second/Third-Degree AV Heart Block, or Severe Bradycardia** (prescribe **Primidone** instead).
> - **Avoid Prescribing Levodopa or Dopamine Agonists for Essential Tremor**, as ET is a non-dopaminergic cerebellothalamic disorder; dopaminergic drugs provide zero benefit and cause nausea, orthostasis, and motor complications.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Augmentation in Restless Legs Syndrome:** Paradoxical worsening of RLS symptoms induced by chronic dopaminergic therapy $\rightarrow$ weaning the dopamine agonist and transitioning to gabapentin/pregabalin + iron repletion resolves the augmentation.
> - **Severe Functional Disability in Essential Tremor:** Inability to eat soup, write checks, or drink liquids $\rightarrow$ weighted utensils, wrist weights, and referral for **VIM Thalamic DBS or MRgFUS Thalamotomy**.
> - **Suicide & Severe Depression in Huntington Disease:** Striatal and cortical atrophy combined with knowledge of genetic fate $\rightarrow$ suicide rate is 4–6× higher than the general population $\rightarrow$ proactive psychiatric surveillance.
> - **Exhaustion & Hyperthermia from Severe Chorea or Hemiballismus:** Violent flinging movements can cause severe physical trauma, dehydration, and rhabdomyolysis.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 56-year-old administrative assistant presents with a 6-year history of bilateral hand shaking that occurs when typing, using a computer mouse, or holding a cup of coffee. Her mother had similar shaking hands. Physical examination demonstrates an **8-Hz bilateral, symmetric action and postural tremor** of both hands when holding arms outstretched and during finger-to-nose testing, with a mild vertical "yes-yes" head tremor; tone, gait, and finger-tapping speed are completely normal (no bradykinesia or rigidity). She notes her tremor "completely vanishes" after having a glass of wine with dinner. She is diagnosed clinically with **Essential Tremor** and initiated on **Propranolol (40 mg PO daily, titrating to 40 mg BID)**, resulting in dramatic reduction in tremor amplitude and restored ability to write and type at work.
