> [!abstract] Headache Disorders
> Benign, recurrent neurovascular or neurobiologic headache syndromes occurring in the absence of exogenous structural pathology, predominantly comprising **Migraine** (trigeminovascular activation), **Tension-Type Headache** (central/myofascial pain sensitization), and **Cluster Headache / Trigeminal Autonomic Cephalalgias** (hypothalamic-trigeminal-autonomic reflex).

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Migraine:** Cortical spreading depression (underlies aura) triggers the **trigeminovascular system** $\rightarrow$ release of vasoactive neuropeptides (**Calcitonin Gene-Related Peptide [CGRP]**, Substance P) $\rightarrow$ neurogenic dural vasodilation, mast cell degranulation, and central sensitization.
> - **Cluster Headache:** Posterior hypothalamic hyperactivity activates the **trigeminal-autonomic reflex** via the sphenopalatine ganglion, driving cranial parasympathetic outflow.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Gepants Bypass Cardiovascular Limits:** Unlike triptans (which cause vasoconstriction), small-molecule **CGRP antagonists (Rimegepant, Ubrogepant)** and **5-$\text{HT}_\text{1F}$ agonists (Lasmiditan)** abort migraines without vasoconstriction and are safe in coronary artery disease (CAD), peripheral vascular disease, or prior stroke/TIA.
> - **Cluster Headache First-Line Abortive:** **100% High-Flow Oxygen (12–15 L/min via non-rebreather mask for 15–20 minutes)** + **Subcutaneous Sumatriptan (6 mg)**; patients pace the room and cannot sit still (unlike migraineurs who lie in a dark, quiet room).
> - **Medication-Overuse Headache (MOH) Thresholds:** Suspect MOH in any headache occurring $\ge 15$ days/month caused by taking **NSAIDs/acetaminophen $\ge 15$ days/month** OR **triptans/opioids/combination analgesics (Fioricet) $\ge 10$ days/month** for $>3$ months; prevention will fail until acute overused drugs are completely stopped!
> - **Migraine Prophylaxis Indication:** Initiate preventive therapy if headaches occur **$\ge 4$ days/month**, cause severe functional disability, or acute therapies are contraindicated/overused.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Migraine (POUND Mnemonic):** **P**ulsatile/throbbing, **O**ne-day duration (4–72 hours), **U**nilateral (60–70%), **N**ausea/vomiting, **D**isabling intensity (aggravated by routine physical activity), accompanied by **photophobia and phonophobia** $\pm$ aura (typically visual scintillating scotoma spreading over $\ge 5$ min, lasting 5–60 min).
> - **Tension-Type Headache (TTH):** Bilateral, dull, non-pulsatile "band-like" or viselike pressing sensation of mild-to-moderate intensity; **NOT aggravated by routine physical activity**; no nausea or vomiting.
> - **Cluster Headache:** Strictly unilateral, excruciating periorbital/temporal piercing pain ("suicide headache") lasting **15 to 180 minutes**, occurring in bouts (1–8 attacks/day for 6–12 weeks), accompanied by **ipsilateral autonomic signs** (lacrimation, conjunctival injection, nasal congestion, rhinorrhea, ptosis, miosis) and severe **restlessness/pacing**.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Neurological exam:** Completely normal in uncomplicated primary headache disorders.
> - **TTH:** Tenderness of pericranial, occipital, and trapezius myofascial trigger points.
> - **Cluster (During Attack):** Ipsilateral Horner-like syndrome (partial ptosis and miosis) + conjunctival injection and lacrimation.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> **ICHD-3 Formal Criteria (Primary Headache Diagnoses):**
>
> - **Migraine without Aura:** $\ge 5$ attacks lasting **4 to 72 hours** (untreated) fulfilling:
>   - **$\ge 2$ Pain Characteristics:** Unilateral location, pulsating quality, moderate/severe intensity, or aggravated by/causing avoidance of routine physical activity.
>   - **$\ge 1$ Associated Feature:** Nausea and/or vomiting, OR photophobia AND phonophobia.
> - **Tension-Type Headache (Frequent Episodic):** $\ge 10$ episodes lasting 30 minutes to 7 days fulfilling:
>   - **$\ge 2$ Pain Characteristics:** Bilateral location, pressing/tightening (non-pulsating) quality, mild/moderate intensity, not aggravated by routine physical activity.
>   - **Both of the following:** No nausea or vomiting, and no more than one of photophobia or phonophobia.
> - **Cluster Headache:** $\ge 5$ attacks of severe unilateral orbital/temporal pain lasting **15 to 180 minutes** fulfilling:
>   - **$\ge 1$ Ipsilateral Autonomic Sign** (conjunctival injection/lacrimation, nasal congestion/rhinorrhea, eyelid edema, forehead/facial sweating, miosis/ptosis) OR a sense of restlessness/agitation.
>   - Attack frequency between **1 every other day and 8 per day**.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Initial**
>
> - **None Required:** Primary headache disorders are **clinical diagnoses**; routine imaging is not indicated in patients with normal neurological exams and no red flags.
>
> **Neuroimaging (MRI Brain with/without Contrast)**
>
> - Indicated **ONLY** if red flags are present (SNOOP10: Systemic symptoms/fever, Neurologic signs, Onset sudden/thunderclap, Older age >50, Pattern change, Positional, Papilledema, Pregnancy, or Immunosuppression).
>
> **Monitoring / Diaries**
>
> - **Headache Diary:** Track monthly headache days (MHD), attack duration, acute analgesic frequency, and triggers to guide preventive therapy and detect medication-overuse headache.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Sudden thunderclap onset** (peaking in <1 minute $\rightarrow$ rule out subarachnoid hemorrhage, RCVS).
> - New-onset focal neurological deficits, cognitive changes, or papilledema.
> - New headache pattern in a patient **$\ge 50$ years old** (temporal arteritis, mass).
> - Headache strictly postural (worse upright = CSF leak; worse recumbent = raised ICP).
> - Progressive headache in patients with active malignancy or HIV.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH):** Thunderclap onset, meningismus, syncope.
> - **Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis (CVST):** Progressive headache, papilledema, seizures, focal deficits.
> - **Giant Cell Arteritis (GCA):** Age >50, jaw claudication, scalp tenderness, visual changes.
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Medication-Overuse Headache (MOH):** Daily/near-daily headache caused by regular use of abortive medications for $>3$ months.
> - **Cervicogenic Headache:** Unilateral occipital pain referred from cervical spine pathology, worsened by neck movement.
> - **Temporomandibular Joint (TMJ) Dysfunction:** Preauricular pain and clicking aggravated by chewing.
>
> **Most Surprising**
>
> - **Trigeminal Neuralgia:** Unilateral, lancinating, electric shock-like facial pain in V2/V3 distribution lasting seconds, triggered by light touch (eating, shaving).

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Rapid, complete relief of acute attack within 2 hours without headache recurrence; reduce monthly headache days (MHD) by **$\ge 50\%$**.
> - **Secondary Target:** Restore functional ability, eliminate emergency department visits, and prevent transformation into Medication-Overuse Headache.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (Status Migrainosus & Acute Cluster)**
>
> - **Administer 100% High-Flow Oxygen via non-rebreather mask (12–15 L/min for 15–20 minutes)** + **Subcutaneous Sumatriptan (6 mg SC)** immediately upon presentation of an acute **Cluster Headache** attack.
> - **Administer an IV Migraine Cocktail** for acute severe refractory migraine or **Status Migrainosus (>72 hours)**:
>   - **[IV Metoclopramide 10 mg OR Prochlorperazine 10 mg]** + **[IV Ketorolac 15–30 mg]** + **[IV Diphenhydramine 25 mg]** + 1 L Normal Saline bolus.
>   - **Add IV Dexamethasone (10 mg single dose)** to reduce the risk of early headache recurrence within 72 hours.
>
> **Acute / Abortive Therapy (Migraine & TTH)**
>
> - **Mild-to-Moderate Migraine or TTH:** **Administer Oral NSAIDs (Ibuprofen 400–600 mg, Naproxen Sodium 500–550 mg) or Acetaminophen/Aspirin/Caffeine combination** at earliest onset of headache.
> - **Moderate-to-Severe Migraine (First-Line):** **Administer an Oral Triptan (Sumatriptan 50–100 mg, Rizatriptan 10 mg, or Zolmitriptan 5 mg PO)** at the earliest onset of pain (not aura); pair with **Naproxen 500 mg** for synergistic efficacy and lower recurrence.
> - **Triptan-Contraindicated / Refractory Migraine:** **Prescribe an Oral CGRP Receptor Antagonist (Rimegepant 75 mg or Ubrogepant 50–100 mg)** or **Lasmiditan (50–100 mg)** (avoid driving for 8 hours due to sedation) to abort attacks safely without vasoconstriction.
>
> **Preventive / Prophylactic Pharmacotherapy (Migraine, TTH, Cluster)**
>
> - **Migraine Prophylaxis (Initiate when $\ge 4$ headache days/month or disabling attacks):**
>   - _First-Line Oral Options:_
>     - **Propranolol (40–160 mg/day PO)** or **Metoprolol** (preferred in young, non-asthmatic, hypertensive patients).
>     - **Topiramate (50–100 mg/day PO)** (preferred if weight loss is desired; watch for cognitive slowing/paresthesias).
>     - **Amitriptyline (10–50 mg PO QHS)** (preferred if comorbid depression, anxiety, insomnia, or tension headache).
>   - _Calcitonin Gene-Related Peptide (CGRP) Monoclonal Antibodies (Targeted First/Second-Line):_ **Administer Erenumab, Galcanezumab, or Fremanezumab SC monthly** for high efficacy and minimal systemic adverse effects.
>   - _Chronic Migraine ($\ge 15$ days/month for $\ge 3$ months):_ **Administer OnabotulinumtoxinA (Botox: 155 units across 31 injection sites q12 weeks)** per the PREEMPT protocol.
> - **Cluster Headache Prophylaxis:** **Initiate Verapamil (high-dose: 240 to 960 mg/day PO divided TID)** as the first-line preventive (mandates baseline and serial ECGs for PR prolongation); bridge with a **short-term Oral Prednisone taper (start 60 mg/day for 5 days, taper over 2–3 weeks)** or **Greater Occipital Nerve (GON) steroid block**.
>
> **Medication-Overuse Headache (MOH) Strategy**
>
> - **Abruptly discontinue the overused acute medication** (triptans, NSAIDs, combination analgesics), educate the patient that rebound headache will temporarily worsen for 7–10 days, and initiate an evidence-based preventive agent (e.g., Topiramate or CGRP mAb) immediately.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Triptans and Ergotamines in patients with Coronary Artery Disease, Prior Myocardial Infarction, Stroke/TIA, Peripheral Vascular Disease, or Uncontrolled Hypertension** to prevent life-threatening coronary or cerebral vasospasm (prescribe a **Gepant** instead!).
> - **Avoid Opioids (Oxycodone, Hydrocodone) and Butalbital-containing compounds (Fioricet)** for primary headache disorders to prevent rapid onset of severe **Medication-Overuse Headache (MOH)** and substance dependency.
> - **Avoid Valproate and Topiramate in Women of Childbearing Potential** without reliable contraception to prevent severe **teratogenicity (neural tube defects with valproate, oral clefts with topiramate)**.
> - **Avoid Verapamil for Cluster Headache without a Baseline ECG and Dose-Escalation ECGs** to prevent fatal high-grade **atrioventricular heart block**.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Status Migrainosus:** Debilitating migraine attack lasting $>72$ hours $\rightarrow$ IV dopamine antagonist + IV ketorolac + IV dexamethasone + IV fluids.
> - **Medication-Overuse Headache:** Transformation from episodic to chronic daily headache $\rightarrow$ complete withdrawal of overused analgesics + early preventive bridge.
> - **Migrainous Infarction:** Ischemic stroke occurring during a typical migraine with aura attack, confirmed by neuroimaging $\rightarrow$ permanently discontinue triptans/ergots/OCPs and optimize stroke secondary prevention.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 28-year-old woman presents with severe, pulsating left-sided headaches occurring 6 days per month for the past 6 months. Attacks last 24 hours, are accompanied by nausea, severe photophobia, and phonophobia, and force her to miss work in a dark room; neurological examination is entirely normal (**Migraine without Aura**). For acute attacks, she is prescribed an **oral triptan (Sumatriptan 50 mg) combined with Naproxen Sodium 500 mg** (instructed to limit use to $<10$ days/month). Because she suffers from $>4$ headache days/month, she is concurrently started on **prophylactic Propranolol (40 mg PO daily)**, achieving a $>50\%$ reduction in headache frequency at 2-month follow-up.
