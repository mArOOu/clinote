> [!abstract] Myasthenic Crisis
> Life-threatening complication of Myasthenia Gravis characterized by acute neuromuscular respiratory failure due to **diaphragmatic and intercostal weakness**, severe **bulbar muscle paralysis** (inability to protect the airway or clear secretions), or both, necessitating emergent non-invasive or invasive mechanical ventilation.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Receptor Destruction:** Autoantibodies targeting post-synaptic neuromuscular junction (NMJ) proteins—**Acetylcholine Receptors (AChR, ~85%)**, **Muscle-Specific Kinase (MuSK, ~5–8%)**, or **LRP4**—cause complement-mediated synaptic destruction and receptor internalization.
> - **Safety Factor Failure:** Repetitive muscle stimulation depletes presynaptic ACh; with depleted post-synaptic receptors, endplate potentials fall below the threshold required to trigger muscle action potentials, resulting in flaccid respiratory muscle exhaustion.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **HOLD Pyridostigmine (Mestinon) during crisis:** Anticholinesterase inhibitors increase bronchial secretions, cause bradycardia, and cloud the distinction between myasthenic crisis and cholinergic crisis; stop pyridostigmine upon intubation and restart only during weaning.
> - **Beware the "Steroid Dip":** Initiating high-dose corticosteroids can trigger a paradoxical, transient worsening of weakness in ~30–50% of patients within the first 5–10 days; **always initiate PLEX or IVIG prior to or concurrently with steroids**.
> - **The "20/30/40 Rule":** Electively intubate if **Forced Vital Capacity (FVC) <20 mL/kg**, **Negative Inspiratory Force (NIF) < -30 $\text{cmH}_2\text{O}$**, or **Maximum Expiratory Pressure (MEP) <40 $\text{cmH}_2\text{O}$** before hypercapnic acidosis develops.
> - **Anti-MuSK Phenotype:** Patients with anti-MuSK antibodies present with predominant bulbar, neck, and respiratory weakness, are frequently **worsened by pyridostigmine**, respond poorly to IVIG, and show superior response to **Plasma Exchange (PLEX)** and **Rituximab**.
> - **Deadly Trigger Medications:** Fluoroquinolones, aminoglycosides, macrolides, beta-blockers, IV magnesium, and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) are potent triggers of crisis.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - Precipitating trigger present in ~70%: **Infection (#1 trigger, ~50%)**, surgery/trauma, pregnancy/postpartum, rapid steroid taper, or **culprit medications**.
> - Severe dyspnea at rest, orthopnea (diaphragm weakness), and inability to count past 10–15 on a single breath.
> - Severe bulbar collapse: "Nasal" voice, severe dysphagia, choking, and inability to swallow oral secretions.
> - Worsening fatigable bilateral ptosis, diplopia, and proximal limb weakness (worse with exertion and toward the end of the day).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Paradoxical abdominal breathing:** Inward motion of the abdomen during inspiration (pathognomonic for severe diaphragmatic weakness).
> - **Weak neck flexion:** Inability to lift the head off the bed correlates directly with diaphragmatic compromise.
> - **Preserved Deep Tendon Reflexes and Sensory Exam:** Normal DTRs and normal sensory exam definitively exclude Guillain-Barré syndrome and spinal cord pathology.
> - **Normal, reactive pupils:** Ocular involvement in MG spares pupillary pupillomotor fibers (differentiates from botulism).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Confirmed Diagnosis of Myasthenia Gravis:**
>   - Serologic confirmation (**Anti-AChR**, **Anti-MuSK**, or **Anti-LRP4 antibodies**), OR
>   - Electrodiagnostic confirmation via **Repetitive Nerve Stimulation (RNS)** showing a **>10% decremental response** at low frequency (2–3 Hz), or **Single-Fiber EMG (SFEMG)** showing increased jitter/blocking.
> - **Criterion for Crisis:** Acute respiratory failure or severe bulbar failure requiring mechanical ventilation (invasive or non-invasive) directly attributable to myasthenic weakness.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Initial**
>
> - **Bedside Pulmonary Mechanics:** Serial **Forced Vital Capacity (FVC)** and **Negative Inspiratory Force (NIF)** q2–4 hours (or Single Breath Count <15–20).
> - **Arterial/Venous Blood Gas:** Monitors for hypercapnia ($\text{PaCO}_2 >45\text{ mmHg}$) and respiratory acidosis (late, ominous signs).
> - **Chest Radiography / Non-Contrast CT Chest:** Identifies aspiration pneumonia (trigger) and screens for **Thymoma** (present in 10–15% of MG; 30% have thymic hyperplasia).
>
> **Confirmatory / Bedside (if new diagnosis)**
>
> - **Ice Pack Test:** Ice pack placed over a ptotic eye for 2 minutes; improvement of ptosis by **$\ge 2$ mm** is >90% sensitive/specific for ocular MG (cooling inhibits acetylcholinesterase).
> - **Serology Panel:** Anti-AChR binding/blocking/modulating antibodies; if negative, order anti-MuSK and anti-LRP4 antibodies.
>
> **Severity / Risk**
>
> - **MGFA Clinical Classification:** Class V defines myasthenic crisis (intubation with or without mechanical ventilation).

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **FVC <20 mL/kg, NIF < -30 $\text{cmH}_2\text{O}$**, or rapid downward trajectory in spirometric parameters.
> - Paradoxical abdominal wall motion during inspiration (diaphragmatic exhaustion).
> - Loss of bulbar protective reflexes with pooling of oral secretions (aspiration risk).
> - Severe neck flexor weakness (inability to lift head against gravity).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Cholinergic Crisis:** Excess acetylcholinesterase inhibitors causing persistent depolarization block $\rightarrow$ flaccid weakness PLUS **SLUDGE/DUMBELS** features (salivation, lacrimation, urination, defecation, GI cramping, emesis, miosis, bradycardia, fasciculations).
> - **Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS):** Ascending flaccid paralysis with **areflexia**, sensory paresthesias, and CSF albuminocytological dissociation.
> - **Botulism:** Descending paralysis, **fixed dilated/unreactive pupils**, severe dry mouth, and clear sensorium.
> - **Lambert-Eaton Myasthenic Syndrome (LEMS):** Small-cell lung cancer association; **hyporeflexia/areflexia**, autonomic dysfunction (dry mouth, erectile dysfunction), and **strength improves with repetitive exercise** (incremental response on RNS).
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS):** Combined upper motor neuron (hyperreflexia, spasticity) and lower motor neuron (atrophy, fasciculations) signs; spares extraocular muscles.
>
> **Most Surprising**
>
> - **Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor (ICI) Myasthenia/Myositis/Myocarditis Overlap:** Fulminant, highly lethal crisis in cancer patients treated with anti-PD-1 agents; accompanied by marked **troponin and CK elevation**.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Secure the airway, prevent hypoxemic/hypercapnic arrest (**SpO2 >92%, PaCO2 <45 mmHg**), and achieve rapid antibody reduction via apheresis or IVIG.
> - **Secondary Target:** Reverse bulbar weakness, extubate safely, and bridge into long-term corticosteroid/immunosuppressive maintenance.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Admit to the ICU immediately** for continuous pulse oximetry, cardiac telemetry, and serial pulmonary mechanics monitoring (FVC/NIF q2–4h).
> - **Hold Pyridostigmine (Mestinon)** immediately upon presentation with crisis or intubation to eliminate profuse bronchorrhea and avoid cholinergic bradycardia.
> - **Initiate Non-Invasive Ventilation (BiPAP)** early in mild-to-moderate respiratory fatigue ONLY if the patient is fully alert, hemodynamically stable, and has intact bulbar function to clear secretions.
> - **Intubate via Rapid Sequence Intubation** when **FVC <15–20 mL/kg**, **NIF < -30 $\text{cmH}_2\text{O}$**, or if bulbar weakness impairs airway protection.
> - **Avoid Succinylcholine entirely** (resistance necessitates massive doses with unpredictable recovery) and **reduce Non-Depolarizing Paralytics (Rocuronium) to 1/3 to 1/2 of standard dose** (extreme sensitivity causes prolonged paralysis; keep **Sugammadex** immediately available).
>
> **First-Line Rapid Immunotherapy (Equal Efficacy — PLEX vs. IVIG)**
>
> - **Perform Plasma Exchange (PLEX: 5 sessions of 1 to 1.5 plasma volume exchanges on alternate days over 10–14 days)** as the **preferred first-line therapy** (faster onset [24–48 hours], superior in MuSK MG, and avoids volume overload) to mechanically remove circulating autoantibodies.
> - **Administer IV Immune Globulin (IVIG: 2.0 g/kg total dose divided over 2 to 5 days [e.g., 0.4 g/kg/day for 5 days or 1.0 g/kg/day for 2 days])** as an equivalent first-line alternative when vascular access or hemodynamic instability precludes PLEX (less effective in anti-MuSK disease).
>
> **Immunosuppressive Bridging & Maintenance**
>
> - **Initiate IV Methylprednisolone (40–60 mg/day) or Oral Prednisone (0.75–1 mg/kg/day)** ONLY **after starting PLEX or IVIG** to establish disease control while preventing the transient steroid-induced weakness flare.
> - **Start Rituximab (375 mg/m² weekly × 4 or two 1,000 mg infusions)** as first-line maintenance in **Anti-MuSK MG** and refractory generalized AChR MG.
>
> **Weaning & Disposition**
>
> - **Restart low-dose oral Pyridostigmine (30–60 mg PO q6h)** only after the patient is stable, secretions are minimal, and extubation planning is underway.
> - **Wean mechanical ventilation** when bulbar function recovers, **FVC >20–25 mL/kg**, **NIF > -30 $\text{cmH}_2\text{O}$**, and the patient can lift their head off the bed against resistance.
> - **Refer for elective Transsternal or Robotic Thymectomy** once stable and in remission for all generalized AChR+ patients aged 18–65 years (MGTX trial) and for all patients with **Thymoma**.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Continuing Pyridostigmine in Intubated Patients** to prevent copious airway secretions and aspiration.
> - **Avoid High-Dose Corticosteroid Monotherapy without concurrent PLEX/IVIG** to prevent precipitating severe paradoxical worsening of weakness.
> - **Avoid Fluoroquinolones (Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin), Aminoglycosides (Gentamicin, Tobramycin), and Macrolides (Azithromycin)** in all MG patients to prevent acute NMJ blockade and worsening crisis.
> - **Avoid Beta-Blockers, Calcium Channel Blockers, Procainamide, and IV Magnesium** to prevent worsening of neuromuscular transmission.
> - **Avoid Standard-Dose Rocuronium / Vecuronium** without neuromuscular monitoring to prevent hours of prolonged paralysis.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Aspiration Pneumonia:** Bulbar paralysis $\rightarrow$ pooled secretions, fever, infiltrates $\rightarrow$ intubation, airway clearance, IV broad-spectrum antibiotics.
> - **Cholinergic Crisis:** Excess pyridostigmine $\rightarrow$ weakness, miosis, fasciculations, profuse sweating, bradycardia $\rightarrow$ stop anticholinesterases, administer IV Atropine (0.5–1 mg).
> - **Ventilator Dependency / Tracheostomy:** Prolonged failure to wean >10–14 days $\rightarrow$ tracheostomy for ongoing respiratory failure.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 42-year-old woman with generalized Myasthenia Gravis maintained on pyridostigmine and low-dose prednisone is prescribed ciprofloxacin for a urinary tract infection. Three days later, she presents to the ED with severe dyspnea, orthopnea, head drop, and inability to swallow her saliva. Exam reveals paradoxical abdominal breathing, bilateral ptosis, an **FVC of 14 mL/kg**, and a **NIF of -22 $\text{cmH}_2\text{O}$**. Pyridostigmine is discontinued immediately, she is electively intubated without neuromuscular paralytics, admitted to the Neuro-ICU, and initiated on **Plasma Exchange (PLEX)** alongside targeted UTI antibiotics, resulting in successful extubation on Day 6.
