> [!abstract] Status Epilepticus
> Neurological emergency defined operationally as **continuous clinical and/or electrographic seizure activity lasting ≥5 minutes**, OR **≥2 discrete seizures without return to baseline consciousness between episodes**, requiring immediate pharmacologic termination before irreversible excitotoxic neuronal death occurs at **30 minutes**.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Receptor Trafficking ("Why Benzodiazepines Fail After 15–30 Minutes"):** During prolonged seizure activity, inhibitory synaptic **$\text{GABA}_\text{A}$ receptors are rapidly internalized (endocytosed)** into the neuron (causing progressive **benzodiazepine resistance**), while excitatory **NMDA and AMPA glutamate receptors** are mobilized to the synaptic membrane $\rightarrow$ massive intracellular $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ influx, mitochondrial failure, and **excitotoxic neuronal necrosis** (especially in the hippocampus/CA1, amygdala, and cortex).
> - **Biphasic Systemic Collapse:**
>   - _Phase 1 (0–30 min — Sympathetic Autonomic Storm):_ Massive catecholamine surge $\rightarrow$ **severe hypertension, tachycardia, hyperpyrexia, hyperglycemia**, diaphoresis, and severe **lactic + respiratory acidosis** (from vigorous skeletal muscle contractions and apnea).
>   - _Phase 2 (>30–60 min — Decompensation):_ Cerebral autoregulation fails $\rightarrow$ **hypotension, hypoglycemia, hyperkalemia, rhabdomyolysis/myoglobinuric AKI**, neurogenic pulmonary edema, and cardiac arrest.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Under-dosing Benzodiazepines is the #1 clinical error:** Give the **FULL guideline dose** immediately (**IV Lorazepam 4 mg** [0.1 mg/kg] OR **IM Midazolam 10 mg** if no IV access); splitting it into tiny 1–2 mg doses allows $\text{GABA}_\text{A}$ receptor internalization and progressions to refractory status!
> - **IM Midazolam (10 mg) is FASTER and superior when IV access is lacking (RAMPART Trial):** Never waste precious minutes hunting for an IV in a violently seizing patient—inject **10 mg IM Midazolam** into the anterolateral thigh immediately.
> - **All 3 Second-Line AEDs are Equally Effective (ESETT Trial):** **Levetiracetam (60 mg/kg, max 4,500 mg)**, **Fosphenytoin (20 mg PE/kg, max 1,500 mg PE)**, and **Valproate (40 mg/kg, max 3,000 mg)** terminate benzodiazepine-refractory status epilepticus in **~50% of patients** with equivalent efficacy!
> - **Patient stops shaking after Benzos/AEDs OR Intubation, but doesn't wake up? = Nonconvulsive Status Epilepticus (NCSE)!** Up to **20–48%** of patients whose motor convulsions stop remain in **electrographic Status Epilepticus** ("electromechanical dissociation")—AND neuromuscular paralysis (**Rocuronium**) for intubation completely hides motor shaking while the brain continues seizing $\rightarrow$ **Continuous EEG (cEEG) is mandatory!**

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Witnessed Tonic-Clonic Convulsions lasting ≥5 minutes** or recurrent convulsions without waking up in between.
> - **Mandatory Etiology Screen ("VITAMINS" / Acute vs. Remote):**
>   - **#1 Cause in Known Epilepsy:** **Antiepileptic Drug (AED) Non-Adherence**, subtherapeutic levels, sleep deprivation, or intercurrent infection.
>   - **New-Onset Status Epilepticus (Always Acute Symptomatic until proven otherwise!):**
>     - _Metabolic:_ **Hypoglycemia**, **Severe Hyponatremia (<120 mEq/L)**, Hypocalcemia, Hypomagnesemia, Uremia, Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State (HHS).
>     - _Toxic / Withdrawal:_ **Alcohol or Benzodiazepine/Baclofen Withdrawal**, **Isoniazid (INH) overdose** (causes pyridoxine [Vitamin B6] depletion!), **Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs)**, **Bupropion**, **Tramadol**, **Cocaine/Amphetamines**, **Theophylline**, **Organophosphates**, **Cefepime / Penicillins / Carbapenems (Imipenem)**.
>     - _Structural / Vascular:_ Acute stroke, **Intracerebral / Subarachnoid / Subdural Hemorrhage**, Traumatic Brain Injury, **Brain Tumor / Metastasis**, Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (**PRES**), **Eclampsia** (pregnant or up to 6 weeks postpartum!).
>     - _Infectious / Autoimmune:_ **Bacterial Meningitis**, **HSV-1 Temporal Lobe Encephalitis**, Cerebral Abscess, Neurocysticercosis, **Autoimmune / Paraneoplastic Encephalitis (Anti-NMDA Receptor)** (presents as **NORSE** [New-Onset Refractory Status Epilepticus] in young women with ovarian teratoma!).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Generalized Convulsive Status Epilepticus (GCSE):** Bilateral tonic stiffening followed by rhythmic clonic jerking of extremities, **upward or lateral conjugate eye deviation**, **forced head turn** (contralateral to seizure focus), cyanosis, frothing at the mouth, **lateral tongue biting** (highly specific for true seizure vs. tip-of-tongue in syncope/PNES), and urinary incontinence.
> - **Subtle / Late Status Epilepticus ("Burned-Out" GCSE) & Nonconvulsive Status Epilepticus (NCSE):**
>   - After 30–60 minutes of GCSE, overt limb jerking ceases due to muscle exhaustion; physical signs shrink to **subtle rhythmic nystagmoid eye twitching**, **eyelid fluttering**, **facial/lip/finger myoclonus**, unexplained coma/stupor, or autonomic instability.
> - **Postictal Todd's Paralysis:** Transient unilateral hemiparesis or gaze palsy after seizure termination pointing to a focal structural lesion.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> **ILAE (International League Against Epilepsy) Dual-Timepoint Criteria ($t_1$ and $t_2$):**
>
> - **Timepoint $t_1$ (Operational Threshold to START Emergency Treatment):**
>   - **≥5 minutes** for **Generalized Tonic-Clonic (Convulsive) Status Epilepticus** (or ≥2 seizures without recovery of consciousness).
>   - **≥10 minutes** for **Focal Status Epilepticus with Impaired Awareness** (or 10–15 minutes for Absence Status).
> - **Timepoint $t_2$ (Threshold at which Long-Term Neuronal Death / Injury Begins):**
>   - **30 minutes** for Generalized Convulsive Status Epilepticus (60 minutes for Focal Status).
> - **Refractory Status Epilepticus (RSE):** Status epilepticus that **persists despite administration of an adequate dose of a First-Line Benzodiazepine PLUS one Second-Line IV Antiepileptic Drug** (requires intubation and continuous anesthetic infusion).
> - **Super-Refractory Status Epilepticus (SRSE):** Status epilepticus that **continues or recurs ≥24 hours after the onset of continuous IV anesthetic therapy** (including recurrence during anesthetic weaning).

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Immediate Bedside & STAT Labs (0–5 Minutes)**
>
> - **1. Point-of-Care Fingerstick Blood Glucose:** **Mandatory FIRST test at Minute 0** in 100% of seizing patients!
> - **2. Basic Metabolic Panel + Ionized Calcium, Magnesium, & Phosphorus:** Identifies **severe Hyponatremia (<120 mEq/L)**, hypocalcemia, or hypomagnesemia (all of which are refractory to standard AEDs until the electrolyte is corrected!).
> - **3. Serum Antiepileptic Drug (AED) Trough Levels:** Phenytoin, Valproate, Levetiracetam, Carbamazepine, Phenobarbital.
> - **4. Urine $\beta$-hCG (Mandatory in all reproductive-age females $\rightarrow$ Eclampsia!)**, **Urine & Serum Toxicology Screen + Ethanol Level**, **CBC**, **Hepatic Panel / Ammonia**, **Troponin**, **Creatine Kinase (CK)** (screen for rhabdomyolysis), and **Arterial Blood Gas + Lactate**.
>   - _Lab Interpretation Pearl:_ An **Anion-Gap Lactic Acidosis (pH <7.0, Lactate >8–15 mmol/L)** is universally present immediately after a generalized tonic-clonic seizure and **resolves spontaneously within 60 minutes** of seizure cessation with IV fluids (**NEVER give IV Sodium Bicarbonate** for seizure-induced lactic acidosis!).
>   - _Peripheral Leukocytosis (WBC 15,000–20,000/µL without bandemia):_ Common transient catecholamine-induced **demargination** of neutrophils; however, if **fever, meningismus, or persistent bandemia** is present, you must rule out **meningitis/encephalitis**!
>
> **Neuroimaging & Electroencephalography**
>
> - **Non-Contrast Head CT (NCCT):** Perform in **ALL patients with first-time Status Epilepticus**, focal neurological deficits, head trauma, cancer, anticoagulation, or failure to return to baseline **ONCE the seizure is controlled and the airway is secured** (Follow with **Brain MRI with/without contrast** once stable).
> - **Continuous Electroencephalography (cEEG) Monitoring:** **Mandatory** in ANY patient who:
>   1. **Does not wake up / return to baseline mentation within 15–30 minutes** after motor convulsions stop (to rule out **Nonconvulsive Status Epilepticus [NCSE]**!),
>   2. **Received a long-acting Neuromuscular Blocker (Rocuronium/Vecuronium)** for intubation, OR
>   3. Is in **Refractory Status Epilepticus** on continuous IV anesthetic infusions (to titrate to **seizure suppression or burst suppression**).
> - **Lumbar Puncture (LP):** Perform (after Head CT rules out mass effect) if **febrile, immunocompromised, or suspected Meningitis / HSV Encephalitis / Autoimmune Encephalitis** (_Note: Prolonged status epilepticus alone can cause a very mild reactive CSF pleocytosis [up to 10–20 WBC/mm³], but any significant pleocytosis must be treated as CNS infection until proven otherwise!_).

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Failure to awaken within 20–30 minutes after motor shaking stops** ($\rightarrow$ **Nonconvulsive Status Epilepticus [NCSE]** until proven otherwise by STAT EEG).
> - **Status Epilepticus in a Pregnant or Postpartum (<6 Weeks) Woman:** **Eclampsia** until proven otherwise ($\rightarrow$ give **IV Magnesium Sulfate** immediately!).
> - **Status Epilepticus + QRS Widening (>100 ms) + Terminal R Wave in aVR on ECG:** **Tricyclic Antidepressant (TCA) Overdose** ($\rightarrow$ give **IV Sodium Bicarbonate**; **Phenytoin is contraindicated**!).
> - **Status Epilepticus in a Patient on Isoniazid (TB Therapy) or Eating Gyromitra Mushrooms:** **Acute Pyridoxine (Vitamin B6) Deficiency** ($\rightarrow$ completely refractory to benzos/AEDs until **IV Pyridoxine [5 g IV]** is administered!).
> - **Fever + Temporal Lobe Epileptiform Discharges (LPDs) / Hemorrhagic Necrosis:** **HSV-1 Encephalitis** ($\rightarrow$ start **IV Acyclovir 10 mg/kg q8h** immediately).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss (True Emergencies Mimicking or Causing SE)**
>
> - **Eclampsia, Severe Hyponatremia (<120 mEq/L), Hypoglycemia, & Toxic Overdoses (INH, TCA, Local Anesthetic Systemic Toxicity [LAST], Organophosphates).**
> - **Acute Basilar Artery Thrombosis ("Convulsive-Like" Decerebrate Posturing):** Brainstem ischemia can cause rhythmic bilateral extensor posturing and coma mistaken for status epilepticus; look for pinpoint/blown pupils and absent brainstem reflexes on CTA!
> - **Post-Anoxic Myoclonic Status Epilepticus (Lance-Adams vs. Acute Post-Cardiac Arrest Myoclonus):** Diffuse myoclonic jerking following cardiac arrest.
>
> **Most Likely (The #1 Mimic of Status Epilepticus in the ED!)**
>
> - **Psychogenic Nonepileptic Seizures (PNES / "Pseudostatus Epilepticus"):** Accounts for up to **10–20% of patients intubated for presumed "refractory status epilepticus"**!
>   - **High-Yield Clinical Clues to PNES (Compare with True GCSE):**
>     | Clinical Feature | **Psychogenic Nonepileptic Seizure (PNES)** | **True Generalized Convulsive Seizure** |
>     | :--- | :--- | :--- |
>     | **Eye Closure** | **Eyes tightly CLOSED** (resists passive eye opening by examiner!) | **Eyes OPEN** and deviated upward/laterally |
>     | **Motor Pattern** | **Asynchronous, out-of-phase** side-to-side head shaking, **pelvic thrusting**, waxing/waning amplitude, opisthotonos | Synchronous tonic stiffening $\rightarrow$ rhythmic clonic jerking |
>     | **Vocalization / Breathing** | Weeping, speaking, or screaming _during_ the event; rapid shallow hyperventilation immediately after | Ictal cry only at the very start; **postictal stertorous (snoring) breathing** and cyanosis |
>     | **Labs & EEG** | **Normal serum Lactate, Normal Prolactin, Normal pH**, and **Normal awake alpha rhythm on EEG** (preserved posterior dominant rhythm) | **Marked Lactic Acidosis**, elevated CK/Prolactin, and ictal spike-wave discharges + postictal slowing on EEG |

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals (American Epilepsy Society [AES] Timed Protocol)**
>
> - **Primary Target:** **Complete termination of BOTH clinical and electrographic seizure activity within <15–20 minutes** of presentation.
> - **Secondary Target:** Immediately reverse life-threatening metabolic/toxic triggers (hypoglycemia, hyponatremia, eclampsia, INH) and prevent excitotoxic brain injury, aspiration, and rhabdomyolysis.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies — Phase 1: Stabilization & Targeted Reversal (Minutes 0 to 5)**
>
> - **1. Support Airway, Breathing, and Circulation (ABCs):** Turn patient into the **lateral decubitus position** to prevent aspiration, apply suction and **100% high-flow $\text{O}_2$ via non-rebreather mask**, insert a soft **nasopharyngeal airway** (**NEVER force a tongue blade, spoon, or fingers between clenched teeth!**), place two IV lines, and attach continuous ECG/pulse oximetry.
> - **2. Check Fingerstick Blood Glucose & Administer Targeted Antidotes Immediately if Indicated:**
>   - **If Glucose <60 mg/dL (or Unknown):** Administer **Thiamine 100–500 mg IV FIRST (or concurrently) followed by 50 mL of 50% Dextrose (D50W IV)** (25 g glucose) in adults (or **2 mL/kg of D25W** in children).
>   - **If Severe Symptomatic Hyponatremia (Na <120 mEq/L):** Infuse a **100–150 mL IV Bolus of 3% Hypertonic Saline over 10–20 minutes** (raises serum $\text{Na}^+$ by 4–6 mEq/L and stops the seizure immediately!).
>   - **If Pregnant or Postpartum (Eclampsia):** Administer **Magnesium Sulfate (4 to 6 g IV loading dose over 15–20 minutes, then 2 g/hr continuous IV infusion)**.
>   - **If Isoniazid (INH) Overdose:** Administer **Pyridoxine (Vitamin B6: 5 g IV [or gram-for-gram matching ingested INH dose])** immediately.
>
> **Phase 2: First-Line Therapy — Benzodiazepines (Minutes 5 to 20 — AES Level A)**
>
> - **Administer a Full Single Loading Dose of a Parenteral Benzodiazepine Immediately at Minute 5** ($\text{GABA}_\text{A}$ agonist — increases frequency of chloride channel opening):
>   - **If IV Access IS Available:** **IV Lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg (4 mg IV push over 2 min; may repeat ×1 in 5 minutes if still seizing — preferred IV agent due to longer CNS duration of action [12–24h] than diazepam!)** _OR_ **IV Diazepam 0.15–0.2 mg/kg (up to 10 mg IV)**.
>   - **If IV Access is NOT Available:** **IM Midazolam 10 mg (for >40 kg; 5 mg for 13–40 kg) injected as a single dose into the muscle** (**First-line when no IV is in place** per RAMPART trial; alternatively, **Intranasal Midazolam** or **Rectal Diazepam [0.2–0.5 mg/kg, max 20 mg]**).
>
> **Phase 3: Second-Line Therapy — Urgent IV Antiepileptic Drug Loading (Minutes 20 to 40 — ESETT Trial)**
>
> - **Administer ONE Full Weight-Based IV Loading Dose of a Second-Line AED** if seizures persist at 10–20 minutes (or even if benzos stopped the seizure, to prevent immediate recurrence once the benzo redistributes!):
>   - **Option 1 — IV Levetiracetam (Keppra): 60 mg/kg IV (max 4,500 mg) infused over 10–15 minutes** (**Preferred in most centers**: zero hepatic CYP interactions, zero cardiac arrhythmia/hypotension risk, safe in liver disease and pregnancy!).
>   - **Option 2 — IV Fosphenytoin: 20 mg PE/kg IV (max 1,500 mg PE) infused at up to 150 mg PE/min** (or **IV Phenytoin 20 mg/kg** at max 50 mg/min; voltage-gated $\text{Na}^+$ channel blocker). _Mandatory Continuous ECG & BP Monitoring:_ Causes **hypotension and cardiac arrhythmias (QT/QRS prolongation)**! (_Prefer Fosphenytoin over Phenytoin because Phenytoin is dissolved in propylene glycol [causes hypotension/bradycardia] and has pH 12, causing catastrophic **"Purple Glove Syndrome"** [distal limb necrosis] if it extravasates!_).
>   - **Option 3 — IV Valproate Sodium: 40 mg/kg IV (max 3,000 mg) infused over 10 minutes** (broad-spectrum; safe hemodynamic profile; **CONTRAINDICATED in Pregnancy, Acute Liver Disease, Mitochondrial Disorders [POLG], or Urea Cycle Defects**).
>   - **Alternative (If the Above 3 are Unavailable):** **IV Phenobarbital 15–20 mg/kg** (causes severe respiratory depression and hypotension) or **IV Lacosamide (200–400 mg IV)**.
>
> **Phase 4: Third-Line Therapy — Refractory Status Epilepticus (Minutes 40 to 60+)**
>
> - **Perform Rapid Sequence Intubation (RSI), Transfer to the ICU, Place on Continuous EEG (cEEG) Monitoring, and Initiate a Continuous IV Anesthetic Infusion** (titrated on cEEG to **complete electrographic seizure suppression or a Burst-Suppression pattern for 24–48 hours**, while continuing maintenance doses of scheduled IV AEDs in the background!):
>   - _RSI Paralytic Rule:_ **Prefer Succinylcholine (if no hyperkalemia/rhabdomyolysis) or use Rocuronium ONLY if Continuous EEG is connected immediately**, because long-acting rocuronium masks motor convulsions for 45–60 minutes!
>   - **Continuous IV Anesthetic Agents (Choose One or Combine):**
>     1. **Midazolam Infusion:** Load 0.2 mg/kg IV, then **0.05–2 mg/kg/hr**.
>     2. **Propofol Infusion:** Load 1–2 mg/kg IV, then **20–200 mcg/kg/min** (_Monitor triglycerides, CK, arterial pH, and ECG daily for **Propofol Infusion Syndrome [PRIS]** if >48 hours at >5 mg/kg/hr!_).
>     3. **Ketamine Infusion (NMDA Receptor Antagonist — Crucial Mechanistic Synergy!):** Load 1.5–4.5 mg/kg IV, then **1–10 mg/kg/hr** (**Unique Advantage:** Because prolonged seizures upregulate **NMDA glutamate receptors** while internalizing $\text{GABA}_\text{A}$ receptors, **Ketamine works when GABAergic drugs fail AND supports blood pressure [no vasopressor requirement]!**).
>     4. **Pentobarbital Infusion:** Load 5–15 mg/kg IV, then **0.5–5 mg/kg/hr** (causes profound hypotension requiring vasopressors, paralytic ileus, and immunosuppression; reserved for Super-Refractory SE).

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Under-Dosing Initial Benzodiazepines** (e.g., giving only 1 or 2 mg of Lorazepam to an adult out of fear of respiratory depression)—**prolonged untreated status epilepticus causes FAR more respiratory failure and need for intubation than a full 4-mg dose of Lorazepam**!
> - **Avoid Phenytoin / Fosphenytoin in Toxicological Seizures (Specifically Tricyclic Antidepressants [TCAs], Cocaine, Local Anesthetics, Theophylline, Isoniazid, or Alcohol Withdrawal)**, as Phenytoin is a Class Ib-like $\text{Na}^+$ channel blocker that **worsens fatal cardiac arrhythmias in TCA/cocaine toxicity** and is completely ineffective against GABA-depletion/withdrawal seizures (use **Benzodiazepines, Phenobarbital, or Propofol** instead!).
> - **Avoid Administering Long-Acting Neuromuscular Blockers (Rocuronium, Vecuronium, Pancuronium) without Immediate Continuous EEG Monitoring**, as paralysis stops the visible shaking while allowing **fatal electrographic status epilepticus** to destroy the cortex unnoticed.
> - **Avoid Administering IV Sodium Bicarbonate for Post-Seizure Lactic Acidosis**, as the acidosis resolves spontaneously within 60 minutes once muscle contractions stop (give $\text{NaHCO}_3$ ONLY if the seizure is caused by **TCA overdose or salicylate toxicity**).
> - **Avoid Valproate in Women of Childbearing Potential / Pregnancy** (causes neural tube defects and severe neurodevelopmental teratogenicity) or in patients with unexplained encephalopathy/liver disease (triggers **fatal hyperammonemic encephalopathy and fulminant hepatic failure**).

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Rhabdomyolysis & Myoglobinuric Acute Kidney Injury (AKI):** Violent prolonged muscle contractions lyse skeletal myocytes $\rightarrow$ **blood positive on urine dipstick with ZERO RBCs on microscopy**, **CK >5,000–50,000 U/L**, and **life-threatening Hyperkalemia** $\rightarrow$ treat with aggressive **IV isotonic crystalloids** (target urine output 200–300 mL/hr).
> - **Propofol Infusion Syndrome (PRIS):** Rare, lethal complication of high-dose (>4–5 mg/kg/hr) prolonged (>48h) Propofol infusion (especially in children or patients on catecholamines/steroids) $\rightarrow$ **Mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation failure** causing the classic pentad of: (1) **Refractory Bradycardia / Brugada-like ECG / Asystole**, (2) **Severe High-Anion-Gap Lactic Acidosis**, (3) **Rhabdomyolysis**, (4) **Hypertriglyceridemia (lipemic blood)**, and (5) **Hepatomegaly / Renal Failure** $\rightarrow$ stop Propofol immediately and switch to Midazolam/Ketamine + ECMO/CRRT!
> - **Neurogenic Pulmonary Edema & Aspiration Pneumonia:** Acute massive sympathetic surge increases pulmonary capillary hydrostatic pressure and permeability during GCSE.
> - **Posterior Shoulder Dislocation & Vertebral Compression Fractures:** Powerful tonic internal rotation/adduction contractions during generalized convulsions classically cause **bilateral or unilateral Posterior Glenohumeral Dislocations** (arm held locked in **internal rotation and adduction**; **"Lightbulb Sign"** on AP shoulder X-ray!).
> - **Hippocampal Sclerosis (Mesial Temporal Sclerosis):** Excitotoxic neuronal loss in the CA1 pyramidal layer of the hippocampus following prolonged status epilepticus $\rightarrow$ leads to chronic drug-resistant **Temporal Lobe Epilepsy**.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 26-year-old man with known epilepsy (non-adherent to levetiracetam for 3 days) is brought in by EMS actively having generalized tonic-clonic convulsions that started **10 minutes ago**. He is cyanotic with upward eye deviation and rhythmic limb jerking (**Generalized Convulsive Status Epilepticus**). He is immediately rolled onto his side, given 100% $\text{O}_2$ via non-rebreather mask, and a **fingerstick blood glucose is checked (112 mg/dL)**. Because IV access is not yet established on arrival, **IM Midazolam 10 mg** is injected immediately into his right thigh while an IV line is placed. Five minutes later, his convulsions continue; he is loaded with **IV Levetiracetam 4,500 mg (60 mg/kg) over 10 minutes**. His motor shaking ceases at Minute 22, but 20 minutes later he remains unresponsive with subtle rhythmic left-beating nystagmus; **continuous EEG (cEEG)** is connected immediately, revealing ongoing **Nonconvulsive Status Epilepticus (NCSE)**, for which he is intubated and started on a **continuous IV Propofol infusion** titrated to electrographic seizure suppression.
