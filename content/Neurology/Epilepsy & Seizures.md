> [!abstract] Epilepsy & Seizures
> **Seizure:** Transient occurrence of signs/symptoms resulting from abnormal, excessive, or hypersynchronous neuronal brain activity.
> **Epilepsy:** Disease of the brain characterized by an enduring predisposition to generate epileptic seizures, with significant neurobiological, cognitive, and psychosocial consequences.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Excitation / Inhibition Imbalance:** Excess glutamatergic neurotransmission (NMDA/AMPA receptor activation, $\text{Na}^+/\text{Ca}^{2+}$ influx) overcomes GABAergic inhibition ($\text{GABA}_\text{A}$ receptor chloride currents), causing paroxysmal depolarization shifts.
> - **Focal vs. Generalized:** Focal seizures originate within neuronal networks limited to one cerebral hemisphere; generalized seizures originate at some point within and rapidly engage bilaterally distributed networks.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Lateral Tongue Biting = Seizure:** Lacerations on the **lateral border of the tongue** are $>95\%$ specific for generalized epileptic seizures; lacerations on the tip of the tongue occur in syncope and psychogenic nonepileptic seizures (PNES).
> - **Provoked Seizures $\ne$ Epilepsy:** Seizures triggered by transient acute insults (severe hyponatremia, hypoglycemia, alcohol withdrawal, acute stroke/TBI) do **NOT** represent epilepsy and do **NOT** warrant long-term antiseizure medications (ASMs).
> - **Narrow-Spectrum Drugs Worsen Generalized Seizures:** Sodium channel blockers (**Carbamazepine, Oxcarbazepine, Phenytoin**) exacerbate **Absence, Myoclonic, and Juvenile Myoclonic Epilepsy (JME)**; always choose a **broad-spectrum ASM (Levetiracetam, Lamotrigine, Valproate)** if the seizure type is unconfirmed!
> - **Women of Childbearing Age:** **Avoid Valproate** (highest rate of major congenital malformations [neural tube defects] and reduced offspring IQ); prefer **Levetiracetam** or **Lamotrigine** (monitor lamotrigine serum levels during pregnancy due to accelerated estrogen-mediated glucuronidation).

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Pre-ictal Features (Aura):** Focal onset clues—epigastric rising sensation, olfactory/gustatory hallucinations, or déjà vu (temporal lobe); contralateral motor clonic jerking (frontal lobe); sensory paresthesias (parietal lobe).
> - **Ictal Phenomena:** Sudden loss of awareness, rhythmic clonic jerking, tonic stiffening, head/eye deviation (typically contralateral to focus), automatisms (lip-smacking, chewing, hand fumbling), or sudden brief myoclonic jerks (especially morning drops in JME).
> - **Post-ictal Phase:** Stertorous breathing, confusion, lethargy, headache, or focal paresis (**Todd's paralysis**, lasting hours, resolving $<24$ hours).
> - **Triggers:** Sleep deprivation, alcohol intake/withdrawal, missed medications, infection/fever, systemic stress.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Post-Event Physical Clues:** Lateral tongue laceration, posterior shoulder dislocation (locked in internal rotation/adduction), vertebral compression fractures.
> - **Neurological Exam:** Focal neurological deficits indicating structural brain pathology (stroke, tumor).
> - **Dermatologic Exam:** Ash-leaf spots, facial angiofibromas (Tuberous Sclerosis); port-wine stain in V1 distribution (Sturge-Weber); café-au-lait macules (NF1).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> **ILAE (2014) Operational Clinical Definition of Epilepsy (Fulfilling ANY 1 of the following 3):**
>
> 1. At least **two unprovoked (or reflex) seizures** occurring **$>24$ hours apart**.
> 2. **One unprovoked (or reflex) seizure** AND a probability of further seizures similar to the general recurrence risk (at least **$\ge 60\%$**) over the next 10 years (e.g., patient with a single seizure + remote structural lesion on brain MRI or epileptiform activity on EEG).
> 3. Diagnosis of an established **Epilepsy Syndrome** (e.g., Childhood Absence Epilepsy, Juvenile Myoclonic Epilepsy).

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Initial (Workup of First Seizure)**
>
> - **Fingerstick Blood Glucose + BMP:** Rule out acute hypoglycemia, hyponatremia, hypocalcemia, hypomagnesemia, and uremia.
> - **Urine Toxicology Screen & Serum Alcohol Level.**
> - **Urine $\beta$-hCG:** Mandatory in all females of childbearing potential.
>
> **Confirmatory / Etiologic Neurodiagnostics**
>
> - **Electroencephalography (EEG):** Routine 30-minute awake and sleep-deprived EEG with activation maneuvers (hyperventilation, photic stimulation) to detect focal or generalized epileptiform discharges (spikes, sharp waves, 3-Hz spike-and-wave in absence). _A normal interictal EEG does NOT rule out epilepsy._
> - **Brain MRI with Epilepsy Protocol:** High-resolution 3D T1, T2, and thin-slice coronal FLAIR sequences angled perpendicular to the long axis of the hippocampus to detect **Mesial Temporal Sclerosis (hippocampal atrophy/hyperintensity)**, **Focal Cortical Dysplasia**, low-grade neoplasms, cavernous malformations, or vascular lesions.
>
> **Long-Term / Diagnostic Uncertainty**
>
> - **Inpatient Video-EEG Monitoring (VEM):** Gold standard to confirm events, differentiate epileptic seizures from Psychogenic Nonepileptic Seizures (PNES), and localize the epileptogenic zone for surgical evaluation.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Seizure duration $\ge 5$ minutes** or failure to regain consciousness between discrete seizures (**Status Epilepticus** $\rightarrow$ immediate emergency resuscitation).
> - New focal neurological deficit or persistent altered mental status $>1–2$ hours post-event.
> - First-ever seizure in an adult $>50$ years old or patient with active cancer (high suspicion for primary brain tumor or metastasis).
> - Fever, meningismus, or immunocompromise (urgent lumbar puncture for meningitis/encephalitis).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Status Epilepticus:** Prolonged continuous seizure activity causing excitotoxic brain injury.
> - **Acute Intracranial Catastrophe:** Intracerebral hemorrhage, subarachnoid hemorrhage, or basilar artery occlusion presenting with convulsive posturing.
> - **Severe Acute Metabolic Derangements:** Hypoglycemia (<40 mg/dL), severe hyponatremia (<120 mEq/L).
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Syncope (Vasovagal, Orthostatic, or Cardiogenic):** Prolonged prodrome (lightheadedness, tunnel vision, diaphoresis), brief myoclonic twitches ("convulsive syncope"), eyes closed during event, immediate recovery of full orientation, tip-of-tongue bite.
> - **Psychogenic Nonepileptic Seizures (PNES):** Eyes tightly closed against resistance, out-of-phase thrashing, pelvic thrusting, side-to-side head shaking, absence of postictal lactic acidosis, normal ictal EEG.
>
> **Less Common**
>
> - **Transient Ischemic Attack (TIA):** Produces negative neurologic symptoms (loss of motor/sensory function) rather than positive paroxysmal symptoms.
> - **REM Sleep Behavior Disorder / Parasomnias.**

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Complete seizure freedom without drug-induced adverse effects; zero breakthrough seizures.
> - **Secondary Target:** Minimize teratogenic risk, prevent seizure-related injury or drowning, address driving restrictions, and prevent **Sudden Unexpected Death in Epilepsy (SUDEP)**.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Manage any active seizure lasting $\ge 5$ minutes as Status Epilepticus** (refer to **[[Status Epilepticus]]**) by administering **IV Lorazepam (4 mg)** or **IM Midazolam (10 mg)** immediately to terminate excitotoxic neuronal injury.
> - **Withhold long-term Antiseizure Medications (ASMs) in the Emergency Department after a single first unprovoked seizure** if the patient has returned to baseline and has a normal neurologic exam, normal non-contrast head CT, and normal laboratory panel (recurrence risk is only 30–40%; outpatient neurology referral for EEG and MRI is standard).
>
> **First-Line Antiseizure Medication (ASM) Monotherapy**
>
> - **Focal Epilepsy (with or without Focal-to-Bilateral Tonic-Clonic Seizures):**
>   - **Initiate Lamotrigine (start 25 mg PO daily, titrating slowly every 2 weeks)** (preferred for tolerability and safety in pregnancy; strict slow titration required to avoid rash).
>   - **Initiate Levetiracetam (500 mg PO BID, titrating to 1,000–1,500 mg BID)** (rapid titration, broad-spectrum, zero hepatic CYP interactions; screen for behavioral/mood side effects).
>   - **Initiate Oxcarbazepine (300 mg PO BID)** or **Lacosamide (100 mg PO BID)** (monitor sodium for hyponatremia with oxcarbazepine).
> - **Generalized Epilepsy (e.g., Juvenile Myoclonic Epilepsy [JME]):**
>   - **Initiate Levetiracetam (500–1,500 mg PO BID)** or **Lamotrigine** as first-line broad-spectrum agents.
>   - **Initiate Valproate (Sodium Valproate / Divalproex: 500–1,500 mg/day PO)** as the most effective agent for generalized epilepsies in **men and postmenopausal women ONLY**.
> - **Childhood Absence Epilepsy:**
>   - **Initiate Ethosuximide (250 mg PO BID)** as the gold-standard first-line therapy (T-type calcium channel blocker; ineffective for tonic-clonic seizures).
>
> **Drug-Resistant (Refractory) Epilepsy & Surgical Evaluation**
>
> - **Refer to a Comprehensive Epilepsy Center for Presurgical Evaluation** when seizures fail to achieve sustained remission after adequate trials of **$\ge 2$ appropriately chosen and tolerated ASMs** (only ~5% of patients achieve seizure freedom with a third medication):
>   - **Resective Epilepsy Surgery (e.g., Anterior Temporal Lobectomy):** Curative in up to 70–80% of patients with medically refractory Mesial Temporal Sclerosis.
>   - **Neuromodulation:** **Vagus Nerve Stimulation (VNS)**, **Responsive Neurostimulation (RNS)**, or **Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS)** for non-resectable or multifocal foci.
>
> **Safety, Counseling, & Lifestyle**
>
> - **Counsel on strict state-mandated Driving Restrictions** (typically requiring **3 to 12 months** of documented seizure freedom before driving resumes).
> - **Enforce safety precautions:** Avoid unsupervised swimming, baths (take showers instead), working at heights, or operating heavy machinery.
> - **Educate on SUDEP prevention:** Adhere strictly to ASMs and avoid alcohol/sleep deprivation (optimizing seizure control is the single most effective way to prevent SUDEP).

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Narrow-Spectrum Sodium Channel Blockers (Carbamazepine, Oxcarbazepine, Phenytoin)** in patients with **Generalized Epilepsies (Absence, Myoclonic, JME)** to prevent paradoxical worsening of seizures and precipitation of status epilepticus.
> - **Avoid Valproate in Women of Childbearing Potential** whenever acceptable alternatives exist to prevent major congenital malformations (**neural tube defects, cardiac anomalies**) and reduced cognitive outcomes/autism in offspring.
> - **Avoid Rapid Dose Escalation of Lamotrigine** (or co-administering with Valproate without cutting the lamotrigine dose by $>50\%$) to prevent life-threatening **Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN)**.
> - **Avoid Starting Long-Term Antiseizure Medications after a First Provoked Seizure** (e.g., alcohol withdrawal or reversible electrolyte disturbances) to prevent unnecessary chronic medication toxicity.
> - **Avoid Abrupt Discontinuation of Antiseizure Medications** to prevent triggering rebound withdrawal seizures and status epilepticus (always taper gradually over weeks to months).

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Sudden Unexpected Death in Epilepsy (SUDEP):** Fatal postictal respiratory depression and autonomic arrest $\rightarrow$ highest risk in uncontrolled generalized tonic-clonic seizures and nocturnal seizures $\rightarrow$ maximize medication adherence to eliminate tonic-clonic seizures.
> - **Posterior Shoulder Dislocation & Fractures:** Violent tonic contraction of the latissimus dorsi and subscapularis during convulsions $\rightarrow$ arm locked in internal rotation and adduction $\rightarrow$ requires emergent orthopedic reduction.
> - **Aspiration Pneumonia:** Aspiration of gastric contents during tonic-clonic seizure or postictal coma $\rightarrow$ position in lateral decubitus position during postictal state.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 22-year-old male college student presents to the ED after a witnessed generalized tonic-clonic seizure that occurred this morning following a 36-hour period of sleep deprivation. His roommate reports the patient frequently dropped his coffee cup in the mornings due to sudden "jerks" of his arms over the past year (**Myoclonic jerks**). Physical exam reveals a lateral tongue bite but normal focal neurology; non-contrast head CT and basic electrolytes are normal. He is diagnosed clinically with **Juvenile Myoclonic Epilepsy (JME)**; narrow-spectrum drugs like carbamazepine are strictly avoided, and he is initiated on broad-spectrum **Levetiracetam (500 mg PO BID)**, counselled on sleep hygiene, and instructed on strict 6-month driving restrictions.
