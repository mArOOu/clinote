> [!abstract] Hydrocephalus
> Pathologic active expansion of the cerebral ventricular system resulting from an imbalance between cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) production (~500 mL/day by choroid plexuses) and absorption (arachnoid villi into the superior sagittal sinus), broadly divided into **Non-Communicating (Obstructive)**, **Communicating**, and **Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH)**.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Non-Communicating (Obstructive):** Physical blockage within the ventricular pathways (**Foramen of Monro, Aqueduct of Sylvius [#1 site], or 4th ventricle outlets of Luschka/Magendie**) prevents CSF from exiting into the subarachnoid space.
> - **Communicating:** Unobstructed ventricular outflow with impaired absorption at the **arachnoid granulations** due to fibrosis/clogging from prior subarachnoid hemorrhage, bacterial meningitis, or carcinomatous meningitis.
> - **Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH):** Chronic communicating hydrocephalus in the elderly with intermittent or normal baseline opening pressures ($<18	ext{ mmHg}$ or $<20	ext{ cmH}_2	ext{O}$); ventricular expansion stretches the **sacral motor fibers of the corona radiata** surrounding the lateral ventricles (explaining early lower-extremity gait and bladder signs!).

> [!tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **NPH Triad ("Wet, Wacky, and Wobbly" in Order of Appearance):**
>   1. **Gait Disturbance ("Wobbly" — FIRST and Most Responsive to Shunting):** Classic **"magnetic gait"** (feet glued to the floor, wide base, short steps, difficulty initiating gait, preserved arm swing).
>   2. **Cognitive Impairment ("Wacky"):** Subcortical psychomotor slowing, apathy, inattention.
>   3. **Urinary Incontinence ("Wet" — LATE):** Frontal urge incontinence due to loss of voluntary detrusor inhibition.
> - **The High-Volume Lumbar Tap Test:** Removal of **30 to 50 mL of CSF via lumbar puncture** with objective, video-timed testing of 10-meter walk speed and stride length before and 30–60 minutes after; positive improvement has a **$>90%$ positive predictive value for long-term VP Shunt success**!
> - **Sunset Eye Sign in Infants:** Forcible downward ocular deviation with the sclera visible above the iris, caused by aqueductal expansion compressing the midbrain tectum (dorsal midbrain / Parinaud syndrome).
> - **ETV vs. VP Shunt (Hardware-Free Cure):** **Endoscopic Third Ventriculostomy (ETV)** fenestrates the floor of the 3rd ventricle into the prepontine cistern; it is the **curative procedure of choice for Aqueductal Stenosis**, completely avoiding permanent foreign shunt hardware!
> - **Shunt Malfunction Rule:** Any patient with a pre-existing Ventriculoperitoneal (VP) Shunt presenting with new headache, lethargy, vomiting, or cognitive decline has **Shunt Malfunction until proven otherwise** $ightarrow$ order an immediate **Shunt Series X-ray and Non-Contrast Head CT**!

| Category | Outflow Pathophysiology | Classic Causes | Primary Treatment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Non-Communicating** (Obstructive) | Physical block INSIDE ventricular system | Aqueductal stenosis, colloid cyst, tumor | Endoscopic Third Ventriculostomy (ETV) or EVD/Shunt |
| **2. Communicating** | CSF exits ventricles but arachnoid absorption impaired | Post-SAH, post-meningitis, choroid papilloma | Ventriculoperitoneal (VP) Shunt |
| **3. Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus** (NPH) | Chronic communicating; normal/intermittent pressure | Idiopathic (age >60); altered compliance | VP Shunt (if tap test positive; improves GAIT first) |

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Pediatric / Congenital:** Rapidly accelerating head circumference crossing growth percentiles, split/splayed cranial sutures, bulging tense non-pulsatile anterior fontanelle, high-pitched "cerebral cry," poor feeding, projectile vomiting, and developmental delay.
> - **Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (Older Adults $ge 60$ years):**
>   - Insidious onset of **gait unsteadiness and frequent falls** (difficulty initiating steps, turning requires multiple small steps).
>   - Daytime urinary frequency and urgency progressing to frank **incontinence**.
>   - Subcortical cognitive slowing (forgetfulness, apathy, psychomotor sluggishness).
> - **Shunt Malfunction / Infection:** Intermittent headache, nausea, neck pain, lethargy, or swelling/redness along the subcutaneous shunt tubing.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Infants:** **Sunset eye sign** (sclera visible above iris), dilated scalp veins, **Macewen sign** ("cracked pot" sound on cranial percussion).
> - **NPH Gait Exam:** **Magnetic, shuffling gait** (feet appear glued to the floor with low step height), broad base, retropulsion on pull test, **preserved arm swing** (distinguishes from Parkinson's disease!).
> - **Reflexes:** Mild lower-extremity hyperreflexia, paratonia (gegenhalten), and frontal release signs (palmomental and grasp reflexes).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Ventricular Enlargement (Hydrocephalus on Imaging):**
>   - **Evans Index $>0.30$** (ratio of the maximum transverse width of the frontal horns to the maximum internal diameter of the skull).
>   - **Bilateral temporal horn width $ge 2	ext{ mm}$**.
>   - **Periventricular transependymal edema** (interstitial CSF leakage).
> - **Probable Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (International NPH Guidelines):**
>   - Age **$ge 60$ years**,
>   - Insidious progression of **gait disturbance** PLUS at least one other domain (cognitive decline or urinary urgency/incontinence),
>   - **Ventriculomegaly (Evans index $>0.30$) disproportionate to cerebral cortical atrophy (DESH Sign: Disproportionately Enlarged Subarachnoid space Hydrocephalus)** with tight high-convexity sulci and enlarged Sylvian fissures, AND
>   - **Normal CSF opening pressure ($<18	ext{ mmHg}$ or $<20–24	ext{ cmH}_2	ext{O}$)** on lumbar puncture.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Initial Neuroimaging**
>
> - **Brain MRI with and without Contrast (Gold Standard for Adults & NPH):**
>   - Assesses ventricular caliber (Evans index $>0.30$), rules out mass lesions, demonstrates **DESH sign** in NPH, and detects the **aqueductal CSF flow void** (prominent jet of low T2 signal in the aqueduct indicating hyperdynamic CSF flow).
> - **Cranial Ultrasound (Pediatric / Neonates):** First-line bedside imaging through the open anterior fontanelle in infants to monitor ventricular size and diagnose **germinal matrix / intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH)**.
>
> **Predictive CSF Drainage Procedures (For NPH)**
>
> - **High-Volume Lumbar Tap Test:** Perform baseline quantitative gait assessment (time and step count for 10-meter walk), perform an LP removing **30 to 50 mL of CSF**, and re-test gait at **30 minutes, 2 hours, and 4 hours** (statistically significant improvement in walking speed or step count predicts high surgical shunt response).
> - **Continuous External Lumbar Drainage (ELD):** Indicated if the tap test is negative or equivocal despite high clinical suspicion; an indwelling lumbar catheter drains **10 mL/hr of CSF continuously for 72 hours** in an inpatient unit (sensitivity $>90–95%$).
>
> **Shunt Malfunction Workup**
>
> - **Shunt Series Plain Radiographs:** X-rays of the skull, neck, chest, and abdomen to trace the entire catheter course to detect **tubing disconnection, fracture, or kinking**.
> - **Shunt Reservoir Aspiration (Shunt Tap):** Performed under sterile conditions to measure opening pressure and aspirate CSF for **Gram stain, culture, and cell count** to evaluate for bacterial ventriculitis.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Acute GCS drop or non-reactive dilated pupil** in an acute hydrocephalus patient or known shunt carrier ($ightarrow$ acute obstructive herniation; requires emergent EVD / shunt revision).
> - **Fever + erythema, tenderness, or swelling along the subcutaneous shunt tract** ($ightarrow$ acute VP shunt infection; requires urgent shunt removal and externalization).
> - **Rapidly enlarging head circumference or tense, bulging fontanelle** in an infant.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Structural & Atrophic Mimics**
>
> - **Hydrocephalus Ex-Vacuo:** Non-pathologic ventricular enlargement secondary to generalized brain parenchymal loss (**Alzheimer's disease, frontotemporal dementia**); characterized by **prominent, widely dilated cerebral cortical sulci matching the ventricular enlargement**, normal opening pressure, and zero response to CSF tap test.
> - **Parkinson's Disease:** Stooped shuffling gait; distinguished by **asymmetric resting tremor, cogwheel rigidity, and masked facies** (NPH lacks a rest tremor and has a wider base).
> - **Subcortical Ischemic Vascular Dementia (Binswanger Disease):** Extensive bilateral periventricular white matter ischemic hyperintensities on T2/FLAIR with lacunar infarcts.
> - **Benign External Hydrocephalus of Infancy:** Enlargement of the frontal subarachnoid spaces with normal ventricles; self-limiting by age 2.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Restore physiological CSF balance, decompress the ventricles, achieve **long-term functional gait improvement in NPH**, and prevent permanent cognitive decline or visual loss.
> - **Secondary Target:** Avoid shunt over-drainage or under-drainage, and minimize hardware-related complications (infection, mechanical obstruction).
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Acute Obstructive Hydrocephalus:** Refer immediately for **Emergent External Ventricular Drain (EVD) Placement** (refer to **[[Acute Hydrocephalus & Raised Intracranial Pressure]]**).
> - **Acute Shunt Infection / Ventriculitis:** **Externalize the infected shunt (remove peritoneal catheter and convert to an EVD)**, administer **Empiric IV Vancomycin (15–20 mg/kg q8–12h) PLUS IV Cefepime or Meropenem** (with or without intrathecal antibiotics), and place a new sterile shunt only after CSF cultures are permanently negative for $ge 7–10$ days.
>
> **Definitive Surgical Management: Non-Communicating (Obstructive) Hydrocephalus**
>
> - **Perform Endoscopic Third Ventriculostomy (ETV) (First-Line Gold Standard):**
>   - Neuroendoscopic rigid scope passed through the right lateral ventricle into the 3rd ventricle to create a blunt 4–6 mm perforation in the thin **tuber cinereum / floor of the third ventricle**, allowing CSF to flow directly into the prepontine basal cisterns (cures **Aqueductal Stenosis** without foreign hardware).
>
> **Definitive Surgical Management: Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH) & Communicating Hydrocephalus**
>
> - **Perform Ventriculoperitoneal (VP) Shunt Placement (First-Line Gold Standard):**
>   - Surgical implantation of a radiopaque catheter in the frontal horn of the lateral ventricle, tunneled subcutaneously to a **programmable differential-pressure valve behind the ear**, connected to distal tubing tunneled into the **peritoneal cavity**.
>   - _Clinical Outcome:_ **Gait improves in $>80–90%$ of patients** with a positive high-volume tap test; urinary incontinence improves in ~50–60%; dementia improves in ~40–50%.
>   - _Alternative Shunt Destinations:_ **Ventriculoatrial (VA) Shunt** (draining into the right atrium) or **Ventriculopleural (VPL) Shunt** (reserved for patients with extensive abdominal adhesions, prior peritonitis, or severe obesity).
>
> **Postoperative Shunt Optimization & Programming**
>
> - **Adjust Programmable Shunt Valves Transcutaneously via Magnetic Programmer:**
>   - _If Under-Draining (Persistent gait ataxia, large ventricles):_ Lower the valve pressure setting to increase CSF drainage.
>   - _If Over-Draining (Low-pressure postural headaches, subdural hygromas):_ Raise the valve pressure setting to decrease CSF drainage.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Performing a Lumbar Puncture in Non-Communicating (Obstructive) Hydrocephalus**, as withdrawing CSF below an intraventricular block triggers rapid, fatal **Brainstem Tonsillar Herniation ("coning")**!
> - **Avoid Shunting Patients with Presumed NPH Who Lack a Gait Disturbance**, as isolated dementia without gait impairment has a very low surgical success rate ($<10–20%$) and usually represents **Alzheimer's disease with hydrocephalus ex-vacuo**.
> - **Avoid Over-Draining CSF via Low-Pressure Shunt Valves**, as excessive siphonage pulls the brain parenchyma away from the skull, tearing bridging veins and precipitating **Subdural Hygromas or Subdural Hematomas** (always use **programmable valves with gravitational anti-siphon devices**).
> - **Avoid Diuretics (Acetazolamide, Furosemide) as Definitive Long-Term Therapy for Hydrocephalus**, as carbonic anhydrase inhibitors only minimally decrease CSF production, fail to halt ventriculomegaly, and cause severe metabolic acidosis and electrolyte wasting.
> - **Avoid Discarding Suspected Shunt Malfunction in Patients with "Normal-Sized" Ventricles on CT**, as a chronically compliant or stiff brain may not dramatically enlarge during acute shunt failure (always compare directly to post-shunting baseline CT!).

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Shunt Infection (~5–10% of cases):** Most commonly caused by skin commensals (**_Staphylococcus epidermidis [#1]_**, _Staphylococcus aureus_, _Cutibacterium acnes_) introduced during surgery $ightarrow$ presents with fever, abdominal pseudocyst, or indolent lethargy $ightarrow$ requires complete shunt removal, externalization, and IV vancomycin.
> - **Subdural Hematoma / Hygroma from Over-Drainage:** Cortical collapse tears bridging veins $ightarrow$ adjust valve to higher resistance; surgical evacuation needed only if large and symptomatic.
> - **Abdominal Pseudocyst:** Distal peritoneal catheter obstruction walled off by omentum $ightarrow$ presents with abdominal pain, distention, or shunt malfunction $ightarrow$ requires laparoscopic relocation of distal catheter to pleural cavity (VA or VPL shunt).
> - **Slit Ventricle Syndrome:** Chronically over-drained, collapsed non-compliant lateral ventricles that intermittently obstruct the catheter tip $ightarrow$ severe episodic low-to-high pressure headaches.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 74-year-old retired accountant presents with an 11-month history of progressive difficulty walking, multiple backward falls, and recent urinary urgency with occasional urge incontinence. His family notes he has become quiet, slow to respond, and apathetic. Physical exam reveals a classic **"magnetic" wide-based shuffling gait** with preserved arm swing and hesitancy on turning, without rigidity or resting tremor. Brain MRI reveals marked ventriculomegaly with an **Evans index of 0.36**, prominent temporal horns, and high-convexity sulcal crowding (**DESH sign**). A **High-Volume Lumbar Tap Test** is performed, removing 40 mL of CSF; 1 hour post-tap, his 10-meter walk time decreases from 24 seconds to 15 seconds (**positive tap test**). He undergoes uncomplicated **Ventriculoperitoneal (VP) Shunt placement with a programmable valve**, resulting in sustained restoration of independent ambulation and resolution of urinary incontinence.
