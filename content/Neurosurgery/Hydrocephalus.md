> [!abstract] Hydrocephalus
> Pathological accumulation of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) within the cerebral ventricular system, causing ventriculomegaly. Classified etiologically into **Communicating (Non-obstructive)** (impaired arachnoid granulation absorption or CSF overproduction) and **Non-Communicating (Obstructive)** (mechanical blockage of ventricular outflow). In older adults, **Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH)** represents a critically underdiagnosed, treatable and reversible cause of dementia, ataxia, and incontinence.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
> - **CSF Dynamics:**
>   - Total adult CSF volume is $\approx 150\text{ mL}$ with a daily production of $\approx 500\text{ mL/day}$ ($0.35\text{ mL/min}$), primarily produced by the choroid plexuses.
>   - Circulation: Lateral ventricles $\rightarrow$ Foramina of Monro $\rightarrow$ Third ventricle $\rightarrow$ Cerebral Aqueduct of Sylvius $\rightarrow$ Fourth ventricle $\rightarrow$ Foramina of Luschka and Magendie $\rightarrow$ Subarachnoid space $\rightarrow$ Resorption into superior sagittal sinus via **arachnoid villi / granulations**.
> - **Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH):**
>   - Reduced compliance of the subarachnoid space and altered perivenous compliance with intermittent elevation of pulse wave amplitudes during REM sleep.
>   - Ventricles enlarge at relatively normal mean resting intracranial pressures ($10–18\text{ cmH}_2\text{O}$), exerting mechanical stretching and shear forces on the periventricular corticospinal tracts destined for the legs, sacral bladder centers, and subcortical frontal-subcortical circuits.
> - **Congenital & Acquired Aqueductal Stenosis:**
>   - Trivehicular hydrocephalus (enlarged lateral and 3rd ventricles with normal/small 4th ventricle) from congenital web/gliosis or post-hemorrhagic/post-meningitic scarring.

> [!tip]- Clinical PEARLS
> - **The Classic Hakim-Adams Triad in NPH (Order of Appearance):**
>   1. **Gait Disturbance (Earliest, most common, and most responsive to shunting):** Magnetic, broad-based, short-stepped, glued-to-the-floor gait.
>   2. **Urinary Incontinence:** Urgency $\rightarrow$ frequency $\rightarrow$ urge incontinence $\rightarrow$ frontal indifference.
>   3. **Cognitive Decline:** Subcortical frontal pattern (psychomotor slowing, apathy, executive dysfunction).
> - **The "Wet, Wobbly, and Wacky" mnemonic:** Incontinence (wet), ataxia (wobbly), cognitive decline (wacky).
> - **The Diagnostic High-Volume Lumbar Tap Test:** Removal of **$30–50\text{ mL}$ of CSF** via LP with objective pre- and post-tap timed gait testing (10-meter walk and timed up-and-go). A $> 20\%$ improvement in gait speed/cadence has a $> 90\%$ positive predictive value for shunt responsiveness.
> - **DESH Sign on MRI:** **D**isproportionately **E**nlarged **S**ubarachnoid-space **H**ydrocephalus (enlarged Sylvian fissures with tight, effaced high-convexity and parasagittal sulci); strong predictor of successful shunt surgery.
> - **Shunt Overdrainage:** Slit ventricles and chronic subdural hygromas/hematomas; managed with programmable gravitational anti-siphon valve adjustments.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH)**
> - **Gait Impairment:** "Magnetic gait" (feet appear stuck to the ground), reduced step height, difficulty initiating steps, broad base, multi-step turns (instability on turning), no arm swing deficit (unlike Parkinson's).
> - **Urinary Symptoms:** Sudden unheralded urge incontinence without warning; patients may exhibit "frontal lack of concern."
> - **Cognitive Profile:** Bradyphrenia (slowed thinking), forgetfulness, lack of initiative; language and praxis are characteristically preserved (unlike Alzheimer's).
> 
> **Chronic Obstructive / Shunt-Dependent Hydrocephalus**
> - Intermittent morning headaches, difficulty with upward gaze (**Parinaud's Syndrome** from pressure on the tectal pretectal area), decline in school/work performance, personality change.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> - **International NPH Guidelines:**
>   - Ventriculomegaly (**Evans Index $> 0.30$**).
>   - Presence of at least 2 components of the Hakim-Adams triad (gait impairment mandatory).
>   - Documented normal opening pressure on lumbar puncture ($< 18–20\text{ cmH}_2\text{O}$ or $< 13–15\text{ mmHg}$).
>   - Absence of other primary neurodegenerative explanations.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> - **Brain MRI with 3D T1, T2, FLAIR, and Flow-Sensitive Phase-Contrast CSF Sequences:**
>   - Evan's Index $> 0.30$, callosal angle $< 90°$ on coronal view.
>   - DESH sign (tight high-convexity sulci with prominent Sylvian fissures).
>   - Flow-void sign in aqueduct of Sylvius on T2 (hyperdynamic CSF jet).
> - **Lumbar Tap Test / 72-Hour Continuous Lumbar Drainage:** Quantitative gait assessment (timed 10m walk test, turn steps) before and after drainage.
> - **CSF Infusion Testing:** Measures CSF outflow resistance ($R_{out} > 12–14\text{ mmHg/mL/min}$ predicts favorable shunt outcome).

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> - Shunt malfunction signs: Headache, vomiting, progressive lethargy, visual obscurations.
> - Fever, abdominal peritonitis signs, or erythema along shunt tubing (shunt infection/bacterial colonization).
> - Postural headache worsening strictly upright and improving flat (shunt overdrainage with intracranial hypotension).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> - Parkinson's Disease and Atypical Parkinsonism (PSP, MSA; presence of resting tremor, rigidity, falls, poor response to levodopa).
> - Alzheimer's Disease (early prominent episodic amnesia, anomia, cortical atrophy without DESH sign).
> - Vascular Dementia (extensive subcortical white matter ischemic leukoaraiosis, step-wise decline).
> - Cervical Spondylotic Myelopathy (spastic gait, hyperreflexia, Babinski sign).

> [!summary]- Management
> **1. Surgical Diversion Procedures**
> - **Ventriculoperitoneal (VP) Shunt (Standard of Care):**
>   - Catheter placed into lateral ventricle via frontal (Kocher's point) or occipital (Keen's point) burr hole, tunneled subcutaneously to the peritoneal cavity.
>   - **Programmable Magnetic Valves $\pm$ Gravitational Anti-Siphon Devices:** Allow non-invasive transcutaneous pressure adjustments in the clinic to optimize drainage and prevent overdrainage.
> - **Ventriculoatrial (VA) or Ventriculopleural (VPL) Shunts:** Alternative targets when the peritoneum is unusable (extensive prior abdominal surgery, peritonitis).
> - **Endoscopic Third Ventriculostomy (ETV):** Procedure of choice for **Obstructive Hydrocephalus** (triventricular hydrocephalus from aqueductal stenosis). Avoids permanent implanted foreign hardware.
> 
> **2. Postoperative Surveillance & Valve Titration**
> - Program valve setting based on symptoms; if gait remains unimproved and ventricles remain dilated, decrease valve pressure setting incrementally.
> - If orthostatic headaches or subdural hygromas develop, increase valve opening pressure.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
> - **Do NOT delay shunting in verified NPH:** Chronic axonal stretch becomes permanent; early shunting produces $> 80\%$ improvement in gait, whereas longstanding disease yields poor recovery.
> - **Never perform uncalibrated ETV in communicating NPH:** ETV fails in communicating hydrocephalus because arachnoid granulations are defective.

> [!error]- Complications
> - Shunt Infection (Staph epidermidis, Staph aureus; requires complete hardware removal, temporary EVD, and targeted IV antibiotics).
> - Shunt Malfunction / Occlusion (proximal choroid plexus plugging or distal peritoneal pseudocyst).
> - Subdural hematoma or hygroma secondary to overdrainage.
> - Abdominal complications (peritoneal pseudocyst, bowel perforation).

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 73-year-old retired accountant is evaluated for an 8-month history of progressive walking difficulty, described by his wife as "his feet being stuck with magnets to the floor." Over the past 3 months, he has developed urinary urgency with frequent urge accidents and mild apathy with psychomotor slowing. Brain MRI demonstrates marked symmetric ventriculomegaly with an Evans index of 0.37, an acute callosal angle of 72°, prominent Sylvian fissures, and effaced high-convexity sulci (DESH pattern). Lumbar puncture opening pressure is 13 cmH2O; 45 mL of CSF is removed. On a standardized 10-meter walk test, his baseline time of 26 seconds with 32 steps improves at 4 hours post-tap to 17 seconds with 22 steps. A diagnosis of idiopathic NPH is confirmed. He undergoes uneventful placement of a right frontal ventriculoperitoneal shunt with an adjustable gravitational valve. At his 3-month follow-up, his gait has returned to near-normal speed, his urinary incontinence has fully resolved, and his mental sharpness has significantly improved.
