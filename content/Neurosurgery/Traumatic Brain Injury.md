> [!abstract] Traumatic Brain Injury
> Biomechanical disruption of normal brain function resulting from blunt acceleration/deceleration forces, penetrating impacts, or blast waves. Stratified by Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) into **Mild** (GCS 13–15), **Moderate** (GCS 9–12), and **Severe** (GCS 3–8 requiring immediate endotracheal intubation). The fundamental management priority is preventing secondary ischemic brain insults (hypoxia and hypotension).

> [!note] Pathophysiology
> - **Primary Brain Injury:** Immediate mechanical tissue deformation occurring at impact, including cortical contusions, lacerations, skull fractures, and shear-strain **Diffuse Axonal Injury (DAI)** at gray-white matter junctions.
> - **Secondary Brain Injury:** Cascade of progressive cellular damage triggered within hours-days by systemic hypotension (MAP drop), arterial hypoxemia, excitotoxicity (excess glutamate release), intracellular calcium overload, free radical generation, mitochondrial collapse, cytotoxic/vasogenic cerebral edema, and elevated intracranial pressure (ICP) causing critical cerebral hypoperfusion ($\text{CPP} = \text{MAP} - \text{ICP}$).

> [!tip]- Clinical PEARLS
> - **"A single episode of hypotension doubles mortality":** In severe TBI, a single drop of SBP $< 90\text{ mmHg}$ (or $< 100–110\text{ mmHg}$ in patients aged 50–69 or $\ge 70$) is independently associated with a 100% increase in mortality (Brain Trauma Foundation guidelines).
> - **Beware the "Lucid Interval":** Classically seen in epidural hematomas (brief concussion $\rightarrow$ complete recovery $\rightarrow$ rapid deterioration with ipsilateral dilated pupil and contralateral hemiparesis due to uncal herniation).
> - **Never hyperventilate prophylactically:** Extreme hyperventilation ($\text{PaCO}_2 < 30\text{ mmHg}$) causes profound cerebral vasoconstriction and worsens focal cerebral ischemia; reserve brief hyperventilation solely for acute impending brain herniation while preparing osmotherapy.
> - **Target CPP 60–70 mmHg:** CPP $< 60\text{ mmHg}$ causes ischemic necrosis; CPP $> 70\text{ mmHg}$ risks hyperemic vasogenic edema and ARDS from excessive vasopressor exposure.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **Initial Assessment & History**
> - Mechanism of trauma (motor vehicle collision, fall from height, assault, blast), presence and duration of loss of consciousness (LOC), post-traumatic amnesia (PTA), seizure at impact, vomiting frequency.
> - Anticoagulant or antiplatelet therapy use (mandatory high-risk factor requiring emergent reversal).
> 
> **Physical Examination**
> - **Full GCS Assessment:** Eye (1–4), Verbal (1–5), Motor (1–6). Record the best motor response.
> - **Pupillary Examination:** Size, symmetry, light reactivity. Unilateral fixed dilated pupil indicates impending uncal (transtentorial) herniation compressing CN III.
> - **Basilar Skull Fracture Signs:** Battle sign (mastoid ecchymosis), Raccoon eyes (periorbital ecchymosis), hemotympanum, CSF otorrhea / rhinorrhea (ring/halo sign on gauze).
> - **Cushing's Triad (Late sign of decompensated ICP):** Hypertension (widened pulse pressure), bradycardia, and irregular/Cheyne-Stokes respirations.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> - **Canadian CT Head Rule (High-risk criteria for neurosurgical intervention in Mild TBI):**
>   1. GCS $< 15$ at 2 hours post-injury.
>   2. Suspected open or depressed skull fracture.
>   3. Any sign of basal skull fracture.
>   4. $\ge 2$ episodes of vomiting.
>   5. Age $\ge 65$ years.
>   6. Amnesia before impact $> 30\text{ minutes}$.
>   7. Dangerous mechanism (pedestrian struck, ejected from vehicle, fall $> 3\text{ feet}$ or 5 stairs).
> - **Marshall CT Classification:** Predicts ICP elevation and mortality based on midline shift, status of mesencephalic perimesencephalic cisterns, and presence of high- or mixed-density lesions $> 25\text{ cm}^3$.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> - **Non-Contrast Head CT (First-line Gold Standard):** Rapidly excludes surgical hematomas, contusions, pneumocephalus, cerebral edema, and midline shift.
> - **CT Cervical Spine:** Mandatory concurrent imaging in all moderate-to-severe blunt head trauma (log-roll and collar precautions until cleared).
> - **Brain MRI (T2* / GRE / SWI):** Modality of choice subacutely for identifying microhemorrhages and punctate shear lesions of **Diffuse Axonal Injury (DAI)** when clinical deficit is out of proportion to CT findings.
> - **Laboratory Workup:** CBC, PT/INR, PTT, fibrinogen, thromboelastography (TEG/ROTEM), blood ethanol, urine toxicology, baseline blood glucose, and electrolytes.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> - Unilateral or bilateral fixed and dilated pupil(s).
> - Rapid drop in GCS by $\ge 2$ points.
> - Progressive asymmetric motor posturing (decorticate or decerebrate).
> - Cushing's response (hypertension + bradycardia).
> - Acute coagulopathy in a patient taking direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) or warfarin.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening Traumatic Complications**
> - Acute Epidural Hematoma (middle meningeal artery rupture).
> - Acute Subdural Hematoma (bridging vein rupture, high parenchymal shear).
> - Traumatic Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (tSAH).
> - Diffuse Axonal Injury (DAI) with cerebral edema.
> 
> **Medical Precipitants of Trauma**
> - Hypoglycemia, alcohol/drug intoxication, syncope, acute ischemic stroke, or post-ictal state causing a secondary fall.

> [!summary]- Management
> **1. Resuscitation & Intensive Care Stabilization (ABCDE)**
> - **Airway/Breathing:** Rapid Sequence Intubation (RSI) if GCS $\le 8$ or loss of protective reflexes; maintain $\text{SpO}_2 \ge 95\%$ and normocapnia ($\text{PaCO}_2\ 35–40\text{ mmHg}$).
> - **Circulation:** Euvolemic isotonic resuscitation (0.9% NaCl or Plasmalyte). Maintain $\text{SBP} \ge 100\text{ mmHg}$ (ages 50–69) or $\ge 110\text{ mmHg}$ (ages 15–49 or $\ge 70$). Use Norepinephrine if vasopressors needed.
> - **Positioning:** Head of bed elevated to 30° with midline head positioning (prevents jugular venous compression).
> 
> **2. Medical ICP Control (Tiered BTF Protocol)**
> - **Tier 1:** Analgosedation (propofol/fentanyl), mild CSF drainage via external ventricular drain (EVD) if in place.
> - **Tier 2:** Osmotherapy: Hypertonic Saline (3% NaCl $250\text{ mL}$ IV bolus, target serum sodium 145–155 mEq/L) or Mannitol 20% (0.5–1.0 g/kg IV, ensure serum osmolality $< 320\text{ mOsm/kg}$).
> - **Tier 3:** High-dose barbiturates (pentobarbital coma), neuromuscular blockade, moderate therapeutic hypothermia (32–35°C), or surgical decompressive craniectomy.
> - **Seizure Prophylaxis:** Levetiracetam (Keppra $1000\text{ mg}$ IV loading, then $500–1000\text{ mg}$ q12h for 7 days) to reduce early post-traumatic seizures.
> 
> **3. Surgical Intervention**
> - Emergent decompressive craniotomy/craniectomy for mass lesions with significant mass effect (midline shift $> 5\text{ mm}$, hematoma volume $> 30\text{ mL}$, or refractory intracranial hypertension $\text{ICP} > 22\text{ mmHg}$).

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
> - **NEVER give systemic corticosteroids:** The landmark CRASH trial proved methylprednisolone significantly *increases* mortality in TBI.
> - **Avoid hypotonic fluids (D5W, 0.45% NaCl):** Rapidly worsens cerebral edema and accelerates uncal herniation.
> - **Do not neglect secondary hypoxia/hypotension:** Transport and imaging must never delay resuscitation.

> [!error]- Complications
> - Transtentorial (uncal), subfalcine, or tonsillar herniation.
> - Post-traumatic epilepsy (early vs late).
> - Post-concussive syndrome (headache, dizziness, sleep disruption, cognitive impairment).
> - Chronic Traumatic Encephalopathy (CTE) after repetitive subconcussive blows.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 24-year-old unhelmeted motorcyclist is brought to the ED following a high-speed collision. Initial GCS is 7 (E1V2M4). Endotracheal intubation is performed with in-line cervical stabilization. SBP is initially 82/50 mmHg; rapid infusion of 2L balanced crystalloids and norepinephrine titration restores SBP to 115 mmHg. Non-contrast head CT reveals a right temporal bone fracture, an overlying biconvex hyperdense collection of 45 mL with 7 mm midline shift, and effacement of basal cisterns. The neurosurgeon is immediately mobilized for urgent right decompressive craniotomy and hematoma evacuation, while 250 mL of 3% hypertonic saline is administered preoperatively. Postoperatively, ICP normalizes to 12 mmHg and the patient is successfully extubated on post-op day 4.
