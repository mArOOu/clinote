> [!abstract] Traumatic Brain Injury
> Acute structural brain damage or physiological disruption of cerebral function resulting from an external mechanical force (blunt, penetrating, blast, or rotational acceleration/deceleration), initiating a dual-phase process of immediate **Primary Injury** (contusion, laceration, axonal shear, hemorrhage) followed by an evolving cascade of **Secondary Injury** (hypoperfusion, ischemia, excitotoxicity, edema, raised ICP).

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Primary vs. Secondary Injury:** Primary mechanical injury occurs at $T=0$ and is irreversible; **Secondary injury evolves over hours to days** driven by mitochondrial failure, glutamate excitotoxicity, free-radical generation, and blood-brain barrier breakdown.
> - **The Deadly "H's" of Secondary Insult:** Secondary brain death is exponentially accelerated by systemic **Hypotension (SBP <100–110 mmHg doubles mortality!)**, **Hypoxia ($	ext{PaO}_2 <60	ext{ mmHg}$ doubles mortality!)**, **Hyperthermia** (increases $	ext{CMRO}_2$), and **Hypocarbia / Hypercarbia**.

> [!tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Blood Pressure Floor Targets (Brain Trauma Foundation 4th Edition):**
>   - Maintain **$	ext{SBP} ge 100	ext{ mmHg}$ for patients aged 50–69 years**.
>   - Maintain **$	ext{SBP} ge 110	ext{ mmHg}$ for patients aged 15–49 or $ge 70$ years**.
> - **Pupillary Asymmetry is the Strongest Bedside Predictor:** A unilateral fixed, dilated pupil ($>1	ext{ mm}$ asymmetry) indicates **uncal transtentorial herniation compressing the ipsilateral CN III** until proven otherwise $ightarrow$ emergent hyperosmolar therapy + immediate surgical decompression!
> - **CRASH-3 Trial Rule (Tranexamic Acid):** Administer **IV Tranexamic Acid (TXA: 1 g IV bolus over 10 min, then 1 g over 8 hours)** within **$<3$ hours of injury** in **Mild-to-Moderate TBI (GCS 9–15)** to reduce head injury mortality (do not use in severe GCS 3–8 as benefit is unproven).
> - **Never Give Steroids in TBI (The CRASH Trial):** High-dose corticosteroids (e.g., Methylprednisolone) **significantly increase mortality** in acute TBI; steroids are strictly contraindicated!
> - **Stop Seizure Prophylaxis at Day 7:** Prophylactic **Levetiracetam (Keppra)** reduces _early_ post-traumatic seizures (first 7 days); it has zero effect on late post-traumatic epilepsy. Discontinue at Day 7!

| Category | Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) | Loss of Consciousness (LOC) | Post-Traumatic Amnesia (PTA) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Mild TBI** (Concussion) | GCS 13–15 (measured 30 min post-injury) | <30 minutes (or none) | <24 hours |
| **2. Moderate TBI** | GCS 9–12 | 30 minutes to 24 hours | 1 to 7 days |
| **3. Severe TBI** | GCS 3–8 ("Less than 8, intubate!") | >24 hours | >7 days |

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Mechanism of Trauma:** High-speed MVC, ejection, pedestrian struck, fall from height, ground-level fall on anticoagulants/antiplatelets, direct blunt assault, or blast wave.
> - **Acute Post-Traumatic Symptoms:** Loss of consciousness, post-traumatic amnesia (retrograde and anterograde), severe headache, repetitive vomiting, dizziness, photophobia.
> - **Baseline Risk Modifiers:** Age $ge 65$, **active anticoagulation (DOACs, Warfarin) or antiplatelet therapy**, bleeding diathesis, or active alcohol/substance intoxication.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Primary Survey (ATLS):** Assess ABCDEs; assign formal **Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS: 3 to 15)** before sedation/intubation.
> - **Pupillary Examination:** Size, symmetry, and direct/consensual light reactivity:
>   - _Unilateral fixed, dilated pupil:_ Uncal herniation (ipsilateral CN III compression).
>   - _Bilateral pinpoint reactive pupils:_ Pontine injury or opioid co-ingestion.
>   - _Bilateral fixed, dilated pupils:_ Severe midbrain ischemia, terminal brainstem herniation, or massive hypoxemia/hypothermia.
> - **Signs of Basilar Skull Fracture:** **Battle sign** (mastoid ecchymosis), **Raccoon eyes** (bilateral periorbital ecchymosis), hemotympanum, CSF rhinorrhea/otorrhea.
> - **Signs of Impending Herniation (Cushing Triad):** Widened pulse pressure hypertension + bradycardia + irregular/apneic respirations.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Mild Traumatic Brain Injury (Concussion):** Acute blunt head trauma resulting in:
>   - **GCS score of 13 to 15** at 30 minutes post-injury,
>   - **Loss of consciousness $<30	ext{ minutes}$** (if present),
>   - **Post-traumatic amnesia $<24	ext{ hours}$**, AND
>   - Transient alteration in mental status (confusion, disorientation, dazed feeling).
> - **Moderate-to-Severe TBI:** Acute blunt or penetrating trauma resulting in a **GCS of 9 to 12 (Moderate)** or **GCS of 3 to 8 (Severe)**, confirmed by structural intracranial pathology on neuroimaging.
> - **Canadian CT Head Rule (Mandatory Non-Contrast Head CT for Mild TBI if ANY criteria met):**
>   - _High Risk for Neurosurgical Intervention:_ GCS $<15$ at 2 hours post-injury; suspected open or depressed skull fracture; any sign of basilar skull fracture; $ge 2$ episodes of vomiting; age $ge 65$ years.
>   - _Medium Risk for Brain Injury on CT:_ Retrograde amnesia $>30$ minutes; dangerous mechanism (pedestrian struck, ejected from vehicle, fall $>3$ feet or 5 stairs).

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Initial / Gold-Standard Neuroimaging**
>
> - **Non-Contrast Head CT (NCCT):**
>   - **Mandatory first-line imaging test** for all moderate and severe TBI, and mild TBI meeting decision rules (Canadian CT Head Rule / New Orleans Criteria).
>   - Staged using the **Marshall or Rotterdam CT Score** (evaluates compression of basal cisterns, midline shift $ge 5	ext{ mm}$, and presence of mass lesions).
> - **CT Cervical Spine (Thin-Slice with Reconstructions):** Mandatory alongside Head CT (up to 10% of severe TBI patients harbor an associated **cervical spine fracture**!).
> - **CT Angiography (CTA) of the Head and Neck:** Mandatory for suspected **Blunt Cerebrovascular Injury (BCVI — Denver Criteria)**: cervical spine fractures involving the transverse foramen, skull base fractures crossing the carotid canal, petrous bone fracture, or soft-tissue neck seatbelt sign.
>
> **Invasive Neuro-Monitoring (Severe TBI — GCS 3–8)**
>
> - **Intracranial Pressure (ICP) Monitoring (External Ventricular Drain [EVD] or Intraparenchymal Bolt):**
>   - **Indications:** All salvageable patients with **Severe TBI (GCS 3–8) and an abnormal Head CT** (hematoma, contusion, swelling, compressed cisterns), OR
>   - Severe TBI with a _normal_ Head CT if **$ge 2$ of the following are present**: (1) Age $>40$ years, (2) Unilateral/bilateral motor posturing, or (3) Systolic BP $<90	ext{ mmHg}$.
> - **Brain Tissue Oxygenation Monitor ($	ext{PbtO}_2$ Catheter):** Targeted to maintain $	ext{PbtO}_2 ge 20	ext{ mmHg}$.
>
> **STAT Laboratory Panel**
>
> - Coagulation panel (PT/INR, aPTT), Platelet count, Fibrinogen, Type and Screen (4 units pRBCs), Arterial Blood Gas, Blood Alcohol, Urine Toxicology, Fingerstick Glucose.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Glasgow Coma Scale $le 8$** (severe TBI; requires immediate endotracheal intubation).
> - **Acute drop in GCS by $ge 2$ points** or loss of a previously reactive pupil.
> - **Hypotension (SBP $<100–110	ext{ mmHg}$)** or **Hypoxia ($	ext{SpO}_2 <90%$ or $	ext{PaO}_2 <60	ext{ mmHg}$)**.
> - **Midline shift $ge 5	ext{ mm}$** or complete obliteration of the mesencephalic basal cisterns.
> - Head trauma in a patient taking **Warfarin, DOACs (Apixaban, Rivaroxaban), or Clopidogrel**.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Secondary & Non-Traumatic Encephalopathies**
>
> - **Severe Acute Intoxication (Ethanol, Opioids, Sedatives):** Frequently co-exists with TBI; masks the true GCS (never assume an abnormal GCS is purely due to alcohol until structural TBI is ruled out with a Head CT!).
> - **Hypoglycemia:** Check point-of-care glucose immediately; mimics severe TBI coma and focal deficits.
> - **Postictal State / Nonconvulsive Status Epilepticus (NCSE):** Seizure triggered by trauma or spontaneous seizure causing secondary trauma.
> - **Spontaneous Aneurysmal SAH or Hypertensive ICH:** Primary intracranial vascular rupture causing a fall or motor vehicle collision.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals (Brain Trauma Foundation Guidelines)**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Maintain **ICP $<22	ext{ mmHg}$**, maintain **Cerebral Perfusion Pressure ($	ext{CPP} = 	ext{MAP} - 	ext{ICP}$) between 60 and 70 mmHg**, maintain **$	ext{SBP} ge 100	ext{ mmHg}$ (ages 50–69) or $ge 110	ext{ mmHg}$ (ages 15–49 or $ge 70$)**, and maintain **$	ext{PaO}_2 ge 100	ext{ mmHg}$** and **$	ext{PaCO}_2	ext{ 35–40 mmHg}$**.
> - **Secondary Target:** Halt coagulopathy within 1 hour, eliminate early post-traumatic seizures, prevent secondary brain edema, and avoid hyperthermia.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (The Golden Hour of Resuscitation)**
>
> - **1. Secure the Airway & Maintain In-Line C-Spine Stabilization:**
>   - **Perform Rapid Sequence Intubation if GCS $le 8$**, rapidly declining consciousness, or hypoxemia, utilizing **Manual In-Line Stabilization (MILS)** (prefer **Etomidate 0.3 mg/kg IV + Rocuronium 1.2 mg/kg IV**; avoid blind neck extension).
> - **2. Resuscitate Blood Pressure Aggressively:**
>   - **Infuse 0.9% Normal Saline** (avoid Lactated Ringer's or hypotonic fluids) and initiate **Norepinephrine** early to maintain SBP $ge 100–110	ext{ mmHg}$ and **MAP $ge 80–90	ext{ mmHg}$**.
> - **3. Administer IV Tranexamic Acid (TXA: 1 g IV bolus over 10 min, then 1 g over 8 hours):**
>   - Infuse within **$<3$ hours of injury** in **Mild-to-Moderate TBI (GCS 9–15)** with intracranial bleeding on CT.
> - **4. Reverse Coagulopathy STAT (Within <60 Minutes):**
>   - **Warfarin:** Administer **4-Factor PCC (25–50 units/kg IV) PLUS 10 mg IV Vitamin K**.
>   - **DOACs (Apixaban, Rivaroxaban):** Administer **Andexanet alfa** (or **4F-PCC 50 units/kg IV**).
>   - **Dabigatran:** Administer **Idarucizumab (5 g IV)**.
>
> **Tiered Protocol for Intracranial Hypertension (ICP >22 mmHg or Acute Herniation)**
>
> - **Tier 1 (Base Neurocritical Protocol):**
>   - Elevate Head of Bed to **30°** with the neck in midline neutral alignment.
>   - Provide continuous **sedation and analgesia (Propofol + Fentanyl)** to prevent coughing, agitation, and fighting the ventilator.
>   - Maintain strict **Normothermia (target core temp 36.0–37.0°C)** using surface cooling (treat fever aggressively with acetaminophen/cooling blankets).
>   - Target strict **euglycemia (140–180 mg/dL)**.
> - **Tier 2 (Hyperosmolar Therapy & CSF Venting):**
>   - **Administer Hypertonic Saline (3% NaCl bolus 250 mL IV over 15 min, or 23.4% NaCl 30 mL via central line)** as preferred first-line therapy (target serum sodium **145–155 mEq/L**; target osmolality $<360	ext{ mOsm/kg}$).
>   - **Administer IV Mannitol (0.5 to 1.0 g/kg IV bolus)** as an alternative in euvolemic patients (target serum osmolality $<320	ext{ mOsm/kg}$).
>   - **Open the External Ventricular Drain (EVD)** to vent CSF intermittently or continuously.
> - **Tier 3 (Refractory Elevated ICP):**
>   - **Temporary Hyperventilation ($	ext{PaCO}_2	ext{ 30–35 mmHg}$):** Utilize **strictly as a temporary bridge for <15–30 minutes** during acute uncal herniation while transporting to the operating room.
>   - **Perform Emergent Decompressive Craniectomy** (large frontotemporoparietal bone flap $ge 12	imes 15	ext{ cm}$) or induce **High-Dose Barbiturate Coma (Pentobarbital)**.
>
> **Seizure Prophylaxis Protocol**
>
> - **Initiate IV Levetiracetam (Keppra: 500 to 1,000 mg IV BID) or Fosphenytoin for 7 DAYS ONLY** in all moderate-to-severe TBI patients to prevent **early post-traumatic seizures** (discontinue after 7 days if no clinical seizures occur).
>
> **Neurosurgical Operative Evacuation Indications**
>
> - Refer to **[[Traumatic Intracranial Hemorrhage]]**:
>   - **Epidural Hematoma:** Volume **$>30	ext{ mL}$** regardless of GCS, or GCS $<9$ with anisocoria.
>   - **Subdural Hematoma:** Thickness **$>10	ext{ mm}$** or midline shift **$>5	ext{ mm}$**.
>   - **Depressed Skull Fracture:** Depressed greater than the thickness of the skull, open/contaminated, or dural tear.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Systemic Corticosteroids (Methylprednisolone, Dexamethasone) in Acute TBI**, as the landmark multicenter **CRASH trial** proved corticosteroids significantly **increase mortality**!
> - **Avoid Hypotension ($	ext{SBP} <100–110	ext{ mmHg}$) and Hypoxia ($	ext{PaO}_2 <60	ext{ mmHg}$)** at all costs; a single episode of hypotension independently doubles mortality in TBI.
> - **Avoid Hypotonic IV Fluids (0.45% Saline, D5W, and Lactated Ringer's [mildly hypotonic])**, as free water rapidly crosses into damaged cerebral tissue, driving catastrophic **cerebral edema and fatal herniation** (use **0.9% Normal Saline** exclusively).
> - **Avoid Routine Prophylactic Hyperventilation ($	ext{PaCO}_2 <30	ext{ mmHg}$)** during the first 24–48 hours, as intense hypocapnic vasoconstriction causes **severe secondary ischemic cerebral infarction**.
> - **Avoid Continuing Antiseizure Medications (ASMs) Beyond 7 Days** in the absence of documented seizures, as prolonged ASMs do not prevent late post-traumatic epilepsy and impair cognitive rehabilitation.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Brainstem Herniation Syndromes (Uncal, Central, Tonsillar):** Refractory ICP exceeding perfusion pressure $ightarrow$ midbrain compression and brain death.
> - **Paroxysmal Sympathetic Hyperactivity (PSH / "Neuro-Storming"):** Diencephalic-brainstem disinhibition $ightarrow$ paroxysmal episodes of tachycardia, hypertension, tachypnea, hyperpyrexia, profuse diaphoresis, and dystonic extensor posturing triggered by minimal stimuli $ightarrow$ treated with **Propranolol, Clonidine, Gabapentin, and Bromocriptine**.
> - **Diabetes Insipidus (DI) & SIADH / Cerebral Salt Wasting:** Pituitary stalk shearing $ightarrow$ central DI (polyuria $>4	ext{–}5	ext{ L/day}$, hypernatremia, low urine osmolality $ightarrow$ treat with **Desmopressin [DDAVP]**).
> - **Post-Concussion Syndrome & Chronic Traumatic Encephalopathy (CTE):** Chronic headaches, cognitive impairment, depression, and executive dysfunction following mild repetitive TBI.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 24-year-old male unrestrained driver is brought to the trauma bay following a high-speed rollover motor vehicle collision. On arrival, he is comatose with a **GCS of 6** (E1, V2, M3: decorticate posturing), vitals reveal BP 86/48 mmHg and HR 118 bpm, and his **right pupil is 6 mm and unreactive** while the left pupil is 3 mm and reactive (**Severe TBI with Uncal Herniation**). Rapid sequence intubation is performed using etomidate and rocuronium with manual in-line stabilization, aggressive resuscitation with **0.9% Normal Saline + Norepinephrine** restores SBP to 115 mmHg, a bolus of **23.4% Hypertonic Saline (30 mL IV)** is pushed, and **IV Levetiracetam (1,000 mg)** is administered. Non-contrast Head CT reveals an **acute right subdural hematoma measuring 14 mm in thickness with 8 mm of midline shift and effacement of the basal cisterns**; he is rushed directly to the neurosurgical OR for an **emergent right decompressive craniectomy and hematoma evacuation**, followed by Neuro-ICU protocol targeting ICP $<22	ext{ mmHg}$ and CPP $60–70	ext{ mmHg}$.
