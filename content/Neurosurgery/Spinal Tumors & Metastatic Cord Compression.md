> [!abstract] Spinal Tumors & Metastatic Cord Compression
> Neoplastic involvement of the spinal column and cord, categorized by anatomical compartment into **Extradural (95% — overwhelmingly Metastatic Epidural Spinal Cord Compression [MESCC])**, **Intradural-Extramedullary (4% — Schwannoma, Meningioma)**, and **Intramedullary (1% — Ependymoma, Astrocytoma)**, carrying a high risk of permanent paraplegia without rapid intervention.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Epidural Venous Congestion:** Hematogenous metastasis to the vertebral body (via Batson's valveless venous plexus) expands into the epidural space $ightarrow$ compresses the thin-walled epidural venous plexus $ightarrow$ vasogenic spinal cord edema, microvascular ischemia, and axonal infarction.
> - **The "Lead" Malignancies (#1 Cause of Extradural MESCC):** **Prostate (~25%) > Breast (~20%) > Lung (~15%) > Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma / Multiple Myeloma (~10%) > Renal Cell Carcinoma (~10%)**.

> [!tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **The Patchell Trial Landmark (Surgery + Radiation > Radiation Alone):** Randomized trial evidence proved that **direct surgical circumferential decompression + instrumented stabilization followed by radiation** is significantly superior to radiation alone for MESCC with spinal instability or motor deficit (84% vs. 57% regained/retained walking ability, with doubled median survival!).
> - **The "Whole-Spine" MRI Rule:** Up to **$>20–30%$ of patients with metastatic cord compression harbor non-contiguous "skip" spinal metastases**; scanning only the lumbar spine is medical negligence—always order a **Whole-Spine MRI (Cervical, Thoracic, and Lumbar)**!
> - **Dexamethasone Dosing:** Administer **IV Dexamethasone (10 to 16 mg IV bolus, then 4 mg IV/PO q6h)** immediately upon clinical suspicion before MRI; pre-treatment neurological status is the single greatest predictor of whether the patient will walk again.
> - **Spinal Instability Neoplastic Score (SINS):** Classifies spinal mechanical stability from 0 to 18 points (Location, Pain, Bone lesion type, Spinal alignment, Vertebral body collapse, Posterolateral element involvement):
>   - **Score 0–6 = Stable.**
>   - **Score 7–12 = Potentially Unstable (Mandatory Spine Surgery Consult!).**
>   - **Score 13–18 = Unstable (Requires Surgical Instrumentation/Stabilization!).**
> - **Radioresistant vs. Radiosensitive Tumors:**
>   - _Radioresistant (Need Upfront Surgery or SBRT):_ **Renal Cell Carcinoma, Melanoma, Colorectal, Thyroid**.
>   - _Radiosensitive (Respond to Radiotherapy alone):_ **Multiple Myeloma, Lymphoma, Small Cell Lung Cancer, Seminoma**.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Localized Spine Pain (>90% of cases, Earliest Symptom):** Constant, progressive, gnawing back pain that is **worse at night and worsened by lying recumbent** (due to distention of Batson's venous plexus when supine; distinguishes malignant pain from benign mechanical back pain, which improves when lying down!).
> - **Radicular Pain:** Sharp, band-like shooting pain radiating across the chest/abdomen (thoracic) or down an extremity (cervical/lumbar), worsened by coughing or straining.
> - **Motor Dysfunction (Late Sign):** Sensation of "heavy legs," stiffness, stumbling, dragging toes, or difficulty climbing stairs; progresses from weakness to complete paraplegia.
> - **Sensory Loss:** Numbness, paresthesias, or cold sensations ascending up the legs to a **discrete dermatomal trunk level**.
> - **Sphincter Dysfunction (Late, Ominous Sign):** Urinary hesitancy, difficulty starting urination, progressing to **painless urinary retention and overflow incontinence**; loss of anal sensation or fecal incontinence.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Spine Exam:** Exquisite, focal percussion tenderness over the involved spinous process.
> - **Sensory Mapping:** Discrete **sensory level** to pinprick (T4 = nipples; T10 = umbilicus).
> - **Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) Signs:** Hyperactive patellar/Achilles reflexes below the lesion, clonus, and positive **extensor plantar response (Babinski sign)**.
> - **Anorectal Exam:** Lax anal sphincter tone and an elevated **post-void residual ($>100–200	ext{ mL}$)** on bladder ultrasound.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Metastatic Epidural Spinal Cord Compression (MESCC):** Radiologic demonstration on **Whole-Spine MRI** of an epidural tumor causing direct displacement or compression of the spinal cord (Bilsky Grade 2 [cord compression with visible CSF] or Grade 3 [cord compression with complete obliteration of CSF space]).
> - **Primary Intradural Spine Tumor:** Demonstration on MRI of an intradural-extramedullary lesion (schwannoma/meningioma) or intramedullary cord-expanding lesion (ependymoma/astrocytoma), confirmed definitively by **histopathological examination**.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Initial / Gold-Standard Neuroimaging**
>
> - **Emergent Whole-Spine MRI with and without IV Gadolinium (Cervical, Thoracic, Lumbar):**
>   - **Mandatory test of choice** (Sensitivity 95%, Specificity 95%).
>   - _T1-Weighted:_ Loss of normal bright fatty bone marrow signal in the vertebral body.
>   - _T2 / STIR:_ Demonstrates high-signal epidural mass, cord edema, and myelomalacia.
>   - _T1 + Gadolinium:_ Intense contrast enhancement outlining the epidural mass and cord displacement.
> - **CT Spine with Thin-Slice Bone Windows:** Essential to evaluate cortical bone integrity, osteolytic/osteoblastic destruction, and calculate the **Spinal Instability Neoplastic Score (SINS)**.
> - **CT Myelography:** Indicated **ONLY** if MRI is strictly contraindicated (pacemaker, incompatible metal).
>
> **Systemic Staging & Workup (If Primary is Unknown)**
>
> - **CT Chest, Abdomen, and Pelvis with IV Contrast:** Uncovers the occult primary malignancy in $>80–90%$ of unknown cases.
> - **Serum/Urine Protein Electrophoresis (SPEP/UPEP) & Free Light Chains:** Screens for **Multiple Myeloma / Plasmacytoma**.
> - **PSA** (men) and **Mammography** (women).
> - **CT-Guided Percutaneous Vertebral Needle Biopsy:** Mandatory to obtain tissue confirmation before radiation/chemotherapy if the patient has no prior cancer history.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Back pain awakening a cancer patient from sleep** (MESCC until proven otherwise).
> - **New, rapidly progressive bilateral lower-extremity weakness**.
> - **Urinary retention (PVR $>100–200	ext{ mL}$)** or overflow incontinence (signals imminent permanent paraplegia).
> - **SINS Score $ge 13$** (overt spinal mechanical instability).
> - **Rapid progression from ambulatory to non-ambulatory within $<24–48$ hours**.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Surgical Emergencies**
>
> - **Spinal Epidural Abscess:** Triad of back pain, fever, and progressive weakness; elevated ESR/CRP ($>95%$); history of IV drug use or spinal hardware.
> - **Spinal Epidural Hematoma:** Sudden, acute knife-like back pain followed immediately by paraplegia in an anticoagulated patient.
> - **Benign Osteoporotic Vertebral Compression Fracture:** Lacks an epidural soft-tissue mass; posterior vertebral cortex is intact; normal MRI marrow signal outside fracture line.
> - **Acute Transverse Myelitis:** Intramedullary inflammatory demyelination; negative bone imaging.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Preserve or restore independent ambulation, relieve spinal cord compression, achieve permanent mechanical spine stability, and eliminate severe pain.
> - **Secondary Target:** Local tumor control, prevent spinal deformity/collapse, and optimize survival via adjuvant systemic therapies.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (Acute Cord Compression Bundle)**
>
> - **1. Administer High-Dose IV Corticosteroids Immediately:**
>   - **Administer Dexamethasone (10 to 16 mg IV bolus, followed by 4 mg IV/PO every 6 hours)** immediately upon clinical suspicion of MESCC **BEFORE waiting for MRI confirmation** (reduces vasogenic edema, restores capillary blood flow; add a **PPI** for ulcer prophylaxis).
> - **2. Place an Indwelling Foley Catheter:** Insert immediately for acute urinary retention (PVR $>100–200	ext{ mL}$) to decompress the neurogenic bladder and prevent hydronephrosis.
> - **3. Maintain Rigid Spinal Precautions:** Flat bedrest and log-rolling until mechanical spinal stability is evaluated by spine surgery.
>
> **Multidisciplinary Decision Framework: The NOMS Framework**
>
> - Assess the 4 pillars: **N**eurologic (grade of cord compression), **O**ncologic (tumor histology/radiosensitivity), **M**echanical (SINS score), and **S**ystemic (patient performance status and life expectancy).
>
> **Neurosurgical Operative Management (The Patchell Protocol Standard)**
>
> - **Indications for Surgical Decompression + Posterior Instrumented Stabilization:**
>   1. **Spinal Instability (SINS Score $ge 7$):** Mechanical instability can _never_ be fixed with radiation alone; requires surgical stabilization!
>   2. **Radio-Resistant Tumors (Renal Cell, Melanoma, Colorectal Adenocarcinoma)** with high-grade cord compression.
>   3. **Direct Cord Compression (Bilsky Grade 2/3) in Ambulatory Patients or Patients with Motor Deficits $<48$ Hours** and a projected life expectancy $>3$ months.
>   4. **Bone Retropulsion** causing severe cord impingement.
>   5. **Unknown Primary Malignancy** requiring open diagnostic biopsy and decompression.
> - **Surgical Technique:** **Direct circumferential decompression** (separation surgery: excising the tumor away from the cord to create a 2–3 mm margin) combined with **multi-level posterior pedicle screw instrumentation and fusion**.
>
> **Radiation Therapy Protocols**
>
> - **1. Conventional External Beam Radiation Therapy (EBRT: 30 Gy in 10 fractions):**
>   - Primary first-line therapy for: (1) **Radiosensitive tumors (Multiple Myeloma, Lymphoma, Small Cell Lung Cancer, Seminoma)** without mechanical instability, (2) Patients with poor functional status (KPS $<50$) or short life expectancy ($<3$ months), or (3) Complete paraplegia established for $>48$ hours.
>   - Adjuvant therapy: Administered 2 to 3 weeks postoperatively following surgical decompression.
> - **2. Stereotactic Body Radiotherapy (SBRT / Spinal Radiosurgery):**
>   - Delivers ultra-high, conformal radiation doses (18–24 Gy in 1–3 fractions) with millimeter precision; highly effective for **radioresistant metastases (Renal cell, Melanoma)** following "separation surgery."
>
> **Percutaneous Vertebroplasty & Kyphoplasty**
>
> - Percutaneous injection of polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA) bone cement into painful, osteolytic vertebral metastases **WITHOUT epidural cord compression**, achieving immediate mechanical stabilization and pain relief.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid High-Dose Corticosteroids in Suspected Spinal Epidural Abscess**, as immunosuppression worsens bacterial bacteremia and sepsis (steroids are indicated for **tumor-related vasogenic edema**, NOT bacterial infection!).
> - **Avoid Laminectomy Alone WITHOUT Stabilization in a Patient with an Unstable Spine (SINS $ge 7$)**, as unroofing the posterior elements in an osteolytic spine converts partial instability into catastrophic **postoperative spinal collapse and permanent quadriplegia**.
> - **Avoid Ordering Only a Lumbar Spine MRI when Cord Compression is Suspected**, as $>20–30%$ of patients have asymptomatic multi-level "skip" metastases elsewhere in the cord (always order a **Whole-Spine MRI**).
> - **Avoid Delaying Corticosteroids or Surgical Referral while Waiting for Advanced Imaging**, as every hour of untreated spinal cord ischemia leads to irreversible axonal death and permanent paralysis.
> - **Avoid Radiotherapy Alone in an Unstable Spine (SINS $ge 13$)**, as radiation does not restore bone integrity and cannot prevent mechanical spinal collapse.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Permanent Paraplegia & Loss of Ambulation:** Occurs if compression is not relieved rapidly; less than 10% of patients who lose the ability to walk before treatment ever regain it.
> - **Neurogenic Shock & Autonomic Dysreflexia:** In high thoracic or cervical cord lesions.
> - **Radiation Myelopathy:** Delayed, irreversible ischemic cord necrosis occurring 6–18 months following high-dose radiation therapy.
> - **Hardware Failure & Wound Breakdown:** Risk heightened by high-dose preoperative steroids and prior irradiation.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 66-year-old man with metastatic prostate cancer presents with 3 weeks of severe mid-thoracic back pain that awakens him from sleep, followed by 36 hours of progressive leg heaviness, difficulty climbing stairs, and daytime urinary hesitancy. Neurological examination reveals 3/5 weakness in the bilateral hip flexors and quadriceps, hyperactive patellar reflexes, bilateral clonus, a **distinct sensory level at the umbilicus (T10)**, and an immediate bladder scan showing a **post-void residual of 550 mL**. High-dose **IV Dexamethasone (16 mg IV bolus)** is administered immediately in the emergency department, a Foley catheter is placed, and an emergent **Whole-Spine MRI** reveals an osteolytic T8 vertebral metastasis with extensive epidural tumor extension causing **severe spinal cord compression (Bilsky Grade 3)** with a **SINS score of 11 (potentially unstable)**. He undergoes emergent **posterior instrumented stabilization (T6–T10) with direct circumferential decompression (separation surgery)**, followed by postoperative stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT), successfully preserving his ability to walk independently.
