> [!abstract] Spinal Tumors & Metastatic Cord Compression
> Spectrum of primary and secondary neoplasms involving the vertebral column and neural elements, classified anatomically into **Extradural (Epidural)**, **Intradural Extramedullary**, and **Intramedullary**. Metastatic Epidural Spinal Cord Compression (MESCC) represents the most common presentation and an oncological emergency requiring multimodal coordination between spine surgery, radiation oncology, and systemic therapy under the **NOMS framework**.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
> - **Anatomical Classification:**
>   - **Extradural (Epidural, 95% of all spinal tumors):** Predominantly hematogenous bony metastases involving the posterior vertebral body and pedicles from **prostate, breast, lung, kidney, colon, and multiple myeloma**. Batson's paravertebral venous plexus facilitates retrograde spread without pulmonary filtration.
>   - **Intradural Extramedullary (4%):** Benign slow-growing lesions within the thecal sac but outside the cord parenchyma, compressing the cord from the perimeter: **Spinal Schwannomas / Neurofibromas** (originating from dorsal sensory nerve roots, dumb-bell shape exiting intervertebral foramen) and **Spinal Meningiomas** (predominantly thoracic spine in older females).
>   - **Intramedullary (1%):** Arise within the cord substance itself: **Ependymoma** (most common intramedullary tumor in adults, cervical/thoracolumbar, central syrinx, well-demarcated cleavage plane) and **Astrocytoma** (most common in children, infiltrative, poorly demarcated).
> - **Mechanism of Neurological Injury in MESCC:** Epidural mass expansion $\rightarrow$ mechanical venous plexus compression $\rightarrow$ secondary venous congestion and vasogenic cord edema $\rightarrow$ arterial hypoperfusion $\rightarrow$ irreversible spinal cord infarction.

> [!tip]- Clinical PEARLS
> - **The Bilsky Scale for ESCC:** Standardized MRI scoring of epidural spinal cord compression:
>   - *Grade 0:* Bone-only disease.
>   - *Grade 1:* Epidural space impingement without thecal sac deformation.
>   - *Grade 2:* Cord compression with visible CSF around cord.
>   - *Grade 3:* Cord compression with complete obliteration of the CSF space (high-grade ESCC requiring surgical separation).
> - **Back Pain Precedes Weakness by Weeks:** Unrelenting, progressive, localized spinal back pain (worse at night, unyielding in supine position) precedes neurological motor weakness and numbness by an average of **7 weeks** in metastatic cord compression.
> - **The Patchell Landmark Trial (Lancet 2005):** Proved that direct circumferential surgical decompression followed by radiotherapy is significantly superior to radiotherapy alone in preserving ambulatory function (84% vs. 57% regained/maintained walking).
> - **SINS (Spine Instability Neoplastic Score):** Evidence-based assessment of spinal stability ($0–6$ stable, $7–12$ indeterminate, $13–18$ unstable requiring surgical stabilization).

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **Symptom Progression**
> - **Pain (Earliest & Most Common, 90%):** Localized deep axial pain (vertebral destruction) and radicular "band-like" radiating pain across the chest or down the legs, typically exacerbated by recumbency (due to distension of Batson's venous plexus when lying flat).
> - **Motor Weakness:** Heavy, stiff, dragging legs, difficulty climbing stairs, progressing to paraplegia.
> - **Sensory Loss:** Ascending numbness or cold sensation with a distinct sensory level on the torso.
> - **Autonomic & Sphincter Dysfunction (Late sign):** Urinary retention, constipation, fecal incontinence, and loss of voluntary anal tone (predicts poor functional recovery if established before surgery).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> - **The NOMS Decision Framework:**
>   - **N (Neurological):** Grade of cord compression (Bilsky scale) and myelopathy status.
>   - **O (Oncological):** Tumor histology and radio-responsiveness (radiosensitive like myeloma/lymphoma vs. radioresistant like renal cell/melanoma/gastrointestinal).
>   - **M (Mechanical):** Spinal stability assessed by the **SINS score** ($> 7$ warrants surgical consult).
>   - **S (Systemic):** Patient medical fitness and oncological life expectancy ($> 3–6\text{ months}$ generally justifies surgical reconstruction).

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> - **Contrast-Enhanced Whole-Spine MRI (Gold Standard):** Must image entire spinal axis (cervical, thoracic, and lumbar) as non-contiguous asymptomatic skip metastases occur in up to 20% of cases.
> - **CT Spine with Bone Windows:** Crucial for evaluating osteolytic destruction of pedicles, posterior wall burst fracture, and biomechanical instability.
> - **CT-Guided Percutaneous Needle Biopsy:** Indicated if patient has suspected metastatic cord compression without a known primary cancer diagnosis (never irradiate an unknown primary without tissue diagnosis unless herniating).

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> - Nocturnal back pain awakening patient from sleep in a known cancer patient.
> - Progressive motor weakness or difficulty ambulating.
> - SINS score $\ge 7$ (mechanical instability).
> - High-grade Bilsky 2 or 3 cord compression.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> - Spondylodiscitis / Epidural Abscess (fever, positive blood cultures, elevated CRP/ESR).
> - Osteoporotic vertebral compression fracture (no soft-tissue epidural mass).
> - Acute Herniated Intervertebral Disc.
> - Spinal Dural Arteriovenous Fistula (progressive myelopathy with serpentine perimedullary vessels on MRI).

> [!summary]- Management
> **1. Emergent Medical Oncology Care**
> - **High-Dose Dexamethasone:** Administer **10–16 mg IV bolus** immediately, followed by 4–8 mg IV q6h with a PPI; rapidly reduces vasogenic cord edema and buys time for definitive therapy.
> - **Analgesia:** Opioids, neuropathic agents (gabapentin/pregabalin).
> - **Immobilization:** Cervical collar or thoracic-lumbar-sacral orthosis (TLSO) brace if SINS indicates instability.
> 
> **2. Surgical Decompression & Stabilization**
> - **Separation Surgery:** Minimally invasive or open posterolateral approach to resect the epidural tumor compressing the thecal sac ("creating a 2–3 mm buffer margin" around the cord) combined with **instrumented pedicle screw stabilization**. This restores canal patency and allows subsequent safe delivery of high-dose stereotactic radiation.
> - **Gross Total Resection:** Feasible for benign intradural extramedullary lesions (schwannomas, meningiomas) and ependymomas with excellent long-term cure rates.
> 
> **3. Radiation Therapy**
> - **Stereotactic Body Radiotherapy (SBRT / Spine SRS):** Delivers highly conformal, ablative radiation doses ($18–24\text{ Gy}$) to the tumor with steep dose fall-off at the spinal cord, achieving $> 85–90\%$ local tumor control even for historically radioresistant histology (renal cell, melanoma).
> - **Conventional External Beam Radiotherapy (cEBRT):** Typically $30\text{ Gy}$ in 10 fractions; primary treatment for highly radiosensitive tumors (myeloma, lymphoma, small cell lung cancer) without mechanical instability.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
> - **Do NOT treat mechanically unstable spines with radiation alone:** Radiation cannot rebuild destroyed bone; radiation without surgical stabilization will accelerate vertebral collapse and paralysis.
> - **Do not delay MRI for days in a cancer patient with localized back pain:** Treat back pain as the window of opportunity before paraplegia develops.

> [!error]- Complications
> - Irreversible paraplegia and permanent loss of sphincter control.
> - Instrumentation failure or pseudoarthrosis.
> - Radiation myelopathy (delayed toxicity causing progressive cord necrosis).
> - CSF leak and surgical wound breakdown in previously irradiated fields.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 64-year-old man with metastatic renal cell carcinoma (RCC) presents with 3 weeks of progressive mid-thoracic back pain that awakens him nightly, and 48 hours of progressive leg weakness and unsteady gait. On examination, he has 4-/5 power in bilateral lower extremities, hyperactive patellar reflexes, and sensory blunting below the T8 dermatome. Whole-spine contrast MRI demonstrates an osteolytic destructive lesion of T7 with an epidural mass causing Bilsky Grade 3 spinal cord compression with intramedullary T2 high signal. His SINS score is 11 (indeterminate stability). Dexamethasone 16 mg IV bolus is immediately administered. Because renal cell carcinoma is notoriously radioresistant to conventional radiation, the patient undergoes urgent neurosurgical "separation surgery": a T7 laminectomy and bilateral transpedicular decompression of the epidural mass, combined with T5–T9 percutaneous pedicle screw fixation. Postoperatively, his leg strength recovers to 5/5. Two weeks later, he receives definitive Stereotactic Body Radiotherapy (SBRT) with 24 Gy in 2 fractions to the T7 vertebra, achieving long-term local disease control.
