> [!abstract] Spinal Trauma
> Mechanical injury to the vertebral column, spinal cord, or cauda equina resulting from high-energy blunt trauma (MVCs, falls, diving: **Cervical [55%] > Thoracolumbar [45%]**), triggering immediate mechanical tissue disruption (**Primary Injury**) followed by ischemia, lipid peroxidation, and cord edema (**Secondary Injury**).

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Neurogenic Shock vs. Spinal Shock:**
>   - _Neurogenic Shock (Hemodynamic Collapse):_ Loss of sympathetic vasomotor tone from injury at or above **T6** $ightarrow$ unopposed vagal tone $ightarrow$ **triad of Hypotension + Paradoxical Bradycardia + Warm, dry, flushed extremities**!
>   - _Spinal Shock (Physiological Conduction Block):_ Transient loss of ALL spinal reflex and motor activity below the injury $ightarrow$ **flaccidity, areflexia, and absent bulbocavernosus reflex**. Its termination is marked by the **return of the bulbocavernosus reflex (S2–S4)** (typically in 24–48 hours), after which spasticity and hyperreflexia emerge.

> [!tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Maintain MAP $ge 85–90	ext{ mmHg}$ for 5 to 7 Days:** The single most critical medical intervention in acute traumatic SCI; perfuses the ischemic cord penumbra using **IV crystalloids + Vasopressors (Norepinephrine / Phenylephrine)**.
> - **Corticosteroids are NOT Recommended:** High-dose methylprednisolone (NASCIS protocol) is **no longer recommended** by AANS/CNS guidelines; it provides **zero functional recovery** and significantly increases lethal **sepsis, pneumonia, and gastrointestinal hemorrhage**!
> - **Sacral Sparing Defines an INCOMPLETE Injury:** Any preserved sensation (light touch or pinprick at S4–S5 perianal skin) OR voluntary motor function (voluntary anal sphincter contraction on DRE) defines an **Incomplete SCI (ASIA B, C, or D)**, conferring high potential for functional recovery!
> - **Incomplete Cord Syndromes Rapid Discriminator:**
>   - _Central Cord (Most common):_ Hyperextension injury in older adults with cervical stenosis $ightarrow$ **Motor deficit: Arms > Legs ("hands can't feed, legs can walk")** with sacral sensory sparing.
>   - _Anterior Cord (Worst prognosis):_ Hyperflexion or anterior spinal artery thrombosis $ightarrow$ **complete paralysis + loss of pain/temp** with **PRESERVED vibration and proprioception (dorsal columns intact)**.
>   - _Brown-Séquard (Hemisection - Stab wound):_ **Ipsilateral** motor paralysis and proprioception loss + **Contralateral** pain and temperature loss 1–2 levels below.

| Syndrome | Typical Mechanism | Motor Deficit Pattern | Sensory Deficit Pattern |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Central Cord Syndrome** (#1) | Hyperextension in elderly with cervical spondylosis | Upper Extremities >>> Lower Extremities | Patchy; Sacral Sparing (perianal intact) |
| **2. Anterior Cord Syndrome** | Hyperflexion, burst fx, or anterior spinal artery occ. | Bilateral complete motor paralysis | Loss of pain & temp; Vibration/Position OK |
| **3. Brown-Séquard Syndrome** | Penetrating trauma (stab) or lateral mass fracture | Ipsilateral spastic paralysis (corticosp.) | Ipsilateral position; Contralateral pain/temp |

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Trauma Mechanism:** High-speed motor vehicle collision, shallow-water diving accident, fall from height, or sports collision.
> - **Immediate Symptoms:** Severe neck or back pain, midline focal pain, numbness, tingling, inability to move extremities.
> - **Autonomic Symptoms:** Difficulty breathing, sensation of warmth/flushing in extremities, lightheadedness.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Primary Survey (ABCDE):**
>   - _Airway / Breathing:_ Diaphragmatic breathing or respiratory arrest indicates high cervical injury (**C3–C5: "C3, 4, 5 keep the diaphragm alive"**).
>   - _Circulation:_ **Neurogenic shock** presents with **hypotension and bradycardia** (contrast with hemorrhagic shock: hypotension + tachycardia!).
> - **Neurological Examination (Formal ASIA / ISNCSCI Protocol):**
>   - _Motor Level Testing (Key Muscle Groups, 0–5 Scale):_ C5 (Elbow flexors), C6 (Wrist extensors), C7 (Elbow extensors), C8 (Finger flexors), T1 (Finger abductors); L2 (Hip flexors), L3 (Knee extensors), L4 (Ankle dorsiflexors), L5 (Long toe extensors), S1 (Ankle plantarflexors).
>   - _Sensory Level Testing:_ Pinprick and light touch across 28 dermatomes bilaterally.
> - **Anorectal Examination (MANDATORY in 100% of Spinal Trauma):**
>   - **Voluntary Anal Contraction (S4–S5):** Evaluates motor sacral sparing.
>   - **Deep Anal Sensation / S4–S5 Light Touch:** Evaluates sensory sacral sparing.
>   - **Bulbocavernosus Reflex (S2–S4):** Squeezing the glans penis/clitoris or tugging the Foley catheter elicits anal sphincter contraction (presence confirms termination of **spinal shock**; absence confirms ongoing spinal shock).
> - **Priapism:** Sustained involuntary erection (signals severe, complete spinal cord injury due to autonomic parasympathetic disinhibition).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Cervical Spine Clearance Criteria (Imaging Decision Rules):**
>   - **NEXUS Low-Risk Criteria (All 5 MUST be met to clear C-spine clinically without imaging):**
>     1. No midline posterior cervical spine tenderness.
>     2. No focal neurological deficit.
>     3. Normal level of alertness (GCS 15).
>     4. No evidence of intoxication.
>     5. No painful, distracting injuries.
> - **American Spinal Injury Association (ASIA) Impairment Scale (AIS):**
>   - **Grade A (Complete):** No motor or sensory function is preserved in the sacral segments S4–S5.
>   - **Grade B (Sensory Incomplete):** Sensory preserved below the neurological level and includes S4–S5, but NO motor function.
>   - **Grade C (Motor Incomplete):** Motor function preserved below the level; $>50%$ of key muscles below the level have muscle grade $<3$.
>   - **Grade D (Motor Incomplete):** Motor function preserved below the level; $ge 50%$ of key muscles below the level have muscle grade $ge 3$.
>   - **Grade E (Normal):** Motor and sensory scores are normal.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Initial / Gold-Standard Bony Imaging**
>
> - **CT Whole Spine with Thin-Slice Multiplanar Reconstructions (Axial, Coronal, Sagittal):**
>   - **Mandatory first-line imaging test** for all trauma patients meeting NEXUS or Canadian C-Spine criteria (sensitivity $>99%$ for fractures, burst injuries, and facet subluxations).
>
> **Soft Tissue & Cord Imaging**
>
> - **Spine MRI (Sagittal T1, T2, STIR, Axial T2):**
>   - **Mandatory in all patients with acute neurological deficits**, obtunded patients, or suspected ligamentous disruption.
>   - Evaluates **spinal cord compression, cord contusion/myelomalacia, intramedullary hemorrhage, epidural hematoma, and disruption of the Posterior Ligamentous Complex (PLC)**.
>
> **Vascular Imaging for Blunt Cerebrovascular Injury (BCVI)**
>
> - **CT Angiography (CTA) of the Neck:** Mandatory if cervical spine fractures involve the **transverse foramen (C1–C6)**, subluxation/dislocation, or unexplained Horner syndrome/neurological deficit, to rule out **Vertebral or Carotid Artery Dissection**.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Neurogenic Shock:** Hypotension (SBP $<90	ext{ mmHg}$) paired with **Bradycardia (HR $<60	ext{ bpm}$)**.
> - **Lesion at or above C3–C5:** Impending diaphragmatic fatigue and fatal respiratory arrest.
> - **Bilateral Facet Dislocation ("Locked Facets"):** Extreme spinal instability with critical cord pinching.
> - **Priapism on physical exam** (indicates severe cord transection).
> - **Rapidly ascending motor or sensory level** (signals expanding hematoma or progressive secondary cord edema).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Trauma Mimics**
>
> - **Hemorrhagic Shock:** Distinguished by **hypotension WITH tachycardia (HR $>100	ext{ bpm}$)**, cold clammy extremities, and pallor (remember: isolated spinal trauma _never_ causes tachycardia unless severe pain/hemorrhagic shock is concurrent!).
> - **Traumatic Spinal Epidural Hematoma:** Rapidly progressive paraplegia within hours of trauma (often in anticoagulated patients); requires emergent surgical evacuation.
> - **Acute Aortic Dissection:** Shearing forces cause Adamkiewicz artery avulsion $ightarrow$ anterior cord syndrome without a vertebral fracture.
> - **Conversion Disorder / Functional Neurological Disorder:** Normal reflexes, normal sphincter tone, normal imaging.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Maintain **Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) $ge 85–90	ext{ mmHg}$ for 5 to 7 days**, secure the airway with in-line cervical stabilization, and perform **early surgical decompression within <24 hours**.
> - **Secondary Target:** Stabilize unstable fracture-dislocations, prevent secondary ischemia, and prevent VTE and pressure ulcers.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (Resuscitation & Spinal Protection)**
>
> - **1. Enforce Strict Rigid Spinal Precautions:** Maintain a **rigid cervical collar, flat bedrest, and log-rolling precautions** until the entire spine is cleared clinically or radiographically.
> - **2. Airway Management with Manual In-Line Stabilization (MILS):** Perform **Video Laryngoscopy or Fiberoptic Intubation while an assistant maintains rigid MILS** (holding the mastoid processes with collar open) to prevent dangerous cervical hyperextension during intubation for lesions at $ge 	ext{C5}$ or GCS $le 8$.
> - **3. Hemodynamic Resuscitation of Neurogenic Shock (Target MAP $ge 85–90	ext{ mmHg}$):**
>   - Infuse **Isotonic Crystalloids (Normal Saline)** cautiously to correct hypovolemia.
>   - **Initiate Vasopressors with Combined $alpha$ and $eta_1$ Activity—Norepinephrine (or Dopamine)**—early to reverse peripheral vasodilation AND correct bradycardia (use **IV Atropine or Glycopyrrolate** for severe refractory bradycardia).
> - **4. Closed Skeletal Traction for Cervical Bilateral Locked Facets:**
>   - Apply **Gardner-Wells Tongs (or halo ring)** starting with 10–15 lbs, adding 5–10 lbs per vertebral level under **serial fluoroscopy and awake neurological exams** to achieve closed reduction before taking to the OR.
> - **5. Insert an Indwelling Foley Catheter** immediately to relieve acute neurogenic urinary retention.
>
> **Definitive Surgical Decompression & Stabilization (The STASCIS Standard)**
>
> - **Perform Early Surgical Decompression and Spinal Instrumentation/Stabilization WITHIN <24 HOURS of Injury (and preferably <12 hours):**
>   - Landmark **STASCIS trial** proved that early surgical decompression ($<24	ext{ hours}$) nearly **triples the rate of $ge 2$-grade ASIA motor recovery** compared to delayed surgery.
>   - Operative approaches: Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF), posterior cervical laminectomy/fusion, or thoracolumbar posterior pedicle screw instrumentation.
>
> **Neurointensive Care & Complication Prevention**
>
> - **Initiate Pharmacologic VTE Prophylaxis (Subcutaneous Enoxaparin 30 mg SC BID or 40 mg daily) within 24 to 72 hours of injury** once intracranial/intraspinal bleeding is stable, paired with **Intermittent Pneumatic Compression (IPC) devices** (extreme risk of fatal pulmonary embolism!).
> - **Prevent Autonomic Dysreflexia (In injuries at or above T6 occurring weeks post-injury):** Sudden hypertensive crisis triggered by a full bladder or fecal impaction $ightarrow$ sit patient upright, loosen clothing, empty bladder/rectum, and give **IV Nicardipine or Nitropaste**.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid High-Dose Systemic Corticosteroids (Methylprednisolone / NASCIS Regimen)** in acute traumatic spinal cord injury; AANS/CNS guidelines formally recommend **AGAINST** routine steroids due to lack of medical efficacy and significantly increased rates of **severe pneumonia, sepsis, and death**.
> - **Avoid Fluid Overload with Excessive Crystalloids in Neurogenic Shock**, as vasodilation cannot be fixed with fluid alone and leads rapidly to **fatal pulmonary edema** (start **vasopressors** early!).
> - **Avoid Blind Neck Hyperextension or Jaw Thrust with Neck Movement during Airway Management**, to prevent converting an incomplete cervical fracture into a permanent, complete spinal cord transection (always use **Manual In-Line Stabilization [MILS]**).
> - **Avoid Letting MAP Drop Below 85 mmHg in the First 5 to 7 Days**, as spinal cord perfusion depends directly on systemic pressure; a single hypotensive dip causes secondary ischemic necrosis.
> - **Avoid Clearing the Cervical Spine Clinically in Patients with Distracting Injuries, Intoxication, or Altered Sensorium** (always obtain a **thin-slice CT scan**).

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Neurogenic Shock & Asystole:** Loss of cardiac accelerator sympathetic tone $ightarrow$ severe bradycardia / cardiac arrest $ightarrow$ treat with **Atropine + Norepinephrine**.
> - **Deep Vein Thrombosis & Fatal Pulmonary Embolism:** Highest risk of all trauma cohorts ($>60–80%$ without prophylaxis) $ightarrow$ mandatory LMWH within 24–72 hours.
> - **Autonomic Dysreflexia (Lesions $ge 	ext{T6}$):** Massive uninhibited sympathetic surge triggered by noxious visceral stimuli (distended bladder, fecal impaction) $ightarrow$ malignant hypertension (SBP $>200–250	ext{ mmHg}$), pounding headache, profuse sweating above lesion, bradycardia $ightarrow$ **elevate head of bed and immediately catheterize bladder**!
> - **Paralytic Ileus & Neurogenic Bowel/Bladder:** Acute loss of autonomic gut control $ightarrow$ place NG tube and initiate scheduled bowel regimen.
> - **Severe Decubitus Pressure Ulcers:** Rapid breakdown over sacrum and heels $ightarrow$ log-roll q2h and use specialized alternating-pressure mattresses.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 22-year-old male dives into shallow water at a lake, strikes his head on the bottom, and is pulled out unable to move his arms or legs. On arrival in the trauma bay with a rigid cervical collar, vitals reveal BP 82/44 mmHg and HR 46 bpm with warm, flushed extremities (**Neurogenic Shock**). Neurological exam demonstrates 0/5 power in all four extremities, an absent bulbocavernosus reflex (**Spinal Shock**), and a sensory level at C4 (**ASIA Grade A**). He is intubated via video laryngoscopy using **Manual In-Line Stabilization**, an infusion of **Norepinephrine** is initiated targeting a **MAP of $ge 85–90	ext{ mmHg}$**, and a thin-slice CT/MRI of the cervical spine reveals a **C5–C6 bilateral jumped facet dislocation with severe spinal cord compression**. He undergoes emergent closed reduction with Gardner-Wells tongs followed by **anterior cervical decompression and fusion (ACDF) within 8 hours of injury**, maintaining MAP $ge 85	ext{ mmHg}$ in the Neuro-ICU for 7 days.
