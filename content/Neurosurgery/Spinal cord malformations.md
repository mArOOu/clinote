> [!abstract] Spinal Cord Malformations
> Congenital anomalies resulting from defective embryonic closure of the neural tube (**Primary Neurulation, Days 26–28**) or abnormal caudal cell mass canalization (**Secondary Neurulation**), divided into **Open Spinal Dysraphism (Spina Bifida Aperta: Myelomeningocele, Meningocele)** and **Closed Spinal Dysraphism (Spina Bifida Occulta: Tethered Cord, Lipomyelomeningocele, Split Cord)**.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Open Dysraphism (Myelomeningocele [MMC]):** Primary neural tube fails to close dorsally $\rightarrow$ exposed, raw neural placode directly continuous with skin margins, leaking CSF $\rightarrow$ in utero amniotic fluid chemical toxicity and mechanical trauma destroy placode neurons (**100% associated with Chiari II malformation and hydrocephalus**).
> - **Tethered Cord Syndrome:** Mechanical anchor (thickened **filum terminale $>2\text{ mm}$**, lipoma, fibrous/osseous spur) fixes the conus medullaris at a low level $\rightarrow$ longitudinal stretch during childhood growth spurts causes **microvascular ischemia and oxidative metabolic failure** of lumbosacral neurons.

> [!tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Lumbosacral Cutaneous Signposts (>80% of Closed Dysraphisms):** Midline cutaneous markers identify occult closed dysraphism before irreversible deficits occur: **hypertrichosis ("faun-tail" tuft of hair)**, **subcutaneous lipoma**, **atypical dimple ($>5\text{ mm}$ diameter, $>2.5\text{ cm}$ above anal verge)**, **hemangioma**, or **dermal sinus pit** $\rightarrow$ order screening ultrasound/MRI!
> - **Tethered Cord Clinical Triad:** (1) **Progressive motor/gait changes** (toe-walking, cavovarus foot, asymmetric calf wasting), (2) **Urologic decline** (new urinary incontinence/bedwetting in a previously toilet-trained child), and (3) **Orthopedic deformities** (progressive scoliosis, foot deformities).
> - **MOMS Trial (Fetal Surgery Paradigm):** Fetal open/fetoscopic repair of myelomeningocele at **19 to 25.9 weeks gestation** halves the need for postnatal hydrocephalus shunting and doubles the rate of independent walking at 30 months compared to standard postnatal repair!
> - **Normal Conus Medullaris Level:** Terminates at L2–L3 at birth, ascending to the **lower border of L1 (or L1–L2 interspace) by 3 months of age**; a conus terminating at or **below L2–L3 after 3 months** defines a low-lying, tethered cord!
> - **Universal Latex Allergy Alert:** $>70\%$ of patients with myelomeningocele develop severe, life-threatening **Type I IgE-mediated latex anaphylaxis** from repeated early mucosal exposures; maintain a strict **Latex-Free Environment from birth**!

| Category | Cutaneous & Neural Coverage | Anatomical Defect | Associated Pathology |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Myelomeningocele (Spina Bifida)** | OPEN; exposed neural placode; leaking CSF | Placode, meninges, & cord herniate through open vertebral arch | Chiari II (100%), Hydrocephalus (>90%), clubfeet, paraplegia |
| **2. Meningocele** | Variable skin coverage; no exposed neural tissue | Meninges & CSF herniate; cord stays within spinal canal | Rarely Chiari II; normal lower extremity motor function |

| **4. Tethered Cord Syndrome** | CLOSED; thickened filum terminale (>2 mm); low-lying conus | Conus medullaris anchored below L1-L2; chronic mechanical traction | Progressive scoliosis, cavovarus foot, new-onset urinary incontinence |
| **5. Diastematomyelia (Split Cord)** | CLOSED; midline hypertrichosis ("faun-tail" tuft of hair) | Cord split into two hemicords by an osseous or fibrous spur/septum | Progressive scoliosis; cord tethering; spur requires surgical excision |

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Myelomeningocele (Prenatal / Neonatal):** Elevated maternal serum alpha-fetoprotein (MSAFP); prenatal ultrasound showing **"Lemon sign"** (frontal bone scalloping) and **"Banana sign"** (curved cerebellar hemisphere in Chiari II); visible fluid-filled dorsal spinal mass leaking CSF at delivery.
> - **Tethered Cord / Occult Dysraphism (Childhood Growth Spurts, Ages 4–12):**
>   - Back pain radiating down legs, worse with flexion or sports.
>   - **Gait Changes:** New toe-walking, clumsy running, frequent tripping, asymmetric shoe wear.
>   - **Urological Regression:** New-onset urinary urgency, frequency, daytime wetting, or nocturnal enuresis in a child who was previously continent for years; recurrent UTIs.
>   - Chronic constipation and encopresis (fecal soiling).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Inspection of the Spine:**
>   - _Open:_ Exposed, reddish neural placode leaking clear fluid.
>   - _Closed / Occult:_ Midline lumbosacral **tuft of coarse hair ("faun tail")**, subcutaneous fatty mass (lipoma), dermal sinus tract (tiny pit, often discharging clear fluid/sebaceous material), capillary hemangioma, or asymmetric gluteal cleft.
> - **Neurological Exam:**
>   - _Open MMC:_ Flaccid lower-extremity paraparesis/plegia, sensory loss matching placode level, absent anal tone, neurogenic bladder.
>   - _Tethered Cord:_ Asymmetric lower-extremity muscle atrophy (calf wasting), mixed UMN/LMN signs (absent ankle reflexes with upgoing Babinski), decreased perineal sensation.
> - **Orthopedic Exam:** Unilateral or bilateral **pes cavus** (high arch), claw toes, **talipes equinovarus (clubfoot)**, leg-length discrepancy, and rapidly progressive juvenile **scoliosis**.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Tethered Cord Syndrome:** Clinical constellation of progressive lower-extremity sensorimotor, urological, or orthopedic decline **PLUS Radiologic Confirmation on Whole-Spine MRI** demonstrating:
>   - Conus medullaris terminating **below the lower border of L1–L2 (after 3 months of age)**, OR
>   - **Thickened filum terminale ($>2\text{ mm}$ in diameter)** at the L5–S1 level, fatty infiltration of the filum, or dorsal cord displacement.
> - **Myelomeningocele (Open Spina Bifida):** Clinical physical identification at birth of an open, non-skin-covered dorsal spinal defect with visible neural placode leaking CSF.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Prenatal Screening**
>
> - **Maternal Serum Alpha-Fetoprotein (MSAFP):** Elevated at 15–20 weeks gestation in $>85–90\%$ of open neural tube defects.
> - **Amniocentesis:** Elevated **Amniotic Fluid AFP** AND presence of **Acetylcholinesterase (AChE)** (AChE is 100% specific for open, non-epithelialized neural defects).
>
> **Postnatal Neuroimaging**
>
> - **Spinal Ultrasound (Screening in Neonates $<3–4$ Months Old):**
>   - Fast, non-sedated bedside screening test through unossified posterior arches; defines the exact **level of the conus medullaris**, evaluates filum thickness, and identifies intra-canal lipomas in infants with skin dimples/hair tufts.
> - **Whole-Spine MRI with and without Contrast (Gold Standard for All Ages):**
>   - **Mandatory test of choice** in infants $\ge 3–4$ months or if ultrasound is abnormal.
>   - Visualizes low-lying conus, **thickened filum terminale ($>2\text{ mm}$)**, lipomyelomeningocele interface, split cord malformation (diastematomyelia spur), dermal sinus tract extension into the theca, and associated **Syringomyelia**.
> - **Brain MRI with Contrast:** Mandatory in all patients with myelomeningocele to stage **Chiari II malformation** (hindbrain herniation) and evaluate for **Hydrocephalus / Ventriculomegaly**.
>
> **Urological & Functional Baseline**
>
> - **Urodynamic Studies (UDS) & Renal-Bladder Ultrasound:** Mandatory baseline tests for all spinal dysraphisms (identifies **Detrusor-Sphincter Dyssynergia [DSD]**, high bladder storage pressures [$>40\text{ cmH}_2\text{O}$], and hydronephrosis/vesicoureteral reflux).

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Open neural placode leaking CSF at birth** (immediate risk of fatal bacterial meningitis and ventriculitis).
> - **Midline skin pit discharging fluid or history of recurrent bacterial meningitis** (patent **Dermal Sinus Tract** tracking into the thecal sac $\rightarrow$ requires emergent prophylactic resection!).
> - **Rapidly progressive urinary incontinence, gait loss, or escalating scoliosis** in a growing child (signals critical cord traction ischemia $\rightarrow$ urgent surgical detethering).
> - **Inspiratory stridor, central apnea, or swallowing difficulty** in an infant with myelomeningocele (signals acute **Chiari II brainstem compression**).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Benign Non-Surgical Mimics**
>
> - **Benign Simple Sacral Dimple:** Located within the gluteal cleft, **$<2.5\text{ cm}$ from the anal verge**, **$<5\text{ mm}$ in diameter**, visible clean epithelial base, no other cutaneous markers, normal neurological exam (**requires ZERO imaging and zero intervention!**).
> - **Cerebral Palsy (Spastic Diplegia):** Toe-walking, scissoring gait, hyperreflexia; distinguished by history of prematurity/hypoxic injury, static course, and **completely normal spine MRI**.
> - **Charcot-Marie-Tooth Disease (CMT):** Hereditary motor-sensory neuropathy; bilateral pes cavus and hammer toes, but symmetric, length-dependent distal sensory loss, normal spine MRI, positive family history.
> - **Sacrococcygeal Teratoma:** Congenital germ cell tumor arising at the coccyx; does not communicate with the spinal canal.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Immediate watertight closure of open defects within 24–48 hours to prevent fatal CNS infection, eliminate mechanical cord traction (**Surgical Detethering**), and preserve lower-extremity motor function.
> - **Secondary Target:** Protect renal function by maintaining low bladder storage pressures (prevent **neurogenic bladder renal failure**), and treat associated hydrocephalus and Chiari II malformation.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (Open Myelomeningocele at Delivery)**
>
> - **1. Protect Exposed Neural Placode Immediately:** Place neonate in the **strict prone position** (or lateral), apply **sterile, warm saline-soaked non-adherent dressings covered with plastic wrap** over the placode to prevent drying and hypothermia, and enforce a **100% LATEX-FREE protocol**.
> - **2. Administer Empiric IV Antibiotics:** **Administer IV Ampicillin (100 mg/kg/day) PLUS Gentamicin (5 mg/kg/day)** immediately to prevent ascending bacterial meningitis.
> - **3. Perform Emergent Postnatal Surgical Repair WITHIN 24 to 48 HOURS of Birth:**
>   - Microsurgical dissection of the neural placode from surrounding skin, tubularization/closure of the neural tube, **watertight closure of the dura mater**, and mobilization of paraspinal fascial/skin flaps to achieve complete primary soft-tissue coverage.
>
> **Fetal In Utero Surgical Repair (The MOMS Protocol Standard)**
>
> - **Perform Maternal-Fetal Open Laparotomy or Fetoscopic Repair at 19 to 25.9 Weeks Gestation** in carefully selected mothers:
>   - Stops secondary chemical damage from amniotic fluid and halts CSF leakage; **halves the requirement for postnatal hydrocephalus shunting** and significantly improves motor ambulation rates at 30 months compared to postnatal surgery.
>
> **Definitive Surgical Management: Tethered Cord Syndrome & Closed Dysraphism**
>
> - **Indications for Surgical Detethering:**
>   1. Any patient with **symptomatic Tethered Cord Syndrome** (progressive gait decline, new urological accidents, foot deformities, or pain).
>   2. **Prophylactic Detethering** in infants with high-risk lesions (**Lipomyelomeningocele, Dermal Sinus Tract, or Split Cord**) to prevent irreversible axonal ischemic damage during rapid adolescent growth spurts.
> - **Perform Surgical Cord Detethering via Laminotomy:**
>   - **Sectioning of the Filum Terminale (Filumectomy):** Dividing the thickened, inelastic filum terminale at L5–S1 to release longitudinal tension on the conus medullaris.
>   - **Resection of Lipoma / Septum:** Subtotal microsurgical resection of lipomyelomeningocele, mobilizing neural tissue into the canal; excision of the osseous/fibrous spur in split cord malformations (diastematomyelia) to reunite hemicords within a single dural tube.
>
> **Long-Term Multidisciplinary Neuro-Urology & Orthopedics**
>
> - **Urological Preservation (First-Line Gold Standard):** Initiate **Clean Intermittent Catheterization (CIC 4–6× daily) PLUS Oral Anticholinergics (Oxybutynin)** or **Intravesical Botulinum Toxin** to treat detrusor hyperreflexia, maintain bladder storage pressure **$<40\text{ cmH}_2\text{O}$**, and prevent vesicoureteral reflux and end-stage renal failure.
> - **Orthopedic Management:** Custom ankle-foot orthoses (AFOs), serial casting for clubfoot, and bracing/posterior spinal fusion for progressive scoliosis.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid ANY Exposure to Natural Rubber Latex Products in Patients with Myelomeningocele**, as repeated surgical/catheter exposures induce severe, life-threatening **Type I Anaphylactic Shock** (use silicone/polyurethane catheters and latex-free surgical gloves exclusively!).
> - **Avoid Placing a Neonate with an Open Myelomeningocele in the Supine Position**, as direct compressive pressure on the exposed neural placode causes irreversible neural tissue ischemia, necrosis, and CSF leakage.
> - **Avoid Blind Instrumentation, Probing, or Needle Puncture of a Midline Lumbosacral Skin Pit or Dermal Sinus**, as probing inoculates surface bacteria directly into the subarachnoid space, triggering **catastrophic purulent bacterial meningitis**.
> - **Avoid Delaying Urological Investigation in Closed Dysraphism / Tethered Cord**, as upper-tract hydronephrosis, vesicoureteral reflux, and permanent renal damage can develop _silently_ without obvious urinary incontinence (always obtain baseline **Urodynamic Studies**!).

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Recurrent Cord Retethering (10–30% of Patients):** Postoperative scar tissue adheres the repaired cord to the dorsal dura $\rightarrow$ re-emergence of pain, worsening gait, and bladder accidents during growth spurts $\rightarrow$ requires repeat surgical detethering.
> - **Neurogenic Bladder Renal Failure:** Uncontrolled detrusor-sphincter dyssynergia and high-pressure urinary storage $\rightarrow$ bilateral hydronephrosis, recurrent pyelonephritis, and **end-stage renal disease (historically the leading cause of death in adult spina bifida)**.
> - **Chiari II Medullary Brainstem Failure:** Infant vocal cord abductor paralysis, stridor, and central apnea $\rightarrow$ emergent VP shunt placement or posterior fossa decompression.
> - **Bacterial Ventriculitis & Meningitis:** From pre-closure CSF leak in open MMC or dermal sinus tract infection (_S. aureus, E. coli_).

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 5-year-old girl who was successfully toilet-trained at age 3 presents with 4 months of new daytime urinary wetting, recurrent urinary tract infections, and complaints of aching pain in both calves after playing. Her mother notes her right foot has developed a high arch (**pes cavus**) and she has started walking on her toes. Examination reveals a small **patch of coarse hair (hypertrichosis) and a capillary hemangioma over the lower lumbar spine**, 4/5 weakness in right ankle dorsiflexion, an absent right Achilles reflex, and an elevated post-void residual of 180 mL on bladder ultrasound. Whole-spine MRI confirms a **low-lying conus medullaris terminating at the L4 vertebral level with a thickened, fatty filum terminale measuring 3.5 mm in diameter (Tethered Cord Syndrome)**. She undergoes an elective **microsurgical lumbar laminotomy and sectioning of the filum terminale (detethering)**, resulting in resolution of her leg pain, stabilization of her foot deformity, and complete recovery of daytime urinary continence.
