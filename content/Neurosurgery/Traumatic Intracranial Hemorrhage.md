> [!abstract] Traumatic Intracranial Hemorrhage
> Spectrum of acute extra-axial and intra-axial bleeding following head trauma. Classified anatomically into **Epidural Hematoma (EDH)**, **Acute Subdural Hematoma (ASDH)**, **Traumatic Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (tSAH)**, and **Cerebral Contusions/Intraparenchymal Hematomas**. Represents neurosurgical emergencies where rapid surgical evacuation prevents irreversible brainstem herniation.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
> - **Epidural Hematoma (EDH):** Blood collects in the potential space between the inner table of the skull and outer periosteal dura. Caused by skull fracture (typically pterion or squamous temporal bone) tearing the **Middle Meningeal Artery (85%)** or dural venous sinuses (15%). High-pressure arterial bleeding causes progressive stripping of the tightly adherent dura, forming a lenticular biconvex mass that does *not* cross cranial suture lines.
> - **Acute Subdural Hematoma (ASDH):** Blood accumulates between the dura and arachnoid mater. Caused by high-acceleration inertial shearing that tears **cortical bridging veins** as they cross into the superior sagittal sinus, or laceration of cortical surface vessels. Unlike EDH, blood is under venous pressure but spreads freely across suture lines, conforming to the cerebral convexity (crescentic shape). Strongly associated with severe underlying cortical contusion and edema.
> - **Traumatic SAH (tSAH) & Contusions:** Microvascular disruption within subarachnoid space and coup/contrecoup cortical parenchymal contusions, commonly in inferior frontal lobes and anterior temporal poles.

> [!tip]- Clinical PEARLS
> - **Radiological Shape Rule:**
>   - **EDH = Biconvex (lenticular):** Bound by suture lines (where dura adheres firmly to skull).
>   - **ASDH = Crescent-shaped (concave-convex):** Not bound by suture lines; crosses sutures but bounded by dural reflections (falx and tentorium).
> - **Mortality Difference:** EDH has a low mortality ($< 10\%$) if evacuated before pupillary dilatation; ASDH carries high mortality ($40–60\%$) because of profound underlying primary parenchymal brain damage.
> - **"The 4-hour Golden Window in ASDH":** Classic neurosurgical data shows that operative evacuation of acute subdural hematoma within 4 hours of injury drastically decreases mortality compared to delayed evacuation.
> - **EDH Vertex & Posterior Fossa:** Vertex EDHs from superior sagittal sinus tears and posterior fossa EDHs from transverse sinus tears may present insidiously with late sudden apnea; always look at the posterior fossa on CT.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **Epidural Hematoma (EDH)**
> - Classic "Lucid Interval" in 20–30%: Initial blow $\rightarrow$ transient LOC $\rightarrow$ patient awakens symptom-free $\rightarrow$ progressive headache, lethargy, rapid coma, ipsilateral mydriasis, contralateral hemiplegia.
> 
> **Acute Subdural Hematoma (ASDH)**
> - Severe deceleration injury (MVC or fall in elderly/anticoagulated); patient is often comatose from the moment of impact with focal neurological deficits.
> 
> **Signs of Imminent Uncal Herniation**
> - Hutchinson's pupil: Ipsilateral pupillary sluggishness $\rightarrow$ fixed dilation (compression of parasympathetic fibers on periphery of CN III).
> - Contralateral hemiparesis from cerebral peduncle compression (Kernohan's notch phenomenon: paradoxical ipsilateral hemiparesis if contralateral peduncle is compressed against opposite tentorial edge).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> - **Surgical Indications for Epidural Hematoma (BTF Guidelines):**
>   - Hematoma volume $> 30\text{ cm}^3$ regardless of GCS.
>   - Thickness $> 15\text{ mm}$ or midline shift $> 5\text{ mm}$ with GCS $< 9$ or focal deficit.
> - **Surgical Indications for Acute Subdural Hematoma (BTF Guidelines):**
>   - Clot thickness $> 10\text{ mm}$ or midline shift $> 5\text{ mm}$ regardless of GCS.
>   - If thickness $< 10\text{ mm}$ and shift $< 5\text{ mm}$, evacuate if GCS decreases by $\ge 2$ points or asymmetric non-reactive pupils or $\text{ICP} > 20\text{ mmHg}$.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> - **Non-Contrast Head CT:**
>   - EDH: Hyperdense, biconvex, lenticular mass adjacent to inner skull table; look for underlying fracture line. A low-density swirl sign ("swirl sign") indicates active hyperacute extravasation.
>   - ASDH: Hyperdense crescentic collection over the hemisphere, extending along the tentorium or falx.
> - **Coagulation Profile & Reversal Testing:** Urgent INR, anti-Xa level, or thromboelastometry to guide reversal of VKAs (PCC + IV Vitamin K) or DOACs (Andexanet alfa, Idarucizumab).

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> - Blown pupil (unilateral fixed mydriasis).
> - Rapid neurological collapse within minutes to hours after a mild head impact.
> - "Swirl sign" on CT (active non-clotted arterial bleeding).
> - Underlying severe coagulopathy or therapeutic anticoagulation.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> - Chronic Subdural Hematoma (crescentic hypodense or mixed-density collection in elderly with minimal trauma).
> - Non-traumatic Intracerebral Hemorrhage (hypertensive, amyloid angiopathy).
> - Aneurysmal Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (thunderclap headache, basal cistern blood).
> - Cerebral venous sinus thrombosis with hemorrhagic infarction.

> [!summary]- Management
> **1. Emergent Medical Stabilization**
> - Intubate immediately for GCS $\le 8$ or rapidly dropping mental status.
> - Immediate reversal of coagulopathy:
>   - Warfarin: 4-Factor Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (PCC 25–50 IU/kg) + Vitamin K 10 mg IV.
>   - Dabigatran: Idarucizumab 5g IV.
>   - Factor Xa inhibitors (Apixaban, Rivaroxaban): Andexanet alfa or high-dose 4-factor PCC (50 IU/kg).
>   - Platelet dysfunction/antiplatelets: Desmopressin (DDAVP $0.4\ \mu\text{g/kg}$ IV) $\pm$ platelet transfusion if emergency surgery planned.
> - Osmotherapy (250 mL 3% NaCl or 1 g/kg Mannitol) while moving directly to the OR.
> 
> **2. Neurosurgical Evacuation**
> - **EDH:** Urgent osteoplastic craniotomy centered over the hematoma and fracture line, evacuation of clot, coagulation of bleeding dural vessels (middle meningeal artery), and dural tenting sutures to the bone edge to prevent re-accumulation.
> - **ASDH:** Large question-mark trauma craniotomy/craniectomy, dural opening, evacuation of clot and irrigation, hemostasis of bleeding cortical bridge veins. Bone flap often left off (decompressive hemicraniectomy) if underlying hemispheric brain swelling is severe.
> - **Postoperative ICU Care:** Invasive arterial line, continuous ICP monitoring, maintain $\text{ICP} < 20–22\text{ mmHg}$ and $\text{CPP} \ge 60–70\text{ mmHg}$.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
> - **Never perform lumbar puncture:** Risk of fatal brain herniation.
> - **Do not delay surgical mobilization for detailed imaging:** If patient is herniating with known EDH, proceed straight to the operating room.
> - **Avoid post-evacuation hypovolemia:** Ensure adequate volume resuscitation to maintain cerebral perfusion.

> [!error]- Complications
> - Transtentorial (uncal) or subfalcine herniation with fatal Duret hemorrhages of the brainstem.
> - Re-accumulation of hematoma.
> - Post-traumatic hydrocephalus (especially in tSAH blocking arachnoid granulations).
> - Persistent vegetative state or severe neurological disability.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 19-year-old college student is struck on the temporal skull with a baseball bat. He experiences 2 minutes of loss of consciousness, awakens completely oriented (GCS 15), and refuses ambulance transport. Two hours later at home, he develops an escalating severe headache and vomiting. By the time EMS arrives, he is obtunded (GCS 8) with a sluggishly reactive 6 mm right pupil and left hemiparesis. Non-contrast head CT demonstrates an acute right squamous temporal fracture and a 40 mL biconvex, hyperdense extra-axial mass with 9 mm midline shift. The patient is intubated, given 250 mL 3% hypertonic saline, and transferred immediately to the operating theatre. A right temporal craniotomy evacuates the epidural clot and ligates the torn anterior branch of the middle meningeal artery. He wakes up extubated 12 hours later with full neurological recovery.
