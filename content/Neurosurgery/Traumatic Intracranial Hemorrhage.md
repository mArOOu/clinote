> [!abstract] Traumatic Intracranial Hemorrhage
> Extra-axial or intra-axial intracranial bleeding resulting from blunt, penetrating, or rotational head trauma, encompassing **Epidural Hematoma (EDH)**, **Subdural Hematoma (SDH)**, **Traumatic Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (tSAH)**, **Cerebral Contusions**, and **Diffuse Axonal Injury (DAI)**, representing the leading cause of trauma-related mortality and long-term disability worldwide.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Epidural Hematoma (EDH):** Skull fracture at the **pterion** lacerates the **Middle Meningeal Artery (MMA)** $ightarrow$ high-pressure arterial blood dissects periosteal dura from the inner skull table $ightarrow$ **biconvex (lenticular) hematoma limited by cranial sutures** (where dura is anchored).
> - **Subdural Hematoma (SDH):** Deceleration/acceleration forces shear **bridging cortical veins** traversing to dural venous sinuses $ightarrow$ low-pressure venous blood collects between the dura and arachnoid $ightarrow$ **crescentic hematoma crossing cranial sutures** (limited only by dural folds: falx and tentorium).
> - **Diffuse Axonal Injury (DAI):** Rotational acceleration/shearing strains sever axons at interfaces of differing density (**gray-white matter junction, corpus callosum, dorsolateral brainstem**).

> [!tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **The CT Shape Rule:**
>   - **EDH = Biconvex / Lens-Shaped:** Bounded by cranial sutures; can cross the midline dural attachments (falx/tentorium).
>   - **SDH = Crescent-Shaped / Concave:** Crosses cranial sutures; bounded by dural reflections (cannot cross the falx/tentorium).
> - **Brain Trauma Foundation (BTF) Surgical Evacuation Thresholds:**
>   - _Epidural Hematoma (EDH):_ Volume **$>30	ext{ mL}$** regardless of GCS, OR any EDH with **GCS $<9$ and anisocoria (blown pupil)** $ightarrow$ Emergent Craniotomy!
>   - _Acute Subdural Hematoma (SDH):_ Clot thickness **$>10	ext{ mm}$** OR midline shift **$>5	ext{ mm}$** regardless of GCS, OR GCS drop $ge 2$ points from injury $ightarrow$ Emergent Decompressive Craniectomy / Craniotomy!
> - **Hypotension Kills in TBI (Single Episode Doubles Mortality):** Maintain **$	ext{SBP} ge 100	ext{ mmHg}$** (ages 50–69) or **$ge 110	ext{ mmHg}$** (ages 15–49 or $ge 70$); never tolerate hypotension!
> - **Chronic SDH in the Elderly:** Cerebral atrophy stretches bridging veins; minor/forgotten ground-level falls weeks prior present insidiously with fluctuating confusion, headache, or focal deficits (CT shows a **hypodense [dark] crescent**).
> - **DAI Clinico-Radiologic Dissociation:** Patient in deep coma (**GCS 3–5**) with a deceptively "normal" or minimally abnormal CT (showing only punctate microhemorrhages) $ightarrow$ order **Brain MRI (SWI / GRE)** to reveal extensive axonal shearing.

| Feature | Epidural Hematoma (EDH) | Acute Subdural Hematoma (SDH) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Vascular Source** | Arterial: Middle Meningeal Artery (MMA) | Venous: Bridging Cortical Veins |
| **CT Morphology** | Biconvex / Lenticular (Hyperdense) | Crescentic / Concave (Hyperdense) |
| **Suture Boundaries** | DOES NOT cross sutures; crosses falx | CROSSES sutures; stopped by falx |
| **Classic Mechanism** | Temporal bone fracture at pterion | Deceleration / High-speed impact |
| **Clinical Course** | Brief LOC $ightarrow$ "Lucid Interval" $ightarrow$ Coma | Progressive coma from onset |
| **Underlying Injury** | Minimal parenchymal damage | Severe underlying brain injury |
| **Prognosis** | Excellent if evacuated promptly | Poor (40–60% mortality) |

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Mechanism of Injury:** High-speed MVC, fall from height, bicycle/pedestrian accident, assault, or minor fall on anticoagulants/antiplatelets.
> - **Loss of Consciousness (LOC) & Lucid Interval:** Classic **"Lucid Interval" in EDH (occurs in ~20–30%)**: Initial traumatic concussion $ightarrow$ temporary recovery of full consciousness $ightarrow$ rapid neurological collapse, coma, and ipsilateral pupillary dilation within hours as the arterial clot expands.
> - **Acute SDH:** Typically severe trauma with immediate, persistent coma or severe depression of consciousness from onset.
> - **Chronic SDH:** Elderly patient or chronic alcohol use disorder presenting weeks after minor trauma with progressive cognitive decline, gait ataxia, headache, or fluctuating hemiparesis.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Primary Survey (ATLS):** ABCDEs; calculate **Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS: 3 to 15)** immediately:
>   - _Severe TBI:_ GCS $le 8$ ("Less than 8, intubate!").
>   - _Moderate TBI:_ GCS 9–12.
>   - _Mild TBI:_ GCS 13–15.
> - **Signs of Transtentorial (Uncal) Herniation:**
>   - **Ipsilateral fixed, dilated ("blown") pupil** (compression of outer parasympathetic fibers of CN III at tentorial incisura) **PLUS contralateral motor hemiplegia** (compression of ipsilateral cerebral peduncle).
>   - _Kernohan Notch Phenomenon:_ False-localizing sign where the contralateral peduncle is compressed against the opposite tentorial edge, causing **ipsilateral hemiplegia**!
> - **Signs of Basilar Skull Fracture:**
>   - **Battle Sign:** Ecchymosis over the mastoid process.
>   - **Raccoon Eyes:** Bilateral periorbital ecchymosis without direct eye trauma.
>   - **Hemotympanum** or CSF otorrhea/rhinorrhea (target/halo sign on gauze).
> - **Cushing Triad (Terminal Physiological Surge from Life-Threatening Raised ICP):**
>   1. **Severe Hypertension** (widened pulse pressure),
>   2. **Bradycardia**, AND
>   3. **Irregular, Cheyne-Stokes, or Apneic Respirations**.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> **Non-Contrast Head CT (NCCT — Gold Standard Imaging Modality):**
>
> - **Epidural Hematoma (EDH):** Homogeneous, **hyperdense biconvex (lens-shaped) extra-axial collection** abutting the inner skull table, confined by cranial sutures, most commonly underlying the squamous temporal bone.
> - **Subdural Hematoma (SDH):** **Crescentic (concave) extra-axial collection** tracking along the cerebral convexity, crossing cranial sutures:
>   - _Acute (<3 days):_ Hyperdense (bright white).
>   - _Subacute (3–21 days):_ Isodense to brain parenchyma.
>   - _Chronic (>21 days):_ Hypodense (dark gray/black, fluid-like).
> - **Cerebral Contusion:** Punctate, heterogeneous hyperdensities ("salt-and-pepper" appearance) with surrounding hypodense edema in the **inferior frontal and anterior temporal poles**.
> - **Diffuse Axonal Injury (DAI):** Small, punctate microhemorrhages ($<10	ext{ mm}$) at the **gray-white junction, corpus callosum, or dorsolateral midbrain**; confirmed definitively by **Brain MRI with Susceptibility-Weighted Imaging (SWI) or Gradient-Echo (GRE)**.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Initial Laboratory & Resuscitation Workup**
>
> - **STAT Coagulation & Hematology Panel:** **PT/INR, aPTT, Platelet count, Fibrinogen, Type and Screen** (mandatory in all trauma to detect and reverse **Trauma-Induced Coagulopathy [TIC]** or anticoagulant use).
> - **Arterial Blood Gas (ABG):** Mandatory to monitor ventilatory targets (**$	ext{PaO}_2 ge 100	ext{ mmHg}$**, **$	ext{PaCO}_2	ext{ 35–40 mmHg}$**).
> - **Blood Alcohol Level (BAC) & Urine Toxicology Screen.**
>
> **Imaging Protocols**
>
> - **Non-Contrast Head CT (NCCT):** Perform immediately based on decision rules (e.g., **Canadian CT Head Rule** for minor head injury: mandatory if GCS $<15$ at 2h, suspected open skull fracture, sign of basilar fracture, $ge 2$ episodes of vomiting, or age $ge 65$).
> - **CT Angiography (CTA) of the Head and Neck:** Mandatory if high risk for **Blunt Cerebrovascular Injury (BCVI — Denver Criteria)**: cervical spine fracture, basilar skull fracture involving the carotid canal, severe neck soft-tissue trauma, or focal deficit unexplained by CT.
>
> **Invasive Monitoring**
>
> - **Intracranial Pressure (ICP) Monitor (External Ventricular Drain [EVD] or Intraparenchymal Bolt):**
>   - Indicated in all **salvageable Severe TBI patients (GCS 3–8) with an abnormal Head CT**, OR
>   - Severe TBI (GCS 3–8) with a _normal_ Head CT if **$ge 2$ of the following are present**: (1) Age $>40$ years, (2) Unilateral/bilateral motor posturing, or (3) Systolic BP $<90	ext{ mmHg}$.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **GCS $le 8$** or acute drop in GCS by $ge 2$ points.
> - **Unilateral fixed, dilated pupil** (signals impending uncal herniation $ightarrow$ emergent hyperosmolar therapy + OR).
> - **Midline shift $ge 5	ext{ mm}$** or complete effacement of the basal cisterns on CT.
> - **Active anticoagulation** (Warfarin, DOACs, Heparin, or Dual Antiplatelets) with any intracranial hemorrhage.
> - **Cushing Triad** (hypertension, bradycardia, irregular breathing $ightarrow$ imminent brainstem herniation).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening Traumatic Mimics**
>
> - **Aneurysmal Subarachnoid Hemorrhage or Spontaneous Intracerebral Hemorrhage:** Aneurysmal rupture or stroke leading secondarily to a motor vehicle crash or ground-level fall.
> - **Tension Pneumothorax or Hemorrhagic Shock from Torso Trauma:** Concomitant shock in poly-trauma (remember: isolated intracranial hemorrhage in an adult **DOES NOT cause hemorrhagic shock**—if an adult with TBI is hypotensive, **look for occult bleeding in the chest, abdomen, pelvis, or extremities**!).
> - **Severe Acute Intoxication (Alcohol, Opioids, Sedatives):** Masks neurological baseline and depresses GCS.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals (Brain Trauma Foundation Guidelines)**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Maintain **Cerebral Perfusion Pressure ($	ext{CPP} = 	ext{MAP} - 	ext{ICP}$) between 60 and 70 mmHg**, maintain **ICP $<22	ext{ mmHg}$**, and strictly avoid secondary brain insults (**Hypotension and Hypoxia**).
> - **Secondary Target:** Immediate reversal of all coagulopathies, early surgical evacuation of mass lesions, and seizure prevention.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (Resuscitation & Hemodynamic Stabilization)**
>
> - **1. Secure Airway & Oxygenation:** **Intubate immediately via Rapid Sequence Intubation if GCS $le 8$**, loss of airway reflexes, or severe respiratory failure; maintain **$	ext{SpO}_2 ge 95%$ and $	ext{PaO}_2 ge 100	ext{ mmHg}$**; maintain strict **normocarbia ($	ext{PaCO}_2	ext{ 35–40 mmHg}$)**.
> - **2. Resuscitate Blood Pressure Aggressively:** **Infuse Isotonic Crystalloids (0.9% Normal Saline) or Blood Products to target $	ext{SBP} ge 100	ext{ mmHg}$ (ages 50–69) or $ge 110	ext{ mmHg}$ (ages 15–49 or $ge 70$)** (add **Norepinephrine** if MAP cannot be maintained with fluids alone to maintain CPP 60–70 mmHg).
> - **3. Reverse Coagulopathy Within <1 Hour:**
>   - **Warfarin:** **Administer 4-Factor Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (4F-PCC: 25–50 units/kg IV) PLUS 10 mg IV Vitamin K** immediately.
>   - **DOACs (Apixaban, Rivaroxaban):** **Administer Andexanet alfa** (or **4F-PCC 50 units/kg IV** if unavailable).
>   - **Dabigatran:** **Administer Idarucizumab (5 g IV)**.
>   - **Antiplatelets (Aspirin, Clopidogrel):** Consider DDAVP (0.3 mcg/kg IV) and platelet transfusion **ONLY IF undergoing emergent open neurosurgery**.
> - **4. Administer IV Tranexamic Acid (TXA: 1 g IV bolus over 10 min, then 1 g over 8 hours)** within **$le 3$ hours of injury** in mild-to-moderate TBI (CRASH-3 trial).
>
> **Emergency Tiered Management of Intracranial Hypertension (ICP >22 mmHg or Acute Herniation)**
>
> - **Tier 1 (Foundational Neurocritical Care):**
>   - Elevate Head of Bed to **30°** with the neck in neutral midline position to maximize cerebral venous drainage.
>   - Provide adequate sedation and analgesia (**Propofol + Fentanyl**) to prevent coughing, agitation, and ventilator dyssynchrony.
>   - Maintain strict **Normothermia (target core temp 36.0–37.0°C)** using targeted temperature management (fever dramatically accelerates brain metabolic rate and ICP!).
> - **Tier 2 (Hyperosmolar Therapy & CSF Drainage):**
>   - **Administer Hypertonic Saline (3% NaCl bolus 250 mL IV over 15 min, or 23.4% NaCl 30 mL via central line)** as preferred first-line therapy (especially if hypotensive; target serum sodium **145–155 mEq/L**).
>   - **Administer IV Mannitol (0.5 to 1.0 g/kg IV bolus)** as an alternative in euvolemic/hypervolemic patients (target serum osmolality $<320	ext{ mOsm/kg}$).
>   - Open the **External Ventricular Drain (EVD)** to drain CSF at 10–20 $	ext{cmH}_2	ext{O}$ pressure.
> - **Tier 3 (Refractory ICP Crisis / Surgical Salvage):**
>   - **Temporary Hyperventilation ($	ext{PaCO}_2	ext{ 30–35 mmHg}$):** Use **strictly as a temporary bridge for <1–2 hours** while wheeling the patient to the Operating Room (causes cerebral vasoconstriction).
>   - **Perform Emergent Decompressive Craniectomy** (large frontotemporoparietal bone flap $>12	imes 15	ext{ cm}$) or initiate **High-Dose Barbiturate Coma (Pentobarbital)**.
>
> **Neurosurgical Operative Indications**
>
> - **Epidural Hematoma (EDH):**
>   - **Perform Emergent Craniotomy and Hematoma Evacuation** if EDH volume **$>30	ext{ mL}$** (regardless of GCS), OR if GCS $<9$ with pupillary asymmetry.
> - **Acute Subdural Hematoma (SDH):**
>   - **Perform Emergent Craniotomy or Decompressive Craniectomy with Clot Evacuation** if SDH thickness **$>10	ext{ mm}$**, midline shift **$>5	ext{ mm}$**, or if GCS drops by $ge 2$ points with loss of pupillary reactivity.
> - **Chronic Subdural Hematoma (cSDH):**
>   - **Perform Burr-Hole Craniostomy with Subdural Closed-System Drainage** (with post-op flat bedrest for 24 hours) for symptomatic mass effect.
>   - Consider **Middle Meningeal Artery (MMA) Embolization** via Interventional Radiology for recurrent or high-risk chronic SDH.
>
> **Post-Traumatic Seizure Prophylaxis**
>
> - **Initiate IV Levetiracetam (Keppra: 500 to 1,000 mg IV BID) or Fosphenytoin for 7 DAYS ONLY** in all severe/moderate TBI patients to prevent **early post-traumatic seizures** (does _not_ prevent late post-traumatic epilepsy; discontinue after 7 days if no seizures occur).

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Systemic Hypotension ($	ext{SBP} <100–110	ext{ mmHg}$) and Hypoxia ($	ext{PaO}_2 <60	ext{ mmHg}$)** at all times; even a single brief episode dramatically increases secondary brain ischemia and doubles mortality!
> - **Avoid Hypotonic IV Fluids (0.45% Normal Saline, D5W, and Lactated Ringer's [mildly hypotonic])**, as free water rapidly crosses the damaged blood-brain barrier and triggers catastrophic **cerebral edema** (use **0.9% Normal Saline** exclusively).
> - **Avoid Corticosteroids (Methylprednisolone, Dexamethasone) in Acute Traumatic Brain Injury**, as the landmark multicenter **CRASH trial** demonstrated that high-dose corticosteroids significantly **increase mortality** in TBI.
> - **Avoid Prolonged Prophylactic Hyperventilation ($	ext{PaCO}_2 <30	ext{ mmHg}$)** during the first 24–48 hours, as severe hypocapnia induces profound cerebral vasoconstriction, causing **secondary cerebral ischemia and infarction**.
> - **Avoid Relying on a "Lucid Interval" to Reassure Discharge in Head Trauma**, as expanding epidural hematomas can cause sudden, fatal herniation hours after normal alert behavior.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Brainstem Herniation Syndromes (Uncal, Central Transtentorial, Tonsillar):** Massive mass effect pushes brain tissue across dural shelves $ightarrow$ compression of the midbrain, Duret hemorrhages, and brain death $ightarrow$ emergent craniotomy.
> - **Trauma-Induced Coagulopathy (TIC):** Tissue factor release + hypothermia + acidosis + consumption $ightarrow$ dramatic expansion of intracranial hematomas $ightarrow$ rapid balanced transfusion (1:1:1 pRBC:FFP:platelets) + TXA.
> - **Post-Traumatic Hydrocephalus:** Blood in the basal cisterns/arachnoid villi impairs CSF resorption $ightarrow$ progressive ventriculomegaly and neurological stagnation $ightarrow$ requires VP shunt placement.
> - **Post-Traumatic Epilepsy:** Occurs in 10–20% of patients with cortical contusions or intracranial hematomas.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 23-year-old male is struck on the right side of the head with a baseball bat during an altercation. He loses consciousness for 2 minutes, awakens fully oriented and alert (**GCS 15 — Lucid Interval**), but 90 minutes later develops a severe headache, projectile vomiting, and rapidly becomes unresponsive (**GCS drops to 6**). On arrival, vitals show BP 178/92 mmHg and HR 52 bpm (**Cushing Triad**), and his **right pupil is 6 mm and unreactive to light** while the left pupil is 3 mm and reactive. He is immediately intubated using rocuronium, administered a bolus of **3% Hypertonic Saline (250 mL)**, and a STAT non-contrast Head CT reveals a **40-mL hyperdense biconvex (lens-shaped) extra-axial hematoma** over the right temporal bone with 8 mm of midline shift (**Acute Epidural Hematoma**). He is rushed **immediately to the Operating Room for an emergent right temporal craniotomy, evacuation of hematoma, and ligation of the torn Middle Meningeal Artery**, resulting in complete neurological recovery.
