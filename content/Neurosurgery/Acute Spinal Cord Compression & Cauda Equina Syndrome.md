> [!abstract] Acute Spinal Cord Compression & Cauda Equina Syndrome
> **Acute Spinal Cord Compression (SCC):** Neurological emergency caused by extrinsic mass effect on the spinal cord (above L1–L2), resulting in **Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) myelopathy**, spastic paresis, and sensory loss below a discrete level.
> **Cauda Equina Syndrome (CES):** Compression of the lumbosacral nerve roots (below L1–L2), resulting in **Lower Motor Neuron (LMN) dysfunction**, flaccid paresis, saddle anesthesia, and neurogenic bladder/bowel dysfunction.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Spinal Cord Compression (above L1–L2):** Compression of the thecal sac $ightarrow$ obstruction of epidural venous plexuses $ightarrow$ vasogenic cord edema $ightarrow$ arterial ischemia, cellular necrosis, and irreversible axonal transection (**Metastatic Epidural SCC [MESCC, ~85%]: Prostate, Breast, Lung, Renal, Myeloma**; also epidural abscess/hematoma).
> - **Cauda Equina (below L1–L2):** Mechanical compression of the lumbar and sacral roots (**L2–S5**) lacking a sturdy epineurium $ightarrow$ ischemia of parasympathetic sacral fibers (**massive central L4–L5 or L5–S1 disc herniation in ~90%**).

> [!tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **The 48-Hour Golden Window in CES:** **Decompressive surgery within <24–48 hours** of onset—specifically _before_ complete painless urinary retention and overflow incontinence develop—is the single greatest predictor of permanent bowel, bladder, and erectile function recovery!
> - **Check Bladder Post-Void Residual (PVR) Immediately:** Urinary dysfunction in CES presents as **overflow incontinence from a flaccid, atonic bladder**; a **PVR >100–200 mL** (or retention >500 mL) has a **>90% sensitivity/negative predictive value** for CES. A normal PVR (<100 mL) makes complete CES highly unlikely!
> - **Steroids FIRST in Malignancy (Do NOT Wait for MRI!):** In suspected Metastatic Epidural Spinal Cord Compression (MESCC), **administer IV Dexamethasone (10–16 mg IV bolus) IMMEDIATELY**; pre-treatment motor status is the #1 predictor of whether the patient will ever walk again.
> - **Order a WHOLE-SPINE MRI, Not Just Lumbar:** In suspected metastatic compression, **$>20–30%$ of patients harbor non-contiguous "skip" metastases** elsewhere in the spine, and the true compression level is often thoracic, despite lumbar symptoms.
> - **Ask About Wiping Sensation:** Inability to feel toilet paper when wiping the perineum after defecation is one of the most specific, early physical markers of **Saddle Anesthesia (S3–S5)**!

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Spinal Pain (>90% of cases):** Severe, unremitting, localized back pain; **worse when lying flat / recumbent at night** (distinguishes malignant/infectious pain from benign mechanical back pain, which improves with recumbency); band-like radicular radiation around chest/abdomen.
> - **Motor Dysfunction:** Heaviness, stiffness, dragging of feet, tripping over carpets, inability to rise from a chair or climb stairs.
> - **Sensory Loss:** Ascending numbness or paresthesias tracking up to a distinct trunk level (cord), or numbness confined to the buttocks, perineum, and inner thighs (**Saddle Anesthesia**).
> - **Autonomic / Sphincter Dysfunction:** Difficulty starting urination, loss of urinary sensation, urinary urgency progressing to **overflow incontinence** (dribbling), loss of anal sphincter control, or new erectile dysfunction.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Spine Exam:** Focal percussion tenderness over the affected spinous processes (classic in metastasis and **Spinal Epidural Abscess**).
> - **Sensory Mapping:** Discrete **sensory level** on the trunk (T4 = nipples; T10 = umbilicus; T12/L1 = groin) in cord compression; **saddle hypoesthesia (S3–S5)** on light touch/pinprick over buttocks and perianal ring.
> - **Reflex Exam:**
>   - _Cord Compression:_ **Hyperreflexia** below the lesion, patellar/ankle **clonus**, and positive **Babinski sign**.
>   - _Cauda Equina:_ **Depressed or absent reflexes (absent Achilles and patellar jerks)**; downgoing Babinski signs.
> - **Digital Rectal Exam (DRE):** Decreased or absent resting anal sphincter tone; **absent anal wink (S4–S5)**; **absent bulbocavernosus reflex (S2–S4)** (loss of anal contraction when squeezing the glans penis or tugging the Foley catheter).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Spinal Cord Compression (SCC / MESCC):** Radiologic demonstration on **Whole-Spine MRI** of direct indentation or displacement of the spinal cord by an extradural or intradural mass, with thecal sac narrowing $pm$ intramedullary cord hyperintensity on T2/STIR (myelomalacia/edema).
> - **Cauda Equina Syndrome (CES):** Documented presence of neurogenic sphincter disturbance (PVR $>100–200	ext{ mL}$ or incontinence), saddle anesthesia, and/or bilateral lower extremity sensorimotor loss **CONFIRMED by Lumbosacral MRI** demonstrating severe mechanical compression of the thecal sac/cauda equina nerve roots below the conus medullaris.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Bedside Assessment**
>
> - **Bladder Ultrasound (Post-Void Residual [PVR]):** **Mandatory immediate bedside test**; perform immediately after voluntary voiding (or place a straight catheter). **PVR $>100–200	ext{ mL}$ confirms neurogenic urinary retention**; PVR $>500	ext{ mL}$ indicates high-grade decompensation.
>
> **Gold-Standard Imaging**
>
> - **Emergent Whole-Spine MRI with and without IV Gadolinium (Cervical, Thoracic, and Lumbar):**
>   - **Mandatory test of choice** (Sensitivity and Specificity $>95%$).
>   - _Why Whole-Spine?_ The clinical level frequently underestimates the anatomical level, and **$>20–30%$ of patients with spinal metastases have multiple non-contiguous lesions**!
>   - _Gadolinium:_ Essential to delineate **Spinal Epidural Abscess** (rim-enhancing collection), leptomeningeal carcinomatosis, and intramedullary tumors.
> - **CT Myelography (Lumbar Puncture with Intrathecal Contrast + CT Spine):** Reserved strictly for patients with absolute contraindications to MRI (non-MRI compatible pacemakers, metallic implants, or extreme claustrophobia).
>
> **Laboratory Diagnostics**
>
> - **ESR and CRP:** Markedly elevated in **Spinal Epidural Abscess** (sensitivity $>98%$) and vertebral osteomyelitis/discitis.
> - **Blood Cultures ($	imes 2$ sets):** Mandatory prior to antibiotics if fever, elevated inflammatory markers, or epidural abscess is suspected (_Staphylococcus aureus_ is the causative agent in $>60–70%$).
> - **Coagulation Panel & Platelets:** Mandatory to evaluate for **Spinal Epidural Hematoma** in anticoagulated patients.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **New or progressive bilateral leg weakness** or gait ataxia.
> - **Saddle anesthesia** (perianal, perineal, or buttock numbness).
> - **New urinary retention, overflow dribbling, or fecal incontinence**.
> - **Unremitting night pain or progressive localized back pain in a patient with active cancer** (MESCC until proven otherwise!).
> - **Triad of Spinal Epidural Abscess:** (1) Severe localized back pain + (2) Fever + (3) Progressive neurological deficits.
> - Sudden paraplegia within hours of an epidural catheter procedure or lumbar puncture (Spinal Epidural Hematoma).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Surgical Emergencies**
>
> - **Spinal Epidural Abscess:** Fever, severe localized spine pain, elevated ESR/CRP; rapid progression from back pain $ightarrow$ radicular pain $ightarrow$ motor weakness $ightarrow$ complete paralysis.
> - **Spinal Epidural Hematoma:** Sudden, knife-like back pain followed immediately by rapid paraplegia in an anticoagulated patient or following neuraxial anesthesia/trauma.
> - **Acute Aortic Dissection / Anterior Spinal Artery Syndrome:** Acute painless or painful infarction of the anterior two-thirds of the spinal cord (Artery of Adamkiewicz ischemia); bilateral paraplegia and loss of pain/temperature sensation with **PRESERVED vibration and proprioception (dorsal columns spared!)**.
> - **Acute Transverse Myelitis (ATM):** Rapidly progressive bilateral myelopathy with distinct sensory level; post-infectious, MS, or NMOSD (requires high-dose IV steroids/PLEX).
>
> **Most Likely Non-Emergent Mimics**
>
> - **Acute Lumbar Radiculopathy (Simple Sciatica):** Unilateral dermatomal radiating pain with preserved bowel/bladder function, normal PVR, and normal perineal sensation.
> - **Neurogenic Claudication (Lumbar Spinal Stenosis):** Bilateral leg pain/heaviness provoked by walking and spinal extension, **completely relieved by sitting down or leaning forward ("shopping cart sign")**; normal at rest.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Immediate mechanical decompression of the thecal sac (**within <24–48 hours for CES; emergent for progressive SCC**), preservation/recovery of independent ambulation, and preservation of urinary/fecal continence.
> - **Secondary Target:** Spinal column stabilization, systemic oncologic/infection control, and prevention of decubitus ulcers and urosepsis.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **1. Administer High-Dose IV Corticosteroids Immediately (in Malignant Compression [MESCC]):**
>   - **Administer Dexamethasone (10 to 16 mg IV bolus, followed by 4 mg IV q6h)** immediately upon clinical suspicion of MESCC **BEFORE waiting for MRI confirmation** to reduce vasogenic cord edema and restore microvascular perfusion (_Add a PPI for gastroprotection_).
> - **2. Place an Indwelling Foley Catheter:** Insert a urinary catheter immediately in all patients with **urinary retention (PVR $>100–200	ext{ mL}$)** or overflow incontinence to decompress the atonic bladder and prevent renal failure.
> - **3. Emergent Surgical Consultation (Neurosurgery / Orthopedic Spine):** Contact spine surgery STAT upon identifying motor deficits, saddle anesthesia, or confirmed imaging compression.
>
> **Definitive Management: Cauda Equina Syndrome (CES)**
>
> - **Perform Emergent Surgical Decompression (Lumbar Laminectomy and Microdiscectomy within <24 to 48 Hours)**:
>   - Surgical emergency; operative decompression performed **within 24–48 hours of onset** significantly improves long-term urinary, bowel, and sexual outcomes compared to delayed surgery.
>
> **Definitive Management: Metastatic Cord Compression (MESCC)**
>
> - **Surgical Decompression PLUS Radiation Therapy (The Gold Standard — Patchell Trial):**
>   - **Perform Direct Circumferential Surgical Decompression with Spinal Instrumentation/Stabilization followed by Radiotherapy (within 2–3 weeks)** for patients with: (1) **Spinal instability (SINS score $ge 7$)**, (2) **Intractable pain**, (3) **Radio-resistant tumors (Renal cell, Melanoma, GI adenocarcinoma)**, (4) **Unknown primary requiring tissue biopsy**, or (5) **Bony retropulsion compressing the cord**.
> - **Primary External Beam Radiation Therapy (EBRT / Stereotactic Body Radiotherapy [SBRT]):**
>   - Indicated as primary therapy for: (1) **Highly radiosensitive tumors (Lymphoma, Myeloma, Small Cell Lung Cancer, Seminoma)**, (2) Patients who are **poor surgical candidates** or have a short life expectancy ($<3$ months), or (3) Patients with established, complete paraplegia $>48$ hours.
>
> **Definitive Management: Spinal Epidural Abscess**
>
> - **Perform Emergent Surgical Decompression (Laminectomy and Evacuation of Pus)** combined with **Empiric IV Antibiotics (IV Vancomycin 15–20 mg/kg q8–12h PLUS Cefepime 2 g IV q8h or Meropenem)** for 4 to 6 weeks (_Non-operative medical management with antibiotics alone is reserved strictly for complete paraplegia $>48–72$ hours or prohibitive surgical risk_).

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid High-Dose Corticosteroids in Suspected Spinal Epidural Abscess**, as immunosuppression worsens bacteremia and sepsis without clinical benefit (steroids are indicated for **tumor-related vasogenic edema**, NOT bacterial infections!).
> - **Avoid Ordering Only a Lumbar Spine MRI when Spinal Cord Compression is Suspected**, as the spinal cord ends at L1–L2; imaging only the lumbar spine completely misses thoracic and cervical cord compression (always order a **Whole-Spine MRI**).
> - **Avoid Delaying Surgical Decompression in Cauda Equina Syndrome Beyond 48 Hours**, as irreversible permanent bladder, bowel, and sexual dysfunction become fixed once complete retention is established.
> - **Avoid Performing a Lumbar Puncture in Suspected Spinal Epidural Abscess or Complete Cord Block**, to prevent introducing bacteria into the subarachnoid space (causing purulent meningitis) or precipitating acute downward cord herniation.
> - **Avoid Relying on Normal Voluntary Voiding to Rule Out Cauda Equina Syndrome**, as patients with overflow incontinence frequently "void" small volumes while retaining $>500	ext{ mL}$ in a paralyzed bladder (always check a **Post-Void Residual**!).

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Permanent Paraplegia / Quadriplegia:** Complete, irreversible loss of voluntary motor function below the lesion level.
> - **Permanent Neurogenic Bladder & Fecal Incontinence:** Lifelong requirement for clean intermittent catheterization or indwelling suprapubic catheter; loss of rectal sphincter tone.
> - **Permanent Erectile Dysfunction & Sexual Anesthesia:** Loss of S2–S4 parasympathetic outflow.
> - **Urosepsis, Hydronephrosis, & Renal Failure:** Secondary to chronic, unperceived urinary retention and high-pressure detrusor-sphincter dyssynergia.
> - **Severe Decubitus Pressure Ulcers & Sepsis:** Resulting from immobility and sensory denervation.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 64-year-old man with a history of metastatic prostate cancer presents to the ED with 2 weeks of progressively worsening mid-thoracic back pain that awakens him from sleep, followed over the past 24 hours by leg heaviness and difficulty climbing stairs. Neurological examination reveals 3/5 weakness in the bilateral hip flexors and quadriceps, brisk 3+ patellar reflexes, bilateral clonus, a **distinct sensory level to pinprick at the umbilicus (T10)**, and an immediate bladder scan revealing **650 mL of acute urinary retention (elevated PVR)**. High-dose **IV Dexamethasone (16 mg IV bolus)** is administered immediately in the emergency department, an indwelling Foley catheter is placed, and an emergent **Whole-Spine MRI** reveals a pathologic T9 vertebral burst metastasis with epidural tumor extension causing **severe spinal cord compression**. He is taken emergently to the neurosurgical OR for **circumferential surgical decompression and posterior pedicle screw stabilization**, followed by adjuvant radiotherapy, successfully preserving his ability to walk.
