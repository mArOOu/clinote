> [!abstract] Inflammatory Bowel Disease
> Chronic, relapsing-remitting immune-mediated inflammation of the gastrointestinal tract comprising **Ulcerative Colitis (UC)** (continuous **mucosal/submucosal** inflammation starting at the rectum and extending proximally, restricted to the colon) and **Crohn's Disease (CD)** (patchy **"skip" transmural** inflammation affecting any segment from mouth to anus, most commonly the terminal ileum and right colon).

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Dysregulated mucosal immunity** against commensal gut microbiota in genetically susceptible hosts (**NOD2/CARD15** mutation in Crohn's):
>   - **Crohn's Disease ($\text{T}_\text{H}1 / \text{T}_\text{H}17$-mediated):** **Transmural** inflammation $\rightarrow$ deep fissuring ulcers ("cobblestoning"), **non-caseating granulomas**, fibrotic **strictures**, sinus tracts, **fistulas**, and intra-abdominal abscesses.
>   - **Ulcerative Colitis ($\text{T}_\text{H}2$-mediated):** **Superficial mucosal/submucosal** inflammation $\rightarrow$ friable mucosa, **crypt abscesses**, **pseudopolyps**, and loss of haustra ("lead-pipe" colon).

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Steroids are for INDUCTION only—NEVER maintenance:** Systemic corticosteroids (prednisone/methylprednisolone) or budesonide must **never** be used for long-term maintenance of UC or CD; always transition to a steroid-sparing biologic, small molecule, or immunomodulator.
> - **5-ASA (Mesalamine) works in UC, NOT Crohn's:** Oral/rectal **5-ASAs** are the cornerstone of mild-to-moderate **Ulcerative Colitis**, but are **ineffective** and **no longer recommended** for Crohn's Disease (which is transmural).
> - **Rule out _C. difficile_ and CMV in every flare:** Every hospitalized IBD flare requires **stool _C. difficile_ testing**, and steroid-refractory UC requires flexible sigmoidoscopy with biopsy to rule out **CMV colitis**.
> - **Extraintestinal activity vs. bowel activity:** **Erythema nodosum, peripheral arthritis, and episcleritis** parallel intestinal flare activity; **Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis (PSC), ankylosing spondylitis/sacroiliitis, uveitis, and pyoderma gangrenosum** run an **independent** course (though colectomy does _not_ cure PSC or axial spondyloarthropathy).

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Ulcerative Colitis (UC):** **Frequent, small-volume bloody diarrhea** with mucus, **tenesmus**, urgency, nocturnal bowel movements, and LLQ cramping relieved by defecation; **smoking is protective** (flares often begin _after_ smoking cessation).
> - **Crohn's Disease (CD):** **RLQ abdominal pain** (mimics appendicitis), chronic non-bloody (or variably bloody) watery diarrhea, low-grade fever, **weight loss**, severe fatigue, oral **aphthous ulcers**, and **perianal pain/drainage**; **smoking worsens CD** and increases postoperative recurrence.
> - **Extraintestinal Manifestations (EIMs) (25–40%):**
>   - _Musculoskeletal:_ Migratory peripheral oligoarthritis (most common EIM) or HLA-B27 axial spondyloarthritis.
>   - _Dermatologic:_ **Erythema nodosum** (tender red nodules on shins) and **Pyoderma gangrenosum** (painful violaceous ulcer with undermined borders; shows **pathergy**—never debride surgically!).
>   - _Ocular:_ **Episcleritis** (mild burning/injection) and **Anterior Uveitis** (painful red eye, photophobia, blurred vision $\rightarrow$ ophthalmic emergency).
>   - _Hepatobiliary:_ **Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis (PSC)** (strongly associated with **UC**; elevated ALP/GGT, "beading" of bile ducts on MRCP; vastly increases cholangiocarcinoma and colorectal cancer risk) and **cholesterol/pigment gallstones** (in ileal Crohn's due to bile acid malabsorption).
>   - _Renal:_ **Calcium oxalate nephrolithiasis** (in ileal Crohn's: unabsorbed fat binds intraluminal $\text{Ca}^{2+}$, leaving free oxalate to be hyperabsorbed in the colon = **enteric hyperoxaluria**).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Vital signs:** Fever and tachycardia indicate a severe flare, abscess, or **Acute Severe Ulcerative Colitis (ASUC)**.
> - **Abdominal exam:** **RLQ fullness/mass** in ileocecal Crohn's; **LLQ tenderness** in UC; **rebound/guarding, tympany, and absent bowel sounds** in toxic megacolon or perforation.
> - **Perianal / Rectal exam (Mandatory):** **Large eccentric anal fissures, complex perianal fistulas, perirectal abscesses, or large edematous skin tags ("elephant ears")** are pathognomonic for **Crohn's Disease** (present in ~30%).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> **Ileocolonoscopy with Mucosal Biopsies** (Gold Standard — requires chronic inflammation criteria plus exclusion of infectious colitis):
>
> - **Ulcerative Colitis:**
>   - _Endoscopy:_ **Continuous, circumferential mucosal inflammation** beginning at the anal verge (**rectum is ALWAYS involved** prior to therapy) and extending proximally (proctitis, left-sided colitis, or pancolitis) with an abrupt transition to normal mucosa; loss of vascular pattern, friability, ulcers, and **pseudopolyps**.
>   - _Histology:_ **Mucosal/submucosal inflammation only**, **crypt architectural distortion** (branching/shortening), **cryptitis and crypt abscesses** (neutrophils in crypt lumen), and basal plasmacytosis.
> - **Crohn's Disease:**
>   - _Endoscopy:_ **Discontinuous "skip lesions"** (patchy inflammation alternating with normal mucosa), **rectal sparing**, **terminal ileal involvement**, aphthous ulcers coalescing into deep longitudinal/serpiginous ulcers (**"cobblestoning"**), strictures, and fistula orifices.
>   - _Histology:_ **Transmural focal chronic inflammation** and **non-caseating granulomas** (highly specific, though found on mucosal biopsy in only ~20–30%).

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **Initial & Flare Workup:** CBC (iron-deficiency/anemia of chronic disease, thrombocytosis), CMP (hypoalbuminemia predicts severe disease/biologic clearance), **CRP and ESR**, **Fecal Calprotectin** (**>150–250 µg/g** indicates active mucosal inflammation; best non-invasive marker to monitor treatment response and differentiate IBD flare from IBS), **Stool _C. difficile_ toxin/PCR** + routine stool multiplex PCR.
> - **Nutritional Screen (especially Crohn's):** Iron panel/ferritin, **Vitamin B12** (absorbed in terminal ileum), folate, Vitamin D, zinc.
> - **Pre-Biologic / Immunomodulator Mandatory Safety Screen:**
>   - **Latent Tuberculosis:** Interferon-Gamma Release Assay (**QuantiFERON-TB Gold**) + Chest X-Ray (mandatory before Anti-TNF, JAK inhibitors, IL-12/23, or IL-23 inhibitors).
>   - **Hepatitis B Virus:** **HBsAg, Anti-HBs, and Total Anti-HBc** (Anti-TNF triggers fatal HBV reactivation).
>   - **TPMT (Thiopurine Methyltransferase) & NUDT15 enzyme/genotype:** Mandatory before starting **Azathioprine or 6-MP** to prevent fatal bone marrow suppression/agranulocytosis.
> - **Serologies (Adjunctive only, NOT diagnostic):** **p-ANCA** (~60–70% of UC); **ASCA** (Anti-_Saccharomyces cerevisiae_ antibodies, ~60% of Crohn's).
>
> **Imaging**
>
> - **CT Enterography (CTE) or MR Enterography (MRE):** Mandatory at diagnosis of **Crohn's Disease** to stage the **small bowel** (identify proximal skip lesions, ** creeping fat**, **"string sign"** strictures, enteroenteric/enterovesical fistulas, or intra-abdominal abscesses; prefer MRE in young patients to avoid radiation).
> - **Pelvic MRI OR Exam Under Anesthesia (EUA) + Endoscopic Ultrasound (EUS):** Gold standard to evaluate **perianal fistulizing Crohn's Disease**.
> - **Supine Abdominal X-Ray (KUB) or CT Abdomen/Pelvis:** Order STAT in severe flares to rule out **Toxic Megacolon** (**transverse colon diameter >6 cm** with loss of haustra) or free perforation.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Acute Severe Ulcerative Colitis (ASUC) (Truelove and Witts Criteria):** **≥6 bloody stools/day** PLUS **≥1 marker of systemic toxicity** (**T >37.8°C**, **HR >90 bpm**, **Hb <10.5 g/dL**, or **ESR >30 mm/hr / CRP >30 mg/L**) $\rightarrow$ requires emergent admission.
> - **Toxic Megacolon:** Transverse colon dilation **>6 cm** + systemic toxicity (fever, tachycardia, leukocytosis, hypotension, altered mental status).
> - **High fever + focal RLQ/pelvic mass or peritonitis** in Crohn's ($\rightarrow$ **intra-abdominal or perirectal abscess**; must drain _before_ giving immunosuppressants).
> - **Complete Small Bowel Obstruction** (stricture in Crohn's).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Infectious Colitis (_C. difficile_, _Campylobacter_, _Shigella_, _Salmonella_, EHEC, _Entamoeba histolytica_, CMV):** Mimics an acute IBD flare or new-onset UC; **never start high-dose steroids for presumed new-onset IBD until infectious causes (especially _Entamoeba_ and _C. diff_) are excluded**.
> - **Ischemic Colitis:** Older patients (>60 y) with sudden cramping LLQ pain and hematochezia; segmental distribution at watershed areas (splenic flexure, rectosigmoid) with **rectal sparing**.
> - **Intestinal Tuberculosis or _Yersinia enterocolitica_ Ileocecitis:** Mimics terminal ileal Crohn's disease with caseating/non-caseating granulomas and RLQ pain; must rule out TB in endemic populations before giving Anti-TNF!
> - **Primary Intestinal Lymphoma / Colorectal Adenocarcinoma:** Mimics stricturing Crohn's or UC.
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS):** Normal **Fecal Calprotectin (<50 µg/g)**, normal CRP, no weight loss, no blood, no nocturnal diarrhea.
> - **NSAID-Induced Enteropathy / Colopathy:** Ulcers and diaphragm-like strictures in the small bowel or colon.
> - **Radiation Colitis / Proctopathy:** History of pelvic radiotherapy.
> - **Diverticular-Associated Colitis (SCAD):** Segmental inflammation confined to the sigmoid colon in elderly patients with diverticulosis, sparing the rectum.
>
> **Less Common**
>
> - **Behçet Disease:** Recurrent oral and genital ulcers, uveitis, and deep punched-out ileocecal ulcers mimicking Crohn's.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target (STRIDE-II "Treat-to-Target"):** **Clinical Remission** (resolution of rectal bleeding/diarrhea/pain), **Biochemical Normalization** (normal **CRP** and **Fecal Calprotectin <150 µg/g**), AND **Endoscopic Mucosal Healing** (Mayo endoscopic subscore 0–1 in UC; absence of ulcers in CD).
> - **Secondary Target:** **Steroid-free remission**, prevention of bowel damage (strictures, fistulas, dysplasia), and avoidance of permanent ostomy.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (Acute Severe Ulcerative Colitis & Toxic Megacolon)**
>
> - **Admit to hospital, start IV Methylprednisolone (60 mg IV daily or 20 mg IV q8h)**, **administer prophylactic subcutaneous LMWH (Enoxaparin)** (ASUC carries a massive **3–4× risk of DVT/PE**; DVT prophylaxis is **safe and mandatory EVEN with active rectal bleeding!**), correct electrolytes ($\text{K}^+, \text{Mg}^{2+}$), send stool for _C. difficile_, and perform an **unprepped flexible sigmoidoscopy with biopsy for CMV** within 24–48 hours.
> - **Administer Rescue Therapy at Day 3–5** with **Infliximab (5–10 mg/kg IV)** OR **IV Cyclosporine (2 mg/kg/day)** (or Upadacitinib in biologic-experienced patients) if the patient fails to respond to 72 hours of IV corticosteroids (still having >8 stools/day, or 3–8 stools/day with CRP >45 mg/L).
> - **Consult Colorectal Surgery on Day 1 of admission** and **perform emergent Subtotal Colectomy with End Ileostomy** if the patient fails 4–7 days of rescue therapy, or immediately if **Toxic Megacolon** fails to improve within 24–48 hours of medical therapy (or if free perforation/massive hemorrhage occurs).
>
> **Ulcerative Colitis Stepwise Outpatient Management**
>
> - **Mild-to-Moderate Ulcerative Proctitis / Left-Sided UC:** **Initiate Topical (Rectal) 5-Aminosalicylate (Mesalamine 1 g suppository daily for proctitis; Mesalamine enema for left-sided colitis) PLUS Oral Mesalamine (≥2.4–4.8 g/day)** for induction AND lifelong maintenance (combined oral + rectal 5-ASA is superior to either alone).
> - **Mild-to-Moderate Pancolitis Failing 5-ASA:** **Add Budesonide MMX (9 mg PO daily for 8 weeks)** (colonic-release steroid with minimal systemic toxicity) or a short course of **Oral Prednisone (40 mg daily, tapered over 8 weeks)** for **induction only**, then maintain on 5-ASA or escalate to advanced therapy.
> - **Moderate-to-Severe UC:** **Initiate Advanced Biologic or Small-Molecule Therapy for BOTH Induction and Maintenance** (skipping 5-ASAs):
>   - **First-line Biologics:** **Infliximab** (preferred over adalimumab; combine with **Azathioprine** to prevent anti-drug antibodies per **SONIC/UC-SUCCESS trials**), **Vedolizumab** (gut-selective $\alpha_4\beta_7$ integrin inhibitor; safest profile, preferred in elderly/infection-prone), **Ustekinumab** (anti-IL-12/23), or **Risankizumab / Mirikizumab / Guselkumab** (selective anti-IL-23p19).
>   - **Oral Small Molecules:** **Upadacitinib** or **Tofacitinib** (JAK inhibitors; extremely rapid onset, reserved in US after TNF failure) or **Ozanimod / Etrasimod** (S1P receptor modulators).
>
> **Crohn's Disease Stepwise Management**
>
> - **Mild-to-Moderate Ileocecal Crohn's Disease:** **Induce remission with Controlled-Ileal-Release Oral Budesonide (9 mg PO daily for 8 weeks, then taper)** (_Note: Do NOT use 5-ASA/Mesalamine for Crohn's; it is ineffective_).
> - **Moderate-to-Severe Luminal Crohn's Disease (or High-Risk Features: Age <30, deep ulcers, extensive small bowel disease, stricturing/penetrating phenotype):** **Initiate Early Top-Down Biologic Therapy for Induction and Maintenance** (use a short course of oral **Prednisone 40–60 mg/day** as a bridge if severely symptomatic):
>   - **First-line Biologics:** **Anti-TNF (Infliximab or Adalimumab)** (ideally combined with **Azathioprine/6-MP or Methotrexate** to prevent immunogenicity and loss of response), **Risankizumab** or **Ustekinumab** (IL-23 / IL-12/23 inhibitors), or **Vedolizumab**.
>   - **Refractory CD:** **Upadacitinib** (selective JAK1 inhibitor).
> - **Fistulizing / Perianal Crohn's Disease:** **Drain any perirectal abscess via Surgical Exam Under Anesthesia (EUA) + placement of non-cutting Setons** FIRST, administer **Ciprofloxacin (500 mg BID) + Metronidazole (500 mg BID)**, and initiate **Infliximab (first-line biologic with strongest evidence for fistula closure) + Thiopurine**.
> - **Intra-Abdominal Abscess (Penetrating CD):** **Administer IV antibiotics + CT-guided percutaneous drainage** (holding steroids/biologics until sepsis is controlled), followed by delayed medical optimization or elective surgical resection.
>
> **Surgical Indications & Cancer Surveillance**
>
> - **Elective Surgery in Ulcerative Colitis (Total Proctocolectomy with Ileal Pouch-Anal Anastomosis [IPAA]):** Indicated for medically refractory disease, steroid dependence, or **colonic dysplasia/adenocarcinoma** (**Surgery is CURATIVE for colonic UC**, though patients may later develop **pouchitis**, treated with **ciprofloxacin/metronidazole**).
> - **Surgery in Crohn's Disease (Bowel-Sparing Strictureplasty or Limited Segmental Resection):** Indicated ONLY for **fibrotic strictures causing obstruction**, medically refractory fistulas/abscesses, or perforation (**Surgery is NOT curative in CD**; ~70–80% develop endoscopic recurrence at the anastomosis within 1 year $\rightarrow$ start postoperative biologic prophylaxis and perform **surveillance colonoscopy 6–12 months post-resection**).
> - **Colorectal Cancer Surveillance Colonoscopy (with Dye Spray Chromoendoscopy or High-Definition White Light):**
>   - **Start 8 years after symptom onset** in all patients with **UC extending proximal to the rectum (left-sided or pancolitis)** or **Crohn's colitis involving ≥1/3 of the colon**, and repeat **every 1–3 years**.
>   - **Start IMMEDIATELY at the time of diagnosis and repeat EVERY 1 YEAR** if the patient has **Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis (PSC)** (plus **annual MRCP + CA 19-9** to screen for cholangiocarcinoma and gallbladder polyps).

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Opioids, Anticholinergics/Antispasmodics, Loperamide, and NSAIDs** during an acute IBD flare, as opioids/anticholinergics/loperamide paralyze colonic motility and directly precipitate **Toxic Megacolon**, while **NSAIDs** trigger mucosal ulceration and IBD relapses.
> - **Avoid Complete Colonoscopy or Barium Enema in Acute Severe Ulcerative Colitis (ASUC) or Toxic Megacolon** due to the extreme risk of **iatrogenic colonic perforation** (perform only a gentle, unprepped **flexible sigmoidoscopy** with minimal air insufflation to biopsy for CMV).
> - **Avoid holding DVT prophylaxis (LMWH) in hospitalized IBD patients with hematochezia**, as active IBD is a profound prothrombotic state and subcutaneous heparin does NOT worsen non-life-threatening mucosal rectal bleeding.
> - **Avoid starting Anti-TNF agents (Infliximab, Adalimumab) in patients with an undrained intra-abdominal or perianal abscess**, active latent TB/HBV, **NYHA Class III/IV Heart Failure**, or **demyelinating disease (Multiple Sclerosis)** to prevent fatal sepsis, heart failure decompensation, or demyelination.
> - **Avoid Surgical Debridement of Pyoderma Gangrenosum**, as trauma triggers **pathergy** (massive necrotic expansion of the ulcer); treat medically with **systemic corticosteroids or Infliximab**.
> - **Avoid Live-Attenuated Vaccines (MMR, Varicella, Live Zoster, Yellow Fever)** once a patient is on biologics, JAK inhibitors, thiopurines, or prednisone ≥20 mg/day (use the **recombinant non-live Shingrix vaccine** for patients on JAK inhibitors or biologics).

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Toxic Megacolon:** Non-obstructive colonic dilation **>6 cm** with systemic toxicity (driven by inducible nitric oxide synthase paralysis of smooth muscle in UC > CD) $\rightarrow$ NPO, NG tube decompression, stop all opioids/anticholinergics, IV fluids, **IV broad-spectrum antibiotics + IV Methylprednisolone**, and **emergent subtotal colectomy** if no improvement within 24–48 hours.
> - **Short Bowel Syndrome & Malabsorption:** Repeated extensive small bowel resections (>100–200 cm) in Crohn's disease $\rightarrow$ **Vitamin B12 deficiency** (megaloblastic anemia, subacute combined degeneration), **bile acid malabsorption** (secretory diarrhea + gallstones), **enteric hyperoxaluria** (calcium oxalate kidney stones), and fat-soluble vitamin (A, D, E, K) deficiencies.
> - **Enterovesical / Colovesical Fistula (Crohn's):** Presents with **pneumaturia** (passing air/bubbles in urine), **fecaluria**, and recurrent polymicrobial UTIs $\rightarrow$ diagnose with CT cystography/MRE; treat with anti-TNF + surgical fistula takedown.
> - **Colorectal Adenocarcinoma:** Risk increases with **duration (>8–10 years)**, **anatomic extent** (pancolitis > left-sided > proctitis), severity of inflammation, and **concomitant PSC** (5× higher risk).

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> **Scenario 1 (Acute Severe UC):** A 26-year-old man with ulcerative pancolitis presents with 9 bloody bowel movements per day, fever (38.3°C), HR 106 bpm, Hb 9.8 g/dL, and CRP 65 mg/L (**ASUC**). Stool _C. difficile_ is negative and an abdominal X-ray shows a transverse colon diameter of 4.5 cm (no toxic megacolon). He is admitted, placed on **subcutaneous enoxaparin for DVT prophylaxis** (despite bloody stools), started on **IV Methylprednisolone 60 mg/day**, undergoes an **unprepped flexible sigmoidoscopy** (excluding CMV), and receives **rescue Infliximab on Day 3** after failing to respond to steroids.
> **Scenario 2 (Crohn's Disease):** A 22-year-old woman who smokes presents with 4 months of RLQ crampy pain, 15-lb weight loss, non-bloody diarrhea, oral aphthous ulcers, and a draining **perianal fistula**. Ileocolonoscopy shows rectal sparing, patchy colonic "skip" ulcers, and severe terminal ileal cobblestoning with non-caseating granulomas on biopsy. After ruling out latent TB and Hepatitis B and performing a surgical exam under anesthesia with **seton placement**, she is counselled on smoking cessation and started on **Infliximab + Azathioprine**.
