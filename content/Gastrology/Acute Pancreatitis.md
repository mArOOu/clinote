> [!abstract] Acute Pancreatitis
> Acute, sterile inflammatory autodigestion of the exocrine pancreas with variable peripancreatic tissue and remote multi-organ involvement, most commonly triggered by gallstones (40%) or heavy alcohol use (30%).

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Premature intra-acinar trypsinogen activation** (via ductal hypertension/obstruction from gallstones or direct acinar toxicity from alcohol/hypertriglyceridemia) $\rightarrow$ zymogen cascade activation (elastase, phospholipase A2) $\rightarrow$ parenchymal autodigestion and fat necrosis.
> - **Cytokine storm (TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6)** $\rightarrow$ systemic capillary leak, massive third-spacing (intravascular depletion), SIRS, and multi-organ failure (ARDS, AKI).
> - **Hypocalcemia:** Free fatty acids released by lipolysis bind extracellular calcium (**saponification**).

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Moderate vs. Aggressive fluids (WATERFALL Trial):** Use **moderate goal-directed Lactated Ringer's** (1.5 mL/kg/hr; 10 mL/kg bolus _only_ if hypovolemic); aggressive hydration (>20 mL/kg bolus + 3 mL/kg/hr) significantly increases fluid overload and pulmonary edema without improving outcomes.
> - **Feed the gut early:** Start oral diet within **24–48 hours** as tolerated (no need to wait for complete pain resolution or normalized lipase); early enteral nutrition preserves the gut barrier and prevents bacterial translocation/infected necrosis.
> - **Lipase level ≠ severity:** Lipase/amylase levels have **zero correlation** with disease severity or prognosis; do not trend daily lipase.
> - **No prophylactic antibiotics:** Never give routine antibiotics for severe or sterile necrotizing pancreatitis; reserve strictly for confirmed/suspected infected necrosis or concurrent cholangitis.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Constant, severe epigastric pain** radiating straight through to the mid-back, often improved by leaning forward.
> - **Nausea, persistent vomiting, and anorexia.**
> - **Etiologic clues:** History of biliary colic/cholelithiasis, heavy alcohol binge, hypertriglyceridemia, recent **ERCP**, abdominal trauma, or offending drugs (azathioprine, valproate, thiazides, GLP-1 agonists, didanosine).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Epigastric tenderness** with guarding; decreased or absent bowel sounds (paralytic ileus).
> - **SIRS / Hypovolemic signs:** Tachycardia, fever, tachypnea, hypotension, oliguria.
> - **Retroperitoneal hemorrhage signs** (rare, <3%, indicate severe necrotizing disease): **Cullen sign** (periumbilical ecchymosis), **Grey Turner sign** (flank ecchymosis), **Fox sign** (inguinal ecchymosis).
> - **Xanthomas or lipemia retinalis:** Seen in hypertriglyceridemia-induced pancreatitis.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> **Revised Atlanta Classification** — Requires **≥2 of the following 3 criteria**:
>
> - **Characteristic abdominal pain:** Acute, persistent, severe epigastric pain often radiating to the back.
> - **Serum lipase (preferred) or amylase ≥3× the upper limit of normal (ULN).**
> - **Characteristic imaging findings** of acute pancreatitis on contrast-enhanced CT (CECT), MRI, or transabdominal ultrasound.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **Initial (Etiology):** Lipase, liver biochemistries (**ALT >150 U/L** has >95% PPV for gallstone pancreatitis), **fasting triglycerides** (**>1000 mg/dL** confirms hypertriglyceridemic etiology), calcium.
> - **Severity / Risk:** BUN, creatinine, and hematocrit at admission and q12–24h (**rising BUN >20 mg/dL** or **hemoconcentration Hct >44%** indicates inadequate resuscitation and predicts severe necrosis), arterial blood gas/lactate.
> - **Interpretation:** Calculate **BISAP score** within 24 hours (**B**UN >25, **I**mpaired mental status, **S**IRS ≥2, **A**ge >60, **P**leural effusion); score **≥3** predicts organ failure and high mortality.
>
> **Imaging**
>
> - **Right Upper Quadrant Ultrasound (RUQ US):** Mandatory in **ALL** patients on admission to evaluate for gallstones and common bile duct (CBD) dilation.
> - **Contrast-Enhanced CT (CECT) Abdomen/Pelvis:** **NOT routinely required on admission** if the diagnosis is clear clinically/biochemically. Order only if diagnostic uncertainty exists, or at **≥72–96 hours** if the patient fails to improve or deteriorates to assess for pancreatic necrosis (necrosis takes 72 hours to fully demarcate).
> - **MRCP or Endoscopic Ultrasound (EUS):** Indicated if choledocholithiasis is suspected without overt cholangitis, or to evaluate idiopathic recurrent pancreatitis.
>
> **Referrals**
>
> - **Gastroenterology:** Emergent ERCP if concurrent acute cholangitis; EUS/MRCP for persistent biliary obstruction.
> - **General Surgery:** Same-admission laparoscopic cholecystectomy for mild gallstone pancreatitis; step-up necrosectomy for infected necrosis.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Persistent organ failure (>48 hours):** Defines **Severe Acute Pancreatitis** (Modified Marshall Score ≥2: PaO2/FiO2 <300, Cr >1.9 mg/dL, or SBP <90 mmHg fluid-unresponsive).
> - **BISAP Score ≥3** or persistent SIRS >48 hours.
> - **Hemoconcentration (Hct >44%)** or **BUN >20 mg/dL** (or rising BUN at 24 hours).
> - **Charcot triad / Reynolds pentad** (indicates concurrent ascending cholangitis requiring urgent ERCP).
> - **Intra-abdominal hypertension (IAH) >12 mmHg** or **Abdominal Compartment Syndrome (>20 mmHg)** with new organ failure.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Perforated Peptic Ulcer:** Sudden-onset rigid surgical abdomen ("board-like"), free intraperitoneal air on upright CXR/CT (can mildly elevate lipase).
> - **Acute Mesenteric Ischemia:** Pain out of proportion to exam, atrial fibrillation/vascular disease, metabolic acidosis/elevated lactate.
> - **Ruptured Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (AAA):** Pulsatile abdominal mass, hypotension, back/flank pain in older smokers.
> - **Inferior Myocardial Infarction:** Epigastric pain, diaphoresis, nausea; rule out with STAT ECG and troponin.
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Acute Cholecystitis / Biliary Colic:** RUQ pain, positive Murphy sign, gallstones without lipase ≥3× ULN.
> - **Choledocholithiasis / Acute Cholangitis:** Jaundice, fever, RUQ pain, cholestatic LFT pattern.
> - **Peptic Ulcer Disease / Gastritis:** Burning epigastric pain, non-radiating, normal lipase.
>
> **Most Surprising**
>
> - **Macroamylasemia / Macrolipasemia:** Benign immune-complex formation causing chronically elevated enzyme levels without abdominal pain or renal clearance.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Hemodynamic stability (MAP ≥65 mmHg), **urine output ≥0.5 mL/kg/hr**, normalization of **BUN (<20 mg/dL)** and **hematocrit** within 24–48 hours, pain control.
> - **Secondary Target:** Prevent pancreatic necrosis, bacterial translocation, fluid overload/ARDS, and recurrent biliary attacks.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Infuse moderate goal-directed Lactated Ringer's (LR)** at **1.5 mL/kg/hr** (preceded by a **10 mL/kg bolus ONLY if hypovolemic**) immediately upon diagnosis to maintain pancreatic microcirculation, minimize SIRS, and prevent hyperchloremic acidosis.
> - **Administer multimodal IV analgesia** (IV opioids such as **fentanyl or hydromorphone** titrated to pain, plus IV acetaminophen) immediately upon presentation to achieve patient comfort.
> - **Perform urgent ERCP with sphincterotomy within 24 hours** if acute gallstone pancreatitis is complicated by **concurrent acute cholangitis** (or within 72 hours if persistent CBD obstruction/jaundice without cholangitis) to decompress the biliary tree.
>
> **Nutritional & Etiology-Specific Therapy**
>
> - **Initiate early oral feeding (low-fat solid or soft diet) within 24–48 hours** as soon as pain is decreasing and nausea subsides to preserve gut mucosal integrity and reduce infectious complications.
> - **Start enteral tube feeding (nasogastric or nasojejunal)** if oral feeding is not tolerated by 48–72 hours in severe pancreatitis to prevent bacterial translocation.
> - **Initiate an IV regular insulin infusion** (0.1–0.3 units/kg/hr with D5W to maintain euglycemia) if **triglycerides are >1000 mg/dL** to activate lipoprotein lipase and lower triglycerides to **<500 mg/dL**.
> - **Perform laparoscopic cholecystectomy during the SAME index admission** once mild gallstone pancreatitis resolves to prevent a 25–30% risk of recurrent biliary events within 6 weeks.
>
> **Management of Necrosis & Escalation**
>
> - **Start broad-spectrum IV antibiotics (e.g., Carbapenem, or Cefepime/Fluoroquinolone + Metronidazole)** ONLY when **infected pancreatic necrosis** is suspected (clinical deterioration/fever at >7–10 days or extraluminal gas on CECT) to penetrate necrotic pancreatic tissue.
> - **Delay invasive necrosectomy for ≥4 weeks** whenever clinically feasible to allow necrotic collections to encapsulate into **Walled-Off Necrosis (WON)**, which markedly reduces procedural mortality.
> - **Perform a "Step-Up" drainage approach** (endoscopic ultrasound-guided **lumen-apposing metal stent [LAMS]** transgastric drainage or CT-guided percutaneous catheter drainage first, followed by minimally invasive endoscopic/video-assisted retroperitoneal necrosectomy if needed) for persistent symptomatic or infected WON.
>
> **Disposition & Follow-up**
>
> - **Admit to ICU** if the patient meets criteria for **Severe Acute Pancreatitis** (persistent organ failure >48h), BISAP ≥3, or requires insulin infusion for hypertriglyceridemia.
> - **Screen for exocrine pancreatic insufficiency (fecal elastase-1) and pancreatogenic (Type 3c) diabetes (HbA1c/fasting glucose)** at 3–6 months following severe or necrotizing pancreatitis.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid aggressive fluid resuscitation (>20 mL/kg bolus + 3 mL/kg/hr)** as demonstrated in the WATERFALL trial to prevent fluid overload, pulmonary edema, and abdominal compartment syndrome.
> - **Avoid Normal Saline (0.9% NaCl) in large volumes** (and strictly avoid hydroxyethyl starch) to prevent hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis, which can directly trigger premature trypsinogen activation and worsen SIRS.
> - **Avoid prophylactic antibiotics** in sterile acute or necrotizing pancreatitis to prevent multidrug-resistant bacterial or fungal superinfection of necrotic tissue.
> - **Avoid Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN)** unless enteral routes are completely contraindicated (e.g., bowel ischemia, perforation, severe compartment syndrome) to prevent catheter sepsis and gut mucosal atrophy.
> - **Avoid early urgent ERCP in gallstone pancreatitis WITHOUT cholangitis** (per APEC trial) as it does not reduce mortality or organ failure and increases procedural complications.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Local Fluid Collections (<4 weeks vs. ≥4 weeks):**
>   - _Interstitial edematous:_ **Acute Peripancreatic Fluid Collection (<4w)** $\rightarrow$ **Pancreatic Pseudocyst (≥4w)**; most resolve spontaneously; drain via EUS only if symptomatic, infected, or rapidly enlarging.
>   - _Necrotizing:_ **Acute Necrotic Collection (<4w)** $\rightarrow$ **Walled-Off Necrosis / WON (≥4w)**.
> - **Infected Pancreatic Necrosis:** Clinical deterioration/sepsis in week 2–4 + gas bubbles in necrotic bed on CECT $\rightarrow$ IV carbapenem + step-up EUS/percutaneous drainage.
> - **Splenic Vein Thrombosis:** Contiguous inflammation of the splenic vein behind the pancreatic tail $\rightarrow$ left-sided (sinistral) portal hypertension and **isolated gastric fundal varices**.
> - **Pseudoaneurysm (Splenic or Gastroduodenal Artery):** Enzymatic erosion into adjacent arterial wall $\rightarrow$ sudden drop in Hb, hemosuccus pancreaticus (GI bleed), or hemorrhage into pseudocyst $\rightarrow$ treat with **urgent IR transcatheter arterial embolization**.
> - **ARDS / Pleural Effusion (typically left-sided):** Cytokine-mediated alveolar capillary leak or transdiaphragmatic lymphatic drainage $\rightarrow$ lung-protective ventilation.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 48-year-old woman presents with 8 hours of severe, constant epigastric pain radiating to her back and persistent vomiting. Vitals show HR 108 bpm and BP 112/70 mmHg; labs reveal lipase 1,850 U/L (>3× ULN), ALT 240 U/L, BUN 16 mg/dL, and Hct 41%. RUQ ultrasound shows cholelithiasis with a normal 4-mm CBD (no cholangitis); she is treated with moderate goal-directed **Lactated Ringer's** (1.5 mL/kg/hr), IV hydromorphone, early low-fat oral diet at 24 hours, and undergoes **same-admission laparoscopic cholecystectomy** prior to discharge.
