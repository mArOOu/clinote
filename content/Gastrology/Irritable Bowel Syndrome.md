> [!abstract] Irritable Bowel Syndrome
> Chronic **Disorder of Gut-Brain Interaction (DGBI)** characterized by **recurrent abdominal pain** associated with **defecation or altered bowel habits** (diarrhea, constipation, or mixed) in the absence of detectable structural or biochemical abnormalities.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Visceral Hypersensitivity:** Lowered pain threshold to normal colonic distention via enteric afferent sensitization (TRPV1, serotonin $\text{5-HT}_3/\text{5-HT}_4$ receptors, mast cell degranulation) and central pain amplification.
> - **Altered GI Motility:** Accelerated transit in IBS-D; delayed colonic transit in IBS-C.
> - **Post-Infectious / Microbiome Dysbiosis:** ~10% of acute infectious gastroenteritis cases trigger persistent low-grade mucosal inflammation, increased intestinal permeability, and **Post-Infectious IBS (PI-IBS)**.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Nocturnal diarrhea or pain NEVER happens in IBS:** If diarrhea or abdominal pain wakes a patient from a sound sleep, it is an **organic** disease (IBD, microscopic colitis, secretory diarrhea)—never IBS.
> - **Make a positive diagnosis (Don't over-scope):** In patients **<45 years old** who meet Rome IV criteria and have **zero alarm features**, diagnose IBS positively using **only 4 tests** (CBC, CRP/Fecal Calprotectin, Celiac serology) without performing a colonoscopy.
> - **Soluble fiber ONLY (Psyllium), NEVER Insoluble fiber (Wheat Bran):** Soluble fiber improves both IBS-C and IBS-D; insoluble fiber (bran) fermentatively worsens bloating, gas, and cramping.
> - **TCAs for IBS-D, SSRIs for IBS-C (if comorbid mood disorder):** Tricyclic antidepressants (**Amitriptyline**) have anticholinergic side effects that slow gut transit (ideal for **IBS-D** + pain); **Linaclotide** is the gold-standard dual agent for **IBS-C** (treats both constipation and visceral pain via cGMP inhibition of pain fibers).

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Crampy lower abdominal pain** that is related to defecation (classically **improved**, though occasionally worsened, by having a bowel movement).
> - **Altered stool frequency and form** coinciding with pain flares, subtyped using the **Bristol Stool Form Scale (BSFS)** on days with abnormal bowel movements:
>   - **IBS-C (Constipation):** >25% hard/lumpy stools (BSFS 1–2) and <25% loose/watery stools.
>   - **IBS-D (Diarrhea):** >25% loose/watery stools (BSFS 6–7) and <25% hard/lumpy stools.
>   - **IBS-M (Mixed):** >25% hard/lumpy stools AND >25% loose/watery stools.
> - **Supportive features:** Severe abdominal **bloating/visible distention** (worsens progressively throughout the day, resolves overnight), passage of **clear/white mucus** per rectum, sensation of incomplete evacuation, postprandial urgency.
> - **High-yield associations:** Fibromyalgia, chronic fatigue syndrome, somatic symptom disorders, generalized anxiety, major depression, temporomandibular joint (TMJ) disorder, chronic pelvic pain.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Well-appearing patient** with stable weight and normal vital signs.
> - Mild, diffuse or LLQ/RLQ abdominal tenderness to palpation (often over a palpable, tender sigmoid colon) **without** guarding, rebound, hepatosplenomegaly, or masses.
> - **Carnett's Sign:** If abdominal tenderness _increases_ or remains unchanged when the patient tenses their abdominal wall muscles (by lifting head/shoulders off the exam table), the pain originates from the **anterior abdominal wall (ACNES)**, not visceral IBS.
> - **Digital Rectal Exam (DRE):** Heme-negative stool; assess for pelvic floor dyssynergia in IBS-C; **complete absence of perianal fistulas, fissures, or skin tags** (which point to Crohn's disease).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> **Rome IV Diagnostic Criteria for IBS** — Must fulfill **ALL** of the following (symptom onset **≥6 months** prior to diagnosis, active during the **last 3 months**):
>
> - **Recurrent abdominal pain** occurring on average **≥1 day per week** in the last 3 months, associated with **≥2 of the following 3 criteria**:
>   1. **Related to defecation** (either improved or worsened by defecation).
>   2. Associated with a **change in frequency of stool**.
>   3. Associated with a **change in form (appearance) of stool**.
> - _Crucial Discriminator:_ **Absence of abdominal pain** rules out IBS (if painless diarrhea $\rightarrow$ Functional Diarrhea; if painless constipation $\rightarrow$ Chronic Idiopathic Constipation).

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs (Minimal Targeted Screening to Rule Out Mimics)**
>
> - **IBS-D / IBS-M Mandatory Screen (ACG/AGA Guidelines):**
>   1. **Celiac Disease Serology:** **IgA anti-Tissue Transglutaminase (tTG-IgA)** + **Total Serum IgA** (mandatory in all IBS-D/M).
>   2. **Inflammatory Markers (Rule out IBD):** **Fecal Calprotectin** (cutoff **<50 µg/g** rules out IBD with >99% NPV) or **Fecal Lactoferrin** + **Serum CRP**.
>   3. **CBC:** Screen for anemia or leukocytosis.
>   4. **Stool _Giardia_ Antigen:** If endemic water exposure.
> - **IBS-C Screen:** CBC (routine metabolic/thyroid panels are low-yield unless clinically indicated).
>
> **Procedures**
>
> - **Colonoscopy:** **NOT indicated** in patients **<45 years old** with typical IBS symptoms, negative Fecal Calprotectin/CRP, and **no alarm features**. Perform colonoscopy ONLY if:
>   - Any **alarm feature** is present.
>   - Patient is **≥45 years old** and due for routine colorectal cancer screening.
>   - Persistent watery diarrhea despite empiric therapy in patients >50 years (to take random biopsies for **Microscopic Colitis**).
> - **Anorectal Manometry + Balloon Expulsion Test:** Order in **IBS-C** patients who fail initial soluble fiber and PEG to rule out overlapping **Dyssynergic Defecation**.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> **Alarm Features (Exclude IBS — Mandate Organic Workup / Colonoscopy):**
>
> - **New symptom onset at age ≥45–50 years**
> - **Nocturnal diarrhea or nocturnal pain** that awakens the patient from sleep
> - **Hematochezia, melena**, or **Iron-Deficiency Anemia**
> - **Unintended weight loss** (>5–10% over 3–6 months)
> - **Elevated Fecal Calprotectin (>50–100 µg/g), Fecal Lactoferrin, or CRP**
> - **Persistent daily severe diarrhea** or **progressive severe abdominal pain**
> - **Family history** of **Colorectal Cancer, IBD, or Celiac Disease** in a first-degree relative
> - **Fever, arthritis, uveitis, erythema nodosum, or perianal disease** (extraintestinal IBD signs)

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Inflammatory Bowel Disease (Crohn's Disease / Ulcerative Colitis):** Distinguished by elevated **Fecal Calprotectin**, nocturnal symptoms, bloody stools, weight loss, and anemia.
> - **Colorectal Adenocarcinoma:** New-onset altered bowel habits in patients ≥45 years, iron-deficiency anemia, occult/overt blood.
> - **Ovarian Carcinoma:** Can masquerade as new-onset "IBS" (bloating, pelvic/abdominal pain, early satiety, constipation) in women >50 years; maintain low threshold for pelvic ultrasound.
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Celiac Disease:** Villous atrophy causing diarrhea, bloating, cramping, and iron deficiency; positive **tTG-IgA**.
> - **Lactose or Fructose Intolerance:** Bloating, flatulence, and osmotic diarrhea temporally linked to dairy or high-fructose intake.
> - **Microscopic Colitis:** Painless watery diarrhea (often nocturnal) in older women on NSAIDs/SSRIs/PPIs.
> - **Bile Acid Diarrhea (BAD):** Accounts for up to **25–30% of patients mislabeled as "IBS-D"**; triggered by excess bile acids entering the colon (post-cholecystectomy or idiopathic).
> - **Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (SIBO):** Postprandial bloating, flatulence, and diarrhea (vitamin B12 deficiency + elevated folate); diagnosed via lactulose/glucose hydrogen breath test.
>
> **Less Common**
>
> - **Endometriosis:** Cyclic lower abdominal pain, dyschezia (painful defecation), and diarrhea/constipation worsening during menses in reproductive-age women.
> - **Anterior Cutaneous Nerve Entrapment Syndrome (ACNES):** Localized abdominal wall pain with a **positive Carnett's sign**; treated with trigger-point lidocaine injection.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Clinically meaningful reduction in **abdominal pain (≥30% decrease)**, normalization of stool consistency (BSFS 3–4), and reduction in bloating.
> - **Secondary Target:** Improve quality of life, restore gut-brain axis regulation, and avoid unnecessary invasive testing or opioid exposure.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **N/A** — Chronic outpatient condition; validate the patient's symptoms as real physiological gut-brain hypersensitivity, provide firm reassurance, and set realistic expectations (chronic management, not instant cure).
>
> **First-Line Foundational Therapy (All Subtypes)**
>
> - **Initiate a short-term trial (4–6 weeks) of a Low-FODMAP Diet** (_Fermentable Oligosaccharides, Disaccharides, Monosaccharides, And Polyols_) guided by a dietitian—consisting of **3 strict phases: (1) Restriction (2–6 weeks), (2) Structured Reintroduction, and (3) Personalized Long-Term Diet**—to reduce osmotic load and colonic gas fermentation (particularly effective for bloating and IBS-D).
> - **Start Soluble Fiber supplementation (Psyllium 10–25 g/day)**, titrated up slowly with water, to improve global symptoms in both IBS-C and IBS-D.
> - **Prescribe On-Demand Antispasmodics or Peppermint Oil** for acute postprandial abdominal cramping: **Enteric-coated Peppermint Oil** (180–225 mg TID; blocks smooth muscle L-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channels; preferred by ACG guidelines) or **Dicyclomine** (10–20 mg PO up to QID) / **Hyoscyamine** (0.125 mg sublingual/PO PRN; _avoid anticholinergics in IBS-C_).
>
> **Targeted Pharmacotherapy for IBS-C (Constipation-Predominant)**
>
> - **Trial Polyethylene Glycol (PEG 3350, 17 g PO daily)** as an inexpensive first-line agent to improve stool frequency and consistency (_Note: PEG improves constipation but often does NOT relieve IBS abdominal pain_).
> - **Initiate a Guanylate Cyclase-C (GC-C) Agonist—Linaclotide (290 mcg PO daily, 30 min before breakfast) or Plecanatide (3 mg PO daily)**—as the **gold-standard first-line prescription therapy for IBS-C**, as intracellular cGMP both stimulates CFTR chloride/water secretion AND inhibits visceral nociceptive pain fibers.
> - **Use Tenapanor (50 mg PO BID)** (inhibits intestinal $\text{Na}^+/\text{H}^+$ exchanger 3 [NHE3]), **Lubiprostone (8 mcg PO BID with food)** (ClC-2 channel activator), or **Tegaserod** as effective second-line alternatives for IBS-C.
>
> **Targeted Pharmacotherapy for IBS-D (Diarrhea-Predominant)**
>
> - **Use Loperamide (2–4 mg PO PRN or 45 min before meals)** to reduce stool frequency, urgency, and fecal incontinence (_Note: Loperamide does NOT improve global IBS abdominal pain_).
> - **Prescribe Rifaximin (550 mg PO TID for 14 days)** (non-absorbable luminal antibiotic) as **first-line targeted therapy for global IBS-D symptoms and bloating** (can be repeated up to 2 additional times if symptoms recur).
> - **Trial a Bile Acid Sequestrant (Cholestyramine 4 g daily–BID or Colesevelam)** empirically for IBS-D, as up to 30% of IBS-D patients have underlying bile acid malabsorption.
> - **Prescribe Eluxadoline (100 mg PO BID)** (mixed $\mu/\kappa$-opioid receptor agonist and $\delta$-opioid antagonist) or **Alosetron (0.5–1 mg PO BID)** (selective $\text{5-HT}_3$ antagonist; restricted under REMS to **women with severe refractory IBS-D**) for severe, debilitating IBS-D.
>
> **Neuromodulators & Brain-Gut Behavioral Therapies (Refractory Visceral Pain)**
>
> - **Initiate a low-dose Tricyclic Antidepressant (TCA)—such as Amitriptyline, Nortriptyline, or Desipramine (10–25 mg PO QHS, titrating to 50 mg over 4–8 weeks)**—as the **most effective central neuromodulator for refractory abdominal pain** across all IBS subtypes (_Amitriptyline is especially beneficial in IBS-D due to anticholinergic slowing of transit; prefer secondary amines like Nortriptyline/Desipramine in IBS-C to minimize constipation_).
> - **Refer for Gut-Directed Psychotherapy** (**Cognitive Behavioral Therapy [CBT]** or **Gut-Directed Hypnotherapy**) in patients with moderate-to-severe symptoms refractory to medical therapy or high GI-specific anxiety.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Opioids (morphine, oxycodone, tramadol) at all costs** for IBS abdominal pain to prevent addiction, opioid-induced constipation, and **Narcotic Bowel Syndrome** (paradoxical central hyperalgesia where escalating opioid doses worsen abdominal pain).
> - **Avoid Eluxadoline in patients WITHOUT a gallbladder (prior cholecystectomy)**, patients with a history of pancreatitis/sphincter of Oddi dysfunction, or those consuming **>3 alcoholic drinks/day**, as eluxadoline causes **Sphincter of Oddi spasm and acute pancreatitis**.
> - **Avoid Alosetron in patients with any history of constipation**, as it can precipitate severe constipation and **Ischemic Colitis** (must be stopped immediately if constipation or hematochezia develops).
> - **Avoid Insoluble Fiber (Wheat Bran)** and **Avoid indefinite Maintenance of Phase 1 Strict Low-FODMAP Elimination** (>6 weeks without reintroduction) to prevent worsening bloating (bran) and gut microbiome depletion/micronutrient deficiencies (prolonged FODMAP restriction).
> - **Avoid IgG Food Sensitivity / Allergy Panels** and **Avoid Routine Probiotics** (per 2021 ACG guidelines), as IgG food panels merely reflect normal physiological food exposure and lead to harmful dietary restrictions.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Narcotic Bowel Syndrome:** Iatrogenic central sensitization from chronic opioid prescribing for functional GI pain $\rightarrow$ requires gradual supervised opioid detoxification while transitioning to a TCA or SNRI (duloxetine).
> - **Avoidant/Restrictive Food Intake Disorder (ARFID) & Malnutrition:** Self-imposed extreme dietary elimination driven by fear of triggering symptoms $\rightarrow$ screen before starting restrictive diets like Low-FODMAP.
> - **Unnecessary Surgeries:** Patients with unrecognized IBS have a 2–3× higher rate of unnecessary **cholecystectomy, appendectomy, and hysterectomy** for misattributed visceral pain.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 28-year-old woman presents with a 1-year history of crampy lower abdominal pain occurring 3–4 days per week that improves immediately after passing loose, watery stools (3–5 times daily during flares), accompanied by afternoon bloating. She has no nocturnal diarrhea, weight loss, or hematochezia; CBC, CRP, **Fecal Calprotectin (<20 µg/g)**, and **tTG-IgA** are all completely normal. A positive diagnosis of **IBS-D** is made **without colonoscopy**; she is started on **soluble fiber (psyllium)**, a dietitian-led **Low-FODMAP diet trial**, and **Rifaximin 550 mg TID for 14 days** (with **low-dose Amitriptyline 10 mg QHS** reserved if visceral pain persists).
