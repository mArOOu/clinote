> [!abstract] Hemorrhoids & Common Anorectal Disorders
> Benign structural, vascular, or infectious pathologies of the anal canal and perianal skin—principally **Hemorrhoids** (symptomatic engorgement/prolapse of anal vascular cushions), **Anal Fissures** (longitudinal mucosal tear distal to the dentate line), and **Anorectal Abscesses / Fistulas** (cryptoglandular suppuration).

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **The Dentate (Pectinate) Line is the crucial anatomical divide:**
>   - **Proximal (Internal Hemorrhoids):** Visceral innervation (inferior hypogastric plexus) $\rightarrow$ **insensitive to pain** (presents with **painless bleeding/prolapse** unless strangulated); simple columnar epithelium; drains via superior rectal vein to **portal system**.
>   - **Distal (External Hemorrhoids, Anal Fissures):** Somatic innervation (**pudendal nerve / inferior rectal branch**) $\rightarrow$ **exquisitely painful**; stratified squamous epithelium; drains to **systemic caval system**.
> - **Anal Fissure Vicious Cycle:** Hard stool tears the poorly perfused **posterior midline** anoderm $\rightarrow$ exposed **internal anal sphincter (IAS) goes into hypertonic spasm** $\rightarrow$ compresses local arterioles $\rightarrow$ **ischemia prevents healing**.
> - **Abscess & Fistula (Cryptoglandular Theory):** Obstruction of mucus-secreting **anal crypt glands** at the dentate line $\rightarrow$ infection tracks into intersphincteric, perianal, or ischiorectal spaces (**Abscess**) $\rightarrow$ 40–50% form a chronic epithelialized tract connecting the internal crypt to the perianal skin (**Fistula-in-ano**).

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Severe anal pain = NOT uncomplicated internal hemorrhoids:** If a patient presents with severe acute anal pain, look for an **Anal Fissure**, **Thrombosed External Hemorrhoid**, **Perianal Abscess**, or **Strangulated (Grade IV) Internal Hemorrhoid**.
> - **72-hour window for Thrombosed External Hemorrhoids:** If presenting **<72 hours** from pain onset, **elliptical surgical excision** under local anesthesia provides immediate cure; if **>72 hours**, the clot is already resorbing—treat **conservatively** (sitz baths, topical lidocaine/nifedipine, stool softeners).
> - **Off-midline (lateral) anal fissures are a major Red Flag:** 90% of benign fissures are **posterior midline** (10% anterior midline in postpartum women). Lateral/multiple/painless fissures mandate workup for **H**IV, **I**BD (**Crohn's Disease**), **D**ermal/Malignancy (Squamous Cell Carcinoma), **S**yphilis, or **T**uberculosis (**Mnemonic: HIDST**).
> - **Never probe or cut a fistula during acute abscess I&D (unless superficial/experienced colorectal surgeon), and NEVER incise an internal hemorrhoid!**

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Internal Hemorrhoids:** **Painless bright red blood per rectum (BRBPR)** coating the outside of the stool, dripping into the toilet bowl, or streaking the toilet paper; prolapsing perianal mass, mucous discharge, pruritus ani (from fecal soiling/mucus).
> - **Thrombosed External Hemorrhoid:** **Sudden-onset, constant, severe perianal pain** and a palpable tender lump at the anal verge, often after heavy lifting, straining, or childbirth; peaks at **48–72 hours** and improves after day 4.
> - **Anal Fissure:** **Tearing, "passing broken glass" pain** _during_ defecation followed by a **deep throbbing ache lasting 1–3 hours post-defecation** (due to internal sphincter spasm) + scant bright red blood on the toilet paper; severe fear of defecation worsens constipation.
> - **Anorectal Abscess:** **Constant, progressive, throbbing perianal/rectal pain** worsened by sitting, walking, or coughing (not just defecation), accompanied by **fever, malaise, or urinary retention**.
> - **Anal Fistula:** Chronic or intermittent **purulent, malodorous, or serosanguinous perianal drainage** and pruritus, often following a prior drained perianal abscess.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **External Inspection (Left lateral decubitus / Sims position):**
>   - _Thrombosed External Hemorrhoid:_ **Tense, bluish-purple, exquisitely tender subcutaneous nodule** distal to the dentate line at the anal verge.
>   - _Anal Fissure:_ Gently parting the buttocks reveals a **linear tear in the posterior midline (6 o'clock)** (or anterior midline at 12 o'clock). **Chronic fissures (>6–8 weeks)** display a **classic triad:** (1) **Sentinel skin pile (tag)** distally, (2) **Exposed white horizontal fibers of the Internal Anal Sphincter** at the base, and (3) **Hypertrophied anal papilla** proximally.
>   - _Perianal / Ischiorectal Abscess:_ Localized perianal **erythema, induration, warmth, and fluctuance** (_Warning:_ **Intersphincteric and Supralevator abscesses** have **ZERO external skin changes** despite severe rectal pain and fever—diagnose via DRE or CT/MRI!).
>   - _Fistula-in-ano:_ Pustule-like external opening with granulation tissue expresar pus upon palpation (**Goodsall's Rule:** External openings _posterior_ to the transverse anal line curve into the **posterior midline** internal crypt; _anterior_ openings (<3 cm) track **radially straight** into the nearest crypt).
> - **Digital Rectal Exam (DRE) & Anoscopy:**
>   - **Internal hemorrhoids are NOT palpable on DRE** unless thrombosed or prolapsed—**Anoscopy is required** to visualize them at the right anterior, right posterior, and left lateral columns.
>   - _Crucial Pitfall:_ **Defer DRE and anoscopy in the acute setting if an acute Anal Fissure is visible**, as the exam causes excruciating pain and sphincter spasm.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Internal Hemorrhoid Grading (Goligher Classification — Mandatory for Treatment Selection):**
>   - **Grade I:** Bulge into the anal canal lumen; **do NOT prolapse** below the dentate line.
>   - **Grade II:** Prolapse beyond the anal verge with straining/defecation, but **reduce spontaneously**.
>   - **Grade III:** Prolapse with straining/defecation and **require manual reduction**.
>   - **Grade IV:** **Chronically prolapsed and irreducible** (high risk of strangulation, thrombosis, and gangrene).
> - **Anal Fissure:** Direct visual identification of a longitudinal linear ulceration in the squamous anoderm distal to the dentate line (Acute <6–8 weeks; Chronic >6–8 weeks with sentinel pile/exposed sphincter fibers).
> - **Anorectal Abscess / Fistula:** Clinical identification of fluctuant perianal collection/draining tract, or confirmation via **CT/MRI Pelvis** or **Surgical Exam Under Anesthesia (EUA)** for deep/complex spaces.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **CBC & Iron Studies:** Check if chronic or heavy hemorrhoidal bleeding is reported (**Iron-deficiency anemia is RARE from hemorrhoids alone**—if anemia is present, you **must perform a full colonoscopy** to rule out a proximal colorectal malignancy!).
> - **WBC, Lactate, Blood Glucose:** Order in anorectal abscess (especially in diabetics or immunocompromised hosts) to screen for **Fournier Gangrene** (necrotizing perineal fasciitis).
>
> **Imaging & Endoscopy**
>
> - **Anoscopy:** First-line office test to visualize internal hemorrhoids and the dentate line.
> - **Colonoscopy vs. Flexible Sigmoidoscopy (When to Scope Bleeding "Hemorrhoids"):**
>   - Never assume BRBPR is purely from visible hemorrhoids without risk stratification!
>   - **Perform a FULL Colonoscopy** if: **Age ≥45 years** (not up to date with screening), **any alarm features** (iron-deficiency anemia, weight loss, change in bowel habits, maroon/dark blood mixed in stool), or **family history of colorectal cancer/IBD**.
>   - **Anoscopy ± Flexible Sigmoidoscopy** is sufficient for patients **<45 years** with classic outlet-type bright red dripping/toilet-paper blood and no alarm features.
> - **Pelvic MRI (with fistula protocol) or Endoscopic Anorectal Ultrasound (EUS):** Gold standard to map **complex, recurrent, or Crohn's-associated anal fistulas** and deep occult **supralevator/intersphincteric abscesses**.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Perianal crepitus, bullae, dishwater-gray necrotic skin, rapid spread of erythema, or septic shock** in a diabetic/immunocompromised patient with an anorectal abscess ($\rightarrow$ **Fournier Gangrene**; requires emergent surgical debridement + broad-spectrum antibiotics).
> - **Severe rectal pain + fever + urinary retention** (even with a normal external perianal exam) ($\rightarrow$ **Deep Intersphincteric or Supralevator Abscess**, OR **Pelvic Sepsis following Rubber Band Ligation**).
> - **Lateral (off-midline), multiple, painless, or non-healing anal fissures** ($\rightarrow$ rule out **Crohn's Disease, HIV, Syphilis, TB, or Anal Squamous Cell Carcinoma**).
> - **Iron-deficiency anemia or positive fecal occult blood** attributed to "hemorrhoids" ($\rightarrow$ **Colorectal Adenocarcinoma** until proven otherwise by colonoscopy).
> - **Palpable indurated/ulcerated anal mass or inguinal lymphadenopathy** ($\rightarrow$ biopsy for **Anal Squamous Cell Carcinoma** [HPV 16/18]).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Colorectal or Anal Canal Carcinoma (Squamous Cell Carcinoma / Cloacogenic / Adenocarcinoma):** Presents with rectal bleeding, pain, palpable mass, or sense of incomplete evacuation; frequently misdiagnosed initially as "hemorrhoids" or "chronic fissure."
> - **Fournier Gangrene (Necrotizing Fasciitis of the Perineum):** Polymicrobial synergistic infection tracking along Colles/Scarpa fascia from an untreated perianal abscess.
> - **Perianal Crohn's Disease:** Multiple/complex fistulas, abscesses, eccentric painless fissures, and large edematous skin tags.
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Pruritus Ani:** Perianal itching caused by excessive hygiene (soaps/wet wipes), dietary irritants (coffee, beer, citrus), **Pinworms (_Enterobius vermicularis_)** (nocturnal itching in children; Scotch tape test), or _Candida_/dermatophyte infection.
> - **Condyloma Acuminata (Anogenital Warts):** HPV 6 and 11; cauliflower-like verrucous papules (Compare with **Condyloma Lata**: smooth, flat-topped, moist plaques of secondary **Syphilis**).
> - **Pilonidal Disease:** Midline pits and fluctuant abscess in the **superior gluteal cleft (sacrococcygeal region)**—4–5 cm _posterior_ to the anal orifice—caused by ingrown hair follicles/debris.
> - **Rectal Prolapse (Procidentia):** Full-thickness protrusion of the rectum showing **concentric mucosal rings** (whereas prolapsed internal hemorrhoids show **radial grooves** between discrete vascular cushions).
>
> **Less Common**
>
> - **Proctalgia Fugax / Levator Ani Syndrome:** Brief, sudden, self-limiting spasms of intense rectal pain (often nocturnal) lasting seconds to minutes with a completely normal exam (functional pelvic floor spasm).
> - **Solitary Rectal Ulcer Syndrome:** Chronic straining/internal rectal intussusception causing bleeding, mucus, and an anterior rectal wall ulcer mimicking malignancy.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Resolution of bleeding, prolapse, and pain; complete mucosal healing of fissures; immediate decompression of abscesses; eradication of fistula tracts **while preserving fecal continence**.
> - **Secondary Target:** Prevent recurrence via lifelong high-fiber/fluid bowel habits and exclude underlying colorectal neoplasia or Crohn's disease.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Perform Urgent Incision and Drainage (I&D)** (cruciate or elliptical incision placed **as close to the anal verge as possible** to shorten any subsequent fistula tract) immediately upon diagnosing a **Perianal or Ischiorectal Abscess**—**do NOT wait for fluctuance, and never treat with antibiotics alone!**
> - **Add Empiric Antibiotics (e.g., Ciprofloxacin + Metronidazole, or Ampicillin-Sulbactam)** after abscess I&D **ONLY** in high-risk patients: **extensive surrounding cellulitis, systemic sepsis/fever, diabetes mellitus, immunosuppression/neutropenia, or prosthetic heart valves**.
> - **Perform Emergent Elliptical Excision (NOT simple incision/clot expression)** under local anesthesia for an **acutely Thrombosed External Hemorrhoid** ONLY if the patient presents **within 72 hours of symptom onset** to provide rapid pain relief and prevent recurrence.
> - **Perform Urgent Surgical Hemorrhoidectomy** for **acutely Strangulated, Irreducible, or Gangrenous Grade IV Internal Hemorrhoids**.
>
> **Stepwise Management of Internal Hemorrhoids (By Goligher Grade)**
>
> - **Foundational Conservative Therapy (All Grades & Thrombosed External >72h):** **Prescribe high-fiber diet + Psyllium supplementation (20–30 g/day) + increased water intake**, warm **Sitz baths** (15 min 2–3× daily to relax sphincter tone), avoidance of straining/reading on the toilet, and short-term topical **Hydrocortisone (<7 days)** or **Topical Nifedipine/Lidocaine** for symptomatic relief.
> - **Office-Based Procedures (Grade I, Grade II, and Select Grade III Failing Conservative Care):**
>   - **Perform Rubber Band Ligation (RBL)** (placing band strictly **≥1–2 cm proximal to the dentate line**) as the **most effective first-line office procedure** (80% cure rate) for Grade I–III internal hemorrhoids.
>   - **Use Injection Sclerotherapy** (preferred in patients on anticoagulants/bleeding disorders or immunocompromised, as there is no mucosal sloughing) or **Infrared Coagulation** as alternative office modalities for Grade I–II hemorrhoids.
> - **Operative Surgical Management (Grade IV, Refractory Grade III, or Combined Internal/External):**
>   - **Perform Excisional Hemorrhoidectomy (Closed Ferguson or Open Milligan-Morgan technique)** as the gold-standard definitive surgery (lowest recurrence rate ~2–5%, though significant postoperative pain), OR **Stapled Hemorrhoidopexy / Doppler-Guided Hemorrhoidal Artery Ligation (HAL)**.
>
> **Stepwise Management of Acute & Chronic Anal Fissures**
>
> - **First-Line Conservative Care (Heals >80% of Acute Fissures):** **Prescribe Psyllium fiber + Polyethylene Glycol (PEG)** to soften stools, warm **Sitz baths** after bowel movements to relax the internal sphincter, and **Topical 2% Lidocaine jelly** before defecation.
> - **First-Line Pharmacologic "Chemical Sphincterotomy" (For Chronic Fissures >6–8 Weeks or Severe Acute Fissures):**
>   - **Apply Topical Calcium Channel Blocker—0.2–0.3% Nifedipine ointment OR 2% Diltiazem ointment (applied topically to the anal canal 2–3× daily for 6–8 weeks)**—as the **preferred first-line medical therapy** (75–90% healing rate with minimal side effects), OR **Topical 0.4% Nitroglycerin (Glyceryl Trinitrate) ointment BID** (effective, but limited by severe **nitrate headaches** in >30% of patients).
> - **Second-Line Non-Surgical Rescue:** **Inject Botulinum Toxin A (Botox, 20–100 units)** into the internal anal sphincter if topical vasodilators fail after 8 weeks (induces temporary 3-month chemical denervation allowing the fissure to heal without permanent incontinence risk).
> - **Gold-Standard Surgical Salvage:** **Perform Lateral Internal Sphincterotomy (LIS)** (dividing the distal internal anal sphincter at the lateral 3 or 9 o'clock position) if medical and Botox therapies fail (**>95% cure rate**, though carries a **~5–10% risk of minor flatus/fecal incontinence**).
>
> **Management of Anal Fistulas (Fistula-in-Ano)**
>
> - **Perform Primary Fistulotomy** (laying open the fistula tract) ONLY for **Simple, Low Fistulas** (intersphincteric or low transsphincteric involving **<30% of the external anal sphincter**) in patients with normal baseline continence (**>90% cure rate**).
> - **Use Sphincter-Sparing Techniques** for **Complex or High Fistulas** (involving **>30% of the external anal sphincter**, anterior fistulas in women, recurrent fistulas, or **Crohn's Disease**): place a **draining Non-Cutting Seton** first to control sepsis, followed by an **Endorectal Mucosal Advancement Flap** or **LIFT Procedure (Ligation of Intersphincteric Fistula Tract)** (plus **Infliximab** if Crohn's disease).

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Simple Incision (Lance) and Clot Evacuation of a Thrombosed External Hemorrhoid** in the ED, as simple incision leads to immediate re-thrombosis and severe bleeding (must perform complete **elliptical excision** if <72h, or **conservative care** if >72h).
> - **Avoid Rubber Band Ligation (RBL) in patients on active Anticoagulants/Clopidogrel** (causes life-threatening delayed hemorrhage when the necrotic eschar sloughs at Day 7–10; use **sclerotherapy** instead) or in **severely Immunocompromised/Neutropenic patients** (risk of fatal **pelvic sepsis**).
> - **Avoid placing a Rubber Band at or distal to the Dentate Line**, as banding somatically innervated squamous anoderm causes immediate, excruciating pain and vasovagal syncope (if severe pain occurs immediately upon banding, **remove the band immediately!**).
> - **Avoid Prolonged Topical Hydrocortisone (>1–2 weeks)** on perianal skin to prevent **perianal skin atrophy, ulceration, and fungal superinfection**.
> - **Avoid Manual Anal Dilatation (Lord's procedure)** for anal fissures (historically performed, now **abandoned** due to catastrophic rates of permanent **fecal incontinence** from uncontrolled sphincter tearing).
> - **Avoid Primary Fistulotomy in High Transsphincteric/Suprasphincteric Fistulas or Active Perianal Crohn's Disease** to prevent permanent **fecal incontinence** and non-healing perineal wounds.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Pelvic Sepsis (Post-Rubber Band Ligation):** Rare, life-threatening complication occurring **3–10 days after RBL** $\rightarrow$ presents with **triad of worsening severe rectal/pelvic pain, fever, and acute urinary retention** $\rightarrow$ requires emergent IV broad-spectrum antibiotics, Foley catheter, and surgical debridement.
> - **Delayed Post-Procedure Hemorrhage:** Occurs **7–14 days** after Rubber Band Ligation or Excisional Hemorrhoidectomy when the necrotic pedicle sloughs.
> - **Postoperative Urinary Retention:** Most common acute complication after hemorrhoidectomy or anorectal surgery (up to 15–20%, due to reflex pelvic floor/detrusor spasm and pain) $\rightarrow$ prevent by limiting perioperative IV fluids (<500 mL) and using multimodal non-opioid analgesia.
> - **Anal Stenosis & Fecal Incontinence:** Late complications of over-aggressive excisional hemorrhoidectomy (removing too much anoderm bridging the columns) or lateral internal sphincterotomy/fistulotomy.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> **Scenario 1 (Chronic Anal Fissure):** A 32-year-old woman with chronic constipation presents with 2 months of severe, "razor-blade" rectal pain during bowel movements that throbs for 2 hours afterward, accompanied by bright red blood on the toilet paper. External inspection by gently parting the buttocks reveals a **posterior midline (6 o'clock) mucosal tear** with a distal **sentinel skin tag** and visible white internal sphincter fibers; DRE and anoscopy are deferred due to pain. She is treated with **Psyllium fiber, warm sitz baths, and Topical 0.3% Nifedipine ointment TID for 8 weeks**, achieving complete fissure healing.
> **Scenario 2 (Perianal Abscess):** A 45-year-old man with Type 2 diabetes presents with 3 days of worsening, constant throbbing perianal pain that prevents him from sitting, and a temperature of 38.4°C. Exam reveals a 3-cm fluctuant, erythematous, exquisitely tender swelling 2 cm lateral to the anal verge. He undergoes **immediate bedside Incision and Drainage** with a cruciate incision placed close to the anal verge, and because he is diabetic with surrounding cellulitis, he is prescribed a short course of **ciprofloxacin and metronidazole** with colorectal surgery follow-up to monitor for subsequent **fistula-in-ano** formation.
