> [!abstract] Viral Hepatitis
> Systemic viral infections with primary hepatotropism (**HAV, HBV, HCV, HDV, HEV**): **HAV & HEV** are fecal-orally transmitted **acute-only** infections (except chronic HEV in transplant recipients); **HBV, HCV, & HDV** are parenterally/sexually/perinatally transmitted and cause **chronic hepatitis, cirrhosis, and Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC)**.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Hepatocyte injury is immune-mediated (NOT directly cytopathic):** Cytotoxic $\text{CD8}^+$ T cells recognize viral antigens on hepatocyte MHC Class I and trigger apoptosis/necrosis (explains why perinatal HBV—with immature immunity—has 90% chronicity with minimal acute symptoms, whereas adult HBV has 95% clearance with brisk acute hepatitis).
> - **HBV persistence & Direct Oncogenesis:** HBV is a partially double-stranded DNA hepadnavirus that forms an indestructible nuclear minichromosome (**cccDNA**, causes reactivation during immunosuppression) and integrates into host DNA (**HBx protein** inhibits p53 $\rightarrow$ **HBV can cause HCC _without_ cirrhosis!**; HCV requires cirrhosis first).

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Treat ALL viremic HCV patients:** Every patient with detectable **HCV RNA** gets **8–12 weeks of pan-genotypic Direct-Acting Antivirals (DAAs: Sofosbuvir/Velpatasvir or Glecaprevir/Pibrentasvir)**, achieving **>95% cure (SVR12)**.
> - **Screen for HBV BEFORE treating HCV or giving Rituximab/Anti-TNF:** Treating HCV with DAAs (or giving anti-CD20/anti-TNF therapy) in a patient with positive **HBsAg** or isolated **Anti-HBc** can trigger **fatal HBV reactivation and fulminant liver failure**.
> - **HCC screening in HBV even WITHOUT cirrhosis:** Perform **RUQ Ultrasound + AFP every 6 months** in **ALL cirrhotic** HBV/HCV patients (even _after_ HCV cure!) AND in **non-cirrhotic HBsAg+ patients** who are **Asian men >40 y, Asian women >50 y, Black/African patients >20 y (or at diagnosis), or have a family history of HCC**.
> - **HDV requires HBsAg:** Hepatitis D is a defective RNA virus that requires the **HBsAg coat** to replicate; **superinfection** of a chronic HBsAg+ carrier with HDV carries the highest risk of rapid cirrhosis and fulminant liver failure.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Acute Viral Hepatitis (3 Phases):**
>   1. _Prodromal (Pre-icteric):_ Anorexia, nausea, vomiting, malaise, low-grade fever, **distaste for cigarettes/food**, dull RUQ ache; **Serum Sickness-like Syndrome** in ~10–20% of acute HBV (fever, urticarial rash, polyarthralgias).
>   2. _Icteric (1–2 weeks later):_ **Dark urine (conjugated bilirubinuria)** precedes jaundice/scleral icterus and pale stools; systemic fever/malaise typically _improve_ as jaundice peaks.
>   3. _Convalescent:_ Resolution of jaundice over 4–8 weeks (HAV may show **relapsing hepatitis** or prolonged cholestasis).
> - **Chronic HBV / HCV:** **Asymptomatic** (or mild fatigue) for decades until presenting with stigmata of **cirrhosis** or **HCC**.
> - **Transmission & Epidemiology Clues:**
>   - **HAV:** Fecal-oral; raw shellfish, contaminated water/produce, daycare, MSM, homelessness, international travel.
>   - **HBV:** Perinatal (vertical transmission is #1 cause globally), sexual contact, IV drug use, needlestick (30% transmission risk).
>   - **HCV:** **IV drug use** (#1 cause), blood transfusion/organ transplant **prior to 1992**, unsterile tattoos, high-risk MSM (HIV+), needlestick (~1.8% risk; low sexual/perinatal risk ~5%).
>   - **HDV:** Endemic regions (Mediterranean, Eastern Europe, Amazon basin, Mongolia) or IV drug users with HBV.
>   - **HEV:** Enteric waterborne (Genotypes 1/2 in Asia/Africa; **20–25% mortality from fulminant liver failure in 3rd-trimester pregnant women!**) or undercooked pork/wild boar/deer (Genotype 3 zoonosis in developed countries; can become chronic in solid-organ transplant recipients).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Acute:** Scleral icterus, jaundice, tender smooth hepatomegaly, mild splenomegaly (10–20%).
> - **Extrahepatic Immune-Complex Manifestations (HBV & HCV):**
>   - **HBV:** **Polyarteritis Nodosa (PAN)** (medium-vessel necrotizing vasculitis: hypertension, mononeuritis multiplex, mesenteric ischemia, renal microaneurysms; 30% are HBsAg+) and **Membranous Nephropathy** (or MPGN).
>   - **HCV:** **Mixed Essential Cryoglobulinemia (Type II/III)** (triad of **palpable purpura** [leukocytoclastic vasculitis], arthralgias, and weakness + low C4 + positive rheumatoid factor + **MPGN**), **Porphyria Cutanea Tard (PCT)** (blistering photosensitivity on dorsal hands + hypertrichosis from uroporphyrinogen decarboxylase inhibition), **Lichen Planus**, Sialadenitis, and **B-cell Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma** (marginal zone).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Acute Hepatitis A:** Positive **Anti-HAV IgM** (Anti-HAV IgG indicates lifelong immunity from past infection or vaccination).
> - **Hepatitis B Serologic Profiles:**
>   - **Acute HBV:** **HBsAg (+)** + **Anti-HBc IgM (+)** (During the **"Window Period"**, HBsAg has cleared and Anti-HBs is not yet detectable $\rightarrow$ **Anti-HBc IgM is the ONLY positive marker!**).
>   - **Chronic HBV:** **HBsAg (+) persisting for >6 months** + **Anti-HBc IgG (+)** + Anti-HBc IgM (–).
>   - **Resolved Past HBV Infection:** HBsAg (–) + **Anti-HBc IgG (+)** + **Anti-HBs (+)**.
>   - **Vaccinated for HBV:** HBsAg (–) + Anti-HBc (–) + **Anti-HBs (+) ONLY** (≥10 mIU/mL).
> - **Active Hepatitis C Infection (Acute or Chronic):** Positive **Anti-HCV Antibody** CONFIRMED by detectable **Quantitative HCV RNA by PCR** (_Anti-HCV (+) with undetectable HCV RNA indicates spontaneous clearance [20%] or past cured infection_).
> - **Hepatitis D Infection:** **HBsAg (+)** + **Anti-HDV Antibody (+)** CONFIRMED by detectable **Quantitative HDV RNA**.
> - **Acute Hepatitis E:** Positive **Anti-HEV IgM** and/or detectable **HEV RNA** in serum/stool.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs & Fibrosis Staging**
>
> - **Universal Screening Guidelines (CDC):** Screen **ALL adults ≥18 years at least once in their lifetime** AND **all pregnant women during every pregnancy** for **BOTH HBV** (**Triple Panel: HBsAg, Anti-HBs, Total Anti-HBc**) and **HCV** (**Anti-HCV with reflex to HCV RNA**).
> - **Chronic HBV Phase & Activity Workup:**
>   - Check **HBeAg**, **Anti-HBe**, **Quantitative HBV DNA (IU/mL)**, **ALT** (\*Note: True normal ALT is **≤35 U/L in men** and **≤25 U/L in women\***), CBC, CMP, INR, **Anti-HDV** (screen all HBsAg+ patients), and coinfection screen (**Anti-HAV IgG, HCV, HIV**).
>   - _Note on Precore / Core Promoter Mutant HBV:_ Patients who are **HBeAg (–)** and **Anti-HBe (+)** can still have **high HBV DNA (>2,000 IU/mL)** and active necroinflammation (**HBeAg-negative Chronic Hepatitis B**) due to a stop-codon mutation preventing HBeAg secretion while viral replication continues.
> - **Pre-Treatment HCV Workup (Simplified AASLD/IDSA Pathway):** CBC, CMP, INR, **Quantitative HCV RNA**, **HBV Triple Panel (HBsAg, Anti-HBs, Anti-HBc)**, HIV Ag/Ab, pregnancy test, and **Non-Invasive Fibrosis Staging** (**FIB-4 Score** calculated from Age, AST, ALT, Platelets [cutoff **<1.45** rules out advanced fibrosis; **>3.25** indicates advanced fibrosis/cirrhosis]).
>   - _Note:_ **HCV Genotype testing is NO LONGER routinely required** before starting pan-genotypic DAAs in treatment-naive patients without decompensated cirrhosis.
>
> **Imaging**
>
> - **Vibration-Controlled Transient Elastography (FibroScan):** Non-invasive gold standard to measure liver stiffness (kPa) and stage fibrosis (**F0–F4**) without liver biopsy.
> - **RUQ Ultrasound + Serum Alpha-Fetoprotein (AFP) every 6 months:** Mandatory **lifelong HCC surveillance** in all HBV/HCV patients with **F3 (bridging fibrosis) or F4 (cirrhosis)**—_including after achieving HCV SVR12 cure_—and in high-risk non-cirrhotic HBV populations.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **New-onset encephalopathy (asterixis, sleep-wake reversal) or INR ≥1.5** in acute viral hepatitis ($\rightarrow$ **Acute Liver Failure**; requires immediate ICU transfer to a Liver Transplant Center and **emergent Entecavir/Tenofovir** if HBV).
> - **Acute Hepatitis E in a 3rd-trimester pregnant patient** (20–25% risk of fulminant hepatic failure and fetal loss).
> - **Planned Rituximab (anti-CD20), stem cell transplant, anthracyclines, Anti-TNF, or high-dose steroids (≥20 mg/day for ≥4 weeks)** in any patient with **HBsAg (+)** OR **HBsAg (–) / Anti-HBc (+)** ($\rightarrow$ high risk of fatal **HBV reactivation**; requires mandatory antiviral prophylaxis).
> - **Liver mass or rising AFP** on surveillance ultrasound ($\rightarrow$ order multiphase contrast-enhanced CT or MRI [LI-RADS] for **Hepatocellular Carcinoma**).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Drug-Induced Liver Injury (DILI) / Acetaminophen Toxicity:** Always check a serum acetaminophen level in acute transaminases >1,000 U/L.
> - **Autoimmune Hepatitis (AIH):** Positive ANA, Anti-Smooth Muscle Antibody (ASMA), Anti-LKM1, and elevated IgG; can be triggered by or mimic acute viral hepatitis.
> - **Ischemic Hepatitis ("Shock Liver") & Acute Budd-Chiari Syndrome.**
> - **Acute Wilson Disease:** Age <40, Coombs-negative hemolytic anemia, low ceruloplasmin, AST:ALT >2.2, ALP:Bilirubin <4.
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Alcohol-Associated Hepatitis:** AST:ALT ≥2:1 with transaminases <300–400 U/L.
> - **Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatohepatitis (MASH / NASH):** AST/ALT mildly elevated (ALT > AST, <200 U/L) with obesity, Type 2 DM, and dyslipidemia.
> - **Non-Hepatotropic Viral Hepatitis:** **EBV** (Infectious Mononucleosis: fever, exudative pharyngitis, posterior cervical lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly), **CMV**, **HSV** (fever + severe anicteric hepatitis in immunocompromised/pregnant).

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **HAV / HEV:** Symptomatic support and prevention of acute liver failure; full spontaneous clinical and biochemical recovery.
> - **Chronic HBV:** **Functional suppression of HBV DNA to undetectable levels**, normalization of ALT, **HBeAg seroconversion** (if HBeAg+), and ideally **HBsAg loss ("functional cure")** to prevent cirrhosis and HCC (_Note: Complete eradication of nuclear cccDNA is NOT achieved with current therapies_).
> - **Chronic HCV:** **Sustained Virologic Response at 12 weeks post-treatment (SVR12)** (undetectable HCV RNA 12 weeks after finishing DAAs = **>99% permanent virologic cure**).
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Start Entecavir (0.5–1 mg PO daily) or Tenofovir (TDF or TAF PO daily) immediately** in any patient with **Acute Hepatitis B complicated by Acute Liver Failure (INR ≥1.5 or encephalopathy)** or severe protracted jaundice (total bilirubin >3–10 mg/dL for >4 weeks) (_Uncomplicated acute HBV in immunocompetent adults requires supportive care only, as >95% clear spontaneously_).
> - **Administer Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) within 24 hours to 2 weeks of exposure:**
>   - **HAV Exposure:** Give **Single-Antigen HAV Vaccine** (healthy patients aged ≥1 year) **PLUS Immune Globulin (IG)** if age >40, immunocompromised, or chronic liver disease.
>   - **HBV Exposure (Perinatal / Needlestick / Sexual in unvaccinated):** Give **Hepatitis B Immune Globulin (HBIG) IM + HBV Vaccine Series (at different anatomical sites)** within **12 hours of birth** for infants born to HBsAg+ mothers (plus give **maternal Tenofovir [TDF] starting at 28 weeks gestation** if maternal **HBV DNA >200,000 IU/mL**!).
>
> **Chronic Hepatitis B Targeted Therapy (AASLD / 2024 WHO Guidelines)**
>
> - **Initiate Indefinite First-Line Oral Nucleos(t)ide Analogue Monotherapy**—specifically **Entecavir (0.5 mg PO daily; 1 mg if prior lamivudine/decompensated)**, **Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate (TDF 300 mg PO daily)**, or **Tenofovir Alafenamide (TAF 25 mg PO daily; preferred over TDF in CKD or osteoporosis)** (_high genetic barrier to resistance_)—in ANY HBsAg+ patient meeting the following criteria:
>   1. **All patients with Cirrhosis (Compensated or Decompensated)** and **any detectable HBV DNA** (regardless of ALT level or HBeAg status!).
>   2. **Immune-Active Chronic HBV:** **ALT >2× ULN** (>70 U/L in men, >50 U/L in women) (or significant fibrosis ≥F2 on FibroScan/biopsy) **PLUS** elevated HBV DNA (**>20,000 IU/mL if HBeAg-positive**; **>2,000 IU/mL if HBeAg-negative**).
>   3. **Acute Liver Failure or Severe Acute HBV**, **Extrahepatic HBV manifestations** (Polyarteritis Nodosa, glomerulonephritis), **Family history of HCC/cirrhosis**, or **Pregnancy with HBV DNA >200,000 IU/mL** (start TDF at 28 weeks).
>   4. **Mandatory Immunosuppression Prophylaxis:** Start Entecavir/Tenofovir **before** initiating **Rituximab (anti-CD20)** or stem cell transplant in **ALL HBsAg (+) patients AND all HBsAg (–) / Anti-HBc (+) patients**, and continue for **≥12–18 months after stopping Rituximab**.
> - **Monitor without immediate antivirals (check ALT and HBV DNA q3–6 months):**
>   - **Immune-Tolerant Phase:** Young patients with HBeAg (+), very high HBV DNA (>1 million IU/mL), persistently **normal ALT**, and no fibrosis.
>   - **Inactive Carrier Phase:** HBeAg (–), Anti-HBe (+), **HBV DNA <2,000 IU/mL**, persistently **normal ALT**, and no fibrosis.
>
> **Hepatitis D & Chronic Hepatitis E Management**
>
> - **Treat Active Chronic HDV (HDV RNA+) with Pegylated Interferon-alfa-2a (for 48 weeks) and/or Bulevirtide** (2 mg SC daily; sodium taurocholate cotransporting polypeptide [NTCP] entry inhibitor approved in Europe) (_Note: Entecavir/Tenofovir do NOT suppress HDV replication unless needed for concurrent active HBV DNA_).
> - **Treat Chronic HEV in solid-organ transplant recipients** by **reducing immunosuppression first**, followed by a **12-week course of Oral Ribavirin** if viremia persists.
>
> **Chronic Hepatitis C Curative Therapy (Pan-Genotypic DAAs — AASLD/IDSA)**
>
> - **Prescribe a First-Line Pan-Genotypic Direct-Acting Antiviral (DAA) Regimen** for **ALL patients with detectable HCV RNA** (including acute HCV—do not wait 6 months for spontaneous clearance):
>   - **Glecaprevir / Pibrentasvir (Mavyret: 3 tablets PO daily with food)** for **8 weeks** (for treatment-naive patients without cirrhosis AND with compensated Child-Pugh A cirrhosis).
>   - **Sofosbuvir / Velpatasvir (Epclusa: 1 tablet PO daily)** for **12 weeks** (for treatment-naive patients without cirrhosis or with compensated Child-Pugh A cirrhosis).
> - **Treat Decompensated HCV Cirrhosis (Child-Pugh Class B or C):** **Prescribe Sofosbuvir / Velpatasvir + Weight-Based Ribavirin for 12 weeks** (or 24 weeks without ribavirin if ribavirin-ineligible) and refer for liver transplant.
> - **Check Quantitative HCV RNA and ALT at ≥12 weeks AFTER completing DAA therapy** to document **SVR12 (Virologic Cure)**.
>
> **Vaccination & Prevention**
>
> - **Vaccinate all chronic HBV and HCV patients against Hepatitis A (HAV)** (and vaccinate all HCV patients against **HBV**) if non-immune, as superimposed acute HAV/HBV on chronic liver disease carries a high mortality rate from fulminant failure.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Protease Inhibitor-containing HCV regimens (Glecaprevir/Pibrentasvir [Mavyret] or Grazoprevir) and Pegylated Interferon in Decompensated Cirrhosis (Child-Pugh Class B or C)**, as NS3/4A protease inhibitors undergo hepatic metabolism and precipitate **fatal hepatic decompensation and liver failure** (use **Sofosbuvir/Velpatasvir** ONLY).
> - **Avoid Co-administration of Sofosbuvir with Amiodarone** to prevent life-threatening **symptomatic bradycardia and cardiac arrest**.
> - **Avoid High-Dose Acid Suppressants (PPIs > Omeprazole 20 mg equivalent) with Sofosbuvir/Velpatasvir or Ledipasvir**, as elevated gastric pH drastically reduces Velpatasvir/Ledipasvir absorption and causes **HCV treatment failure**.
> - **Avoid Ribavirin in Pregnancy (or in male partners of pregnant women) and for 6 months after stopping therapy** (Category X potent **teratogen**), and avoid in severe anemia/hemolysis or end-stage renal disease.
> - **Avoid Lamivudine, Adefovir, or Telbivudine for Chronic HBV**, as they have a low genetic barrier to resistance (>70% resistance at 5 years with Lamivudine; always use **Entecavir, TDF, or TAF**).
> - **Avoid Abrupt Discontinuation of Entecavir or Tenofovir in Cirrhotic HBV Patients**, as stopping nucleos(t)ide analogues triggers severe **rebound HBV viremia and fatal hepatic flares**.
> - **Avoid Discontinuing 6-Month HCC Ultrasound Surveillance after curing HCV (SVR12) in patients who already had F3 bridging fibrosis or F4 cirrhosis prior to treatment**—their HCC risk is reduced by ~70% but remains above the threshold requiring lifelong screening!

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC):** Chronic HBV (via integration + inflammation) and HCV (via cirrhosis) account for >75% of global HCC $\rightarrow$ screen with **RUQ Ultrasound + AFP q6m**.
> - **HBV Reactivation:** Abrupt surge in HBV DNA (≥2 log IU/mL) or **seroreversion from HBsAg (–) to HBsAg (+)** during Rituximab, chemotherapy, or HCV DAA therapy $\rightarrow$ presents with severe hepatitis flare, jaundice, and acute liver failure $\rightarrow$ prevent with prophylactic **Entecavir or Tenofovir**.
> - **Mixed Cryoglobulinemic Vasculitis (HCV):** Palpable purpura, arthralgias, peripheral neuropathy, and **Membranoproliferative Glomerulonephritis (MPGN)** with low C4 $\rightarrow$ curing HCV with **DAAs** resolves mild-to-moderate vasculitis; add **Rituximab + Plasmapheresis** for severe renal/neurologic involvement.
> - **Fibrosing Cholestatic Hepatitis:** Rare, rapidly fatal variant of HBV or HCV occurring in profoundly immunosuppressed solid-organ transplant recipients $\rightarrow$ massive viral replication becomes **directly cytopathic** to hepatocytes $\rightarrow$ severe cholestasis and rapid graft loss.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> **Scenario 1 (Chronic HCV):** A 44-year-old man with a history of remote IV drug use screens positive for **Anti-HCV Antibody** with a confirmatory **HCV RNA of 1,800,000 IU/mL**. Labs show AST 58 U/L, ALT 74 U/L, platelets 210,000/µL (FIB-4 = 1.1, ruling out cirrhosis), and his HBV triple panel shows HBsAg (–), Anti-HBc (–), and Anti-HBs (–). He is vaccinated against HAV and HBV, treated with **8 weeks of Glecaprevir/Pibrentasvir (Mavyret)**, and achieves undetectable HCV RNA 12 weeks post-treatment (**SVR12 cure**); because he had no advanced fibrosis (F0–F2), no further liver follow-up or HCC screening is needed.
> **Scenario 2 (HBV Reactivation Prophylaxis):** A 62-year-old woman is diagnosed with Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma and scheduled to receive **R-CHOP (containing Rituximab)**. Pre-chemotherapy screening reveals **HBsAg (–), Anti-HBc Total (+), and Anti-HBs (+)** with undetectable HBV DNA (resolved past HBV infection). Because Rituximab carries a >10–20% risk of fatal **HBV seroreversion**, she is started on prophylactic **Entecavir 0.5 mg PO daily** prior to chemotherapy and continued on it for **12–18 months after her final dose of Rituximab**.
