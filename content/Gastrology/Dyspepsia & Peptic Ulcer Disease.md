> [!abstract] Dyspepsia & Peptic Ulcer Disease
> **Dyspepsia:** Chronic or recurrent epigastric pain/burning, postprandial fullness, or early satiation (~75% is **Functional Dyspepsia** with negative endoscopy; ~25% is organic, primarily **PUD**, GERD, or malignancy).
> **Peptic Ulcer Disease (PUD):** Mucosal defect **≥5 mm** penetrating through the **muscularis mucosae** into the submucosa of the stomach or duodenum, driven in >90% of cases by **_Helicobacter pylori_** or **NSAIDs**.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **_H. pylori_:** Urease-producing Gram-negative rod; **antral-predominant** infection depletes somatostatin (D-cells) $\rightarrow$ hypergastrinemia and high acid $\rightarrow$ **duodenal ulcers**; **pangastritis/corpus** infection destroys parietal cells $\rightarrow$ hypochlorhydria, mucosal atrophy, **gastric ulcers**, and adenocarcinoma.
> - **NSAIDs / Aspirin:** Direct topical weak-acid epithelial injury + systemic **COX-1 inhibition** $\rightarrow$ depleted mucosal prostaglandins ($\text{PGE}_2, \text{PGI}_2$) $\rightarrow$ reduced mucus, bicarbonate, and mucosal blood flow.
> - **Anatomic complications:** **Posterior** duodenal ulcers erode into the **gastroduodenal artery** (massive hemorrhage) or pancreas; **anterior** duodenal ulcers perforate freely into the peritoneal cavity.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Age 60 is the magic EGD cutoff (ACG/CAG):** In uninvestigated dyspepsia, perform **prompt EGD** if age **≥60 years** (or if alarm features are present); if **<60 years without alarm features**, use a non-invasive **_H. pylori_ "test-and-treat"** strategy first.
> - **Always wash out before _H. pylori_ testing:** Stop **PPIs for ≥2 weeks** and **antibiotics/bismuth for ≥4 weeks** prior to Urea Breath Test (UBT), stool antigen, or endoscopic biopsy to prevent false-negative results (H2RAs/antacids do not need a prolonged washout).
> - **Scope Gastric Ulcers twice, Duodenal Ulcers once:** All **gastric ulcers** require **biopsy at index EGD** AND **repeat surveillance EGD in 8–12 weeks** to confirm healing and rule out gastric adenocarcinoma (5–10% of malignant ulcers appear benign initially); uncomplicated duodenal ulcers do not require repeat EGD.
> - **Bismuth Quadruple Therapy (or Vonoprazan-based therapy) is first-line:** Empiric Clarithromycin triple therapy is **no longer recommended** in North America/Europe unless local clarithromycin resistance is proven <15% and the patient has zero prior macrolide exposure.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Duodenal Ulcer (DU):** Gnawing/burning epigastric pain occurring **2–5 hours after meals** (when acid is unbuffered) and **at night (11 PM–2 AM)**; classically **relieved by food or antacids**.
> - **Gastric Ulcer (GU):** Epigastric pain **worsened or triggered immediately by eating** $\rightarrow$ leads to food aversion, anorexia, and **weight loss**.
> - **NSAID-induced ulcers:** Frequently **silent/asymptomatic** until presenting abruptly with GI hemorrhage or perforation.
> - **Functional Dyspepsia subtypes:** **Postprandial Distress Syndrome (PDS)** (bothersome postprandial fullness or early satiation) vs. **Epigastric Pain Syndrome (EPS)** (epigastric pain or burning not exclusively relieved by defecation).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Uncomplicated Dyspepsia/PUD:** Mild localized epigastric tenderness or completely normal exam.
> - **Perforation:** **Sudden, excruciating generalized abdominal pain**, **board-like abdominal rigidity**, rebound tenderness, absent bowel sounds, tachycardia, and **shoulder-tip pain** (phrenic nerve irritation from subdiaphragmatic free air).
> - **Gastric Outlet Obstruction (GOO):** Early satiety, recurrent projectile non-bilious vomiting of undigested food, hypochloremic hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis, and a **succussion splash** on abdominal auscultation.
> - **Malignancy clues (Gastric Cancer):** Palpable **Virchow node** (left supraclavicular), **Sister Mary Joseph nodule** (periumbilical), **Irish node** (left anterior axillary), **Blumer shelf** on DRE, or acanthosis nigricans.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Peptic Ulcer Disease:** Direct visualization on **EGD** of a discrete mucosal break **≥5 mm** in diameter with perceptible depth (extending through the muscularis mucosae) in the stomach or duodenum.
> - **Active _H. pylori_ Infection:** Positive **13C/14C Urea Breath Test (UBT)**, **fecal monoclonal antigen test**, or **endoscopic gastric biopsy** (rapid urease test or histology) (_Note: Serum IgG serology cannot distinguish active from past cured infection and should NOT be used_).
> - **Functional Dyspepsia (Rome IV Criteria):** **≥1** symptom (postprandial fullness, early satiation, epigastric pain, or epigastric burning) with **onset ≥6 months ago**, active for the **past 3 months**, AND **no structural disease on EGD** to explain the symptoms.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **Initial (<60 y, no alarm features):** **Stool _H. pylori_ monoclonal antigen** or **Urea Breath Test (UBT)**, CBC (screen for iron-deficiency anemia).
> - **Refractory / Multiple / Distal Duodenal Ulcers (Zollinger-Ellison Screen):** Check **Fasting Serum Gastrin** (while off PPI for ≥1 week) and gastric pH.
>   - _Interpretation:_ **Fasting gastrin >1,000 pg/mL** + **gastric pH <2** is diagnostic of **Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome (gastrinoma)**. If gastrin is equivocal (100–1,000 pg/mL), perform a **Secretin Stimulation Test** (paradoxical rise in gastrin ≥120 pg/mL confirms gastrinoma; normal gastric G-cells are _inhibited_ by secretin).
>
> **Imaging**
>
> - **Upright Chest X-Ray or CT Abdomen/Pelvis:** Order STAT if **perforation** is suspected to detect **pneumoperitoneum** (free air under the diaphragm).
> - **Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD) with Biopsy:** Mandatory first-line test if **age ≥60**, **any alarm features**, or failure of test-and-treat + 4–8 weeks of PPI therapy.
>
> **Referrals**
>
> - **Gastroenterology:** EGD for alarm symptoms, age ≥60, gastric ulcer surveillance, or refractory _H. pylori_.
> - **General Surgery:** Immediate emergent consultation for **perforated peptic ulcer** (Graham patch repair) or refractory gastric outlet obstruction.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> **Dyspepsia Alarm Features (Mandate Prompt EGD):**
>
> - **New-onset dyspepsia at age ≥60 years**
> - **Unintended weight loss** or progressive **dysphagia / odynophagia**
> - **Overt GI bleeding** (hematemesis, melena) or **unexplained Iron-Deficiency Anemia**
> - **Persistent/recurrent vomiting** (suggests gastric outlet obstruction)
> - **Palpable abdominal mass or lymphadenopathy**
> - **Family history of upper GI malignancy** in a first-degree relative
> - **Sudden-onset rigid abdomen** (Perforation $\rightarrow$ surgical emergency)

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Gastric Adenocarcinoma or MALT Lymphoma:** Indistinguishable clinically from benign GU; mandates biopsy and repeat EGD of all gastric ulcers.
> - **Acute Coronary Syndrome (Inferior MI):** Epigastric "indigestion" with exertional component, diaphoresis, or dyspnea (especially in elderly, women, and diabetics).
> - **Perforated Peptic Ulcer / Acute Pancreatitis:** Sudden severe epigastric pain radiating to the back; differentiate via lipase and upright CXR/CT.
> - **Chronic Mesenteric Ischemia:** Severe postprandial pain ("intestinal angina") 30–60 min after eating, causing severe food fear and weight loss in patients with atherosclerotic disease.
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Functional Dyspepsia:** Accounts for ~70–80% of chronic dyspepsia after negative workup.
> - **Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD):** Retrosternal burning rising toward the neck, regurgitation, sour taste.
> - **Biliary Colic / Cholelithiasis:** Episodic severe RUQ/epigastric pain lasting 1–5 hours after fatty meals; normal between attacks (unlike continuous dyspepsia).
> - **Medication-Induced Dyspepsia:** NSAIDs, oral bisphosphonates, iron/potassium supplements, metformin, GLP-1 receptor agonists, macrolides, SSRIs.
>
> **Most Surprising**
>
> - **Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome (Gastrinoma):** Multiple ulcers, ulcers in the **distal duodenum/jejunum**, refractory ulcers, + chronic secretory diarrhea/steatorrhea (acid inactivates pancreatic lipase); **25% associated with MEN1** (_Parathyroid hyperplasia, Pituitary adenoma, Pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor_ $\rightarrow$ always check **serum calcium**).
>
> **Less Common**
>
> - **Gastroparesis:** Postprandial fullness, nausea, vomiting of undigested food hours after eating in long-standing diabetes or post-viral states (diagnose with **4-hour solid-phase gastric emptying scintigraphy** after ruling out obstruction via EGD).
> - **Curling Ulcer / Cushing Ulcer:** Stress ulcers in severe burns/hypovolemia (**Curling**) or elevated ICP/vagal hyperstimulation (**Cushing**).

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Complete mucosal ulcer healing, **confirmed _H. pylori_ eradication**, and symptom resolution.
> - **Secondary Target:** Prevent ulcer bleeding/perforation, exclude underlying gastric malignancy, and prevent gastric cancer progression.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Keep NPO, place a nasogastric tube, infuse IV crystalloids, start IV PPI + broad-spectrum IV antibiotics (e.g., Piperacillin-Tazobactam or Ceftriaxone + Metronidazole), and consult General Surgery STAT** upon diagnosing a **perforated peptic ulcer** (free intraperitoneal air) for emergent laparoscopic **Graham omental patch repair**.
> - _For Acute Bleeding PUD:_ Refer to **[[Upper GI Bleeding]]** protocol (restrictive transfusion Hb <7 g/dL, high-dose IV PPI, EGD within 24h).
>
> **Stepwise Approach to Uninvestigated Dyspepsia**
>
> - **Perform prompt EGD** as the initial step if **age ≥60 years** OR if **any alarm feature** is present to rule out upper GI malignancy.
> - **Perform non-invasive _H. pylori_ testing (Urea Breath Test or Stool Antigen)** as the initial step in patients **<60 years WITHOUT alarm features** ("test-and-treat" strategy), and initiate an empiric **4–8 week trial of once-daily PPI (e.g., Omeprazole 20–40 mg PO daily, 30–60 min before breakfast)** if _H. pylori_ is negative.
>
> **_H. pylori_ Eradication Regimens (14 Days Mandatory)**
>
> - **Prescribe Bismuth Quadruple Therapy for 14 days** (**PPI BID + Bismuth subsalicylate/subcitrate QID + Tetracycline 500 mg QID + Metronidazole 500 mg TID–QID**) as **first-line empiric therapy** in all patients (including penicillin-allergic patients) due to high global clarithromycin resistance.
> - **Prescribe Vonoprazan Dual or Triple Therapy for 14 days** (**Vonoprazan 20 mg BID + Amoxicillin 1,000 mg TID** [ ± **Clarithromycin 500 mg BID**]) or **Rifabutin Triple Therapy (Talicia)** as highly effective modern first-line alternatives (per 2024 ACG guidelines).
> - **Switch to a salvage regimen with different antibiotics for 14 days** (e.g., **Bismuth Quadruple** if not used initially, **Vonoprazan-based therapy**, **High-Dose Amoxicillin Dual Therapy**, or **Levofloxacin Triple Therapy** if susceptibility-proven) if first-line eradication fails.
> - **Perform mandatory Test-of-Cure** using a **Urea Breath Test or Stool Antigen ≥4 weeks after completing therapy** (and after holding PPIs for ≥2 weeks) in **ALL** treated patients to confirm eradication.
>
> **Ulcer Healing & NSAID Management**
>
> - **Discontinue all NSAIDs immediately** upon diagnosing PUD and prescribe a **PPI once daily for 4 weeks (for uncomplicated duodenal ulcers) or 8 weeks (for gastric ulcers)** to achieve complete mucosal healing.
> - **Continue daily PPI co-therapy indefinitely** (and switch from a non-selective NSAID to **Celecoxib [COX-2 inhibitor] + PPI**) if the patient has an absolute requirement to remain on NSAIDs or low-dose aspirin.
> - **Initiate prophylactic PPI therapy** in patients WITHOUT a prior ulcer history who start chronic NSAIDs ONLY if they have **high-risk GI features** (**Age >65**, **high-dose NSAID**, or **concurrent Aspirin, Corticosteroids, or Anticoagulants**).
>
> **Functional Dyspepsia Management (After Negative EGD & _H. pylori_ Eradication)**
>
> - **Trial a low-dose Tricyclic Antidepressant (TCA)** (e.g., **Amitriptyline or Nortriptyline 10–25 mg PO QHS** for 8–12 weeks) as first-line neuromodulator therapy if an 8-week trial of PPI fails, particularly for **Epigastric Pain Syndrome (EPS)**.
> - **Trial a Prokinetic agent** (e.g., **Metoclopramide**, **Domperidone**, or **Buspirone 10 mg TID** to relax the gastric fundus) for refractory **Postprandial Distress Syndrome (PDS)**.
>
> **Disposition & Follow-up**
>
> - **Perform a repeat surveillance EGD at 8–12 weeks** in **ALL patients with a Gastric Ulcer** (and in duodenal ulcers _only_ if symptoms persist or the ulcer was giant >2 cm) to document complete healing and biopsy any non-healing defect to exclude occult malignancy.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Clarithromycin Triple Therapy (PPI + Clarithromycin + Amoxicillin)** as empiric first-line _H. pylori_ treatment unless local clarithromycin resistance is documented <15% and the patient has never taken a macrolide (e.g., azithromycin Z-Pak), to prevent treatment failure (>20–30% failure rate).
> - **Avoid using _H. pylori_ Serum IgG Serology** for diagnosis or test-of-cure, as antibodies remain positive for years after cure ("serologic scar") and lead to inappropriate retreatment.
> - **Avoid testing for _H. pylori_ while the patient is taking a PPI (within 2 weeks) or antibiotics/bismuth (within 4 weeks)** to prevent false-negative Urea Breath Tests, stool antigen tests, and endoscopic biopsies.
> - **Avoid Substituted Doxycycline in place of Tetracycline** in Bismuth Quadruple Therapy, as doxycycline results in significantly lower _H. pylori_ eradication rates.
> - **Avoid Misoprostol** in women of childbearing potential (and generally prefer PPIs for NSAID prophylaxis), as misoprostol is an **abortifacient (Category X)** and causes severe dose-limiting secretory diarrhea.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Upper GI Hemorrhage (Most common complication):** Posterior duodenal ulcer erodes into the **gastroduodenal artery** (or lesser curvature gastric ulcer into the **left gastric artery**) $\rightarrow$ hematemesis/melena $\rightarrow$ treat with endoscopic hemoclips/thermocoagulation + high-dose PPI.
> - **Free Perforation:** Anterior duodenal or gastric ulcer perforates into peritoneal cavity $\rightarrow$ sudden rigid abdomen, **pneumoperitoneum** under diaphragm on upright CXR $\rightarrow$ emergent laparoscopic **Graham patch**.
> - **Penetration (Contained Perforation):** Posterior ulcer erodes directly into an adjacent solid organ (**pancreas** most common, or liver/biliary tree) without free air $\rightarrow$ constant back pain refractory to antacids + elevated serum lipase.
> - **Gastric Outlet Obstruction (GOO):** Chronic cycles of pyloric/duodenal ulceration and fibrotic scarring (or antral carcinoma) $\rightarrow$ succussion splash, non-bilious vomiting, hypochloremic hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis $\rightarrow$ NG decompression, IV normal saline + KCl, PPI, and **endoscopic balloon dilation** (after biopsy rules out malignancy).
> - **Gastric MALT Lymphoma & Adenocarcinoma:** Chronic _H. pylori_ gastritis induces mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) proliferation $\rightarrow$ **low-grade stage IE _H. pylori_-positive MALToma is cured in >75% of cases by _H. pylori_ antibiotic eradication alone!**

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 42-year-old man presents with 3 months of gnawing epigastric pain that wakes him up around 1 AM and improves temporarily after eating food; he denies weight loss, dysphagia, melena, or NSAID use. Because he is **<60 years old with no alarm features**, a **stool _H. pylori_ monoclonal antigen test** is ordered instead of an immediate EGD, which returns positive. He is prescribed **14 days of Bismuth Quadruple Therapy** (omeprazole BID, bismuth subsalicylate QID, tetracycline QID, and metronidazole QID), stops all PPI therapy after treatment completion, and undergoes a **fecal antigen test-of-cure 4 weeks later** confirming eradication.
