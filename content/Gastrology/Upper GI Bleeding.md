> [!abstract] Upper GI Bleeding
> Acute intraluminal hemorrhage originating proximal to the ligament of Treitz, broadly categorized into non-variceal (predominantly peptic ulcer disease) and portal hypertensive (variceal) etiologies.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Non-variceal:** Acid/peptic erosion into submucosal arteries (e.g., gastroduodenal or left gastric artery) or mucosal sheer tear (Mallory-Weiss).
> - **Variceal:** Portal hypertension (HVPG >12 mmHg) causes portosystemic collateral dilation and rupture (most commonly gastroesophageal junction).
> - **BUN/Cr elevation:** Intraluminal digestion and absorption of blood proteins coupled with renal hypoperfusion.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Transfusion threshold:** Restrictive strategy (**Hb <7 g/dL**) improves survival in both variceal and non-variceal UGIB compared to liberal transfusion (which increases portal pressure and rebleeding).
> - **Hematochezia + hemodynamic instability** is an UGIB until proven otherwise (massive brisk bleeding speeds up gut transit).
> - **Initial hemoglobin** may be falsely normal in acute massive bleeding due to whole blood loss prior to fluid equilibration; rely on vitals, not initial Hb.
> - **Prokinetic before scope:** IV erythromycin (250 mg, 30–90 min prior to EGD) clears gastric clots and reduces the need for repeat endoscopy.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Hematemesis** (bright red blood = brisk active bleed; coffee-ground = slower/stopped bleed exposed to gastric acid)
> - **Melena** (black, tarry, foul-smelling stool; requires ≥50 mL blood in GI tract)
> - **Hematochezia** (occurs in ~10–15% of massive UGIB due to rapid cathartic transit of blood)
> - **Risk factors:** NSAIDs, antiplatelets/anticoagulants, prior peptic ulcers, _H. pylori_, heavy alcohol use, known cirrhosis
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Hemodynamic instability:** Resting tachycardia (earliest sign), orthostatic hypotension (≥15–20% volume loss), supine hypotension/shock (≥40% volume loss)
> - **Stigmata of chronic liver disease:** Jaundice, spider angiomata, ascites, splenomegaly, caput medusae (points to variceal source)
> - **Digital rectal exam (DRE):** Melena or maroon/bright red blood

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD):** Direct visualization of bleeding source or stigmata of recent hemorrhage (Forrest classification) proximal to the ligament of Treitz.
> - **Glasgow-Blatchford Score (GBS) = 0–1:** Mandatory threshold to safely identify very-low-risk patients suitable for outpatient management without inpatient EGD.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **Initial:** Type and crossmatch (at least 2 units pRBCs), CBC, CMP, PT/INR, aPTT, fibrinogen (if cirrhosis), lactate.
> - **Interpretation:** **BUN:Cr ratio >30:1** strongly suggests UGIB over LGIB.
>
> **Imaging**
>
> - **CT Angiography (CTA):** Indicated if EGD fails to localize bleeding or patient is too unstable/unsuitable for EGD (detects bleeding rates ≥0.3–0.5 mL/min).
> - **Nasogastric (NG) lavage:** **NOT routinely recommended** (does not improve outcomes and has poor negative predictive value due to pyloric spasm).
>
> **Referrals**
>
> - **Gastroenterology:** Emergent consultation for all admitted UGIB patients for EGD within 12–24 hours.
> - **Interventional Radiology (IR):** Immediate notification if high-risk rebleeding occurs post-EGD or for TIPS in refractory variceal bleeding.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - Hemodynamic instability (SBP <90 mmHg, HR >100 bpm, syncope)
> - Ongoing brisk hematemesis or hematochezia
> - Known or suspected cirrhosis / portal hypertension
> - Coagulopathy or active anticoagulation/antiplatelet therapy
> - Glasgow-Blatchford Score ≥2

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Esophageal/Gastric Varices:** Associated with cirrhosis; high acute mortality.
> - **Peptic Ulcer Disease (PUD):** Most common cause (~50%); posterior duodenal ulcers erode into gastroduodenal artery.
> - **Aortoenteric Fistula:** History of aortic graft + "herald bleed" followed by massive exsanguination; requires immediate CTA/vascular surgery.
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Erosive Esophagitis / Gastritis / Duodenitis:** NSAID/alcohol-induced mucosal injury; rarely causes massive bleeding.
> - **Mallory-Weiss Tear:** Longitudinal mucosal laceration at GEJ following retching/vomiting.
>
> **Most Surprising**
>
> - **Dieulafoy Lesion:** Aberrant, large-caliber submucosal artery eroding through normal overlying mucosa, causing sudden massive bleeding.
>
> **Less Common**
>
> - **Gastric Antral Vascular Ectasia (GAVE):** "Watermelon stomach"; causes chronic occult or overt bleeding.
> - **Hemobilia / Hemosuccus Pancreaticus:** Bleeding from biliary tree or pancreatic duct into ampulla of Vater.
>
> **Non-Critical**
>
> - **Swallowed Epistaxis / Hemoptysis:** Mimics hematemesis/melena.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Hemodynamic stability (MAP ≥65 mmHg, HR <100 bpm), **Hb 7–9 g/dL** (or **8–10 g/dL** in active CAD/ACS), endoscopic hemostasis.
> - **Secondary Target:** Prevent 30-day rebleeding, aspiration pneumonia, and decompensation in cirrhotics (SBP, hepatic encephalopathy).
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Place two large-bore IVs (≥18G) and infuse isotonic crystalloids** (500 mL bolus) immediately upon presentation with hemodynamic instability to restore tissue perfusion.
> - **Intubate for airway protection** prior to EGD in patients with ongoing massive hematemesis, severe encephalopathy, or agitation to prevent fatal aspiration.
> - **Transfuse pRBCs** when **Hb <7 g/dL** (or **<8 g/dL** if preexisting cardiovascular disease) or in exsanguinating shock to maintain oxygen delivery without inducing rebound portal hypertension.
> - **Reverse anticoagulation immediately** using **4-factor PCC + IV Vitamin K** (for warfarin) or **Idarucizumab/Andexanet alfa** (for DOACs) in life-threatening bleeding to facilitate clot formation.
>
> **Pre-Endoscopic Pharmacotherapy**
>
> - **Administer IV PPI (e.g., Pantoprazole 80 mg bolus or BID)** upon presentation for suspected non-variceal UGIB to neutralize gastric pH (target pH >6) and stabilize platelet clots.
> - **Start Octreotide** (50 mcg IV bolus, then 50 mcg/hr infusion for 2–5 days) or **Terlipressin** immediately if variceal bleeding is suspected to induce splanchnic vasoconstriction and lower portal pressure.
> - **Give Ceftriaxone 1 g IV daily** (up to 7 days) immediately in all cirrhotic patients with UGIB to prevent bacterial translocation, sepsis, and early rebleeding.
> - **Infuse Erythromycin 250 mg IV** 30–90 minutes prior to EGD to accelerate gastric emptying and improve endoscopic visualization.
>
> **Definitive Endoscopic & Rescue Therapy**
>
> - **Perform EGD within 24 hours** (or **within 12 hours** for suspected variceal bleed or persistent instability) once resuscitated to achieve definitive mechanical/thermal hemostasis (clips, thermocoagulation + epinephrine injection for ulcers; **band ligation** for esophageal varices; **cyanoacrylate glue** for gastric varices).
> - **Continue high-dose PPI (IV or PO BID for 72 hours)** following endoscopic hemostasis of high-risk ulcers (Forrest Ia–IIb: active bleed, visible vessel, or adherent clot) to prevent early rebleeding.
> - **Repeat EGD** upon first recurrence of non-variceal bleeding to attempt a second endoscopic hemostasis.
> - **Perform Transcatheter Arterial Embolization (TAE)** via IR if a second endoscopic attempt fails in non-variceal bleeding to occlude the culprit vessel and avoid high-risk surgery.
> - **Perform emergent TIPS** (within 72 hours) if variceal bleeding fails endoscopic band ligation (or as preemptive therapy in high-risk Child-Pugh C [10–13] or B with active bleeding) to decompress the portal system; use **balloon tamponade (Sengstaken-Blakemore tube)** or esophageal stent for ≤24 hours as a temporary bridge to TIPS.
>
> **Disposition & Follow-up**
>
> - **Discharge home from ED** with outpatient PPI and prompt follow-up if **Glasgow-Blatchford Score is 0–1** to avoid unnecessary hospitalization.
> - **Admit to ICU** if hemodynamically unstable, active hematemesis, or variceal bleed to allow continuous monitoring and rapid escalation.
> - **Test for _H. pylori_ (biopsy or stool antigen)** in all peptic ulcer bleeds, eradicate if positive, and **confirm eradication ≥4 weeks** after completing therapy to prevent recurrent ulcer bleeding.
> - **Resume aspirin for secondary cardiovascular prevention** immediately (within 1–3 days) once hemostasis is achieved, paired with a PPI, as mortality from thrombotic events outweighs rebleeding risk.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid over-transfusion (Hb >9–10 g/dL)** in acute UGIB (especially variceal) to prevent rebound portal hypertension and clot dislodgement.
> - **Avoid epinephrine injection monotherapy** during EGD for bleeding ulcers to prevent high rates of rebleeding (must always be combined with clips or thermal coagulation).
> - **Avoid routine FFP or platelet transfusions** in cirrhotic patients based solely on elevated INR or mild thrombocytopenia (platelets >50,000/µL) to prevent volume overload and worsening portal hypertension.
> - **Avoid permanent discontinuation of secondary-prevention aspirin or anticoagulants** after hemostasis to prevent fatal thromboembolic events (stroke, MI, stent thrombosis).

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Aspiration Pneumonia:** Hematemesis + altered mental status $\rightarrow$ intubate early before EGD.
> - **Hepatic Encephalopathy:** Intraluminal blood acts as a massive protein/ammonia load in cirrhotics $\rightarrow$ treat with lactulose and rifaximin.
> - **Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP) / Sepsis:** High risk of gut translocation in cirrhosis $\rightarrow$ prevented by mandatory IV ceftriaxone prophylaxis.
> - **Demand Myocardial Ischemia (Type 2 MI):** Severe anemia/hypotension $\rightarrow$ check ECG/troponins if chest pain or CAD history; use <8 g/dL Hb transfusion cutoff.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 62-year-old man with osteoarthritis on daily ibuprofen presents with two episodes of coffee-ground emesis and black tarry stools. Vitals show HR 115 bpm and BP 96/60 mmHg; Hb is 8.2 g/dL and BUN/Cr is 48/1.1. Two 18G IVs are placed, a 500 mL crystalloid bolus and IV pantoprazole are administered, pRBCs are held (Hb >7 g/dL), and EGD within 24 hours reveals a duodenal ulcer with a visible vessel (Forrest IIa) treated with epinephrine injection and hemoclips.
