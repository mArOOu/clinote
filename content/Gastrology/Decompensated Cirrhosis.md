> [!abstract] Decompensated Cirrhosis
> Transition from compensated cirrhosis to clinically overt portal hypertension and hepatic synthetic dysfunction, defined by the onset of **ascites**, **variceal hemorrhage**, **hepatic encephalopathy (HE)**, or **jaundice**; median survival drops from >12 years to ~2 years without transplantation.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Sinusoidal Portal Hypertension (HVPG ≥10 mmHg = clinically significant; ≥12 mmHg = variceal bleed risk):** Fibrotic architectural distortion + intrahepatic vasoconstriction $\rightarrow$ splanchnic nitric oxide (NO) overproduction $\rightarrow$ **splanchnic arterial vasodilation**.
> - **Ascites & Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS-AKI):** Splanchnic pooling causes _effective arterial hypovolemia_ $\rightarrow$ persistent RAAS/SNS/ADH activation $\rightarrow$ avid renal Na+/water retention (ascites/hyponatremia) and extreme **renal cortical vasoconstriction** (HRS-AKI).
> - **Hepatic Encephalopathy:** Portosystemic shunting + hepatocyte dysfunction allow gut-derived neurotoxins (**ammonia**, mercaptans, GABAergic mediators) to bypass the liver and induce astrocyte swelling.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Tap every belly on admission:** Perform a **diagnostic paracentesis** in **ALL** cirrhotic patients with ascites admitted to the hospital (even without fever or abdominal pain) to rule out **Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP)**; delays >12 hours double mortality.
> - **Albumin saves lives in 3 specific settings:** (1) **SBP** (Day 1: 1.5 g/kg; Day 3: 1.0 g/kg), (2) **Large-Volume Paracentesis >5 L** (6–8 g per liter removed), and (3) **HRS-AKI** (1 g/kg on Day 1, then 20–40 g/day alongside vasoconstrictors).
> - **Carvedilol is the Baveno VII game-changer:** Start **Carvedilol** (6.25 mg daily $\rightarrow$ 6.25 mg BID) in patients with clinically significant portal hypertension (CSPH) or small/large varices to prevent **first decompensation** (ascites/bleeding) and improve survival—_provided MAP ≥65 mmHg and SBP ≥90 mmHg_.
> - **Do NOT trend serum ammonia:** Ammonia levels do not correlate reliably with HE grade and do not rule in or rule out hepatic encephalopathy; HE is a **clinical diagnosis**.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Decompensating triggers (ALWAYS search for the precipitant):** GI bleeding, **infection** (SBP, UTI, pneumonia), dehydration/over-diuresis, constipation, electrolyte derangements (hypokalemia/alkalosis increases NH3 conversion), sedatives (opioids, benzodiazepines), TIPS placement, or portal vein thrombosis.
> - **Symptoms:** Increasing abdominal girth/weight gain, dyspnea (tense ascites or **hepatic hydrothorax**), sleep-wake reversal/confusion, hematemesis/melena, jaundice, oliguria.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Ascites:** Shifting dullness (requires ≥1,500 mL fluid), fluid wave, bulging flanks.
> - **Hepatic Encephalopathy:** **Asterixis** (negative myoclonus), temporal/spatial disorientation, lethargy, hyperreflexia, fetor hepaticus.
> - **Stigmata of portal hypertension / hyperestrogenism:** Spider angiomata, palmar erythema, gynecomastia, testicular atrophy, caput medusae, splenomegaly, jaundice.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Portal Hypertensive Ascites:** **Serum-Ascites Albumin Gradient (SAAG) ≥1.1 g/dL** (with ascitic total protein **<2.5 g/dL** confirming cirrhosis; ≥2.5 g/dL indicates cardiac/post-sinusoidal ascites).
> - **Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP):** Ascitic fluid **Absolute Polymorphonuclear (PMN) leukocyte count ≥250 cells/mm³** in the absence of an intra-abdominal surgical source (viscus perforation).
> - **Hepatorenal Syndrome-AKI (HRS-AKI):**
>   - Cirrhosis with ascites + **AKI** (increase in sCr **≥0.3 mg/dL within 48h** or **≥50% from baseline** within 7 days).
>   - **No response after 2 consecutive days** of diuretic withdrawal and volume expansion with **IV Albumin (1 g/kg/day, max 100 g/day)**.
>   - Absence of shock, current/recent nephrotoxic drugs (NSAIDs), and structural kidney disease (proteinuria <500 mg/day, <50 RBCs/HPF, normal renal ultrasound).

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **Diagnostic Paracentesis:** Cell count with differential (**PMN count**), total protein, **albumin** (paired with simultaneous serum albumin for SAAG), and **bedside inoculation of ascitic fluid into blood culture bottles** (≥10 mL each into aerobic/anaerobic bottles prior to antibiotics).
>   - _Note:_ If secondary bacterial peritonitis (perforation) is suspected, check ascitic **total protein (>1 g/dL), glucose (<50 mg/dL), and LDH (>ULN)** (**Runyon's criteria**).
> - **Routine Blood Work:** CBC (thrombocytopenia = earliest lab sign of portal hypertension via splenic sequestration), CMP, PT/INR, total bilirubin (calculate **MELD 3.0** and **Child-Pugh score**), blood/urine cultures.
> - **Urinalysis & Urine Electrolytes:** Urine sodium (**UNa <20 mEq/L** and **FEUrea <20%** in HRS-AKI), urine sediment (casts).
>
> **Imaging**
>
> - **RUQ Ultrasound with Doppler:** Screen for **Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC)** (mandatory every 6 months alongside AFP in all cirrhotics), assess **portal/hepatic vein patency** (rule out portal vein thrombosis as cause of acute decompensation), and guide paracentesis.
> - **Non-Contrast Head CT:** Indicated in first-time HE, focal neurologic deficits, or history of falls/ coagulopathy to rule out subdural hematoma.
>
> **Referrals**
>
> - **Liver Transplant Evaluation:** Refer **ALL** patients upon their first decompensating event or when **MELD 3.0 ≥15**.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Fever, abdominal pain, new/worsening HE, or unexplained AKI** in a patient with ascites ($\rightarrow$ **SBP** until proven otherwise).
> - **PMN ≥250 cells/mm³** on paracentesis, or polymicrobial ascitic culture (**suspect perforated viscus** $\rightarrow$ get upright CXR/CT abdomen).
> - **West Haven Grade III–IV Encephalopathy** (inability to protect airway $\rightarrow$ requires ICU intubation).
> - **Rising creatinine (≥0.3 mg/dL in 48h)** or oliguria (**HRS-AKI**).
> - **Hematemesis, melena, or hemodynamic instability** (variceal hemorrhage).
> - **Refractory hypotension (SBP <90 mmHg or MAP <65 mmHg)** or **Severe Hyponatremia (Na <125 mEq/L)**.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss (Acute Decompensation Mimics/Precipitants)**
>
> - **Secondary Bacterial Peritonitis (Perforated Viscus):** Polymicrobial ascitic fluid, PMN often >1,000–10,000/mm³, meets ≥2 Runyon's criteria; requires emergent surgical exploration (mortality ~100% if treated with antibiotics alone).
> - **Subdural Hematoma / Intracranial Hemorrhage:** Mimics hepatic encephalopathy in coagulopathic cirrhotics with frequent falls.
> - **Acute Portal Vein Thrombosis (PVT):** Sudden worsening of ascites or variceal bleeding; may indicate underlying **Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC)** invading the portal vein.
> - **Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN):** Differentiated from HRS-AKI by history of shock/nephrotoxins, muddy brown granular casts, UNa >40 mEq/L, and elevated urinary NGAL.
>
> **Most Likely (Differential of Ascites by SAAG)**
>
> - **SAAG ≥1.1 g/dL (Portal Hypertension):**
>   - _Ascitic Protein <2.5 g/dL:_ **Cirrhosis**, Late Budd-Chiari, Massive liver metastases.
>   - _Ascitic Protein ≥2.5 g/dL:_ **Right-sided Heart Failure**, Constrictive Pericarditis, Early Budd-Chiari.
> - **SAAG <1.1 g/dL (Non-Portal Hypertension):**
>   - **Peritoneal Carcinomatosis** (positive cytology), **Tuberculous Peritonitis** (lymphocytic predominance, elevated ADA), **Pancreatic Ascites** (ascitic amylase >1,000 U/L), **Nephrotic Syndrome**.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Resolution of encephalopathy, sterilization of ascitic fluid (PMN <250/mm³), **weight loss of 0.5 kg/day** (without peripheral edema) or **1.0 kg/day** (with edema), **reversal of HRS-AKI** (sCr within 0.3 mg/dL of baseline).
> - **Secondary Target:** Prevent variceal bleeding, recurrent SBP/HE, and bridge to **Orthotopic Liver Transplantation (OLT)**.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Perform diagnostic paracentesis immediately** (without giving prophylactic FFP or platelets) upon admission in any cirrhotic patient with ascites to rule out SBP.
> - **Administer IV Ceftriaxone (2 g IV daily for 5–7 days)** (or Piperacillin-Tazobactam/Carbapenem if nosocomial/recent hospitalization) immediately if **ascitic PMN ≥250 cells/mm³** to treat SBP (_Most common organisms: E. coli, Klebsiella, Streptococcus_).
> - **Infuse 25% IV Albumin (1.5 g/kg on Day 1 [max 100 g], then 1.0 g/kg on Day 3)** alongside antibiotics in all patients with **SBP** (especially if Cr >1.0 mg/dL, BUN >30 mg/dL, or Bilirubin >4 mg/dL) to prevent HRS-AKI and reduce mortality by two-thirds.
> - **Intubate for airway protection** in patients presenting with **Grade III or IV Hepatic Encephalopathy** to prevent fatal aspiration pneumonia.
>
> **Management of Ascites & Portal Hypertension**
>
> - **Restrict dietary sodium to <2 g/day (88 mmol/day)** and initiate oral diuretics in a **100:40 ratio** (**Spironolactone 100 mg + Furosemide 40 mg PO daily**, titrating up every 3–5 days to max 400 mg / 160 mg) for moderate-volume ascites to maintain normokalemia and mobilize fluid.
> - **Perform therapeutic Large-Volume Paracentesis (LVP)** as first-line treatment for **tense (Grade 3) or refractory ascites**, and **infuse 25% IV Albumin (6–8 g per liter of fluid removed)** whenever **>5 Liters** are drained to prevent **post-paracentesis circulatory dysfunction (PPCD)** and HRS.
> - **Evaluate for Transjugular Intrahepatic Portosystemic Shunt (TIPS)** in patients with **refractory ascites** or **refractory hepatic hydrothorax** (requiring ≥3 LVPs per month or diuretic-intolerant) who have preserved cardiac function and no severe HE (MELD <18).
> - **Initiate a Non-Selective Beta-Blocker (NSBB)**—preferably **Carvedilol (6.25 mg daily $\rightarrow$ 6.25 mg BID)** or **Propranolol/Nadolol** (titrated to HR 55–60 bpm)—or perform **Endoscopic Variceal Ligation (EVL)** for primary prophylaxis of medium/large esophageal varices.
>
> **Management of Hepatic Encephalopathy (HE)**
>
> - **Identify and correct the underlying precipitant** (treat SBP/UTI, correct hypokalemia/alkalosis, stop sedatives/diuretics, control GI bleed) as the single most decisive step in resolving acute HE.
> - **Administer Lactulose (20–30 g [30–45 mL] PO/NG every 1–2 hours)** until the first bowel movement, then titrate to achieve **2–3 soft stools per day** (or administer **300 mL lactulose + 700 mL water as a retention enema q4–6h** in Grade III–IV coma) to acidify the colon and trap $\text{NH}_3$ as non-absorbable $\text{NH}_4^+$.
> - **Add Rifaximin (550 mg PO BID)** if HE fails to respond to lactulose within 24–48 hours, and continue **Lactulose + Rifaximin** indefinitely as secondary prophylaxis after a recurrent episode of overt HE.
>
> **Management of Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS-AKI)**
>
> - **Hold all diuretics and beta-blockers** and administer a **2-day volume challenge with 25% IV Albumin (1 g/kg/day, max 100 g/day)** upon diagnosing AKI in cirrhosis.
> - **Start Terlipressin (0.85–1 mg IV q4–6h)** (first-line vasopressor) **PLUS IV Albumin (20–40 g/day)** if HRS-AKI is confirmed (alternatively, use **Norepinephrine infusion** in the ICU, or oral **Midodrine [7.5–15 mg TID] + SC Octreotide [100–200 mcg TID]** if terlipressin is unavailable/contraindicated) to reverse splanchnic vasodilation and restore renal perfusion (target MAP increase ≥10–15 mmHg).
>
> **Disposition & Secondary Prophylaxis**
>
> - **Start lifelong secondary SBP prophylaxis with Ciprofloxacin (500 mg PO daily) or TMP-SMX (1 DS tablet PO daily)** prior to discharge in **all patients who survive an episode of SBP** (and consider primary prophylaxis in low-protein ascites <1.5 g/dL with advanced liver/renal dysfunction).
> - **Provide high-protein nutrition (1.2–1.5 g/kg/day) with a late-evening complex carbohydrate/protein snack** to reverse sarcopenia (muscle is the primary extrahepatic site for ammonia detoxification).

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid NSAIDs, Aminoglycosides, and ACEIs/ARBs** in all patients with cirrhosis and ascites, as NSAIDs inhibit renal vasodilatory prostaglandins and precipitate **acute kidney injury / HRS-AKI**.
> - **Avoid holding Non-Selective Beta-Blockers (NSBBs)** in uncomplicated ascites; HOWEVER, **HOLD NSBBs immediately** if the patient develops **SBP, HRS-AKI, hypotension (SBP <90 mmHg or MAP <65 mmHg), or serum Na <130 mEq/L** to prevent fatal hemodynamic collapse.
> - **Avoid Terlipressin** in patients with **ACL-Failure Grade 3 (≥3 organ failures), SpO2 <90% / hypoxia, active coronary/mesenteric ischemia, or serum Cr >5.0 mg/dL** (per CONFIRM trial) to prevent fatal **respiratory failure** and ischemic events.
> - **Avoid dietary protein restriction** in Hepatic Encephalopathy, as protein restriction worsens **sarcopenia** and skeletal muscle wasting, which reduces extrahepatic ammonia clearance and worsens HE.
> - **Avoid indwelling peritoneal drains or repeated therapeutic thoracentesis/chest tubes** for **hepatic hydrothorax** (pleural fluid in cirrhosis); chest tubes cause massive protein/electrolyte depletion, superinfection (empyema), and high mortality (treat with Na restriction, diuretics, or TIPS).
> - **Avoid free water restriction** unless serum sodium is **<120–125 mEq/L** with neurologic symptoms, as aggressive water restriction worsens effective hypovolemia and prerenal AKI.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Hepatic Hydrothorax:** Transdiaphragmatic movement of ascitic fluid through congenital diaphragmatic defects into the pleural space (85% **right-sided**) $\rightarrow$ treat with diuretics/TIPS; **never place a chest tube**.
> - **Hepatopulmonary Syndrome (HPS):** Intrapulmonary vascular dilations (shunting) $\rightarrow$ **platypnea** (dyspnea worsened by sitting up) and **orthodeoxia** (SpO2 drops ≥5% upright); diagnosed via **contrast-enhanced "bubble" echocardiogram** showing microbubbles in the left heart after **3–6 cardiac cycles** $\rightarrow$ **curable ONLY by liver transplantation** (MELD exception points granted if PaO2 <60 mmHg).
> - **Portopulmonary Hypertension (PoPH):** Pulmonary arterial vasoconstriction/remodeling (mean PAP >20 mmHg, PVR ≥3 Wood units) $\rightarrow$ treat with pulmonary vasodilators (prostacyclins, PDE-5 inhibitors); severe uncorrected PoPH (mPAP >45–50 mmHg) is an **absolute contraindication to liver transplant**.
> - **Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC):** ~1–4% annual incidence in cirrhosis $\rightarrow$ screen with **RUQ Ultrasound + serum AFP every 6 months**.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 58-year-old man with alcohol-associated cirrhosis and ascites is brought to the ED with 2 days of progressive confusion and lethargy. Vitals show T 37.8°C, HR 98 bpm, and BP 96/58 mmHg; exam reveals scleral icterus, tense ascites, and **asterixis** (Grade II HE). Labs show Na 129 mEq/L, Cr 1.6 mg/dL (baseline 0.9), Bilirubin 4.5 mg/dL, and INR 1.9; an immediate **diagnostic paracentesis** reveals SAAG 1.6 g/dL and **480 PMNs/mm³** (confirming **SBP** as the precipitant of his HE and AKI). Diuretics and beta-blockers are held, and he is treated immediately with **IV Ceftriaxone (2 g daily)**, **25% IV Albumin (1.5 g/kg Day 1, 1.0 g/kg Day 3)**, and oral **Lactulose** titrated to 3 soft stools daily.
