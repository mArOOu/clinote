> [!abstract] Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease
> Retrograde movement of gastric contents (acid, pepsin, bile) into the esophagus or beyond, causing troublesome symptoms (heartburn, regurgitation) and/or mucosal complications (erosive esophagitis, peptic stricture, Barrett's esophagus).

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Transient Lower Esophageal Sphincter Relaxations (TLESRs):** Most common mechanism (~90%); vagally mediated LES relaxations occurring _independently_ of swallowing, triggered by gastric distention after meals.
> - **Anatomic anti-reflux barrier disruption:** Resting LES hypotension (<10 mmHg) or **sliding hiatal hernia** (loss of crural diaphragm pinch).
> - **Barrett's metaplasia:** Chronic acid/bile injury induces transdifferentiation of normal distal **stratified squamous epithelium** into acid-resistant **columnar epithelium with goblet cells** (intestinal metaplasia $\rightarrow$ low-grade dysplasia $\rightarrow$ high-grade dysplasia $\rightarrow$ esophageal adenocarcinoma).

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **PPI timing is everything:** #1 cause of "PPI failure" is improper timing; PPIs **must be taken 30–60 minutes BEFORE a meal** (typically breakfast) so active drug is in the bloodstream when meal-stimulated parietal cell $\text{H}^+/\text{K}^+$ ATPase pumps are inserted into the membrane.
> - **Do not biopsy an inflamed esophagus for Barrett's:** If severe erosive esophagitis (**LA Grade C or D**) is seen on EGD, treat with high-dose PPI for **8–12 weeks** and **repeat EGD**; inflammation mimics dysplasia on histology and obscures underlying Barrett's.
> - **Extraesophageal GERD (cough, hoarseness, asthma) WITHOUT heartburn:** Do NOT give prolonged empiric PPI trials if typical reflux symptoms (heartburn/regurgitation) are absent; perform **objective reflux testing (pH-impedance monitoring) off PPI** first.
> - **Mandatory lifelong PPI indications (Never deprescribe):** **LA Grade C or D erosive esophagitis**, **Barrett's esophagus**, or **history of peptic esophageal stricture**.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Classic typical symptoms (high specificity):** **Heartburn (pyrosis)** (retrosternal burning rising toward the neck, worse postprandially or when supine/bending over) and **regurgitation** (effortless return of sour/bitter gastric fluid into the pharynx).
> - **Atypical / Extraesophageal symptoms:** Chronic cough, laryngitis/hoarseness, globus sensation, non-cardiac chest pain, adult-onset or refractory asthma, dental enamel erosion.
> - **Water brash:** Reflex vagal hypersecretion of salty saliva in response to esophageal acid.
> - **Exacerbating medications/foods:** Calcium channel blockers, nitrates, anticholinergics, theophylline, progesterone (relax LES); NSAIDs, bisphosphonates, potassium/iron (direct pill esophagitis); high-fat meals, alcohol, chocolate, peppermint, caffeine, late-night meals.
>
> **Physical**
>
> - Typically normal in uncomplicated GERD.
> - **Extraesophageal signs:** Posterior laryngeal erythema/edema, dental erosions on lingual/palatal tooth surfaces, wheezing.
> - **Systemic scleroderma signs:** Sclerodactyly, Raynaud phenomenon, telangiectasias (**CREST syndrome** causes severe smooth muscle atrophy of the distal esophagus and an incompetent LES).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
>
> - **Clinical Diagnosis (Uncomplicated Typical GERD):** Classic symptoms of heartburn and/or regurgitation WITHOUT alarm features that respond to an **8-week empiric trial of once-daily PPI**.
> - **Endoscopic Diagnosis (Lyon Consensus 2.0 — Conclusive Evidence of GERD):**
>   - **Los Angeles (LA) Grade B, C, or D Erosive Esophagitis**, **Biopsy-proven Barrett's Esophagus**, or **Peptic Esophageal Stricture** on EGD _(Note: LA Grade A esophagitis is inconclusive and can be seen in healthy controls)_.
> - **Ambulatory Reflux Monitoring (Wireless Bravo pH Capsule or 24h Catheter pH-Impedance off PPI):**
>   - **Acid Exposure Time (AET) >6%** of total monitoring time (pH <4) definitively confirms pathologic GERD (especially in **Non-Erosive Reflux Disease [NERD]**, which makes up ~60–70% of GERD).
> - **Barrett's Esophagus (ACG Criteria):** Endoscopic visualization of **≥1 cm of salmon-colored columnar mucosa** extending proximal to the gastroesophageal junction **PLUS** histologic confirmation of **specialized intestinal metaplasia (acid-mucin-containing goblet cells)**.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **CBC:** Order if alarm symptoms present to screen for iron-deficiency anemia from occult mucosal oozing or malignancy.
>
> **Imaging & Procedures**
>
> - **Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD):** **NOT required** for initial diagnosis of typical GERD without alarm symptoms. Indicated for:
>   1. **Any alarm symptom** (dysphagia, weight loss, GI bleeding, anemia, persistent vomiting).
>   2. **Failure to respond** to an 8-week trial of optimized PPI therapy (perform EGD **off PPI for 2–4 weeks** if possible to avoid masking erosive esophagitis or **Eosinophilic Esophagitis [EoE]**).
>   3. **Barrett's Esophagus screening** in patients with chronic GERD (>5 years) **PLUS ≥3 risk factors** (**Age >50**, **Male sex**, **White race**, **Central obesity**, **Tobacco smoking**, or **First-degree relative with Barrett's/esophageal adenocarcinoma**).
> - **Ambulatory pH / pH-Impedance Monitoring:** Gold standard when EGD is normal (**NERD**), for atypical extraesophageal symptoms, or **mandatory prior to anti-reflux surgery**.
> - **High-Resolution Esophageal Manometry:** **NOT used to diagnose GERD**; however, it is **MANDATORY prior to anti-reflux surgery (fundoplication)** to rule out **achalasia** or absent contractility (scleroderma), which would cause severe postoperative dysphagia.
> - **Barium Esophagram:** **NOT recommended** to diagnose GERD (extremely insensitive and non-specific); useful only for evaluating anatomic dysphagia/strictures or large paraesophageal hiatal hernias.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> **Alarm Features (Mandate Prompt EGD):**
>
> - **Dysphagia** (difficulty swallowing solids $\rightarrow$ rule out **peptic stricture**, **Schatzki ring**, **Eosinophilic Esophagitis**, or **adenocarcinoma**)
> - **Odynophagia** (painful swallowing $\rightarrow$ strongly suggests **infectious esophagitis** [Candida, CMV, HSV] or **pill esophagitis**, rarely uncomplicated GERD)
> - **Unintended weight loss** or anorexia
> - **GI bleeding** (hematemesis, melena) or **Iron-Deficiency Anemia**
> - **Persistent/recurrent vomiting**
> - **New-onset atypical symptoms at age ≥60 years**

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
>
> - **Acute Coronary Syndrome / Angina Pectoris:** Always rule out cardiac ischemia first in patients with retrosternal chest pain before attributing it to "non-cardiac chest pain" or GERD.
> - **Esophageal Adenocarcinoma / Squamous Cell Carcinoma:** Progressive solid-to-liquid dysphagia and weight loss.
>
> **Most Likely**
>
> - **Eosinophilic Esophagitis (EoE):** Young atopic males with solid-food **dysphagia / food impaction** and PPI-refractory heartburn; EGD shows stacked circular rings ("trachealization"), linear furrows, white exudates, and **≥15 eosinophils/HPF** on biopsy.
> - **Functional Heartburn / Reflux Hypersensitivity (Rome IV):** Typical heartburn with normal EGD, normal Acid Exposure Time (<4%) on pH testing, and failure to respond to PPI; treated with **low-dose TCAs/SSRIs**.
> - **Pill-Induced Esophagitis:** Acute retrosternal pain and **odynophagia** due to swallowing pills without water before lying down (**Doxycycline/tetracyclines**, **oral bisphosphonates**, **NSAIDs**, **potassium chloride**, **iron**); shows discrete punched-out mid-esophageal ulcers (at aortic arch/left mainstem bronchus compression).
> - **Infectious Esophagitis:** Immunocompromised (HIV CD4 <100–200, chemotherapy, inhaled steroids) with severe **odynophagia**:
>   - _Candida albicans:_ Adherent cottage-cheese white plaques.
>   - _HSV:_ Small, deep punched-out vesicles/ulcers ("volcano ulcers"); multinucleated giant cells/Cowdry type A bodies at ulcer **edge**.
>   - _CMV:_ Large, shallow linear ulcers; "owl's eye" basophilic intranuclear/intracytoplasmic inclusions at ulcer **base**.
>
> **Less Common**
>
> - **Achalasia:** Dysphagia to _both_ solids and liquids from onset + regurgitation of **undigested, non-acidic food** + nocturnal cough; often misdiagnosed as "refractory GERD" for years.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Complete resolution of troublesome heartburn/regurgitation and complete healing of erosive esophagitis.
> - **Secondary Target:** Prevent peptic stricture formation and arrest progression of **Barrett's esophagus** to **esophageal adenocarcinoma**.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **N/A** — Non-emergent outpatient condition (unless complicated by acute esophageal food impaction over a peptic stricture or upper GI hemorrhage; see **[[Upper GI Bleeding]]**).
>
> **First-Line Lifestyle & Pharmacotherapy (Typical Uncomplicated GERD)**
>
> - **Implement targeted lifestyle modifications** in all patients: **weight loss** (strongest evidence if BMI >25 or recent weight gain), **elevate head of bed by 6–8 inches** + **left lateral decubitus sleeping position** + **avoid meals within 2–3 hours of bedtime** (for nocturnal symptoms), smoking cessation, and trigger food avoidance.
> - **Initiate an 8-week empiric trial of a Standard-Dose PPI once daily** (e.g., **Omeprazole 20 mg, Pantoprazole 40 mg, or Esomeprazole 40 mg PO once daily**, taken strictly **30–60 minutes before breakfast**) as first-line medical therapy for typical GERD without alarm symptoms (Alternatively, **Vonoprazan 20 mg daily** [potassium-competitive acid blocker] provides rapid, meal-independent acid suppression for severe erosive esophagitis).
> - **Step down to the lowest effective dose, on-demand PPI, or H2-Receptor Antagonist (Famotidine 20 mg PO daily/BID)** after 8 weeks if symptoms resolve and the patient does NOT have LA Grade C/D esophagitis or Barrett's esophagus.
>
> **Stepwise Escalation for Refractory Symptoms**
>
> - **Verify adherence and optimize PPI timing (30–60 min pre-meal)**, then **escalate to Twice-Daily (BID) PPI** (before breakfast and before dinner) or switch to a CYP2C19-independent PPI (Rabeprazole, Esomeprazole) or **Vonoprazan** for 4–8 weeks if once-daily PPI provides incomplete relief.
> - **Add a bedtime H2-Receptor Antagonist (Famotidine 20–40 mg QHS)** for breakthrough nocturnal acid reflux (note: tachyphylaxis often develops within 2–4 weeks), **Sodium Alginate (Gaviscon)** postprandially to form a physical raft barrier over the gastric acid pocket, or **Baclofen (5–20 mg TID)** (a $\text{GABA}_\text{B}$ agonist that inhibits TLESRs) for refractory regurgitation/belching.
> - **Perform EGD (off PPI for 2–4 weeks if no prior erosive disease)** followed by **ambulatory pH-impedance monitoring** if symptoms fail to respond to 8 weeks of BID PPI therapy to distinguish true refractory GERD from functional heartburn.
>
> **Management of Complications (Severe Esophagitis, Strictures, Barrett's Esophagus)**
>
> - **Maintain lifelong continuous PPI therapy** in all patients with **LA Grade C or D erosive esophagitis** (and repeat EGD after 8–12 weeks of PPI to confirm healing and rule out underlying Barrett's esophagus).
> - **Perform endoscopic bougie or balloon dilation** combined with **lifelong continuous PPI therapy** for symptomatic **peptic esophageal strictures** presenting with solid-food dysphagia.
> - **Surveil and treat Barrett's Esophagus according to histologic dysplasia grade (all require lifelong daily PPI):**
>   - **Non-Dysplastic Barrett's:** Repeat surveillance EGD with 4-quadrant biopsies (every 2 cm = **Seattle protocol**) every **3–5 years**.
>   - **Low-Grade Dysplasia (confirmed by expert GI pathologist):** Perform **Endoscopic Eradication Therapy (Radiofrequency Ablation [RFA])** (preferred) OR strict endoscopic surveillance at 6 and 12 months, then annually.
>   - **High-Grade Dysplasia or Intramucosal Adenocarcinoma (T1a):** Perform **Endoscopic Mucosal Resection (EMR)** of any visible nodules/lesions followed by **Radiofrequency Ablation (RFA)** of all remaining Barrett's mucosa.
>
> **Surgical & Endoscopic Intervention**
>
> - **Refer for Laparoscopic Fundoplication** (Nissen 360° complete wrap or Toupet 270° partial wrap if impaired motility), **Magnetic Sphincter Augmentation (LINX)**, or **Roux-en-Y Gastric Bypass** (preferred in obese patients with BMI ≥35) ONLY in patients with **objectively documented GERD** (abnormal pH study/esophagitis + normal manometry) who wish to discontinue lifelong PPIs or have refractory mechanical regurgitation despite acid suppression.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid Anti-Reflux Surgery (Fundoplication) in patients whose symptoms are completely unresponsive to PPI therapy** unless objective pH-impedance testing proves ongoing pathologic reflux, as most "PPI non-responders" have functional heartburn or motility disorders and will worsen after surgery.
> - **Avoid performing Fundoplication without preoperative High-Resolution Esophageal Manometry** to prevent catastrophic postoperative dysphagia in patients with undiagnosed **achalasia** or **scleroderma esophagus**.
> - **Avoid Metoclopramide** as routine therapy for GERD (per ACG guidelines) due to lack of efficacy and high risk of irreversible **tardive dyskinesia**, extrapyramidal symptoms, and QT prolongation.
> - **Avoid stopping PPIs in patients with Barrett's Esophagus or LA Grade C/D Esophagitis** out of fear of observational PPI side effects (osteoporosis, dementia, CKD); randomized trials (**COMPASS trial**) disproved causal links for most adverse events except a modest increase in **enteric infections (_C. difficile_, _Campylobacter_, _Salmonella_)**.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Erosive Esophagitis (LA Classification Grades A–D):** Mucosal breaks in distal esophagus $\rightarrow$ can cause chronic occult blood loss and iron-deficiency anemia.
> - **Peptic Esophageal Stricture:** Concentric fibrotic narrowing at the squamocolumnar junction from chronic deep ulceration $\rightarrow$ presents with **progressive dysphagia to solids ONLY** (often accompanied by paradox _improvement_ in heartburn as the stricture blocks acid from rising) $\rightarrow$ treat with endoscopic dilation + lifelong PPI.
> - **Barrett's Esophagus:** Present in 5–15% of patients with chronic GERD; increases risk of **Esophageal Adenocarcinoma** by 30–40× (~0.2–0.5% annual progression risk in non-dysplastic Barrett's; ~7%/year in high-grade dysplasia).
> - **Schatzki Ring:** Thin, symmetric mucosal ring at the squamocolumnar junction associated with hiatal hernia and reflux $\rightarrow$ classic cause of **intermittent solid-food dysphagia ("steakhouse syndrome")**.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 56-year-old white man with obesity (BMI 33), a 20-pack-year smoking history, and 10 years of daily heartburn presents with new-onset difficulty swallowing solid meats over the past 2 months, though liquids pass easily. Because he has **dysphagia (an alarm feature)** and multiple risk factors for Barrett's esophagus, an **EGD** is performed immediately, revealing a benign distal **peptic stricture** and 3 cm of salmon-colored mucosa above the GEJ. Biopsies confirm **non-dysplastic Barrett's esophagus** (columnar epithelium with goblet cells); he undergoes **endoscopic balloon dilation** of the stricture, is started on **lifelong daily omeprazole** (taken 30 minutes before breakfast), and is scheduled for surveillance EGD in **3–5 years**.
