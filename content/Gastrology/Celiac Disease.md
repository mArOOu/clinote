> [!abstract] Celiac Disease
> Chronic, immune-mediated enteropathy of the proximal small intestine (duodenum and proximal jejunum) triggered by dietary **gluten** (**gliadin** fraction of wheat, barley, and rye) in genetically susceptible individuals carrying **HLA-DQ2 (90–95%)** or **HLA-DQ8 (5–10%)**.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Tissue Transglutaminase (tTG)** deamidates alcohol-soluble **gliadin** peptides in the lamina propria $\rightarrow$ presented via **HLA-DQ2/DQ8** on APCs to $\text{CD4}^+$ T cells $\rightarrow$ mucosal cytokine release ($\text{IFN-}\gamma, \text{TNF-}\alpha$) and intraepithelial $\text{CD8}^+$ T-cell cytotoxicity.
> - **Proximal small bowel damage:** Enterocyte destruction causes **villous atrophy**, **crypt hyperplasia**, and **intraepithelial lymphocytosis** $\rightarrow$ loss of brush-border enzymes (secondary lactase deficiency) and malabsorption of proximal nutrients (**Iron, Folate, Calcium, Vitamin D**).

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Never start a Gluten-Free Diet (GFD) BEFORE testing:** Serologies (tTG-IgA) and duodenal biopsies **normalize on a gluten-free diet**; patients must be eating gluten daily before serology and EGD to avoid false-negative results.
> - **Check Total IgA alongside tTG-IgA:** Selective **IgA deficiency** is 10–15× more common in Celiac Disease (~2–3%); if Total IgA is low, tTG-IgA will be **falsely negative** $\rightarrow$ order **IgG Deamidated Gliadin Peptide (DGP-IgG)** and **tTG-IgG**.
> - **#1 cause of "Non-Responsive Celiac Disease" is inadvertent gluten ingestion:** Hidden gluten in medications/supplements, soy sauce, malt, Communion wafers, or cross-contaminated oats accounts for >80% of persistent symptoms—not true Refractory Celiac Disease.
> - **Dermatitis Herpetiformis = Celiac Disease of the skin:** Biopsy-proven Dermatitis Herpetiformis (granular IgA deposits at dermal papillae) is **100% pathognomonic** for gluten sensitivity and **does NOT require a duodenal biopsy** to confirm Celiac Disease.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Classic GI symptoms (Malabsorptive):** Chronic or recurrent diarrhea, **steatorrhea** (bulky, pale, foul-smelling, greasy floating stools), postprandial bloating, flatulence, abdominal cramping, anorexia, and weight loss (or **failure to thrive / stunted growth** in children).
> - **Non-Classic / Extraintestinal presentations (More common in adults than classic diarrhea!):**
>   - _Hematologic:_ **Unexplained Iron-Deficiency Anemia** refractory to oral iron supplementation (most common adult extraintestinal manifestation; iron is absorbed in the duodenum).
>   - _Metabolic Bone:_ **Premature Osteopenia / Osteoporosis** or osteomalacia (from calcium and Vitamin D malabsorption + secondary hyperparathyroidism).
>   - _Dermatologic:_ **Dermatitis Herpetiformis** (intensely pruritic, symmetric papulovesicular rash on **extensor surfaces**: elbows, knees, buttocks, posterior scalp).
>   - _Neurologic / Psychiatric:_ Peripheral neuropathy, **"gluten ataxia"** (cerebellar ataxia), chronic migraines, depression, "brain fog."
>   - _Hepatic / Reproductive:_ Unexplained mild **transaminitis (AST/ALT elevation)**, recurrent aphthous stomatitis, dental enamel defects, delayed puberty, unexplained infertility, or recurrent miscarriage.
> - **High-risk associated conditions (Screen even if asymptomatic):** **Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus** (5–10% prevalence), **Autoimmune Thyroiditis** (Hashimoto/Graves), **Selective IgA Deficiency**, **Autoimmune Hepatitis / PBC**, **Down Syndrome**, **Turner Syndrome**, **Williams Syndrome**, and **first-degree relatives** of celiac patients (10% risk).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - Often completely normal in non-classic adult disease.
> - **Malnutrition signs:** Muscle wasting, pallor (anemia), angular cheilitis/atrophic glossitis (iron/B-vitamin deficiency), easy bruising (Vitamin K deficiency), peripheral edema (hypoalbuminemia), Chvostek/Trousseau signs (hypocalcemia).
> - **Skin exam:** Grouped excoriated vesicles/crusts over the **elbows, knees, and buttocks** (**Dermatitis Herpetiformis**—vesicles are often scratched off, leaving only erosions).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> **Adult Confirmation (ACG 2023 Guidelines)** — Requires **BOTH** of the following while the patient is consuming a **gluten-containing diet**:
>
> 1. **Positive Celiac-Specific Serology:** Elevated **IgA anti-Tissue Transglutaminase (tTG-IgA)** (or IgA Anti-Endomysial Antibody [EMA-IgA] / DGP-IgG if IgA-deficient).
> 2. **Characteristic Duodenal Histology on EGD Biopsy (Marsh Classification Stage 2 or 3):**
>    - **Increased Intraepithelial Lymphocytes (IELs >25 per 100 enterocytes)**,
>    - **Crypt Hyperplasia** (elongation), AND
>    - **Villous Atrophy** (partial to total blunting/flattening of duodenal villi = **Marsh 3**).
>
> - _Exceptions to Mandatory Duodenal Biopsy:_
>   1. **Skin Biopsy-Proven Dermatitis Herpetiformis** (Direct Immunofluorescence showing **granular IgA deposits** at the tips of dermal papillae).
>   2. **Pediatric No-Biopsy Pathway (ESPGHAN / ACG 2023):** Symptomatic children (and select adults unable/unwilling to undergo EGD) with **tTG-IgA ≥10× Upper Limit of Normal (ULN)** PLUS a **positive Anti-Endomysial Antibody (EMA-IgA)** on a **second, separate blood draw**.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **First-Line Diagnostic Screen (On Gluten-Containing Diet):**
>   - **IgA anti-Tissue Transglutaminase (tTG-IgA)** (95% sensitivity, 95% specificity) **PLUS Total Serum IgA** (to rule out false-negative from selective IgA deficiency).
>   - _If Total IgA is Low (<7 mg/dL):_ Order **IgG Deamidated Gliadin Peptides (DGP-IgG)** and **tTG-IgG**.
>   - _Note:_ **Anti-Gliadin Antibodies (AGA)** are obsolete and should **NEVER** be ordered due to poor sensitivity/specificity.
> - **HLA-DQ2 / HLA-DQ8 Genetic Testing (Rule-Out Test Only):**
>   - **>99% Negative Predictive Value (NPV)** (if _both_ HLA-DQ2 and DQ8 are negative, Celiac Disease is **permanently excluded**), but poor Positive Predictive Value (30–40% of the general white population carries HLA-DQ2/DQ8 without having Celiac Disease).
>   - **Use ONLY in 2 clinical scenarios:** (1) Patient already started a **strict Gluten-Free Diet prior to testing** and refuses a gluten challenge, OR (2) **Serology-histology discrepancy** (e.g., negative tTG-IgA with villous atrophy on biopsy).
>   - _If HLA-DQ2/DQ8 is positive in a patient already on a GFD:_ Perform a **Gluten Challenge** (**3–10 g of gluten/day [~1–2 slices of wheat bread]** for **2–8 weeks**) followed by tTG-IgA and EGD biopsy.
> - **Baseline Micronutrient & Complication Panel (At Diagnosis):** CBC, **Ferritin / Iron panel**, **Folate**, **Vitamin B12**, **25-OH Vitamin D**, Calcium, Alkaline Phosphatase, **AST/ALT**, Copper, Zinc.
>
> **Imaging & Procedures**
>
> - **Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD) with Multiple Duodenal Biopsies:**
>   - **Mandatory Biopsy Protocol:** Obtain **at least 1–2 biopsies from the duodenal bulb** (9 o'clock and 12 o'clock positions) **AND ≥4 biopsies from the distal (second/third) duodenum** because villous atrophy is notoriously **patchy**.
>   - _Endoscopic features:_ Scalloping of duodenal folds, loss/flattening of circular folds (plicae circulares), mucosal fissuring ("cracked-mud" appearance), and nodularity.
> - **DEXA Bone Density Scan:** Order at diagnosis in adults with malabsorption, prolonged delay in diagnosis, or high osteoporotic risk to screen for metabolic bone disease.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> **Signs of Refractory Celiac Disease (RCD) or Enteropathy-Associated T-Cell Lymphoma (EATL):**
>
> - **Relapse of severe diarrhea, malabsorption, and weight loss** after years of previous good control on a strict gluten-free diet
> - **Persistent symptoms and villous atrophy >12 months** despite verified 100% strict adherence to a gluten-free diet (negative tTG-IgA)
> - **Night sweats, unexplained fever, severe abdominal pain, GI bleeding, or bowel perforation/obstruction** (classic presentation of **Ulcerative Jejunitis** or **EATL**)

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss (Causes of Non-Celiac Villous Atrophy — Negative tTG-IgA)**
>
> - **Olmesartan-Induced Enteropathy:** Angiotensin receptor blocker (**Olmesartan**) causes severe chronic diarrhea, profound weight loss, and **celiac-like total villous atrophy** that is **completely unresponsive to a gluten-free diet** and has negative tTG-IgA; resolves rapidly upon stopping olmesartan.
> - **Enteropathy-Associated T-Cell Lymphoma (EATL):** High-grade intestinal lymphoma complicating untreated or Type II Refractory Celiac Disease.
>
> **Most Likely (Clinical & Histologic Mimics)**
>
> - **Non-Celiac Gluten Sensitivity (NCGS) / Fructan Intolerance (IBS):** Bloating, abdominal pain, and diarrhea after eating wheat, with **negative tTG-IgA and normal duodenal biopsy**; usually driven by fermentable **fructans (FODMAPs)** in wheat rather than gluten immunogenicity.
> - **Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (SIBO) & Secondary Lactase Deficiency:** Frequently **coexist** with Celiac Disease (due to impaired motility and lost brush-border lactase) and cause persistent diarrhea despite a gluten-free diet.
> - **Other Causes of Duodenal Villous Atrophy (Seronegative):**
>   - **Common Variable Immunodeficiency (CVID):** Recurrent sinopulmonary infections + Giardia + low IgG/IgA/IgM + **absence of plasma cells** in duodenal lamina propria.
>   - **Chronic Giardiasis:** Blunted villi with pear-shaped trophozoites on biopsy or positive stool _Giardia_ antigen.
>   - **Autoimmune Enteropathy:** Positive anti-enterocyte or anti-goblet cell antibodies.
>   - **Tropical Sprue:** Chronic malabsorption + megaloblastic anemia (folate/B12 deficiency) in travelers/residents of tropical regions (Caribbean, India, SE Asia); affects the **entire small intestine** and responds to **tetracycline + folic acid**.
>   - **Whipple Disease:** _Tropheryma whipplei_ infection in older men causing arthralgias, weight loss, steatorrhea, lymphadenopathy, and CNS/ocular signs (oculomasticatory myorhythmia); **PAS-positive foamy macrophages** in lamina propria.
>   - **Crohn's Disease of the Duodenum** or **Eosinophilic Gastroenteritis**.

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Complete clinical symptom resolution (typically within 2–4 weeks), **normalization of tTG-IgA serology** (within 6–12 months), and **complete mucosal villous healing** (within 1–2 years).
> - **Secondary Target:** Correct micronutrient deficiencies, restore bone mineral density, and prevent **Enteropathy-Associated T-Cell Lymphoma (EATL)** and small bowel adenocarcinoma.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **N/A** — Chronic outpatient disease _(Rare exception: **Celiac Crisis**—acute life-threatening profuse secretory diarrhea, severe hypokalemia, hypocalcemia, metabolic acidosis, and hypovolemic shock—requires ICU resuscitation, electrolyte repletion, and **IV Methylprednisolone / Oral Budesonide**)_.
>
> **First-Line Definitive Therapy**
>
> - **Initiate a Strict, Lifelong Gluten-Free Diet (GFD)** paired with **mandatory referral to an expert Registered Dietitian** upon confirming the diagnosis:
>   - **Strictly Eliminate:** **B**arley (including malt/beer), **R**ye, **O**ats (unless certified pure/uncontaminated gluten-free oats, introduced slowly), and **W**heat (including spelt, kamut, farro, semolina, triticale) (**Mnemonic: BROW**).
>   - **Safe Starches:** Rice, corn (maize), potatoes, quinoa, buckwheat, millet, amaranth, tapioca, soy.
> - **Replete all identified micronutrient deficiencies** (oral or IV **Iron**, **Folic Acid**, **Vitamin D**, **Calcium**, **Vitamin B12**, zinc, copper) and advise a temporarily **lactose-free diet for the first 4–8 weeks** until regenerating villous brush-border lactase recovers.
> - **Administer the Pneumococcal Vaccine (PCV20 or PCV15 $\rightarrow$ PPSV23)** to all patients with Celiac Disease (per ACG 2023 guidelines), as ~30% have **functional hyposplenism** (splenic atrophy) and increased risk of overwhelming pneumococcal sepsis.
>
> **Management of Dermatitis Herpetiformis**
>
> - **Prescribe Oral Dapsone (50–100 mg PO daily)** alongside a **lifelong Gluten-Free Diet** for **Dermatitis Herpetiformis** to provide rapid relief of intense pruritus and skin lesions within **48–72 hours** (while waiting 6–12 months for the GFD alone to control the rash; **screen for G6PD deficiency before starting Dapsone** and monitor for methemoglobinemia/hemolysis).
>
> **Stepwise Evaluation of Non-Responsive & Refractory Celiac Disease (RCD)**
>
> - **Step 1 (Verify Diagnosis & Check tTG-IgA):** If symptoms persist >6–12 months, repeat **tTG-IgA** and perform a **meticulous dietitian audit**; an **elevated tTG-IgA confirms ongoing inadvertent gluten exposure** (#1 cause, >80%).
> - **Step 2 (Exclude Overlapping Mimics if tTG-IgA is Normal):** Test for **Microscopic Colitis** (random colonic biopsies), **Exocrine Pancreatic Insufficiency** (fecal elastase), **SIBO** (breath test or empiric rifaximin), **Lactose/Fructose intolerance**, or **IBS**.
> - **Step 3 (Repeat EGD with Duodenal Biopsy & T-Cell Flow Cytometry / TCR Gene Rearrangement):** If severe villous atrophy persists for **>12 months** despite a verified strict GFD and negative tTG-IgA, classify into **Refractory Celiac Disease (RCD)**:
>   - **RCD Type I (Normal polyclonal $\text{CD3}^+\text{CD8}^+$ intraepithelial lymphocytes):** Benign prognosis (~90% 5-year survival); **treat with Open-Capsule Oral Budesonide (9 mg/day)** ± **Azathioprine**.
>   - **RCD Type II (Aberrant clonal intraepithelial T-cells lacking surface CD3/CD8 + monoclonal TCR rearrangement):** Pre-malignant "cryptic intraepithelial lymphoma" (~50% progress to **EATL** with <50% 5-year survival); **perform PET-CT and capsule endoscopy/enteroscopy** to rule out overt EATL, and refer to a specialized center for **Budesonide, Cladribine, or Autologous Stem Cell Transplantation**.
>
> **Follow-up & Monitoring**
>
> - **Recheck tTG-IgA (or DGP) and nutritional labs at 3–6 months, 12 months, and annually thereafter** to monitor dietary adherence (tTG-IgA titer should steadily fall and normalize by 12–24 months; persistent elevation indicates ongoing gluten exposure).
> - **Consider a follow-up EGD at 2 years** in adults to confirm complete **mucosal healing** (especially in older adults or those with severe initial presentation, as persistent villous atrophy despite normal serology carries increased lymphoma/osteoporotic fracture risk).

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid initiating a Gluten-Free Diet BEFORE completing serologic AND endoscopic biopsy confirmation**, as even 2–4 weeks of gluten restriction can normalize duodenal histology and tTG-IgA, trapping the patient in diagnostic limbo requiring a painful prolonged gluten challenge.
> - **Avoid relying on Symptom Improvement after starting a Gluten-Free Diet to "diagnose" Celiac Disease**, as the vast majority of patients who feel better on a GFD have **Irritable Bowel Syndrome (fructan/FODMAP reduction)** rather than Celiac Disease (positive predictive value <10%).
> - **Avoid ordering Anti-Gliadin Antibodies (AGA-IgA/IgG)** for Celiac screening due to high false-positive rates (always order **tTG-IgA + Total IgA**).
> - **Avoid ordering HLA-DQ2/DQ8 as a routine initial screening test**, as 30–40% of healthy individuals test positive, leading to unnecessary anxiety and endoscopies.
> - **Avoid prescribing Dapsone without first checking a G6PD level** to prevent severe acute **hemolytic anemia** and **methemoglobinemia** (Note: Dapsone clears the rash of Dermatitis Herpetiformis but **does NOT protect the small intestine** or prevent lymphoma—the patient **must still follow a strict GFD**).

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Enteropathy-Associated T-Cell Lymphoma (EATL) & Small Bowel Adenocarcinoma:** Rare, aggressive malignancies of the jejunum/ileum caused by chronic uncontrolled mucosal inflammation $\rightarrow$ **strict adherence to a lifelong Gluten-Free Diet reduces lymphoma risk back to baseline population levels**.
> - **Ulcerative Jejunitis:** Multiple chronic benign-appearing ulcers and strictures in the jejunum associated with RCD Type II $\rightarrow$ presents with abdominal pain, fever, bleeding, or perforation.
> - **Functional Hyposplenism (Splenic Atrophy):** Seen on peripheral blood smear as **Howell-Jolly bodies**, target cells, and thrombocytosis $\rightarrow$ predisposes to fatal sepsis from encapsulated bacteria (_Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae, Neisseria meningitidis_) $\rightarrow$ vaccinate against pneumococcus!
> - **Osteoporosis & Pathologic Fractures:** Driven by malabsorption of Calcium/Vitamin D and chronic inflammatory cytokines $\rightarrow$ screen with DEXA and supplement Calcium (1,000–1,200 mg/day) + Vitamin D.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 34-year-old woman with Hashimoto thyroiditis is referred for persistent microcytic anemia (Hb 9.6 g/dL, MCV 71 fL, ferritin 5 ng/mL) that has failed 6 months of oral ferrous sulfate, accompanied by mild postprandial bloating and an intensely itchy vesicular rash on both elbows. While she continues her normal gluten-containing diet, labs reveal a markedly elevated **tTG-IgA (>100 U/mL)** with normal Total IgA, and an **EGD with 6 duodenal biopsies** (bulb and distal duodenum) demonstrates **intraepithelial lymphocytosis, crypt hyperplasia, and total villous atrophy (Marsh 3c)**. She is counselled by a dietitian on a **strict lifelong Gluten-Free Diet**, receives **oral Dapsone** for rapid relief of her **Dermatitis Herpetiformis** (after normal G6PD testing), receives **IV iron** and the **pneumococcal vaccine**, and has a DEXA scan ordered.
