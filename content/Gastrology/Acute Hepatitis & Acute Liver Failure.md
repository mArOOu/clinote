> [!abstract] Acute Hepatitis & Acute Liver Failure
> **Acute Hepatitis / Acute Liver Injury (ALI):** Abrupt hepatocellular necrosis (<26 weeks) marked by steep transaminase elevations, +/- coagulopathy (INR ≥1.5) _without_ encephalopathy.
> **Acute Liver Failure (ALF):** Progression of acute liver injury to synthetic failure (**INR ≥1.5**) AND **hepatic encephalopathy** in a patient _without_ preexisting cirrhosis.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
>
> - **Hepatocyte necrosis/apoptosis:** Triggered by direct toxic metabolites (**NAPQI** in acetaminophen overdose after glutathione depletion) or immune-mediated cytotoxic T-cell lysis (viral hepatitis, AIH).
> - **Cerebral edema (hallmark cause of death in ALF):** Failure of hepatic urea cycle $\rightarrow$ severe **hyperammonemia (>150–200 µmol/L)** $\rightarrow$ ammonia crosses BBB and is converted to **glutamine** inside astrocytes $\rightarrow$ osmotic astrocyte swelling, intracranial hypertension, and brainstem herniation.
> - **Hemodynamic collapse:** Massive cytokine release and systemic nitric oxide production $\rightarrow$ high-output distributive shock (low SVR) + multiorgan failure (AKI).

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
>
> - **Give NAC even in non-acetaminophen ALF:** IV **N-acetylcysteine (NAC)** is mandatory for _all_ acetaminophen toxicity AND improves transplant-free survival in **non-acetaminophen early-stage ALF** (Grade I–II encephalopathy).
> - **ALT >1,000 U/L differential (Only 4 main causes):** **I**schemic hepatitis ("shock liver"), **D**rug/toxin (**A**cetaminophen, Amanita), **V**iral hepatitis (A–E, HSV), or **A**utoimmune hepatitis (plus acute Budd-Chiari or Wilson crisis).
> - **Falling AST/ALT can be ominous:** Rapidly declining transaminases alongside a **rising INR and bilirubin** indicates total hepatocyte exhaustion ("burnout"), NOT recovery.
> - **Wilsonian ALF clues:** Coombs-negative hemolytic anemia + **AST:ALT >2.2** + **Alkaline Phosphatase : Total Bilirubin ratio <4** (low ALP).

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
>
> - **Nonspecific viral/toxic prodrome:** Fatigue, anorexia, nausea, vomiting, low-grade fever, RUQ discomfort.
> - **Cholestatic phase:** Dark urine (conjugated bilirubinuria), pale acholic stools, jaundice, pruritus.
> - **Risk factor screen:** All OTC pain/cold meds (**acetaminophen**), herbal supplements (_Kava, Kratom, green tea extract_), wild mushrooms (_Amanita phalloides_), IV drug use (HBV/HDV/HCV), travel/fecal-oral exposure (HAV/HEV; **HEV is fulminant in pregnancy**), sexual history, hypotension/heart failure (ischemic).
>
> **Physical**
>
> - **Jaundice / scleral icterus** and tender hepatomegaly (conversely, a **shrinking liver span** on percussion indicates massive hepatic necrosis).
> - **Hepatic Encephalopathy (West Haven Grades I–IV):**
>   - _Grade I:_ Subtle inattention, euphoria/anxiety, **reversed sleep-wake cycle** (earliest sign).
>   - _Grade II:_ Lethargy, disorientation to time, **asterixis** (flapping tremor).
>   - _Grade III:_ Somnolence (arousable), marked confusion, clonus/hyperreflexia.
>   - _Grade IV:_ Coma, decerebrate/decorticate posturing, **loss of pupillary reflexes** (impending cerebral herniation).
> - **Absence of chronic liver disease stigmata:** No spider angiomata, caput medusae, or splenomegaly (except in Budd-Chiari).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> **Acute Liver Failure (ALF)** — Requires **ALL 4** mandatory criteria:
>
> - **Hepatic Encephalopathy** of any degree (West Haven Grade I–IV).
> - **Coagulopathy with INR ≥1.5** (not secondary to anticoagulants).
> - **Acute illness duration <26 weeks** (Hyperacute <7 days; Acute 7–21 days; Subacute >21 days to <26 weeks).
> - **Absence of preexisting cirrhosis** (Note: _De novo_ presentation of acute Wilson disease, vertically acquired HBV, or Autoimmune Hepatitis **ARE** classified as ALF even if underlying fibrosis is present).

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>
> - **Initial Etiology Panel:** **Serum Acetaminophen level** (mandatory in ALL patients), viral serologies (**Anti-HAV IgM, HBsAg, Anti-HBc IgM**, Anti-HCV + HCV RNA, Anti-HEV IgM; HSV/VZV PCR if immunosuppressed/pregnant), **Autoimmune markers** (ANA, ASMA, total IgG), **Ceruloplasmin** (low in Wilson, though can be falsely normal as an acute-phase reactant), urine toxicology, pregnancy test.
> - **Severity / Monitoring (q4–6h in ALF):** PT/INR, **arterial ammonia** (**>150–200 µmol/L** strongly predicts cerebral herniation), **fingerstick blood glucose q1–2h** (profound hypoglycemia from lost gluconeogenesis/glycogen), arterial lactate, ABG, CBC, CMP, phosphate, fibrinogen.
> - **Interpretation (King's College Criteria for Urgent Transplant Listing):**
>   - _Acetaminophen ALF:_ **Arterial pH <7.30** after fluid resuscitation **OR** all 3 of: **INR >6.5** + **Creatinine >3.4 mg/dL** + **Grade III/IV encephalopathy**.
>   - _Non-Acetaminophen ALF:_ **INR >6.5** **OR** ≥3 of: Age <10 or >40, unfavorable etiology (DILI, indeterminate), jaundice-to-encephalopathy >7 days, **INR >3.5**, **Bilirubin >17.5 mg/dL**.
>
> **Imaging**
>
> - **RUQ Ultrasound with Doppler:** Mandatory to evaluate hepatic/portal vein patency (**rule out Budd-Chiari syndrome**), biliary tree (rule out obstruction), and liver surface nodularity (rule out underlying cirrhosis).
> - **Non-Contrast Head CT:** Indicated in Grade III/IV encephalopathy or sudden neurologic decline to exclude intracranial hemorrhage (insensitive for early cerebral edema).
>
> **Referrals**
>
> - **Liver Transplant Center:** **Immediate transfer/referral at the FIRST sign of ALF (any encephalopathy + INR ≥1.5)** before the patient becomes too unstable to transport.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
>
> - **Any altered mental status, sleep-wake reversal, or asterixis** in a patient with acute hepatitis (signals transition from ALI to **ALF**).
> - **INR ≥1.5** (or rapidly rising PT/INR) with **hypoglycemia**.
> - **Arterial ammonia >150 µmol/L** or **arterial pH <7.30**.
> - **Cushing triad** (hypertension, bradycardia, irregular respirations), pupillary sluggishness, or decerebrate posturing (indicates impending **cerebral herniation**).
> - **Acute Kidney Injury (Cr >2.0 mg/dL)** or oliguria.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss (Etiologies of ALF)**
>
> - **Acetaminophen (APAP) Toxicity:** #1 cause of ALF in Western countries (~50%); dose-dependent (>7.5–10 g single ingestion or >4 g/day staggered therapeutic misadventure in alcoholics/malnourished); AST/ALT often **>3,000–10,000 U/L** with relatively low bilirubin initially.
> - **Ischemic Hepatitis ("Shock Liver"):** Profound hypotension, sepsis, or acute heart failure; AST/ALT >1,000–5,000 U/L with early massive **LDH surge (ALT:LDH ratio <1.5)**; improves rapidly upon hemodynamic restoration.
> - **Acute Viral Hepatitis (HAV, HBV ± HDV, HEV, HSV):** AST/ALT 1,000–3,000 U/L; **HSV hepatitis** presents with fever, leukopenia, and _anicteric_ fulminant hepatitis (frequently lacks mucocutaneous lesions).
> - **Acute Wilson Disease Crisis:** Young patient (<40 y), Coombs-negative hemolytic anemia, rapidly progressive renal failure, **AST:ALT >2.2**, and **ALP:Total Bilirubin ratio <4**.
>
> **Most Likely (Non-ALF Acute Hepatitis)**
>
> - **Uncomplicated Acute Viral Hepatitis (A, B, C):** Self-limiting in >95% of HAV and adult-acquired HBV; HCV rarely causes ALF but becomes chronic in ~75%.
> - **Idiosyncratic Drug-Induced Liver Injury (DILI):** Amoxicillin-clavulanate, isoniazid, phenytoin, nitrofurantoin, TMP-SMX, statins, herbal supplements.
> - **Alcoholic Hepatitis:** AST:ALT ratio **≥2:1** with both typically **<300–400 U/L** (if >500 U/L, rule out concurrent acetaminophen toxicity or ischemia).
>
> **Most Surprising**
>
> - **Budd-Chiari Syndrome:** Acute hepatic vein thrombosis (myeloproliferative neoplasms, pregnancy, OCPs) causing sudden RUQ pain, tender hepatomegaly, ascites, and ALF.
> - **Pregnancy-Related Liver Emergencies (3rd Trimester):** **HELLP syndrome** or **Acute Fatty Liver of Pregnancy (AFLP)** (presents with hypoglycemia, hyperammonemia, DIC, and ALF).

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
>
> - **Primary Target:** Maintain **MAP ≥65–75 mmHg** (to ensure cerebral perfusion pressure), **Euglycemia (BG 110–180 mg/dL)**, **Serum Na 145–155 mEq/L** (in high-grade encephalopathy), **Arterial Ammonia <100 µmol/L**.
> - **Secondary Target:** Prevent cerebral herniation, reverse etiology-specific hepatotoxicity, and bridge to spontaneous hepatic regeneration or **emergent orthotopic liver transplantation (OLT)**.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
>
> - **Administer IV N-Acetylcysteine (NAC)** immediately (20-hour protocol: 150 mg/kg over 1h $\rightarrow$ 50 mg/kg over 4h $\rightarrow$ 100 mg/kg over 16h; continue infusion until INR <1.5) in **ALL suspected acetaminophen overdoses** AND in **all non-acetaminophen ALF with Grade I–II encephalopathy** to replenish glutathione and improve microcirculatory oxygen delivery.
> - **Give Activated Charcoal (1 g/kg PO)** ONLY if the patient presents **within 4 hours** of a known acute acetaminophen ingestion and is fully awake/protecting their airway.
> - **Infuse continuous 10% Dextrose (D10W)** and monitor blood glucose q1–2h upon diagnosing ALF to prevent fatal neuroglycopenia.
> - **Intubate for airway protection** (using propofol for sedation + fentanyl) when the patient progresses to **Grade III or IV hepatic encephalopathy** to prevent aspiration and control PaCO2.
> - **Resuscitate with isotonic crystalloids and Norepinephrine** (first-line vasopressor; add Vasopressin if needed) if hypotensive to maintain **MAP ≥65–75 mmHg** and preserve cerebral perfusion.
>
> **Etiology-Specific Targeted Therapy**
>
> - **Start Entecavir or Tenofovir PO daily** immediately in **ALF due to Acute Hepatitis B** (Note: uncomplicated acute HBV without ALF requires supportive care only).
> - **Administer Acyclovir (10 mg/kg IV q8h)** immediately if **HSV or VZV hepatitis** is suspected (immunocompromised, pregnant, or fever + anicteric hepatitis) without waiting for PCR confirmation.
> - **Administer IV Methylprednisolone (40–60 mg/day)** ONLY for **severe Autoimmune Hepatitis (AIH)** without advanced encephalopathy (discuss with transplant center first, as steroids increase sepsis risk).
> - **Initiate Urgent Plasma Exchange (Plasmapheresis)** as a life-saving bridge to transplantation in **acute Wilson disease crisis** to rapidly remove circulating free copper (D-penicillamine is ineffective acutely).
> - **Deliver the fetus emergently** after maternal stabilization if ALF is caused by **Acute Fatty Liver of Pregnancy (AFLP) or HELLP syndrome**.
>
> **Neurocritical Care & Escalation (Cerebral Edema / Organ Failure)**
>
> - **Elevate head of bed to 30°, minimize patient stimulation, and administer prophylactic Hypertonic Saline (3% NaCl)** in patients with Grade III/IV encephalopathy or ammonia >150 µmol/L to target **serum sodium 145–155 mEq/L** and prevent cerebral edema.
> - **Administer a bolus of Hypertonic Saline or IV Mannitol (0.5–1.0 g/kg)** (with transient hyperventilation to PaCO2 30–35 mmHg only as a rescue bridge) upon signs of **acute intracranial hypertension or impending herniation**.
> - **Initiate Continuous Renal Replacement Therapy (CRRT)** early (rather than intermittent hemodialysis) for acute kidney injury, metabolic acidosis, or **severe hyperammonemia (>150–200 µmol/L)** to clear ammonia without causing rapid osmotic/hemodynamic shifts that worsen cerebral edema.
> - **Start empirical broad-spectrum IV antibiotics and antifungals** if the patient develops SIRS, unexplained progression to Grade III/IV encephalopathy, or refractory hypotension, as infection frequently lacks fever/leukocytosis in ALF.
>
> **Disposition & Follow-up**
>
> - **Manage uncomplicated Acute Viral Hepatitis (normal INR, normal mentation) outpatient** with supportive hydration, strict alcohol/hepatotoxin avoidance, and serial LFT/INR checks until resolution.
> - **Admit all ALF patients to an ICU at a Liver Transplant Center** and evaluate urgently using **King's College Criteria** for Status 1A emergent liver transplantation.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
>
> - **Avoid routine FFP or coagulation factor administration** solely to "correct" an elevated INR in the absence of active bleeding or an invasive procedure, as it obscures the **INR trend** (the single most important prognostic marker of liver recovery vs. need for transplant) and causes volume overload.
> - **Avoid Lactulose or Rifaximin in ALF** (unlike in cirrhosis), as lactulose causes gaseous bowel distention (which complicates transplant surgery) and intravascular volume depletion without proven mortality benefit in ALF.
> - **Avoid Intermittent Hemodialysis (IHD) and hypotonic fluids (e.g., Lactated Ringer's or 0.45% NaCl)** in ALF to prevent abrupt drops in serum osmolality and MAP, which directly precipitate fatal cerebral edema and herniation (always use **CRRT**).
> - **Avoid Benzodiazepines and Opioids** in patients with acute liver injury or Grade I–II encephalopathy to prevent precipitating coma and masking neurological deterioration.
> - **Avoid Nephrotoxic drugs (NSAIDs, aminoglycosides) and IV contrast** to prevent precipitating hepatorenal/acute kidney injury.

> [!error]- Complications
>
> - **Cerebral Edema & Brainstem Herniation:** Most classic fatal complication (especially in hyperacute/acetaminophen ALF with ammonia >200 µmol/L) $\rightarrow$ treat with 3% hypertonic saline, mannitol, and CRRT.
> - **Sepsis / Fungal Infection:** Impaired hepatic opsonin/complement synthesis + Kupffer cell dysfunction $\rightarrow$ occurs in >50% of ALF patients and may disqualify patient from transplant $\rightarrow$ low threshold for pan-culturing and empiric piperacillin-tazobactam/meropenem + echinocandin.
> - **Profound Hypoglycemia:** Depleted hepatic glycogen + impaired gluconeogenesis $\rightarrow$ continuous D10W infusion.
> - **Spontaneous Bleeding (uncommon, <10%):** Despite high INR, simultaneous loss of procoagulants AND anticoagulants (Protein C/S, antithrombin) creates "rebalanced hemostasis"; bleeding usually results from **thrombocytopenia (<50,000/µL) or hypofibrinogenemia (<100 mg/dL)** (often gastrointestinal mucosal oozing $\rightarrow$ give prophylactic **IV PPI** to all ICU ALF patients).

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 24-year-old woman presents with nausea, RUQ pain, and confusion 36 hours after ingesting a bottle of extra-strength acetaminophen. Vitals show HR 112 bpm and BP 102/64 mmHg; exam reveals scleral icterus and **asterixis** (Grade II hepatic encephalopathy). Labs show AST 6,400 U/L, ALT 7,200 U/L, total bilirubin 3.8 mg/dL, **INR 3.2**, glucose 52 mg/dL, creatinine 1.8 mg/dL, and arterial ammonia 140 µmol/L. **IV N-acetylcysteine (NAC)** and a **D10W infusion** are started immediately, **FFP is withheld** (no active bleeding, preserving INR as a prognostic marker), and she is emergently transferred to a **Liver Transplant Center ICU**.
