> [!abstract] Cardiac Arrest & Sudden Cardiac Death
> **Cardiac Arrest** is the abrupt cessation of mechanical cardiac pump function resulting in absent systemic perfusion. **Sudden Cardiac Death (SCD)** is unexpected, non-traumatic death from a cardiac cause within 1 hour of symptom onset (or within 24 hours of last being seen alive and symptom-free if unwitnessed).

> [!note] Pathophysiology
> - Primary electrical (VF/pVT) or mechanical (PEA/asystole) collapse causes abrupt cessation of cardiac output, producing cerebral ischemia within 10–15 seconds and irreversible neuronal death within 4–6 minutes.
> - Most common underlying substrate in adults $>35$ years is coronary artery disease (plaque rupture/ischemia); in $<35$ years, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, channelopathies, and congenital anomalies predominate.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
> - Agonal gasps are a sign of cardiac arrest, not breathing—do not delay CPR to check airway.
> - Chest compression fraction must exceed 80%; pauses $>10$ seconds precipitously drop coronary perfusion pressure.
> - Epinephrine is given **immediately** in non-shockable rhythms (PEA/Asystole), but **after the 2nd shock** in shockable rhythms (VF/pVT).
> - Do not perform neuroprognostication before 72 hours post-ROSC (and after rewarming/sedation clearance).

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
> - Sudden, unheralded collapse and loss of consciousness.
> - Optional brief prodrome (chest pain, dyspnea, palpitations, presyncope) minutes before collapse.
> - History of structural heart disease, reduced LVEF ($<35\%$), prior MI, or family history of sudden death.
> 
> **Physical**
> - Unresponsive to verbal and painful stimuli.
> - Absent central pulse (carotid or femoral) checked for $\le 10$ seconds.
> - Absent normal breathing (apnea or agonal gasps).
> - Pallor, peripheral cyanosis, and fixed/dilated pupils (late sign) (30-120s after complete circulation arrest).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> - **Clinical Cardiac Arrest (Mandatory Triad):**
>   - Unresponsiveness + Apnea/Agonal breathing + Absent central pulse (confirmed in $\le 10$ seconds).
> - **Rhythm Classification (via ECG monitor / Defibrillator):**
>   - *Shockable:* Ventricular Fibrillation (VF) or Pulseless Ventricular Tachycardia (pVT).
>   - *Non-Shockable:* Pulseless Electrical Activity (PEA) or Asystole.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
> - *Arterial/Venous Blood Gas + Lactate:* Intra-/post-ROSC to quantify hypoxemia, hypercapnia, and metabolic acidosis.
> - *Electrolytes ($\text{K}^+$, $\text{Mg}^{2+}$, $\text{Ca}^{2+}$), Glucose:* Identify rapidly correctable metabolic triggers (e.g., hyperkalemia).
> - *High-sensitivity Troponin:* Post-ROSC to evaluate acute coronary syndrome.
> - *Toxicology Screen:* If drug overdose or ingestion is suspected.
> 
> **Imaging**
> - *Point-of-Care Ultrasound (POCUS):* Rapidly identify reversible mechanical etiologies during planned compression pauses (tamponade, RV strain/massive PE, pneumothorax, severe hypovolemia).
> - *12-Lead ECG:* Mandatory immediately post-ROSC to assess for STEMI/ST-elevation.
> - *Chest X-Ray / CT Brain/Chest:* Post-ROSC to confirm tube/line placement and exclude intracranial hemorrhage, aortic dissection, or massive PE.
> 
> **Referrals**
> - *Interventional Cardiology:* Immediate activation for emergent cardiac catheterization (STEMI or high-suspicion ACS).
> - *Intensive Care Unit (ICU):* For targeted temperature management and post-resuscitation care.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> - **Reversible Causes (5 H's and 5 T's):** Hypovolemia, Hypoxia, Hydrogen ion (acidosis), Hypo/Hyperkalemia, Hypothermia; Tension pneumothorax, Tamponade (cardiac), Toxins, Thrombosis (pulmonary), Thrombosis (coronary).
> - **$\text{ETCO}_2 < 10\text{ mmHg}$ during CPR:** Indicates inadequate chest compressions or low likelihood of ROSC; abrupt rise to $>35–40\text{ mmHg}$ indicates ROSC.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
> - Massive Pulmonary Embolism
> - Tension Pneumothorax
> - Cardiac Tamponade
> - Aortic Dissection / Ruptured AAA
> 
> **Most Likely**
> - Acute Myocardial Infarction / Ischemia
> - Severe Electrolyte Derangement (Hyperkalemia)
> 
> **Most Surprising**
> - Channelopathy (Brugada syndrome, Long QT syndrome, CPVT)
> 
> **Less Common**
> - Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM), Arrhythmogenic RV Cardiomyopathy (ARVC)
> 
> **Non-Critical**
> - Vasovagal Syncope (brief LOC, prompt full recovery, palpable pulse)
> - Coma / Status Epilepticus with preserved central pulse

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
> - **Primary Target:** Rapid Return of Spontaneous Circulation (ROSC) and restoration of perfusing rhythm.
> - **Post-ROSC Hemodynamic Targets:** $\text{MAP} \ge 65\text{ mmHg}$ ($\text{SBP} \ge 90\text{ mmHg}$), $\text{SpO}_2\text{ 92–98\%}$, normocapnia ($\text{PaCO}_2\text{ 35–45 mmHg}$), normoglycemia ($140–180\text{ mg/dL}$), and strict normothermia ($32–36^\circ\text{C}$ or fever avoidance $<37.5^\circ\text{C}$).
> 
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
> - **Initiate high-quality CPR** immediately upon confirmed pulse absence (rate 100–120/min, depth 5–6 cm, complete chest recoil, minimize pauses $<10\text{s}$, 30:2 ratio or continuous compressions with advanced airway) to generate critical coronary and cerebral perfusion pressure.
> - **Attach manual defibrillator/monitor** STAT upon arrival to identify rhythm category (Shockable vs. Non-Shockable).
> 
> **Shockable Algorithm (VF / Pulseless VT)**
> - **Deliver unsynchronized shock (120–200 J biphasic or 360 J monophasic)** immediately upon recognizing VF/pVT to depolarize the myocardium and terminate the arrhythmia, followed by immediate CPR resumption for 2 minutes before rhythm recheck.
> - **Administer Epinephrine 1 mg IV/IO** after the 2nd unsuccessful shock (repeat every 3–5 minutes) to augment aortic diastolic pressure.
> - **Administer Amiodarone 300 mg IV/IO bolus** (alternative: Lidocaine 1–1.5 mg/kg IV/IO) after the 3rd shock (2nd dose: Amiodarone 150 mg or Lidocaine 0.5–0.75 mg/kg) to treat refractory VF/pVT.
> 
> **Non-Shockable Algorithm (PEA / Asystole)**
> - **Administer Epinephrine 1 mg IV/IO** STAT upon identifying PEA/Asystole (repeat every 3–5 minutes) to optimize coronary perfusion pressure.
> - **Identify and treat underlying 5 H's and 5 T's** immediately throughout CPR cycles to correct reversible etiology.
> 
> **Post-Cardiac Arrest Care (Post-ROSC)**
> - **Initiate Targeted Temperature Management (TTM)** immediately in comatose post-ROSC patients (maintain constant $32–36^\circ\text{C}$ for $\ge 24\text{ hours}$, or actively prevent fever $<37.8^\circ\text{C}$ for 72 hours) to attenuate reperfusion brain injury.
> - **Activate emergent coronary angiography** STAT for all post-ROSC patients with STEMI on ECG or high suspicion of cardiac ischemia to achieve immediate mechanical reperfusion.
> - **Titrate IV Norepinephrine infusion (0.05–0.5 mcg/kg/min)** post-ROSC when hypotensive to maintain $\text{MAP} \ge 65\text{ mmHg}$.
> 
> **Disposition & ICU Management**
> - **Admit to Cardiac/Medical ICU** following ROSC for continuous hemodynamic monitoring, neuro-monitoring (continuous EEG for status epilepticus), and formal neuroprognostication after $\ge 72$ hours.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
> - **Avoid pausing chest compressions $>10$ seconds** during pulse checks, rhythm analysis, or intubation to prevent complete loss of coronary perfusion pressure.
> - **Avoid hyperventilation ($>10\text{ breaths/min}$ or excessive volume)** during CPR and post-ROSC to prevent increased intrathoracic pressure (reduced venous return) and cerebral vasoconstriction.
> - **Avoid routine rapid pre-hospital IV cold-fluid cooling** post-ROSC to prevent fluid overload, pulmonary edema, and rearrest.
> - **Avoid premature neuroprognostication and withdrawal of life-sustaining therapy before 72 hours post-ROSC** to prevent inappropriate termination of viable neurological recoveries.

> [!error]- Complications
> - Anoxic-Ischemic Encephalopathy $\rightarrow$ coma, myoclonus, seizures $\rightarrow$ TTM, continuous EEG, delayed multimodal neuroprognostication.
> - Post-Cardiac Arrest Myocardial Dysfunction $\rightarrow$ acute left ventricular failure, hypotension $\rightarrow$ inotropic support (Dobutamine/Milrinone) $\pm$ mechanical circulatory support (Impella/VA-ECMO).
> - CPR-Related Trauma (rib/sternal fractures, pneumothorax, hepatic laceration) $\rightarrow$ post-ROSC chest wall instability or unexplained hypotension $\rightarrow$ STAT CXR/CT, chest tube placement.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 62-year-old male with a history of hypertension suddenly collapses at a restaurant. A bystander initiates compressions, and EMS arrives within 6 minutes, identifying ventricular fibrillation. After two 200 J biphasic shocks and 2 minutes of CPR, Epinephrine 1 mg is given; a third shock plus Amiodarone 300 mg leads to ROSC. Post-ROSC 12-lead ECG reveals an anterior STEMI, and the patient is transported directly to the cardiac cath lab for emergent PCI under targeted temperature management.
