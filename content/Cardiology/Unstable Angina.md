> [!abstract] Unstable Angina
> Clinical syndrome of acute myocardial ischemia without detectable myocardial necrosis (biomarker negative). It is part of the Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS) spectrum and is an urgent precursor to myocardial infarction.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
> - Rupture or erosion of an atherosclerotic plaque leads to non-occlusive platelet aggregation and thrombus formation.
> - Causes transient, severe mismatch between myocardial oxygen supply and demand resulting in ischemia, but not yet myocyte cell death.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
> - Unstable angina and NSTEMI are clinically indistinguishable at presentation; UA is diagnosed retrospectively only after serial cardiac troponins remain negative. **Initial management is identical.**
> - A normal ECG does not rule out UA; capture ECGs *during* episodes of pain if possible to catch transient ischemic changes.
> - **Do not discharge a patient with convincing unstable symptoms just because the *initial* troponin is negative; serial evaluation is mandatory.**
> - Elderly, diabetic, and female patients often present with "anginal equivalents" like dyspnea, profound fatigue, or nausea rather than chest pain.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History (One of three principal presentations)**
> - **Rest Angina:** Occurring at rest and usually lasting >20 minutes.  **(vulnerable plaque)**
> - **New-Onset Severe Angina:** New onset within 2 months of at least Canadian Cardiovascular Society (CCS) Class III severity. (**intra-plaque hemorrhage**)
> - **Crescendo Angina:** Previously diagnosed angina that is distinctly more frequent, longer in duration, or lower in threshold.
>
> **Physical**
> - Often normal between episodes.
> - During pain: May see diaphoresis, transient S4 or S3 gallop, or new transient systolic murmur (ischemic mitral regurgitation).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> - **Clinical symptoms meeting one of the three principal presentations** (Rest, New-Onset Severe, or Crescendo angina).
>      In practice, it means that during your initial history-taking, the patient's story fits at least one of these three specific clinical patterns
>  '
> - **PLUS absence of cardiomyocyte necrosis:** Serial cardiac troponin levels must remain below the 99th percentile upper reference limit.
>   repeat (e.g., at 1–3 hours) are essential to differentiate UA from NSTEMI.
> '
> - *Note: Transient ischemic ECG changes (ST-depression or T-wave inversion) support the diagnosis but are not strictly required if history is classic.*

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
>- **CBC, BMP, Coags:** Baseline prior to initiation of antithrombotic therapy.
>
> **Imaging**
> - **Serial 12-lead ECGs:** On arrival and repeated during any recurrence of pain.
> - **Transthoracic Echocardiogram:** Evaluate for wall motion abnormalities (may be transient) and baseline function.
>
> **Risk Stratification**
> - **TIMI or GRACE Scores:** Calculate to guide decision between early invasive (cath) vs. ischemia-guided (stress test) strategies.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> - Pain refractory to nitroglycerin and morphine.
> - Hemodynamic instability (hypotension, acute HF).
> - Dynamic ST-segment changes on ECG.
> - Sustained ventricular arrhythmias.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
> - NSTEMI / STEMI (ruled out by serial troponins/ECG)
> - Aortic Dissection
> - Pulmonary Embolism
> - Tension Pneumothorax
>
> **Most Likely**
> - Stable Angina
> - GERD / Esophageal spasm
> - Musculoskeletal chest pain (costochondritis)
>
> **Less Common**
> - Acute Pericarditis

> [!summary]- Management
> *Note: Initial management is identical to NSTEMI.*
>
> **Treatment Goals**
> - **Primary Target:**Immediate relief of ischemia and prevention of progression to myocardial infarction.
> - **Secondary Target:**Stabilization of atherosclerotic plaque and prevention of long-term MACE.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
> - **Chewable Aspirin (162–325 mg STAT)** immediately upon suspicion to inhibit platelet cyclooxygenase (unless anaphylaxis).
> - **Sublingual Nitroglycerin (0.4 mg q5min x3)** for active ischemic pain to reduce preload/afterload (ensure SBP >90 mmHg, no recent PDE-5 inhibitors).
> - **Supplemental Oxygen** only if SaO2 <90% or respiratory distress to correct hypoxemia.
>
> **Early Targeted Therapy (Antithrombotic)**
> - **P2Y12 Inhibitor Loading Dose** ASAP to establish Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (DAPT). Preferred: Ticagrelor or Prasugrel (if angio planned); Alternative: Clopidogrel.
> - **Therapeutic Anticoagulation Initiation** immediately to prevent thrombus propagation. Options: Unfractionated Heparin (IV) or Enoxaparin (SQ).
>
> **Stabilization & Risk Stratification**
> - **Oral Beta-Blocker (e.g., Metoprolol tartrate)** within first 24 hours in stable patients (no HF, no hypotension/bradycardia) to reduce myocardial oxygen demand.
> - **High-Intensity Statin (e.g., Atorvastatin 80 mg)** initiated early for plaque stabilization.
>
> **Disposition & Further Workup**
> - **Admit to telemetry/CCU** for serial troponins and continuous monitoring.
> - **Coronary Angiography (Early Invasive)** for high-risk scores (TIMI/GRACE) or refractory symptoms.
> - **Non-invasive Stress Testing** for stabilized, lower-risk patients once ruled out for MI.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
> - **Avoid NSAIDs (non-aspirin)** in acute ACS as they increase major adverse cardiac events (MACE).
> - **Avoid Nitrates** if phosphodiesterase-5 (PDE-5) inhibitors (e.g., sildenafil) used within 24–48h to prevent severe hypotension.
> - **Avoid discharging patients** with convincing unstable angina history based solely on a single negative initial troponin and ECG.

> [!error]- Complications
> - Progression to NSTEMI or STEMI
> - Arrhythmias (VT/VF, less common than in MI)
> - Cardiogenic shock (rare in isolated UA unless severe multivessel disease)

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 58-year-old female with diabetes presents with new-onset retrosternal pressure occurring at rest lasting 25 minutes. Initial ECG is normal. Initial high-sensitivity troponin is negative. She is administered aspirin 325mg, loaded with clopidogrel, started on enoxaparin, and admitted for serial troponins. Subsequent troponins remain negative, confirming Unstable Angina. She undergoes an inpatient stress echocardiogram showing inducible ischemia and is referred for elective catheterization.
