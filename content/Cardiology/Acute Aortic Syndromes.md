> [!abstract] Acute Aortic Syndromes
> A spectrum of interrelated, life-threatening emergency aortic wall pathologies comprising **Acute Aortic Dissection (AAD)**, **Intramural Hematoma (IMH)**, and **Penetrating Atherosclerotic Ulcer (PAU)**. Driven by wall tension and pulsatile shear stress ($dP/dt$), leading to fatal aortic rupture or branch-vessel malperfusion.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
> - Intimal disruption allows pressurized blood to cleave the aortic media, creating a false lumen (AAD); rupture of *vasa vasorum* without an intimal flap causes IMH; deep atherosclerotic ulcer erodes through the internal elastic lamina into the media (PAU).
> - High wall shear stress ($dP/dt$) and systemic hypertension drive longitudinal propagation, leading to acute aortic regurgitation, branch malperfusion, hemopericardium/tamponade, or fatal rupture.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
> - Always administer **IV Beta-blockers BEFORE vasodilators**: vasodilators given alone cause reflex tachycardia and increase $dP/dt$, which accelerates dissection tear propagation and rupture.
> - Stanford Type A involves the ascending aorta ($\rightarrow$ **STAT emergent surgical repair**); uncomplicated Stanford Type B involves descending aorta only ($\rightarrow$ **anti-impulse medical therapy $\pm$ TEVAR**).
> - Check bilateral upper extremity blood pressures; an inter-arm $\text{SBP}$ difference $>20\text{ mmHg}$ or a unilateral pulse deficit strongly suggests AAS.
> - Type A dissection can retrograde-dissect into the Right Coronary Artery (RCA) ostium, presenting as an inferior STEMI—thrombolysis in this scenario is fatal.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
> - Abrupt, instantaneous maximal-intensity "tearing," "ripping," or "sharp" chest pain (Type A) or back/interscapular pain (Type B).
> - Pain that migrates along the anatomical path of dissection propagation.
> - Syncope (indicates cardiac tamponade, cerebral malperfusion, or aortic rupture).
> - Predisposing factors: chronic hypertension (70%), Marfan/Ehlers-Danlos, bicuspid aortic valve, prior aortic aneurysm, or third-trimester pregnancy.
> 
> **Physical**
> - Inter-arm blood pressure discrepancy ($>20\text{ mmHg}$ SBP) or asymmetric/absent peripheral pulses.
> - New early diastolic decrescendo murmur of acute aortic regurgitation (Type A).
> - Hypotension / Shock (ominous; suggests hemopericardium/tamponade or free rupture) vs. severe refractory hypertension (common in Type B).
> - Focal neurological deficits (Horner syndrome, stroke from carotid occlusion, paraplegia from spinal artery/Adamkiewicz ischemia).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> - **Mandatory Anatomical Imaging Confirmation:**
>   - **AAD (Acute Aortic Dissection):** Visualization of an intimal flap separating true and false lumens.
>   - **IMH:** Hyperdense, circular or crescentic thickening of the aortic wall ($\ge 5\text{ mm}$) without an identifiable intimal tear/flap.
>   - **PAU (Penetrating Aortic Ulcer):** Contrast-filled outpouching penetrating through aortic plaque into the media.
> - **Stanford Classification:**
>   - **Type A:** Any involvement of the **ascending aorta** (proximal to brachiocephalic artery), regardless of entry site.
>   - **Type B:** Involvement restricted to the **descending aorta** (distal to left subclavian artery).

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
> - *D-dimer:* High negative predictive value ($<500\text{ ng/mL}$ essentially rules out AAS in low-risk patients; elevated levels mandate immediate definitive imaging).
> - *Troponin, CBC, Type & Crossmatch (6–10 units PRBCs), Creatinine, Lactate:* Baseline organ damage assessment and surgical prep.
> 
> **Imaging**
> - *CT Angiography (CTA) Chest, Abdomen, & Pelvis:* First-line, gold-standard confirmatory imaging in hemodynamically stable patients.
> - *Transesophageal Echocardiography (TEE) / Bedside POCUS:* TEE is gold standard for unstable patients or CTA contraindications; POCUS rapidly identifies pericardial tamponade, aortic root dilatation ($>4\text{ cm}$), or severe AR.
> - *12-Lead ECG:* Exclude primary STEMI and screen for low voltage/electrical alternans (tamponade).
> 
> **Referrals**
> - *Cardiothoracic Surgery (Type A) / Vascular Surgery (Type B):* STAT intra-facility activation.
> - *Intensive Care Unit (ICU):* For invasive arterial blood pressure monitoring and continuous anti-impulse infusion.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> - **Hemodynamic Collapse / Tamponade:** Hypotension, elevated JVP, pulsus paradoxus (hemopericardium).
> - **Malperfusion Syndromes:** Stroke (carotids), paraplegia (Adamkiewicz), acute renal failure (renals), mesenteric ischemia (SMA; metabolic acidosis/high lactate), acute limb ischemia (femoral/iliac).
> - **Contained Rupture / Periaortic Hematoma:** Left-sided hemothorax, mediastinal hematoma.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
> - Acute Coronary Syndrome (STEMI / NSTEMI)
> - Massive Pulmonary Embolism
> - Pericardial Tamponade (non-dissection etiology)
> - Esophageal Rupture (Boerhaave syndrome)
> - Tension Pneumothorax
> 
> **Most Likely**
> - Hypertensive Emergency with Musculoskeletal Chest Wall Pain
> - Acute Pericarditis / Myocarditis
> 
> **Most Surprising**
> - Acute Ischemic Stroke / Spinal Ischemia from painless dissection
> 
> **Less Common**
> - Acute Pancreatitis, Biliary Colic, Renal Colic (for abdominal Type B)
> 
> **Non-Critical**
> - Costochondritis, Severe GERD

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
> - **Heart Rate Target:** $\text{HR } < 60\text{ bpm}$ (reduce aortic wall shear stress $dP/dt$).
> - **Blood Pressure Target:** $\text{SBP } 100–120\text{ mmHg}$ (or lowest tolerable pressure maintaining cerebral, coronary, and renal perfusion).
> - **Primary Outcome:** Prevent aortic rupture and reverse/prevent branch malperfusion.
> 
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
> - **Place bilateral large-bore IVs and radial arterial line** immediately upon presentation to establish rapid resuscitation access and accurate continuous blood pressure monitoring.
> - **Administer IV Opioids (Fentanyl 25–50 mcg or Morphine 2–4 mg)** STAT upon presentation to suppress sympathetic surge and reduce pain-driven tachycardia/hypertension.
> 
> **Medical Anti-Impulse Therapy (Universal First Step)**
> - **Administer IV Esmolol (loading 500 mcg/kg over 1 min, then 50–200 mcg/kg/min) or Labetalol (10–20 mg IV bolus q10min)** STAT to achieve $\text{HR } < 60\text{ bpm}$ and blunt myocardial contractility.
> - **Add IV Nicardipine (infusion 5–15 mg/h) or Sodium Nitroprusside (0.5–3.0 mcg/kg/min)** ONLY AFTER achieving $\text{HR } < 60\text{ bpm}$ to titrate $\text{SBP}$ down to $100–120\text{ mmHg}$.
> 
> **Definitive Surgical / Endovascular Management**
> - **Perform emergent open surgical repair (interposition graft $\pm$ aortic root/valve reconstruction)** STAT for all **Stanford Type A** dissections/IMH to prevent fatal rupture, tamponade, and coronary occlusion.
> - **Perform Thoracic Endovascular Aortic Repair (TEVAR)** immediately for **Complicated Stanford Type B** (malperfusion, persistent pain, refractory hypertension, rapid aortic expansion $>4\text{ mm}$, or rupture) to cover the primary tear and depressurize the false lumen.
> - **Maintain medical anti-impulse therapy** for **Uncomplicated Stanford Type B** in an ICU setting to achieve conservative aortic stabilization.
> 
> **Disposition & Surveillance**
> - **Admit directly to Cardiothoracic / Vascular ICU** for all acute presentations.
> - **Schedule surveillance CTA/MRA** at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months post-discharge, then annually, to detect late false lumen aneurysmal degeneration or progression.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
> - **Avoid vasodilators (Nitroprusside, Nicardipine, Hydralazine) without prior beta-blockade** in acute aortic dissection to prevent reflex tachycardia and elevated $dP/dt$, which accelerates dissection propagation and rupture.
> - **Avoid thrombolysis, full-dose anticoagulation, or P2Y12 inhibitors** in patients with suspected AAS (even if ECG shows ST-elevation) to prevent fatal hemopericardium and catastrophic aortic rupture.
> - **Avoid pericardiocentesis for hemopericardium/tamponade due to Type A dissection** (unless patient is in pre-arrest/arrest shock) to prevent dislodging the thrombus, accelerating bleeding, and precipitating sudden death.

> [!error]- Complications
> - Acute Hemopericardium / Tamponade $\rightarrow$ hypotension, JVD, electrical alternans $\rightarrow$ emergent transfer to OR for sternotomy (avoid pre-op pericardiocentesis).
> - Acute Severe Aortic Regurgitation $\rightarrow$ wide pulse pressure, flash pulmonary edema, diastolic murmur $\rightarrow$ emergent surgical repair with aortic valve suspension/replacement.
> - Malperfusion Syndrome (Mesenteric/Renal/Limb) $\rightarrow$ metabolic acidosis, elevated lactate, abdominal pain, anuria, cold pulseless limb $\rightarrow$ emergent operative/TEVAR fenestration or branch stenting.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 63-year-old male with long-standing poorly controlled hypertension presents with sudden, maximal-intensity "tearing" chest pain radiating between his scapulae. Vital signs show BP 195/110 mmHg in the right arm, 160/90 mmHg in the left arm, and HR 96 bpm. Bedside TTE reveals no pericardial fluid, and emergent CTA demonstrates a Stanford Type A dissection extending from the ascending aorta into the iliac arteries. IV Esmolol is started immediately, followed by Nicardipine once HR $<60\text{ bpm}$, and Cardiothoracic Surgery is mobilized for emergent operative repair.
