> [!abstract] Cardiac Tamponade
> A life-threatening hemodynamic emergency caused by accumulation of pericardial fluid under elevated pressure, leading to compression of all cardiac chambers, impaired diastolic filling, severe reduction in stroke volume, and obstructive shock.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
> - Intrapericardial pressure exceeds intracardiac diastolic filling pressures (first collapsing low-pressure right heart chambers), restricting venous return.
> - Exaggerated ventricular interdependence: inspiratory RV expansion shifts the interventricular septum leftward, drastically reducing LV end-diastolic volume and stroke volume (pulsus paradoxus).

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
> - Beck's Triad (hypotension, JVD, muffled heart sounds) is present in $<40\%$ of cases; absence does not rule out tamponade.
> - Pulsus paradoxus is an inspiratory $\text{SBP}$ drop $>10\text{ mmHg}$; absent in ASD, severe AR, and low-pressure tamponade.
> - **Avoid positive pressure ventilation / intubation** prior to decompression: positive intrathoracic pressure eliminates preload and triggers immediate cardiac arrest.
> - Because of the steep pericardial pressure-volume curve, removing as little as **$20–50\text{ mL}$** of fluid produces immediate hemodynamic recovery.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
> - Acute or progressive dyspnea, orthopnea, profound fatigue, and presyncope/syncope.
> - Preceding chest pain (pericarditis, dissection) or history of malignancy (lung, breast, lymphoma), uremia, recent cardiac instrumentation, or chest trauma.
> 
> **Physical**
> - **Beck's Triad:** Hypotension, Jugular Venous Distension (elevated JVP with absent $y$-descent), and muffled heart sounds.
> - **Pulsus Paradoxus:** Exaggerated inspiratory $\text{SBP}$ decline $>10\text{ mmHg}$.
> - Tachycardia and tachypnea (compensatory mechanism to preserve cardiac output).
> - Signs of hypoperfusion / obstructive shock (cool, clammy extremities, oliguria, altered sensorium).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> - **Mandatory Clinical & Echocardiographic Criteria:**
>   - Presence of pericardial effusion with documented hemodynamic compromise confirmed by:
>     1. **Right Atrial (RA) late-diastolic/systolic collapse** ($>33\%$ of the cardiac cycle).
>     2. **Right Ventricular (RV) early diastolic collapse** (inward indentation of RV free wall).
>     3. **Inferior Vena Cava (IVC) plethora** (dilated IVC with $<50\%$ inspiratory collapse).
>     4. **Significant respiratory transvalvular flow variation** on Doppler ($>25–40\%$ tricuspid increase, $>25\%$ mitral decrease during inspiration).

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
> - *Troponin, BUN/Creatinine, CBC, Coagulation profile:* Screen for myocardial infarction, uremia, underlying infection, and bleeding risk.
> - *Pericardial Fluid Analysis (Post-drainage):* Cytology (malignancy), Gram stain, bacterial/TB/fungal cultures, protein, LDH, and cell count.
> 
> **Imaging**
> - *Transthoracic Echocardiography (TTE) / POCUS:* First-line, gold-standard diagnostic tool (assesses chamber collapse, effusion volume, and IVC dynamics).
> - *12-Lead ECG:* Low QRS voltage (amplitude $<5\text{ mm}$ in limb leads) and electrical alternans (beat-to-beat QRS amplitude oscillation).
> - *Chest X-Ray:* Symmetrical, globular "water-bottle" cardiac silhouette (seen in large, chronic accumulations; normal in hyperacute tamponade).
> 
> **Referrals**
> - *Cardiology / Cardiothoracic Surgery:* STAT activation for bedside pericardiocentesis or surgical pericardiotomy.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> - Pulsus paradoxus $>20\text{ mmHg}$ or peripheral pulse disappearance on normal inspiration.
> - Bradycardia or junctional rhythm (indicates impending PEA arrest).
> - Hemopericardium associated with Stanford Type A aortic dissection or post-infarction myocardial wall rupture (contraindication to percutaneous tap).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
> - Tension Pneumothorax (hyperresonance, absent breath sounds, tracheal deviation)
> - Massive Pulmonary Embolism (acute RV failure without pericardial effusion)
> - Stanford Type A Aortic Dissection with retrograde rupture
> - Acute Right Ventricular Infarction
> 
> **Most Likely**
> - Constrictive Pericarditis (prominent $y$-descent, pericardial knock, calcification)
> - Severe Hypovolemic Shock
> 
> **Most Surprising**
> - Low-Pressure Cardiac Tamponade (hypovolemic patients presenting with normal/low JVP)
> 
> **Less Common**
> - Severe Acute Asthma / COPD Exacerbation (causes pulsus paradoxus without effusion)
> 
> **Non-Critical**
> - Hemodynamically stable pericardial effusion without chamber collapse

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
> - **Primary Target:** Immediate mechanical decompression to restore ventricular filling, achieving $\text{SBP} \ge 90\text{ mmHg}$ ($\text{MAP} \ge 65\text{ mmHg}$) and reversal of pulsus paradoxus.
> - **Secondary Target:** Prevent fluid re-accumulation and treat the underlying systemic cause.
> 
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
> - **Administer IV Isotonic Crystalloid Bolus (500–1000 mL normal saline)** STAT upon diagnosing hypotension to augment intravascular volume, increase right-sided filling pressures, and bridge to decompression.
> - **Perform ultrasound-guided Pericardiocentesis** STAT in hemodynamically unstable patients or cardiac arrest (subxiphoid or apical approach) to aspirate fluid and rapidly restore cardiac output.
> 
> **Surgical & Interventional Management**
> - **Perform Surgical Pericardial Window / Subxiphoid Pericardiotomy** when effusion is purulent, loculated, recurrent, post-traumatic, or due to Type A dissection to ensure complete evacuation and obtain pericardial biopsy.
> - **Place an indwelling pigtail catheter** during pericardiocentesis to maintain continuous gravity drainage until output falls to $<25–30\text{ mL}/24\text{ hours}$.
> 
> **Stepwise Escalation & Pharmacotherapy**
> - **Titrate IV Norepinephrine (0.05–0.5 mcg/kg/min) $\pm$ Dobutamine** if profound shock persists despite fluid loading while preparing for pericardiocentesis to support vascular tone and inotropy.
> 
> **Disposition & Surveillance**
> - **Admit directly to Cardiac ICU** for post-drainage hemodynamic monitoring and indwelling drain management.
> - **Perform repeat Echocardiography** at 24–48 hours post-procedure (and prior to catheter removal) to ensure complete resolution without reaccumulation.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
> - **Avoid positive pressure mechanical ventilation and heavy sedation before decompression** in tamponade to prevent abolishing venous return and precipitating fatal cardiovascular collapse.
> - **Avoid diuretics, nitrates, and vasodilators** in cardiac tamponade to prevent reducing preload and worsening obstructive shock.
> - **Avoid standalone percutaneous pericardiocentesis for Type A aortic dissection or ventricular free-wall rupture** (except as an extreme bridge during cardiac arrest) to prevent worsening hemorrhage; immediate emergency OR transfer for sternotomy is required.

> [!error]- Complications
> - Cardiac Arrest (PEA) $\rightarrow$ unresponsiveness, loss of pulse $\rightarrow$ immediate subxiphoid decompression and ACLS protocol.
> - Right Ventricular or Coronary Artery Laceration during pericardiocentesis $\rightarrow$ sudden rapid hemorrhagic drainage or hemothorax $\rightarrow$ emergent open surgical repair.
> - Effusive-Constrictive Pericarditis $\rightarrow$ persistent elevated JVP and elevated right atrial pressures despite successful fluid evacuation $\rightarrow$ pericardiectomy.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 54-year-old woman with metastatic breast cancer presents to the ED with progressive dyspnea, orthopnea, and lightheadedness. Examination reveals BP 84/60 mmHg with an inspiratory SBP drop of 18 mmHg (pulsus paradoxus), HR 120 bpm, elevated JVP, and faint heart sounds. Bedside POCUS confirms a large circumferential pericardial effusion with early RV diastolic collapse and a dilated, non-collapsing IVC. After a 500 mL saline bolus, emergency ultrasound-guided subxiphoid pericardiocentesis is performed, evacuating 200 mL of straw-colored fluid with immediate BP improvement to 118/76 mmHg.
