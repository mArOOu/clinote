> [!abstract] Life-threatening Arrhythmias
> Severe electrical disturbances of cardiac rate or rhythm that cause acute hemodynamic collapse, profound hypoperfusion, or carry an immediate risk of degeneration into ventricular fibrillation and cardiac arrest.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
> - Extreme tachycardias critically curtail ventricular diastolic filling time and coronary perfusion; extreme bradycardias cause fixed low cardiac output ($CO = HR \times SV$).
> - Driven by reentry (scar-related VT, accessory pathways), triggered activity (early afterdepolarizations in Torsades de Pointes), or conduction system destruction (His-Purkinje ischemia/fibrosis).

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
> - **Signs of Instability:** Hypotension, altered mental status, ischemic chest pain, acute pulmonary edema, or shock $\rightarrow$ mandate immediate electrical intervention (Cardioversion or Pacing).
> - **Synchronized vs. Unsynchronized:** Use *synchronized cardioversion* for monomorphic VT, SVT, and AF; use *unsynchronized defibrillation* for Polymorphic VT / Torsades (the device cannot synchronize on variable QRS peaks).
> - **Pre-excited AF (WPW (Wolff-Parkinson-White)+ AF):** Irregular, wide-complex tachycardia $\rightarrow$ use **Procainamide** or DC cardioversion; **NEVER give AV-nodal blockers** (Adenosine, Beta-blockers, CCBs, Digoxin).
> - Treat all regular wide-complex tachycardias as **Ventricular Tachycardia** until proven otherwise.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
> - Sudden palpitations, severe presyncope, syncope, or sudden collapse.
> - Ischemic chest pain or sudden severe dyspnea (flash pulmonary edema).
> - Background of prior MI/ischemic cardiomyopathy, reduced LVEF, channelopathy (LQTS, Brugada), or QT-prolonging drugs.
> 
> **Physical**
> - Extreme heart rates: Tachycardia ($\text{HR} > 150\text{ bpm}$) or severe Bradycardia ($\text{HR} < 40\text{ bpm}$).
> - Signs of shock: Hypotension, diaphoresis, cool/mottled extremities, altered sensorium.
> - Bilateral pulmonary crackles, tachypnea, and jugular venous distension.
> - Cannon *a*-waves in jugular venous pulsations (AV dissociation).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> - **Mandatory 12-Lead ECG / Telemetry Criteria:**
>   - **Monomorphic VT:** $\ge 3$ consecutive wide QRS complexes ($>120\text{ ms}$) at $\text{HR} > 100\text{ bpm}$ with constant morphology $\pm$ AV dissociation, capture beats, or fusion beats.
>   - **Polymorphic VT / Torsades de Pointes (TdP):** Wide QRS complexes continuously shifting axis/amplitude ("twisting around the baseline") with underlying baseline prolonged QTc ($>500\text{ ms}$).
>   - **Pre-excited AF (WPW):** Irregularly irregular wide-complex tachycardia with fluctuating QRS morphology and extremely rapid ventricular rates (often $>200–250\text{ bpm}$).
>   - **Mobitz Type II 2nd-Degree Block:** Intermittent non-conducted P waves with constant PR intervals before and after dropped beats.
>   - **3rd-Degree (Complete) AV Block:** Complete AV dissociation where P waves and QRS complexes fire independently (atrial rate $>$ ventricular escape rate).

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
> - *Serum Potassium and Magnesium:* Mandatory STAT to identify correctable hypokalemia or hypomagnesemia ($<2.0\text{ mg/dL}$).
> - *High-sensitivity Troponin:* Screen for acute coronary syndrome triggering the arrhythmia.
> - *Digoxin level & Toxicology screen:* If digitalis toxicity or drug-induced long QT is suspected.
> 
> **Imaging**
> - *12-Lead ECG (STAT):* Differentiate wide vs. narrow QRS, assess QTc interval, and evaluate ischemic ST-changes.
> - *Transthoracic Echocardiography (TTE):* Post-stabilization assessment of LVEF, regional wall motion abnormalities, and structural heart disease.
> 
> **Referrals**
> - *Electrophysiology (EP) / Interventional Cardiology:* STAT activation for temporary/permanent pacing, catheter ablation, or ICD implantation.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> - **The 5 Instability Criteria:** Hypotension, Altered Mental Status, Ischemic Chest Pain, Acute Heart Failure/Pulmonary Edema, Signs of Shock.
> - Polymorphic VT degenerating into Ventricular Fibrillation (VF).
> - High-grade AV block with wide QRS escape rhythm $<30\text{ bpm}$.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
> - Ventricular Fibrillation / Pulseless VT
> - Acute ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction (STEMI)
> - Massive Pulmonary Embolism (sinus tachycardia with S1Q3T3/RV strain)
> 
> **Most Likely**
> - Monomorphic VT vs. SVT with Aberrant Conduction
> - Atrial Fibrillation / Flutter with Rapid Ventricular Response (RVR)
> 
> **Most Surprising**
> - Pre-excited AF (WPW) mimicking Polymorphic VT
> - Severe Hyperkalemic "Sine-Wave" mimicking wide-complex VT
> 
> **Less Common**
> - Bidirectional VT (Digoxin toxicity or CPVT)
> 
> **Non-Critical**
> - Frequent PVCs / Non-sustained VT without hemodynamic compromise

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
> - **Primary Target:** Immediate restoration of sinus rhythm or perfusing rate, maintaining $\text{MAP} \ge 65\text{ mmHg}$ and resolving signs of hypoperfusion/ischemia.
> - **Secondary Target:** Correction of underlying metabolic/ischemic triggers and prevention of recurrence.
> 
> **Immediate / Emergencies (Unstable Rhythms)**
> - **Perform Synchronized Electrical Cardioversion (100–200 J biphasic)** STAT for unstable Monomorphic VT, unstable SVT, or unstable pre-excited AF to immediately restore cardiac output.
> - **Deliver immediate Unsynchronized Defibrillation (200 J biphasic)** STAT for Polymorphic VT / Torsades de Pointes or pulseless VT to prevent rapid progression to asystolic cardiac arrest.
> - **Administer Atropine 1 mg IV bolus (repeat q3–5min, max 3 mg)** STAT for symptomatic sinus bradycardia or nodal AV block to restore chronotropy.
> - **Initiate Transcutaneous Pacing (TCP)** immediately if Atropine fails or in unstable infranodal blocks (Mobitz II, 3rd-degree) to re-establish mechanical ventricular capture.
> 
> **Targeted Pharmacotherapy (Stable Tachyarrhythmias)**
> - **Administer IV Procainamide (20–50 mg/min infusion up to 17 mg/kg) or IV Amiodarone (150 mg over 10 min)** for stable sustained Monomorphic VT to achieve chemical cardioversion.
> - **Administer IV Magnesium Sulfate 2 g bolus over 1–2 minutes** STAT for Torsades de Pointes (TdP) to suppress early afterdepolarizations and stabilize the cardiac membrane.
> - **Administer IV Procainamide (or Ibutilide)** for stable Pre-excited AF (WPW) to prolong accessory pathway refractoriness without accelerating AV conduction.
> - **Initiate IV Isoproterenol (2–10 mcg/min) or Overdrive Pacing** for pause-dependent recurrent TdP to accelerate basal heart rate and shorten the QT interval.
> 
> **Targeted Pharmacotherapy (Refractory Bradyarrhythmias)**
> - **Titrate IV Dopamine (5–20 mcg/kg/min) or IV Epinephrine (2–10 mcg/min)** when pacing is pending or ineffective in unstable bradycardia to sustain arterial perfusion pressure.
> 
> **Definitive Management & Disposition**
> - **Place a Temporary Transvenous Pacemaker (TVP)** emergently for persistent high-grade AV block (Mobitz II / 3rd-degree) or pacing-dependent bradycardia.
> - **Admit directly to Cardiac ICU** for continuous rhythm surveillance and evaluate for Implantable Cardioverter-Defibrillator (ICD) or Permanent Pacemaker (PPM) prior to discharge.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
> - **Avoid AV-nodal blocking agents (Adenosine, Beta-blockers, Diltiazem/Verapamil, Digoxin)** in Pre-excited AF (WPW) to prevent shunting conduction down the accessory pathway and triggering fatal Ventricular Fibrillation.
> - **Avoid Atropine** in Mobitz Type II and wide-complex 3rd-degree AV block (infranodal disease) to prevent paradoxically worsening the block or precipitating asystole.
> - **Avoid synchronized mode during cardioversion of Polymorphic VT / Torsades** to prevent failure of the defibrillator to fire due to continuously variable R-wave tracking.
> - **Avoid QT-prolonging antiarrhythmics (Amiodarone, Procainamide, Sotalol)** in Torsades de Pointes to prevent lengthening the QT interval and precipitating fatal ventricular arrhythmias.

> [!error]- Complications
> - Degeneration to VF / Asystole $\rightarrow$ pulselessness $\rightarrow$ immediate ACLS protocol, high-quality CPR, and unsynchronized defibrillation.
> - Acute Pulmonary Edema / Cardiogenic Shock $\rightarrow$ tachypnea, severe hypoxemia, low MAP $\rightarrow$ immediate electrical cardioversion, non-invasive positive pressure ventilation (NIV), and inotropes.
> - Loss of Mechanical Capture during Pacing $\rightarrow$ severe bradycardia, hypotension $\rightarrow$ verify pad position, increase current output ($\text{mA}$), and switch to emergency Transvenous Pacing.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 67-year-old male with a history of prior ischemic cardiomyopathy presents with sudden palpitations, diaphoresis, and lightheadedness. Blood pressure is 76/48 mmHg, HR is 190 bpm, and monitor reveals a wide-complex, regular tachycardia (monomorphic VT). After rapid IV sedation, a 100 J synchronized biphasic shock is delivered, immediately converting the rhythm to normal sinus at 78 bpm with BP recovering to 122/76 mmHg. An Amiodarone infusion is started, and he is transferred directly to the Cardiac ICU for ICD evaluation.
