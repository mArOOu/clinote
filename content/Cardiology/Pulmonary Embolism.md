> [!abstract] Pulmonary Embolism
> Occlusion of the pulmonary arterial bed by a dislodged thrombus (most commonly originating from deep veins of the lower extremities), resulting in ventilation-perfusion mismatch, acute right ventricular strain, and potential obstructive circulatory collapse.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
> - Mechanical vascular obstruction and neurohumoral vasoconstriction acutely increase right ventricular (RV) afterload, leading to RV dilation, wall ischemia, and acute RV failure.
> - Leftward interventricular septal bowing severely compromises left ventricular (LV) diastolic filling, precipitating reduced cardiac output and obstructive shock.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
> - **Rule of Thumb on Suspicion:** If PE is suspected in a high-probability or unstable patient, start **anticoagulation immediately** before sending the patient to imaging.
> - **PERC Rule:** Apply the Pulmonary Embolism Rule-out Criteria ONLY in patients with a *low* pretest probability; all 8 criteria must be negative to rule out PE without a D-dimer.
> - **ECG Findings:** Sinus tachycardia is the most common rhythm; the classic $S_1Q_3T_3$ pattern, T-wave inversions in V1–V4, and new RBBB indicate severe acute RV strain.
> - **Systemic Thrombolysis (e.g., Alteplase):** Indicated strictly for **High-Risk (Massive) PE** with hemodynamic instability, NOT routinely for intermediate/low-risk PE.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
> - Sudden-onset, unexplained dyspnea and tachypnea (most common).
> - Pleuritic chest pain (indicates peripheral pulmonary infarction) $\pm$ hemoptysis.
> - Presyncope, syncope, or sudden collapse (indicates massive central/saddle embolus).
> - Virchow triad risk factors: recent immobilization/surgery ($<4\text{ weeks}$), active cancer, prior VTE, estrogen therapy, or hypercoagulable state.
> 
> **Physical**
> - Tachypnea ($\text{RR} > 20/\text{min}$) and Tachycardia ($\text{HR} > 100\text{ bpm}$).
> - Hypoxemia ($\text{SpO}_2 < 95\%$) refractory to low-flow oxygen.
> - Accentuated pulmonic component of the second heart sound ($P_2$), elevated JVP, or tricuspid regurgitation murmur.
> - Unilateral lower extremity swelling, erythema, and calf tenderness (concomitant DVT).
> - Hypotension / Obstructive Shock ($\text{SBP} < 90\text{ mmHg}$ or drop $\ge 40\text{ mmHg}$ for $>15\text{ minutes}$) in High-Risk PE.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> - **Mandatory Confirmatory Imaging / Diagnostic Thresholds:**
>   - **CT Pulmonary Angiography (CTPA):** Direct visualization of intraluminal filling defects in pulmonary arterial branches (Gold Standard).
>   - **Ventilation/Perfusion (V/Q) Scan:** High-probability scan ( $\ge 2$ large segmental mismatched perfusion defects with normal ventilation) when CTPA is contraindicated (severe renal failure, iodinated contrast allergy).
>   - **Clinical Decision Pathway Thresholds:**
>     - *Low Pretest Probability (Wells $<2$ or Geneva $<4$):* PERC negative $\rightarrow$ PE excluded (no labs); PERC positive $\rightarrow$ D-dimer required.
>     - *Intermediate Pretest Probability (Wells 2–6):* Age-adjusted D-dimer ($[\text{age} \times 10\text{ mcg/L}]$ if $>50\text{ years}$); if elevated $\rightarrow$ CTPA mandatory.
>     - *High Pretest Probability (Wells $>6$):* Proceed directly to CTPA (D-dimer is contraindicated/useless).

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
> - *D-dimer (High-sensitivity ELISA):* High negative predictive value ($<500\text{ mcg/L}$ or below age-adjusted cutoff excludes PE in low/intermediate probability).
> - *Cardiac Biomarkers (High-sensitivity Troponin & BNP/NT-proBNP):* Mandatory for risk stratification (elevations confirm myocardial injury and RV neurohormonal stress).
> - *Arterial Blood Gas (ABG):* Hypoxemia, hypocapnia (respiratory alkalosis), and elevated alveolar-arterial ($A\text{-}a$) oxygen gradient.
> 
> **Imaging**
> - *CT Pulmonary Angiography (CTPA):* Confirms anatomical occlusion and evaluates RV/LV diameter ratio ($>1.0$ indicates RV strain).
> - *Bedside Echocardiography (TTE) / POCUS:* First-line in unstable patients; assesses **McConnell sign** (RV free-wall akinesia with apex sparing), tricuspid regurgitant jet velocity, and RV dilation.
> - *Lower Extremity Compression Duplex Ultrasound:* Confirms DVT source; diagnostic surrogate in unstable patients unable to undergo CTPA.
> 
> **Referrals**
> - *Pulmonary Embolism Response Team (PERT) / Interventional Radiology / Cardiothoracic Surgery:* STAT activation for High-Risk PE or failing Intermediate-High Risk PE.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> - **Hemodynamic Instability:** $\text{SBP} < 90\text{ mmHg}$, vasopressor requirement, or cardiac arrest (High-Risk/Massive PE).
> - **Intermediate-High Risk PE Triad:** CTPA-confirmed PE + RV strain on Echo/CT + elevated Troponin/BNP.
> - **Syncope or Bradycardia / PEA:** Indicates impending catastrophic RV pump failure.

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
> - Acute Coronary Syndrome (STEMI / NSTEMI)
> - Stanford Type A Aortic Dissection
> - Cardiac Tamponade
> - Tension Pneumothorax
> 
> **Most Likely**
> - Acute Exacerbation of COPD / Asthma
> - Community-Acquired Pneumonia / Pleurisy
> 
> **Most Surprising**
> - Amniotic Fluid Embolism, Fat Embolism, or Air Embolism
> 
> **Less Common**
> - Acute Pericarditis / Myocarditis
> 
> **Non-Critical**
> - Musculoskeletal Chest Wall Pain, Panic Disorder with Hyperventilation

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
> - **Primary Target:** Rapid restoration of pulmonary arterial patency, preservation of RV output, and maintenance of systemic perfusion ($\text{MAP} \ge 65\text{ mmHg}$, $\text{SpO}_2 \ge 92\%$).
> - **Secondary Target:** Prevention of thrombus propagation, recurrent VTE, and chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH).
> 
> **Immediate / Emergencies (High-Risk / Massive PE)**
> - **Administer Unfractionated Heparin (UFH) IV bolus (80 units/kg) followed by infusion (18 units/kg/h)** immediately upon strong clinical suspicion before imaging to arrest clot propagation.
> - **Administer Systemic Thrombolysis (Alteplase 100 mg IV infusion over 2 hours, or 50 mg IV bolus in cardiac arrest)** STAT upon confirming High-Risk PE with persistent hypotension to rapidly dissolve the occluding thrombus and unload the failing right ventricle.
> - **Perform Catheter-Directed Mechanical Thrombectomy or Surgical Pulmonary Embolectomy** STAT when systemic thrombolysis is contraindicated or has failed in unstable patients.
> - **Initiate IV Norepinephrine (0.05–0.5 mcg/kg/min)** immediately in hypotensive shock to restore coronary perfusion pressure to the right ventricle.
> 
> **Intermediate-Risk PE (Submassive)**
> - **Administer Low-Molecular-Weight Heparin (LMWH, Enoxaparin 1 mg/kg SC q12h) or Fondaparinux** immediately for Intermediate-High and Intermediate-Low Risk patients to provide predictable therapeutic anticoagulation.
> - **Transfer to Step-Down Unit / ICU for continuous hemodynamic monitoring** in Intermediate-High Risk patients to detect early decompensation and deliver rescue thrombolysis if hemodynamic collapse occurs.
> 
> **Low-Risk PE & Anticoagulation Continuation**
> - **Initiate Direct Oral Anticoagulants (DOACs: Apixaban 10 mg PO BID for 7 days then 5 mg BID, or Rivaroxaban 15 mg PO BID for 21 days then 20 mg daily)** immediately as first-line therapy for stable Low-Risk PE without renal/GI contraindications.
> - **Maintain therapeutic anticoagulation for a minimum of 3 months** for provoked PE (or indefinite anticoagulation for unprovoked PE or active malignancy) to prevent recurrent fatal VTE.
> 
> **Disposition Triggers**
> - **Consider outpatient discharge within 24 hours** for Low-Risk PE meeting all **Hestia Criteria** or $\text{sPESI} = 0$ with intact social support and verified DOAC access.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
> - **Avoid aggressive IV fluid resuscitation ($>500\text{ mL}$)** in PE-induced shock to prevent overdistending the right ventricle, worsening leftward septal displacement, and further crashing LV preload.
> - **Avoid ordering D-dimer in high pretest probability patients** to prevent false reassurance and fatal delays in obtaining definitive CTPA.
> - **Avoid routine systemic thrombolysis in hemodynamically stable (Low or Intermediate-Risk) PE** to prevent catastrophic major bleeding (intracranial hemorrhage risk $\approx 1.5–2\%$) without proven mortality benefit.
> - **Avoid using DOACs** in patients with severe renal impairment ($\text{CrCl} < 15–30\text{ mL/min}$), severe hepatic disease, or Antiphospholipid Syndrome (APLS) to prevent drug toxicity or recurrent thrombosis (use Warfarin/UFH instead).

> [!error]- Complications
> - Obstructive Shock / Cardiac Arrest (PEA) $\rightarrow$ sudden collapse, narrow complex rhythm $\rightarrow$ immediate CPR, Alteplase 50 mg IV push, and emergency mechanical decompression.
> - Major / Intracranial Hemorrhage post-thrombolysis $\rightarrow$ acute neurological deficit, severe drop in Hb $\rightarrow$ stop thrombolytic/heparin, administer Cryoprecipitate, TXA, and emergent reversal agents.
> - Chronic Thromboembolic Pulmonary Hypertension (CTEPH) $\rightarrow$ persistent progressive exertional dyspnea $\ge 3\text{ months}$ post-PE $\rightarrow$ referral for V/Q scan and pulmonary endarterectomy evaluation.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 55-year-old female presents to the ED with acute-onset dyspnea and right-sided pleuritic chest pain 10 days after a total knee replacement. Vital signs show HR 118 bpm, BP 128/82 mmHg, RR 24/min, and $\text{SpO}_2\text{ 91\%}$ on room air. Wells score is 6 (high risk), prompting an immediate therapeutic dose of Enoxaparin followed by CTPA, which reveals bilateral pulmonary emboli with RV/LV ratio $>1.1$. Normal troponin and stable hemodynamics place her in the Intermediate-Low Risk category; she is admitted for step-down monitoring and switched to Apixaban with uneventful recovery.
