> [!abstract] Hypertensive Emergency
> Severe elevation in blood pressure (typically SBP $\ge 180\text{ mmHg}$ **and/or** DBP $\ge 120\text{ mmHg}$) associated with acute, life-threatening, progressive **target-organ damage (TOD)** affecting the brain, heart, aorta, kidneys, or eyes.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
> - Abrupt surge in systemic vascular resistance overcomes vascular autoregulation.
> - Triggers mechanical endothelial shear stress, fibrinoid necrosis of arterioles, and platelet activation, causing organ hypoperfusion and microvascular ischemia.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
> - The absolute BP value does not define an emergency; the presence of **acute target organ damage** does (urgency = high BP without TOD; emergency = high BP with TOD).
> - **Never normalize BP precipitously** in general hypertensive emergencies; lowering BP too fast shifts perfusion below the autoregulatory threshold, causing ischemic stroke, myocardial infarction, or blindness.
> - **Aortic dissection is the exception:** SBP must be lowered rapidly to $< 120\text{ mmHg}$ and HR $< 60\text{ bpm}$ within 20 minutes; always give beta-blockers *before* vasodilators to prevent reflex tachycardia and increased shear stress ($dP/dt$).
> - **Acute ischemic stroke is permissive:** Do not lower BP unless $> 220/120\text{ mmHg}$ (or $> 185/110\text{ mmHg}$ if eligible for IV thrombolysis/thrombectomy).
> - Avoid sublingual nifedipine and IV hydralazine boluses due to unpredictable, precipitous BP drops and reflex tachycardia.
 
> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
> - Medication non-adherence (most common cause) or recent sympathomimetic use (cocaine, amphetamines, decongestants).
> - Neurologic: Severe headache, confusion, altered mental status, visual disturbances, seizures, focal deficits.
> - Cardiovascular: "Tearing" chest/back pain, orthopnea, acute dyspnea, palpitations.
> - Renal: Oliguria, hematuria, generalized edema.
>
> **Physical**
> - Bilateral BP measurements in both arms (unequal pulse/BP $\Delta > 20\text{ mmHg}$ suggests aortic dissection).
> - Fundoscopy: Flame hemorrhages, cotton-wool spots, hard exudates, papilledema (Grade III/IV retinopathy).
> - Cardiovascular & Pulmonary: S3/S4 gallop, new aortic regurgitation murmur, jugular venous distension, diffuse pulmonary crackles.
> - Neurologic: Altered sensorium, focal motor/sensory deficits, signs of increased ICP.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> - **Severe Blood Pressure Elevation:** SBP $\ge 180\text{ mmHg}$ and/or DBP $\ge 120\text{ mmHg}$.
> - **PLUS Confirmed New or Worsening Acute Target Organ Damage:**
>   - *Neurologic:* Hypertensive encephalopathy, ischemic stroke, intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH), subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH).
>   - *Cardiovascular:* Acute aortic dissection, acute coronary syndrome (ACS), acute cardiogenic pulmonary edema.
>   - *Renal:* Acute kidney injury (rapid rise in creatinine, oliguria, red cell casts/proteinuria).
>   - *Ocular:* Grade III/IV hypertensive retinopathy (papilledema, hemorrhages).
>   - *Obstetric / Hematologic:* Eclampsia/severe preeclampsia, thrombotic microangiopathy (MAHA).

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Initial (Immediate)**
> - **12-lead ECG:** Check for acute ischemia, ST-T wave changes, left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH), or strain.
> - **STAT Point-of-Care Glucose:** Rule out hypoglycemia mimicking focal deficits or encephalopathy.
> - **Urinalysis:** Identify hematuria, proteinuria, and red blood cell casts (indicates acute nephrosclerosis).
>
> **Confirmatory / Severity Labs**
> - **BMP & Serum Creatinine/BUN:** Assess acute kidney injury and baseline electrolytes prior to antihypertensive therapy.
> - **Cardiac Troponin & BNP:** Detect acute subendocardial ischemia and acute heart failure.
> - **CBC with Peripheral Smear:** Check for schistocytes/thrombocytopenia (microangiopathic hemolytic anemia).
> - **Urine Toxicology Screen:** Detect cocaine, amphetamines, or sympathomimetics.
>
> **Targeted Imaging**
> - **Non-contrast Head CT:** STAT if focal neurologic deficits, severe encephalopathy, or sudden severe headache (rule out stroke/ICH/SAH).
> - **CT Angiography (Chest/Abdomen):** STAT if tearing chest/back pain or unequal pulses/BP (rule out aortic dissection).
> - **Chest X-ray / Point-of-Care Ultrasound (POCUS):** Assess for pulmonary edema (B-lines), cardiomegaly, or widened mediastinum.
>
> **Referrals**
> - **ICU Admission & Cardiology/Neurology/Nephrology:** STAT depending on the affected organ system.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> - Tearing back/chest pain radiating to the interscapular region (Aortic Dissection).
> - Progressive lethargy, seizures, or coma (Hypertensive Encephalopathy / ICH).
> - Acute flash pulmonary edema with severe hypoxia.
> - Pregnancy $> 20\text{ weeks}$ with severe BP elevation and headache/RUQ pain (Eclampsia / HELLP).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
> - Acute Aortic Dissection
> - Intracranial Hemorrhage (ICH / SAH)
> - Acute Ischemic Stroke with reactive hypertension
> - Flash Pulmonary Edema / Acute MI
>
> **Most Likely**
> - Hypertensive Urgency (severe BP elevation *without* acute target organ damage)
> - Medication Non-adherence / Rebound Hypertension (e.g., abrupt clonidine or beta-blocker withdrawal)
>
> **Most Surprising / Secondary Causes**
> - Pheochromocytoma crisis
> - Sympathomimetic toxicity (cocaine, methamphetamine)
> - Renal artery stenosis crisis (flash pulmonary edema with normal EF)
>
> **Less Common**
> - Scleroderma Renal Crisis
> - Eclampsia / Preeclampsia

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
> - **Standard Hypertensive Emergency:** Reduce Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) by maximum $20\text{--}25\%$ within the first 1 hour; if stable, target BP to $\sim 160/100\text{ mmHg}$ over next 2–6 hours; normalize BP gradually over 24–48 hours.
> - **Acute Aortic Dissection:** Rapidly target SBP $< 120\text{ mmHg}$ and HR $< 60\text{ bpm}$ within the first 20 minutes.
> - **Acute Pulmonary Edema / ACS:** Rapidly reduce SBP to $< 140\text{ mmHg}$ within the first 1 hour while resolving ischemia/congestion.
> - **Severe Preeclampsia / Eclampsia:** Reduce SBP to $< 140\text{ mmHg}$ within the first 1 hour.
> - **Acute Ischemic Stroke:** Permissive hypertension; reduce only if BP $> 220/120\text{ mmHg}$ (target $15\%$ reduction in 24h) OR if receiving thrombolysis/thrombectomy (target BP $< 185/110\text{ mmHg}$ pre-tPA and $< 180/105\text{ mmHg}$ post-tPA).
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
> - **Place Continuous Arterial Line and IV Access** STAT upon presentation to allow precise real-time BP monitoring and safe titration of parenteral infusions.
> - **Administer Supplemental Oxygen / BiPAP** immediately if flash pulmonary edema is present to correct acute hypoxemia and reduce preload/afterload.
>
> **Targeted Parenteral Pharmacotherapy (Select by Organ System)**
> - **IV Nicardipine (5–15 mg/h titratable)** or **Clevidipine (1–32 mg/h)** continuously as versatile first-line agents in hypertensive encephalopathy, acute renal injury, or intracranial hemorrhage to smoothly lower afterload without cardiac depression.
> - **IV Labetalol (10–20 mg bolus q10min or 1–2 mg/min infusion)** upon presentation of acute aortic dissection, pregnancy-associated hypertension, or ICH to provide combined alpha- and beta-adrenergic blockade.
> - **IV Esmolol Infusion (50–300 mcg/kg/min)** initiated *prior* to vasodilators in acute aortic dissection to suppress cardiac contractility ($dP/dt$) and maintain HR $< 60\text{ bpm}$.
> - **IV Nitroglycerin (5–200 mcg/min)** immediately for ACS or acute pulmonary edema to reduce cardiac preload, LV filling pressures, and myocardial oxygen demand.
> - **IV Hydralazine (5–10 mg slow IV)** or **IV Labetalol** in severe preeclampsia/eclampsia to prevent maternal cerebrovascular complications.
>
> **Stepwise Escalation & Transition**
> - **Add Second-Line IV Infusion (e.g., Fenoldopam 0.1–1.6 mcg/kg/min)** if refractory renal compromise persists to maintain renal cortical blood flow.
> - **Initiate Overlapping Oral Antihypertensives** after 12–24 hours of sustained hemodynamic stability to facilitate safe weaning of parenteral infusions.
>
> **Disposition & Monitoring**
> - **Admit directly to the Intensive Care Unit (ICU)** for continuous arterial line monitoring and hourly target organ assessments.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
> - **Avoid Sublingual / Immediate-Release Nifedipine** in all hypertensive crises to prevent precipitous, uncontrolled BP drops leading to cerebral ischemia and death.
> - **Avoid Vasodilators without prior Beta-Blockade (e.g., pure nitroprusside/nicardipine alone)** in acute aortic dissection to prevent reflex sympathetic tachycardia and accelerated aortic shear stress.
> - **Avoid Beta-Blockers (Labetalol/Esmolol)** in acute decompensated heart failure with pulmonary edema or severe bradycardia/heart block to prevent acute cardiogenic collapse.
> - **Avoid Beta-Blockers alone** in cocaine/amphetamine toxicity or suspected pheochromocytoma to prevent unopposed alpha-adrenergic vasoconstriction (use phentolamine or benzodiazepines + CCBs).
> - **Avoid Sodium Nitroprusside in renal/hepatic impairment** or for prolonged infusions ($> 48\text{h}$) to prevent lethal cyanide/thiocyanate toxicity.
> - **Avoid Aggressive BP Reduction** in acute ischemic stroke unless thresholds ($> 220/120$ or $> 185/110\text{ mmHg}$) are breached to prevent infarct expansion in the ischemic penumbra.

> [!error]- Complications
> - Ischemic stroke / watershed infarction (from overzealous, rapid BP reduction).
> - Hemorrhagic stroke / Intracerebral hemorrhage.
> - Aortic rupture or extension of dissection plane.
> - Acute non-oliguric to oliguric renal failure requiring hemodialysis.
> - Cyanide/thiocyanate neurotoxicity (confusion, metabolic acidosis with nitroprusside).

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 56-year-old male with poorly controlled chronic hypertension presents with a severe global headache, confusion, blurred vision, and a BP of 220/130 mmHg. Fundoscopy reveals bilateral retinal flame hemorrhages and papilledema; head CT excludes acute hemorrhage. He is diagnosed with hypertensive encephalopathy and admitted to the ICU, where an arterial line is placed and an IV nicardipine infusion is started, achieving a 20% reduction in MAP over the first hour with rapid clearance of his confusion.

> [!info] Personal Experience
> - A common error on rounds is confusing Hypertensive Urgency (asymptomatic high BP) with Hypertensive Emergency; giving IV drips to asymptomatic patients in the ED or step-down unit causes iatrogenic harm.
> - When managing aortic dissection, remember: **"Beta-blocker first, vasodilator second"**—never invert the order.
