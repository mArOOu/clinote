> [!abstract] Cardiogenic Shock
> A life-threatening state of low cardiac output resulting in end-organ hypoperfusion and cellular hypoxia, most commonly secondary to acute myocardial infarction (AMI) or acute decompensated heart failure, despite adequate intravascular volume.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
> - Severe myocardial injury severely $\downarrow$ Cardiac Output (CO) and Stroke Volume (SV), causing systemic hypotension and $\uparrow$ filling pressures.
> - Compensatory sympathetic activation $\uparrow$ systemic vascular resistance (SVR/afterload), which further increases myocardial oxygen demand and worsens ischemia/contractility.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
> - **Epinephrine is inferior to Norepinephrine** in cardiogenic shock—it is associated with a higher incidence of refractory shock and lactic acidosis.
> - **"Normotensive shock" exists:** Young patients or those with severe baseline hypertension may maintain a "normal" BP while suffering profound hypoperfusion. Always check lactate and clinical signs.
> - **Right Ventricular (RV) shock is different:** Requires cautious volume loading, avoidance of high PEEP (which impedes venous return), and early use of pulmonary vasodilators/inotropes.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
> - Recent or active chest pain (ACS).
> - Worsening dyspnea, orthopnea, or medication non-compliance (HF exacerbation).
> - Palpitations or syncope (arrhythmogenic shock).
> 
> **Physical**
> - **Perfusion:** Cold, clammy, mottled extremities; delayed capillary refill.
> - **Vitals:** Sustained hypotension (SBP < 90), tachycardia, narrow pulse pressure.
> - **Congestion:** Jugular venous distension (JVD), bibasilar pulmonary crackles.
> - **End-organ:** Altered mental status (agitation, confusion), profound oliguria.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> Strictly defined by the triad of:
> - **Sustained Hypotension:** SBP < 90 mmHg for $\geq$ 30 minutes, or requiring vasopressors to maintain MAP $\geq$ 65 mmHg.
> - **Hemodynamic Impairment:** Cardiac index (CI) $\leq$ 2.2 L/min/m$^2$ AND Pulmonary Capillary Wedge Pressure (PCWP) > 15 mmHg.
> - **End-Organ Hypoperfusion:** Elevated arterial lactate (> 2 mmol/L), altered mentation, or oliguria (< 0.5 mL/kg/h).

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Labs**
> - **Arterial Lactate:** Critical for gauging severity and monitoring response to therapy.
> - **High-Sensitivity Troponin & BNP/NT-proBNP:** Confirm ischemic or heart failure etiology.
> - **ABG, Creatinine, LFTs:** Assess extent of end-organ damage (shock liver, AKI).
> 
> **Imaging & Diagnostics**
> - **STAT 12-Lead ECG:** Rule out STEMI, ongoing ischemia, or hemodynamically unstable arrhythmias.
> - **STAT Echocardiogram:** Assess LVEF, RV function, and rule out mechanical complications (e.g., ventricular septal rupture, acute severe mitral regurgitation, tamponade).
> - **Chest X-Ray:** Evaluate for pulmonary edema.
> 
> **Referrals & Invasive Tests**
> - **Pulmonary Artery Catheter (Swan-Ganz):** Indicated if shock etiology is mixed/unclear, or if patient is unresponsive to initial vasopressor/inotropic therapy.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> - Rising lactate despite optimization of MAP (> 65 mmHg).
> - New loud holosystolic murmur (suggests catastrophic mechanical complication like papillary muscle rupture or VSR).
> - Worsening oxygenation/ARDS pattern indicating secondary systemic inflammatory response (SIRS).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
> - Massive Pulmonary Embolism (Obstructive)
> - Cardiac Tamponade (Obstructive)
> - Tension Pneumothorax (Obstructive)
> 
> **Most Likely**
> - Severe Hypovolemic Shock (presents with flat neck veins, clear lungs)
> - Septic Shock (warm extremities early, wide pulse pressure)
> 
> **Less Common**
> - Acute Fulminant Myocarditis
> - Takotsubo Cardiomyopathy

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
> - **Primary Target:** Maintain MAP $\geq$ 65 mmHg and Cardiac Index > 2.2 L/min/m$^2$.
> - **Secondary Target:** Demonstrate lactate clearance, reverse end-organ hypoperfusion, and successfully revascularize if ischemic.
> 
> **Immediate / Emergencies**
> - **Initiate Norepinephrine** via central line (temporize peripherally if needed) when MAP < 65 mmHg to rapidly restore systemic and coronary perfusion pressures.
> - **Activate Cath Lab emergently** if the patient presents with suspected acute MI (STEMI/NSTEMI) to perform life-saving mechanical revascularization (PCI/CABG).
> - **Apply Non-Invasive Ventilation (CPAP/BiPAP) or Intubate** if profound respiratory distress or severe hypoxia to reduce the work of breathing and decrease LV afterload.
> 
> **Inotropic Support & Decongestion**
> - **Add Dobutamine or Milrinone** when tissue hypoperfusion/elevated lactate persists despite an adequate MAP to actively augment myocardial contractility and stroke volume.
> - **Administer IV Furosemide** once hemodynamics are stabilized (or concurrently with pressor support) to actively relieve pulmonary and systemic congestion.
> 
> **Mechanical Circulatory Support (MCS) Escalation**
> - **Deploy Temporary MCS (Impella, IABP, VA-ECMO)** if shock is refractory to $\geq$ 2 vasoactive agents or clinical deterioration continues, to mechanically offload the ventricle and preserve end-organ perfusion.
> 
> **Disposition**
> - **Admit to Cardiac Intensive Care Unit (CICU)** immediately upon recognition to secure continuous hemodynamic monitoring, PAC placement, and specialist multidisciplinary care (Shock Team).

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
> - **Avoid Beta-blockers and non-dihydropyridine CCBs** in any patient with suspected or confirmed cardiogenic shock to prevent a fatal reduction in inotropy and chronotropy.
> - **Avoid aggressive IV fluid boluses** in patients with signs of pulmonary edema or elevated right-sided pressures to prevent catastrophic worsening of respiratory failure.
> - **Avoid routine use of Epinephrine** as a first-line vasopressor to prevent severe increases in myocardial oxygen demand, tachyarrhythmias, and metabolic acidosis.

> [!error]- Complications
> - Multi-Organ Dysfunction Syndrome (MODS): Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN), Shock Liver.
> - Arrhythmias: VT/VF due to worsening ischemia or pro-arrhythmic inotropes.
> - Limb Ischemia: Complication of profound vasoconstriction or MCS insertion (femoral access).

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 68-year-old male with a history of an anterior STEMI 3 days ago presents to the ED with severe progressive dyspnea, confusion, and cold/mottled extremities. His BP is 78/50 mmHg, HR is 115 bpm, and exam reveals elevated JVP and bilateral coarse crackles. He requires immediate central venous access for Norepinephrine, STAT echocardiography to rule out a ventricular septal rupture, and urgent CICU admission.
