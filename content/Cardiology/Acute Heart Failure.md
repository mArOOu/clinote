> [!abstract] Acute Heart Failure (AHF)
> Rapid onset or worsening of symptoms and signs of heart failure requiring urgent medical evaluation and hospitalization, characterized by pulmonary/systemic congestion, with or without reduced cardiac output and hypoperfusion.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
> - Abrupt increase in left ventricular (LV) filling pressures causes retrograde hydrostatic fluid transudation into the pulmonary interstitium and alveoli.
> - Triggered by neurohormonal activation (RAAS, sympathetic surge), acute ischemia, arrhythmias, or severe fluid/salt retention, leading to vascular redistribution and impaired organ perfusion.


> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
> - Classify hemodynamics immediately using the **Nohria-Stevenson criteria**: "Warm & Wet" (~80%, congested, well-perfused), "Cold & Wet" (congested + hypoperfused/shock), "Cold & Dry" (hypovolemic/over-diuresed), "Warm & Dry" (compensated).
> - **Flash Pulmonary Edema** is usually a vascular redistribution problem due to hypertensive surge, not total-body volume overload; prioritize aggressive IV vasodilators (nitroglycerin) over high-dose diuretics.
> - **Do NOT routinely stop chronic beta-blockers** in mild-to-moderate decompensation unless the patient is in cardiogenic shock, severe bradycardia, or has low-output hypoperfusion.
> - **Never start a NEW beta-blocker** during acute decompensation; wait until euvolemia and hemodynamic stability are achieved.
> - Look for the **CHAMPIT** triggers: **C**oronary syndrome, **H**ypertensive emergency, **A**rrhythmia, **M**echanical complication, **P**ulmonary embolism, **I**nfection, **T**amponade.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
> - Rapidly progressive dyspnea, orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea (PND), or rest dyspnea.
> - Acute weight gain (fluid accumulation) or profound fatigue, anorexia, and confusion (low cardiac output).
> - Precipitating factors: Dietary/medication non-adherence, NSAID use, recent infection, ischemia, uncontrolled hypertension.
>
> **Physical**
> - *Congestion ("Wet"):* Elevated jugular venous pressure (JVP > 8 cm H₂O), positive hepatojugular reflux, bilateral pulmonary crackles/rales, S3 gallop, peripheral pitting edema, ascites.
> - *Hypoperfusion ("Cold"):* Cool/clammy extremities, narrow pulse pressure, systolic BP < 90 mmHg, altered mental status, oliguria (urine output < 0.5 mL/kg/h).

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria
> - **Clinical syndrome:** Acute onset of typical symptoms (dyspnea, orthopnea) and physical signs of congestion/hypoperfusion.
> - **PLUS Markedly elevated natriuretic peptides:**
>   - **BNP:** $\ge 100\text{ pg/mL}$ (or $> 400\text{ pg/mL}$ for acute decompensation).
>   - **NT-proBNP:** Age-adjusted cutoffs: $> 450\text{ pg/mL}$ (<50y), $> 900\text{ pg/mL}$ (50–75y), $> 1800\text{ pg/mL}$ (>75y).
> - **PLUS Objective structural/functional cardiac abnormality:** Confirmed by echocardiography, elevated filling pressures on imaging/POCUS, or hemodynamic monitoring.

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Initial (STAT)**
> - **12-lead ECG:** Identify triggers like acute myocardial infarction, arrhythmias (e.g., rapid AF), or conduction blocks.
> - **Point-of-Care Ultrasound (POCUS):** Rapidly evaluate lung B-lines (pulmonary edema), IVC distension/plethora (elevated CVP), and gross LV ejection fraction.
> - **High-Sensitivity Troponin:** Detect acute coronary syndrome or myocyte necrosis from strain.
> - **BMP & Serum Creatinine/BUN/Lactate:** Assess for cardiorenal syndrome, electrolyte derangements, and tissue hypoperfusion.
>
> **Imaging & Structural**
> - **Chest X-ray:** Check for cardiomegaly, pulmonary vascular congestion (cephalization, Kerley B lines), alveolar bat-wing edema, or pleural effusions.
> - **Transthoracic Echocardiography (TTE):** Perform within 24–48 hours (STAT if hemodynamic instability) to assess EF, diastolic dysfunction, valve pathology, or tamponade.
>
> **Referrals**
> - **Cardiology / Heart Failure Team:** Inpatient consultation for optimization and advanced HF evaluation if refractory.
> - **ICU / CCU:** STAT transfer if cardiogenic shock, respiratory failure requiring NIV/intubation, or requiring IV inotropes/vasopressors.

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> - Cardiogenic shock: SBP < 90 mmHg with signs of hypoperfusion (lactate > 2 mmol/L, cold extremities, oliguria).
> - Impending respiratory exhaustion / severe hypoxia refractory to non-invasive ventilation.
> - Hemodynamically unstable ventricular arrhythmias (VT/VF) or new high-grade AV block.
> - New harsh systolic murmur with sudden pulmonary edema (papillary muscle rupture / VSD).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
> - Acute Pulmonary Embolism
> - Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS)
> - Massive Myocardial Infarction
> - Cardiac Tamponade
> - Tension Pneumothorax
>
> **Most Likely**
> - COPD / Asthma Exacerbation
> - Severe Pneumonia / Sepsis
> - Acute Kidney Injury with volume overload
>
> **Less Common**
> - Acute Severe Valvular Regurgitation (endocarditis, chordae rupture)
> - Cirrhotic ascites with hydrothorax

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
> - **Hemodynamic Target:** SBP $\ge 90\text{ mmHg}$, MAP $> 65\text{ mmHg}$, heart rate 60–100 bpm, SpO2 $\ge 92\%$.
> - **Decongestion Target:** Net negative fluid balance of $1\text{--}2\text{ L/day}$, complete resolution of orthopnea, JVP $< 8\text{ cm H₂O}$, absence of pulmonary crackles and peripheral edema.
> - **Organ Perfusion Target:** Serum lactate $< 2\text{ mmol/L}$, urine output $> 0.5\text{ mL/kg/h}$, stabilization of renal function.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (Resuscitation & Flash Edema)**
> - **Apply Non-Invasive Positive Pressure Ventilation (BiPAP / CPAP)** immediately for acute pulmonary edema or respiratory distress (RR > 25, SpO2 < 90%) to recruit collapsed alveoli, reduce work of breathing, and decrease LV preload/afterload.
> - **Administer IV Nitroglycerin Infusion (10–200 mcg/min)** STAT if SBP > 110 mmHg (especially in hypertensive crisis / flash pulmonary edema) to rapidly lower LV filling pressures and reduce pulmonary congestion.
> - **Administer Supplemental Oxygen** only if SpO2 < 90% or PaO2 < 60 mmHg to correct hypoxemia.
>
> **First-Line Decongestion ("Warm & Wet")**
> - **Administer IV Loop Diuretic Bolus (Furosemide 1–2.5x the chronic oral home dose IV, or 40–80 mg IV if naïve)** within 60 minutes of arrival to initiate prompt natriuresis and venodilation.
> - **Reassess Diuretic Response at 2 and 6 Hours** (spot urine sodium $> 50\text{--}70\text{ mEq/L}$ at 2h or hourly urine output $> 100\text{--}150\text{ mL/h}$) to identify diuretic resistance early.
> - **Double the IV Loop Diuretic Dose or Add Sequential Nephron Blockade (e.g., Metolazone 2.5–5 mg PO or IV Chlorothiazide)** if diuretic response at 2–6 hours is inadequate to overcome resistance.
>
> **Management of Hypoperfusion / Cardiogenic Shock ("Cold & Wet" or "Cold & Dry")**
> - **Initiate IV Inotrope (Dobutamine 2–20 mcg/kg/min or Milrinone 0.25–0.75 mcg/kg/min)** immediately in "Cold & Wet" patients with SBP 85–90 mmHg to augment myocardial contractility and improve forward cardiac output.
> - **Initiate IV Vasopressor (Norepinephrine 0.05–0.5 mcg/kg/min)** if severe hypotension (SBP < 85 mmHg or MAP < 65 mmHg) persists to restore coronary and systemic perfusion pressure.
> - **Administer Cautious Crystalloid Fluid Challenge (250–500 mL bolus)** only in "Cold & Dry" profile (hypovolemic, low JVP, no crackles) to restore adequate ventricular preload.
>
> **In-Hospital Optimization & GDMT Initiation ("Warm & Dry")**
> - **Continue Chronic GDMT (ACEi/ARB/ARNI, MRA, SGLT2i)** during admission unless frank shock, significant hyperkalemia, or severe acute kidney injury occurs.
> - **Initiate SGLT2 Inhibitor (Empagliflozin 10 mg or Dapagliflozin 10 mg daily)** early in-hospital once hemodynamically stable to reduce all-cause mortality, cardiovascular death, and rehospitalization.
> - **Initiate / Up-titrate Oral Beta-Blocker (Bisoprolol, Carvedilol, or Metoprolol Succinate)** at low doses *only after* the patient is fully decongested and off IV inotropes for at least 24 hours.
>
> **Disposition**
> - **Admit to ICU/CCU** if requiring inotropes, vasopressors, invasive arterial line, or non-invasive/invasive mechanical ventilation.
> - **Admit to Telemetry/Step-down** for standard decongestion and serial weight/electrolyte monitoring.

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
> - **Avoid Routine IV Morphine/Opioids** in acute heart failure to prevent increased risk of mechanical ventilation, ICU admission, and elevated in-hospital mortality.
> - **Avoid Inotropes (Dobutamine/Milrinone)** in normotensive, well-perfused "Warm & Wet" patients to prevent lethal arrhythmias, myocardial ischemia, and increased long-term mortality.
> - **Avoid Vasodilators (Nitroglycerin/Nitroprusside)** if SBP < 90 mmHg, severe aortic stenosis, or recent phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor use to prevent profound, refractory shock.
> - **Avoid Starting New Beta-Blockers** during active decompensation or congestion to prevent worsening acute heart failure and cardiogenic collapse.
> - **Avoid Abrupt Cessation of Diuretics upon Mild Creatinine Rise** if the patient remains overtly congested ("pseudo-worsening renal function") to ensure complete decongestion.

> [!error]- Complications
> - Cardiogenic shock and multi-organ failure (hepatic congestion/shock liver, ischemic bowel).
> - Type 1 Cardiorenal Syndrome (acute renal tubular/hemodynamic dysfunction).
> - Severe electrolyte disturbances (hypokalemia, hyponatremia, hypomagnesemia from aggressive diuresis).
> - Ventricular arrhythmias (VT/VF) and sudden cardiac death.
> - Hospital-acquired infections and ventilator-associated pneumonia.

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 68-year-old female with a history of HFrEF (EF 30%) presents to the emergency department with severe orthopnea, dyspnea at rest, and 4 kg weight gain over 5 days. Physical exam reveals SBP 165/95 mmHg, HR 105 bpm, JVP of 12 cm H₂O, bilateral bibasilar crackles, and 2+ pretibial edema (Warm & Wet profile). Point-of-care ultrasound confirms diffuse bilateral B-lines. She is started on BiPAP, given IV furosemide 80 mg (2x her home dose), and started on low-dose IV nitroglycerin infusion, leading to rapid symptomatic relief and 3 liters of diuresis over 12 hours.

> [!info] Personal Experience
> - The most common reason for early readmission is **incomplete decongestion at discharge**. Never discharge a patient based solely on symptom improvement; ensure normalization of JVP, resolution of edema, and transition to a stable oral diuretic regimen for at least 24 hours.
> - In hypertensive flash pulmonary edema, high-dose IV nitroglycerin boluses (e.g., 1–2 mg IV q3–5min) can resolve pulmonary congestion and eliminate the need for intubation within minutes.
