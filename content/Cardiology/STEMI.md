> [!abstract] STEMI (ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction)
> Transmural myocardial ischemic necrosis resulting from acute, complete, and persistent occlusion of a coronary artery. This is a medical emergency requiring immediate reperfusion therapy.

> [!note] Pathophysiology
> - Rupture or erosion of a vulnerable atherosclerotic plaque triggers platelet aggregation and formation of an occlusive, fibrin-rich thrombus.
> - Complete cessation of blood flow leads to rapid transmural transmural ischemia and subsequent necrosis.

> [!Tip]- Clinical PEARLS
> - **"Time is Muscle":** Total ischemic time determines infarct size; rapid recognition and cath lab activation are paramount.
> - **Do NOT wait for troponin results** to activate the cath lab if diagnostic ST-elevation is present on ECG with appropriate symptoms.
> - **Inferior MI Caution:** In patients with inferior STEMI (leads II, III, aVF), suspect right ventricular (RV) involvement. Avoid nitrates and diuretics as these patients are highly preload-dependent and may develop profound hypotension. Get a right-sided ECG (V4R).
> - **Sgarbossa Criteria:** Use modified Sgarbossa criteria to diagnose STEMI in the presence of LBBB or paced rhythm.

> [!attention]- Clinical Presentation
> **History**
> - "Crushing" retrosternal pressure, heaviness, or squeezing, often lasting >30 minutes, unrelieved by rest/nitrates.
> - Radiation to left arm, jaw, neck, or back.
> - Profound autonomic symptoms: Diaphoresis, nausea, vomiting, impending sense of doom.
>
> **Physical**
> - Patient often appears anxious, pale, and diaphoretic (Levine's sign).
> - Signs of heart failure: Jugular venous distension, pulmonary rales, S3 gallop.
> - Signs of cardiogenic shock: Hypotension, tachycardia, cool/clammy extremities, altered mental status.
> - New pansystolic murmur may indicate ventricular septal rupture or severe acute mitral regurgitation.

> [!check]- Diagnostic Criteria / Confirmation Requirements
> - **Symptoms compatible with myocardial ischemia** (e.g., chest pain, dyspnea).
> - **PLUS New ST-segment elevation at the J-point in ≥2 contiguous leads** with cutoffs:
>     - ≥1 mm (0.1 mV) in all leads other than V2–V3.
>     - Leads V2–V3: ≥2 mm in men ≥40y; ≥2.5 mm in men <40y; ≥1.5 mm in women.
> - *OR* **STEMI equivalents:** New/presumed new LBBB meeting Sgarbossa criteria OR isolated posterior MI findings (ST depression V1–V4 with tall R waves).

> [!fail]- Further Investigations
> **Initial (STAT)**
> - **12-lead ECG:** Perform and interpret within 10 minutes of first medical contact (FMC). Serial ECGs if initial is non-diagnostic but suspicion remains high.
> - **Cardiac Troponin (hs-cTn):** Draw baseline, but **do not wait for results** to initiate reperfusion strategy.
> - **CBC, BMP, Coags (PT/INR, PTT):** Baseline labs (do not delay cath).
>
> **Imaging & Structural**
> - **Bedside Transthoracic Echo:** Only if diagnosis is uncertain or hemodynamic instability exists, to assess wall motion abnormalities and rule out mechanical complications/dissection. **Must not delay angiography.**

> [!bug]- Red Flags
> - Cardiogenic shock / Hemodynamic instability.
> - Electrical storm (sustained VT/VF).
> - Acute pulmonary edema.
> - Mechanical complications (free wall rupture, VSD, papillary muscle rupture).

> [!todo]- Differentials
> **Life-Threatening & Can't Miss**
> - Aortic Dissection (must rule out if suggestive history/exam before anticoagulation/lytics)
> - Massive Pulmonary Embolism
> - Tension Pneumothorax
> - Esophageal Rupture (Boerhaave's syndrome)
>
> **Most Likely Mimics**
> - Acute Pericarditis (diffuse ST elevation, PR depression)
> - Prinzmetal's (variant) Angina
> - Early Repolarization (benign variant)
> - Stress Cardiomyopathy (Takotsubo)

> [!summary]- Management
> **Treatment Goals**
> - **Primary Target:** Achieve rapid reperfusion to minimize infarct size and reduce mortality (Goal FMC-to-device <90 mins).
> - **Secondary Target:** Prevent recurrent ischemia, deadly arrhythmias, and mechanical complications.
>
> **Immediate / Emergencies (Pre-Cath Stabilization)**
> - **ACTIVATE CATH LAB STAT** immediately upon identification of STEMI on ECG.
> - **Chewable Aspirin (162–325 mg)** immediately to inhibit platelet aggregation (ensure patient chews it).
> - **Supplemental Oxygen** only if SaO2 <90% or in respiratory distress.
> - **Sublingual Nitroglycerin (0.4 mg q5min x3)** for ischemic pain if SBP >90 mmHg (avoid in inferior/RV MI or recent PDE-5 inhibitor use).
> - **IV Fentanyl or Morphine** for severe pain refractory to nitrates to reduce sympathetic surge (but not in RV STEMI).
>
> **Reperfusion Strategy (Choose ONE based on capability)**
> - **Primary Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI):** Preferred strategy if available within 120 minutes of FMC. (Goal: Door-to-balloon <90 min).
> - **Fibrinolysis (e.g., Tenecteplase):** Indicated only if primary PCI cannot be performed within 120 minutes of FMC and no contraindications exist. (Goal: Door-to-needle <30 min), followed by transfer to PCI center in a delay of 2 to 24 hours IF SUCCESS OF FIBRINOLYSIS.
>
> **Adjunctive Medical Therapy (Before/During Reperfusion)**
> - **P2Y12 Inhibitor Loading Dose** ASAP. Preferred: Ticagrelor (180 mg) or Prasugrel (60 mg); use Clopidogrel (600 mg) if lytics used or potent agents contraindicated.
> - **Parenteral Anticoagulation** initiated immediately. UFH or Bivalirudin typically used in cath lab; Enoxaparin often used with fibrinolysis.
> - **High-Intensity Statin (e.g., Atorvastatin 80 mg)** ASAP regardless of baseline lipid levels.
> - **IV Beta-Blockers** only for acute hypertensive / tachycardia control in stable patients without signs of HF or shock risk. Avoid early routine use.
> - Post reperfusion meds at least for 12 months: Aspirin for life, P2Y12 inhibitor, High-Intensity Statins for life, Beta-Blockers, ACE Inhibitors or ARBs, Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonists (MRA). 

> [!warning]- Contraindications & Pitfalls
> - **Avoid Nitrates and Opioids** in suspected right ventricular infarction (Inferior STEMI with ST-elevation in V4R) to prevent profound hypotension from preload reduction. Treat hypotension with IV fluid boluses.
> - **Avoid Fibrinolysis** in patients with absolute contraindications (e.g., prior intracranial hemorrhage, ischemic stroke <3mo, active internal bleeding, aortic dissection).
> - **Avoid Beta-Blockers** in patients with acute heart failure, signs of low output state, or risk factors for shock (age >70, HR >110, SBP <120) to prevent cardiogenic shock.

> [!error]- Complications
> - Cardiogenic Shock (highest risk in anterior MI)
> - Severe Arrhythmias (VF, VT, complete heart block, new AF)
> - Acute Pump Failure / Pulmonary Edema
> - Mechanical Complications (rupture of free wall, septum, or papillary muscle—usually day 3–7)
> - LV Apical Thrombus and embolic stroke

> [!success]- Typical Scenario
> A 55-year-old male smoker presents with 45 minutes of crushing substernal chest pain and marked diaphoresis. ECG performed within 5 minutes of arrival shows 3mm ST-elevation in leads V2-V4. A "Code STEMI" is immediately activated. He is given 325mg chewable aspirin and a loading dose of ticagrelor, and transferred directly to the cardiac catheterization lab for primary PCI, achieving successful reperfusion within 75 minutes of arrival.
